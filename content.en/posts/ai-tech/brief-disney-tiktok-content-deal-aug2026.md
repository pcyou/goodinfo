## [Brief] Disney Agrees Deal to Let TikTokers Use Its Films and TV Shows

**[Core Summary]** Walt Disney Company has reached a content licensing agreement with TikTok, allowing select creators to use clips from Disney films and TV shows in their videos. This partnership marks a major breakthrough in copyright between traditional media giants and short-video platforms, expected to reshape the user-generated content (UGC) ecosystem.

### Event Details

According to BBC reports, the agreement will allow qualifying TikTok creators to use short clips from Disney movies, TV series, and animated works. Specific licensing scope and usage conditions have not been fully disclosed, but are expected to cover core IPs including Disney, Pixar, Marvel, and Star Wars.

This is Disney's first content licensing agreement of this scale with a short-video platform. Previously, Disney primarily protected its intellectual property through official channels and strict copyright management. This partnership reflects traditional media's strategic adaptation to new distribution models in the digital age.

### Panoramic Perspective

This agreement has far-reaching implications for the content creation ecosystem. TikTok users have long faced deletion and account penalties for using copyrighted content. Disney's licensing will provide legal protection for creators while bringing the brand enormous social media exposure.

From a business model perspective, this "licensing + revenue sharing" model could become an industry standard. Creators gain legal rights to use materials, while copyright holders benefit from platform traffic. YouTube's Content ID system has validated this model's feasibility, and the Disney-TikTok partnership takes it to new heights.

However, licensing scope and restrictions will be critical. If limitations are too strict (e.g., only allowing specific clips, prohibiting commercial use), practical effects may be limited. The creator community's response will determine the model's long-term sustainability.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Creator Response**: The TikTok creator community welcomed this news, though some worry licensing conditions may restrict creative freedom. "What we need is flexible usage rights, not rigid permission lists," said one creator with millions of followers.

**Industry Analysis**: Media analysts note Disney's move responds to competitive pressure. Netflix and HBO Max have established more open content ecosystems on short-video platforms, and Disney needs to catch up to maintain cultural influence.
