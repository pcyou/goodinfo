## Core Summary

A collective lawsuit filed by multiple US states against Meta officially went to trial on August 19. The plaintiffs allege that Meta's Facebook and Instagram platforms deliberately addicted underage users through algorithmic recommendations and product design, causing serious harm to their mental health. Meta argued in court that the concept of "social media addiction" itself is invalid, attempting to negate the core allegation at its root. This case is considered a landmark lawsuit in US tech regulation, and its verdict could profoundly impact the operational models of the entire social media industry.

## Event Details

**Plaintiff's Argument: Algorithm Design Deliberately Induces Addiction**

The plaintiff's legal team stated in opening arguments that Meta knowingly exposed its products to harm minors while still employing gambling-like design elements to extend user engagement time. Plaintiff witness Arturo Béjar, a former Meta engineer, testified that internal company data showed a positive correlation between teenage users' time spent on Instagram and depressive symptoms, but management chose to ignore these warning signals.

The plaintiffs are requesting the court to force Meta to make structural changes to Instagram and Facebook, including: eliminating algorithmic recommendations for underage users, removing addictive design features like "infinite scroll," and establishing an independent youth protection oversight mechanism.

**Meta Defense: Denies Existence of Addiction Concept**

Meta's legal team adopted an aggressive strategy in their defense, directly questioning the scientific validity of "social media addiction." Expert witnesses hired by the company stated that academia currently lacks a unified definition of "social media addiction" and cannot prove a causal relationship between social media use and mental health issues.

Meta also emphasized that the company has introduced multiple youth protection measures in recent years, including default privacy settings, usage time reminders, and parental monitoring tools. Company lawyers argued that plaintiffs failed to prove Meta's product design is the primary cause of youth mental health issues, noting that family environment, school pressure, and other factors are equally important.

**Trial Focus: Algorithm Transparency and Platform Responsibility**

The core dispute in the trial centers on whether social media platforms should be held responsible for consequences caused by their algorithmic recommendations. Plaintiffs argue that Meta's algorithms actively push harmful content to underage users, and the platform should bear corresponding responsibility. Meta invokes Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act, claiming platforms are not liable for user-generated content.

Legal experts point out this case could become a turning point in redefining tech platform responsibility boundaries. If plaintiffs win, it would set a precedent allowing states to file similar lawsuits against tech companies' product designs.

## Panoramic Perspective

This case's significance extends far beyond the individual lawsuit—it touches on the most core ethical issue of the digital age: while tech companies pursue user growth and commercial profits, should they bear social responsibility for protecting underage users?

From an industry impact perspective, if the court rules against Meta and mandates product design changes, it will create a chain reaction across the entire social media industry. Platforms like TikTok, YouTube, and Snapchat could face similar lawsuits and be forced to adjust their core business models. Widely adopted product designs like "infinite scroll," algorithmic recommendations, and notification pushes could all face legal challenges.

From a regulatory trend perspective, this case reflects the global tightening of regulation on tech giants. The EU has already passed the Digital Services Act and Digital Markets Act, imposing strict oversight on tech platforms. While the US has not yet passed comprehensive federal tech regulation legislation, states are filling regulatory gaps through litigation and state-level legislation.

Notably, this case also reveals information asymmetry within the tech industry. Former engineers' testimony shows Meta's internal research long ago discovered potential harm to teenagers from its products, but this information was not publicly disclosed. If this "knowing non-disclosure" behavior is legally confirmed, it will severely damage public trust in the tech industry.

Multiple parent rights organizations expressed support for the plaintiffs' position, believing social media platforms must be responsible for the youth mental health crisis.

**Tech Industry Position**

The tech industry association issued a statement emphasizing the industry has already made significant efforts in youth protection. The statement noted that forcing product design changes could have unintended consequences, including limiting teenagers' access to beneficial information and social connections. Industry representatives believe regulation should be achieved through legislation rather than litigation.
