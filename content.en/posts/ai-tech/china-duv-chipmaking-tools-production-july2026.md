## Core Summary

According to an exclusive Reuters report, China has started mass production of homegrown immersion deep ultraviolet (DUV) lithography equipment. This breakthrough marks a major milestone in China's push for semiconductor manufacturing independence and could ease its long-standing reliance on foreign suppliers such as the Netherlands-based ASML. The source revealed that the equipment has entered actual production stages, though it has not yet reached the most advanced process nodes.

## Event Details

Reuters, citing sources familiar with the matter, reported that Chinese domestic companies have successfully launched batch production of immersion DUV lithography tools. Such equipment is essential for manufacturing advanced chips and has long been dominated by the Dutch company ASML. Immersion DUV technology can be used to produce chips at process nodes ranging from 14nm to 28nm, widely applied in automotive electronics, consumer devices, and industrial control.

The report notes that while domestic equipment still lags behind international benchmarks in precision and stability, it can already meet some commercial production needs. The Chinese government has in recent years aggressively promoted semiconductor self-sufficiency strategies, channeling substantial funding through national integrated circuit investment funds to support local equipment makers in overcoming technological barriers.

The disclosure of this mass production news comes as the global semiconductor industry faces geopolitical restructuring. The United States continues to tighten export controls on chips to China, restricting the flow of advanced lithography equipment and technology. The Dutch government, under U.S. pressure, has also further limited ASML's exports of high-end lithography machines to China.

Industry analysts believe that while domestic DUV equipment mass production cannot fully replace imports in the short term, it holds significant strategic importance for safeguarding domestic chip supply chains. Particularly in mature process nodes, scaled application of domestic equipment will help reduce dependence on external supply.

The global semiconductor industry is undergoing profound transformation. Countries are intensifying domestic investments to mitigate supply chain concentration risks. As the world's largest chip consumer market, China's self-sufficiency progress will deeply influence the global industrial landscape.

## Panoramic Perspective

The mass production of domestic immersion DUV lithography equipment represents an important milestone in China's semiconductor self-sufficiency strategy. Lithography machines are often called the crown jewel of the semiconductor industry, with extreme technical complexity spanning precision optics, fluid dynamics, and automated control. For years, the global lithography market has been nearly monopolized by ASML, making it a key lever in international chip competition.

From a supply chain perspective, the 14nm to 28nm process nodes covered by immersion DUV technology, while not at the cutting edge, represent the most widely used mature processes. Automotive chips, microcontrollers, power management ICs, and IoT sensors all depend heavily on these nodes. China's enormous demand in these downstream applications provides ample market space and iterative optimization opportunities for domestic equipment.

Notably, semiconductor equipment self-sufficiency involves not just hardware breakthroughs but full-chain support including software, processes, and materials. The localization progress of peripheral elements such as photoresists, photomasks, and inspection equipment equally determines the overall controllability of the industrial chain. The disclosure of this DUV equipment mass production suggests China may have also achieved coordinated progress across multiple supporting areas.

From a global perspective, the semiconductor industry is forming a multipolar pattern. The United States, Europe, Japan, South Korea, and mainland China are all increasing domestic investments. While this trend strengthens regional supply chain resilience, it may also lead to duplicated investment and efficiency losses, ultimately raising global chip costs. Finding the right balance between self-sufficiency and international cooperation will remain a long-term challenge for policymakers worldwide.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Reuters analysis** considers China's domestic DUV equipment mass production an important step toward semiconductor self-sufficiency, but notes considerable distance remains before fully replacing international leading equipment. The report highlights that equipment yield and stability will be key tests for commercial success.

**Semiconductor industry observers** point out that the breakthrough in immersion DUV technology means China has stronger autonomous supply capability in mature process nodes, which will enhance bargaining power for domestic chip makers and potentially reshape competition in certain market segments.

**International strategic research institutions** suggest that U.S. and allied export control policies have, to some extent, accelerated China's semiconductor self-sufficiency process. While restrictions create short-term obstacles for China's advanced node development, they may long-term foster an alternative supply chain independent of Western technology systems.

**Domestic industry insiders** expressed cautious optimism about the mass production news, viewing it as evidence of technological breakthroughs and reflecting the sustained impact of national investment in critical core technology research. They also acknowledge that in more advanced fields like extreme ultraviolet lithography, the gap with international leaders still requires time to close.
