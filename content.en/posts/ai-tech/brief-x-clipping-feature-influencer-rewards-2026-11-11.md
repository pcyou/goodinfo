## Key Facts

X announced on Monday a built-in clipping tool that lets users trim a moment out of any video, add their own take and post it without leaving the platform. Every clip carries a "Clip from" label that links back to the original video, letting users see more about the creator and their content. Both creators and clippers can earn from views through the platform's Original Content Rewards program, which pays on specific levels of audience engagement and is limited to eligible creators. Influencers increasingly pay specialist firms to handle clipping, and large companies now use such services for marketing campaigns, with political campaigns using the technique as well. X added subscriber-only "exclusive threads" in March and launched a livestreaming studio with a $1 million creator fund in July.

## Wider Perspective

Clipping is not technically difficult. The hard part is turning the redistribution of long-form content from something creators do on their own into something a platform can pay for. What X has done is pull a form of labour that has long been informal within creator circles into a revenue-sharing structure, placing the re-clipper and the original creator on the same payout chain. That is structurally different from the short-video ecosystem, where a recommendation engine decides distribution: X has no such video-ranking system, so it is giving creators tools and asking them to handle second-order distribution themselves.

The "Clip from" label is the detail worth noticing. It acknowledges attribution and routes traffic back to the original author's page, which in practice trades a slice of value for ecosystem legitimacy. The original creator gets visibility, the re-clipper gets paid labour, and the platform gets a supply of material without having to rebuild a recommendation engine.

Whether the arrangement holds depends on whether the rewards program is sustainable. Since influencers already pay specialist firms to handle clipping, that labour carries an established market price. For X's program to attract the work, its per-view payout has to approach or exceed that price, or users have no reason to move work they are already doing elsewhere onto the platform — and on that front, X is competing against rivals with far deeper pockets and far stronger algorithms.
