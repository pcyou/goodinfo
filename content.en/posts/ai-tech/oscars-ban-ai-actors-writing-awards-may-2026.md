## Oscars Ban AI Actors and AI-Generated Writing from Awards Eligibility

The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences has officially announced that AI-generated performances and screenplays will no longer be eligible for Oscar consideration. The decision marks Hollywood's first definitive boundary on human creativity amid the rapid encroachment of AI technology into film production.

Under the new rules, any performance generated by AI — including deepfake face replacements, AI-created virtual character performances, and fully algorithm-driven digital humans — will be ineligible for acting category awards. Additionally, screenplays written independently by AI or primarily generated by AI will be excluded from Best Original Screenplay and Best Adapted Screenplay categories.

"The Oscars exist to celebrate the creativity and extraordinary craft of human artists," the Academy stated. "While AI technology offers new tools for filmmaking, the core value of our awards has always been the recognition of human creative spirit."

The decision has drawn polarized reactions across the industry. Supporters argue the move protects the legitimate rights of actors and writers, shielding the creative sector from AI disruption. The Writers Guild of America (WGA) released a statement calling it a vital affirmation of "the irreplaceable nature of human creativity."

Critics, however, point out that AI-assisted creation has already become integral to modern filmmaking. From visual effects to screenplay polishing, AI tools are deeply embedded in the production pipeline. A blanket ban on AI eligibility may create gray areas — defining the boundary between "AI-generated" and "AI-assisted" will prove challenging.

Notably, the new rules do not prohibit AI use in film production; they simply exclude AI-generated creative outputs from award consideration. Studios can still use AI for visual effects, post-production, and other technical work, but these contributions cannot be recognized in performance or writing categories.

The policy comes against a backdrop of rapid AI adoption in the entertainment industry. Between 2025 and 2026, several Hollywood studios began experimenting with AI-generated characters and scenes, triggering strong pushback from actors and writers. Previous strikes had already yielded partial agreements on AI usage, and the Academy's new rules represent a further extension of that trend.

Industry analysts suggest the policy could have far-reaching implications for future film production. On one hand, it will encourage studios to use AI technology more cautiously; on the other, it may drive the industry to establish more comprehensive AI usage standards and certification systems.
