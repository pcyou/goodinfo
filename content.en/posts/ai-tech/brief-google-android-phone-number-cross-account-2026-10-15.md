**[Core Summary]**
Media reports indicate that Google is quietly linking users' phone numbers across multiple signed-in accounts on Android for identity verification purposes. While the move simplifies verification flows, it has prompted questions from privacy groups and lawmakers about user awareness and data control.

**[Event Details]**
- **Technical Details**: When users sign in to multiple Google accounts on Android, the system automatically uses the bound phone number for cross-account identity verification without requiring additional user authorization.
- **User Reaction**: Privacy groups point out that this approach makes it hard for users to tell whether the number in one account has been linked to other accounts, with potential implications for account security and privacy.
- **Company Response**: Google emphasizes the mechanism is intended to improve account security and cross-device sign-in experience, and that an opt-out option exists, though critics say it is buried too deeply.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**
The episode reflects the ongoing tension between "security convenience" and "user control" at large tech platforms. Cross-account identity linkage improves the user experience, but without transparent notice and an easily accessible opt-out, it can be perceived as a stealth expansion of data usage that may attract stricter regulatory scrutiny and more granular compliance requirements.
