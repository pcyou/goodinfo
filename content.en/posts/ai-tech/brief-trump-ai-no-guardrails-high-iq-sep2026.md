## [Brief] Trump Says AI Doesn't Need Guardrails, Only a 'High IQ' President

Former President Donald Trump has publicly argued that artificial intelligence does not need formal regulatory guardrails, asserting that strong presidential judgment alone is sufficient to oversee the rapidly developing technology. The remarks, reported by NBC News, came amid intensifying debate in Washington over whether Congress should codify mandatory safety requirements for frontier AI models, and stand in sharp contrast to calls from Anthropic, OpenAI's safety team and several Senate Democrats for binding oversight mechanisms.

## Panoramic Analysis

Trump's remarks shift the central question of AI governance from what kind of technical safety standards should be established to whether personal presidential judgment alone can replace institutionalized regulation. The shift carries special meaning at a key juncture in the U.S. AI regulatory legislative process. It sits in direct contrast to Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark's simultaneous mandatory kill switch initiative — the former emphasizes institutionalized and technical safety mechanisms, the latter emphasizes personalized political judgment. This divergence will expose the U.S. AI regulatory legislative process to more complex political horse trading, and related legislation is expected to see more contentious back-and-forth over the next 12 to 18 months. On the international coordination side, the EU AI Office has made clear that it will continue to push the second-stage implementation rules of the AI Act, and the direction of the mandatory framework differs significantly from the U.S. Republican policy preference, which could further widen the policy gap between the U.S. and Europe on AI governance standards.

## Comparison of Multiple Viewpoints

**The Trump camp** emphasizes the sufficiency of presidential personal judgment and the flexibility of executive orders for AI governance, opposing a mandatory legislative framework that would weigh on executive efficiency. **Anthropic and several Democratic senators** insist that AI governance requires institutionalized and auditable mandatory safety standards. **The OpenAI safety team** sits in the middle, emphasizing graceful degradation mechanisms and cross-vendor coordination. **The EU AI Office** has made clear that it will continue to push the second-stage implementation rules of the AI Act, and the direction of the mandatory framework differs significantly from the U.S. Republican policy preference. **The academic and civil society community** broadly warn that the absence of institutionalized guardrails will significantly raise the development risk of frontier AI models and could trigger irreversible damage to the public interest.
