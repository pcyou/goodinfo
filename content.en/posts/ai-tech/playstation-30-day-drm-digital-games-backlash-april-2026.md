# Sony's PlayStation Digital Games Hit With 30-Day Online Check Requirement, Sparking Player Outrage

> Since late April 2026, multiple PS4 and PS5 users have reported that newly purchased digital games require an online license check every 30 days, rendering games unplayable without periodic internet verification, triggering a massive backlash from the gaming community.

---

A controversy over digital game ownership is rapidly spreading through the global PlayStation community. Since late April, a growing number of PS4 and PS5 users have reported that newly purchased digital games now display a **30-day online verification timer**, and if the console fails to connect to PlayStation Network for a license check within that period, the purchased game will **become unplayable**.

## How It Unfolded

According to GameSpot, the policy appears to have been **quietly introduced** in early April 2026 through a system update, with Sony making no official announcement. Multiple players discovered that after purchasing new digital games, a countdown "license expiry" prompt appeared in their game library, requiring them to connect to PlayStation Network at least once every 30 days for verification.

VICE magazine's reporting further revealed that PlayStation customer support representatives have given **conflicting answers** when responding to player inquiries. Some representatives confirmed the existence of the 30-day verification mechanism, while others suggested it might be a system bug, stating that Sony has not officially released any policy changes.

## Player Backlash

The news has sparked intense frustration within the gaming community. Many players argue that this practice effectively **strips consumers of control over content they have purchased**.

"I paid for the game, and now I can't play my own stuff because I can't connect to the internet?" wrote one Reddit user in the discussion thread. "This isn't just about DRM — it's telling us: you never really owned what you bought."

On social media, large numbers of players are calling for a collective boycott of Sony and urging legislators to protect consumer rights for digital goods.

## Industry Implications

Analysts note that if Sony's policy is confirmed and maintained long-term, it could have significant repercussions across the gaming industry:

- **Digital game sales may suffer**: Players may become hesitant to purchase digital titles, opting for physical copies instead
- **Consumer rights debate escalates**: The question of digital goods ownership could once again become a legislative priority
- **Competitive landscape shifts**: Rivals such as Microsoft Xbox and Nintendo may seize the opportunity to emphasize their more lenient digital rights policies

As of now, Sony has not issued an **official statement** on the matter. Kotaku reports that players are closely monitoring whether Sony will respond to the controversy or reverse the policy.

## Context

In recent years, digital rights management (DRM) policies in the gaming industry have been a persistent source of controversy. While DRM is designed to protect developers' intellectual property, overly restrictive measures often harm consumers' legitimate rights. This incident once again highlights the fundamental tension between "ownership" and "licensing" in the digital age.
