**Key Facts**

Meta removed multiple Instagram accounts linked to the 'No Kings' national protest day on October 17, triggering widespread backlash in US political circles and on social media. Critics accuse Meta of systematically suppressing a specific political cause, while Meta says the accounts were actioned for platform-policy violations.

**What We Know**

On October 17, the 'No Kings' national protest unfolded across multiple US cities, with participants opposing the current administration's policy direction and governing style. During the protest, several Instagram accounts that had long organised and amplified the movement — some with hundreds of thousands of followers — were taken down by Meta, and the action quickly became a trending topic on social media.

Meidas News and other US outlets reported the timing and the scale of the takedowns. Critics argue that when big tech platforms act on content during election cycles and major political moments, they materially affect public discourse and civic participation. Meta has cited its community rules and automated review systems, saying the removals were not motivated by the protest theme but by policy violations.

The episode lands on top of a broader content-moderation controversy and has drawn attention from members of Congress, civic groups and free-speech organisations. It is likely to be cited as a leading example in the next round of US legislative debates on tech-platform accountability.

**Wider Perspective**

Content-moderation decisions by large tech platforms at major political moments carry a strong amplification effect on public discourse. Even when a takedown is procedurally compliant, it is easily read as a statement of political posture by the platform itself, which in turn raises the bar for transparency and external scrutiny. The episode is likely to feed into a renewed push in the US for tech-platform accountability legislation.
