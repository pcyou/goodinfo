## 📰 Article

### The End of an Era

**Ask.com**, formerly known as **Ask Jeeves**, the pioneering search engine that once competed with Google in the early days of the internet, officially shut down its search service on May 1, 2026. Parent company **IAC** posted a brief farewell message on the website: "As IAC continues to sharpen its focus, we have made the decision to discontinue our search business, which includes Ask.com. After 25 years of answering the world's questions, Ask.com officially closed on May 1, 2026."

### From Butler to Being Replaced by AI

Ask Jeeves was founded in 1996 by Garrett Gruener and David Warthen. It was known for its distinctive "English butler" persona — users could ask questions in natural language, and the system would attempt to provide precise answers rather than just a list of links. This concept was remarkably ahead of its time.

After rebranding to Ask.com in 2001, the platform once became the world's fourth-largest search engine. However, as Google rose to dominance and search algorithms evolved rapidly, Ask.com's market share steadily declined.

Ironically, Ask Jeeves' shutdown comes precisely at a moment when **AI chatbots are fundamentally transforming how people search**. The Verge's Liz Lopatto recently wrote about the "Ask Jeeves-ification of online search" through AI chatbots — when AI can answer questions in natural language, Ask Jeeves' vision from 30 years ago has finally been realized, just not by Ask Jeeves itself.

### Another Page in Internet History

Ask.com's closure marks the departure of yet another internet pioneer. From Yahoo!'s decline to AOL's transformation, and now Ask.com's shutdown, early internet companies are gradually fading from the landscape.

IAC has been steadily streamlining its portfolio in recent years, spinning off companies like Vimeo and Match Group. The closure of its search business represents another step in this strategic refocusing.
