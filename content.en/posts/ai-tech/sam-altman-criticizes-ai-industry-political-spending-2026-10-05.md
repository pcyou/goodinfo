[Key Summary]
OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has publicly stated that the AI industry's massive spending on political lobbying and PR has not produced the regulatory outcomes it hoped for and is "clearly not working." He urged peers to rethink strategy and invest more in substantive engagement with lawmakers.

[Background]
Altman's remarks come as California, New York and Texas are preparing a fresh wave of AI safety and transparency legislation. Industry alliances have spent hundreds of millions of dollars on lobbying to prevent overly strict rules, but several bills have nonetheless advanced. Altman acknowledged that money alone is not enough to influence complex legislative dynamics.

[Multiple Perspectives]
Some peers agree with Altman, saying the AI industry must invest more in public communication and technical transparency. Critics counter that Altman's statement may be aimed at seizing the moral high ground under the guise of "industry reflection" to gain more leverage for OpenAI itself in the regulatory game.

[Big Picture]
Altman's comments mark a "strategic reflection" moment for the US AI industry in its regulatory battle. More than twenty jurisdictions worldwide have adopted or drafted dedicated AI laws since the start of 2026, and the EU AI Act has moved into comprehensive enforcement. If AI companies continue to rely on traditional lobbying tactics, they may lose critical voice as the regulatory framework takes shape. Altman's pivot could push the wider industry to reassess its role in public policy.
