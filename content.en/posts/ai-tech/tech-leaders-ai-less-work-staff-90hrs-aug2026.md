## Core Summary

According to BBC reports, tech executives frequently claim that artificial intelligence will improve efficiency, reduce human workload, and give people more free time. However, the reality is quite different—employees at multiple tech companies report their working hours have extended to 90 hours per week. This contradiction has sparked deep reflection on AI's actual impact.

## Event Details

BBC's investigative report reveals a thought-provoking phenomenon:

**Executive Promises:** Tech leaders repeatedly emphasize in public that AI will automate repetitive tasks, freeing people from tedious work and giving them more time for creative work and personal life.

**Employee Reality:** However, voices from frontline workers tell a different story. Multiple interviewees report that AI tools haven't reduced workload but instead increased work intensity. They need to:

- Learn and adapt to new AI tools
- Verify and correct AI-generated content
- Handle complex problems AI cannot solve
- Complete more tasks in shorter timeframes

This "AI paradox" reflects the huge gap between technological promises and actual application.

## Broader Perspective

This phenomenon reveals a deeper issue in technological change: productivity gains don't necessarily translate to reduced working hours. Historically, every technological revolution promised "less working time," but reality often brought increased work intensity or transformed work content.

AI adoption is reshaping work models in the tech industry. On one hand, companies expect AI to improve efficiency and reduce costs; on the other hand, employees face skill update pressure and blurred work boundaries. When AI handles some tasks, employees shift from "executors" to "supervisors" and "decision-makers," often accompanied by higher responsibility pressure and longer decision-making time.

More concerning, this extended working time may lead to long-term health issues and talent attrition. The tech industry already faces high-intensity work pressure; if AI tools don't genuinely improve work-life balance, it may exacerbate the industry's talent crisis.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Corporate Management View:** Many tech executives believe AI tool adoption requires a transition period, and short-term workload increase is normal. They expect efficiency to significantly improve as employees gain proficiency and AI capabilities advance.

**Employee Position:** Frontline workers point out that AI tools have steep learning curves and many are immature, requiring substantial human intervention. Additionally, companies often raise performance expectations after introducing AI, doubling employee pressure.

**Labor Economist View:** Experts note AI's dual impact. Short-term may see "technological unemployment" coexist with "increased work intensity"; long-term requires redefining work value and distribution mechanisms to truly realize technological dividends.

**Social Observer:** Commentators note this reflects distorted technology application under capital logic—productivity gains from technological progress are converted to corporate profits rather than employee welfare.
