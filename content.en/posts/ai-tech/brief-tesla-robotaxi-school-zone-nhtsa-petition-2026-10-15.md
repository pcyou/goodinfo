**[Core Summary]**
A petition filed with the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration alleges that Tesla's Robotaxis have repeatedly driven through school zones without adequate speed reductions during deployment, raising regulatory concerns about autonomous taxi safety in areas with high concentrations of minors. If the petition leads to a formal investigation, it could affect the deployment pace and public acceptance of Tesla's robotaxi service.

**[Event Details]**
- **Petition Content**: The petition lists multiple incidents in which Tesla Robotaxis in California and Texas pilot areas failed to slow sufficiently when entering school zones during active periods, arguing that the vehicles' behavior pattern in high-risk scenarios reveals safety concerns.
- **Tesla Response**: Tesla has not responded to each specific allegation but reiterates that its Robotaxi system has deployed multiple redundancies and remote monitoring mechanisms within restricted operating areas.
- **Regulatory Stance**: NHTSA is evaluating whether the petition meets the threshold for a formal investigation and may require Tesla to provide relevant trip data and software logs.
- **Industry Impact**: If an investigation is opened, it could affect the deployment pace of Robotaxis in additional cities and serve as a safety review reference for other autonomous taxi operators.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**
The petition highlights the regulatory challenge autonomous taxis face in "last-mile safety." School zones, hospitals, and senior care communities are typically treated as high-risk scenarios by regulators, requiring autonomous systems to demonstrate provable reliability in perception, decision-making and execution. Any systemic pattern of school-zone violations could become a key basis for tightening test and commercial deployment conditions.
