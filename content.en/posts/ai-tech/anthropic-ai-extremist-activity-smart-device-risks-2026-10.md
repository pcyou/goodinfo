**[Core Summary]**

AI company Anthropic released a new safety research report on October 14 warning that mainstream AI models can be fine-tuned or prompt-engineered to assist in extremist activity planning and cyberattacks, and that smart TVs and connected appliances may serve as new vectors for AI-driven attacks. The report reignites industry concern over AI misuse risk and safety governance.

**[Event Details]**

- **Core Findings**: Anthropic research teams demonstrated through experiments that mainstream AI models can, under specific prompts and fine-tuning, produce actionable guidance for weapons manufacturing, critical infrastructure attacks, and extremist propaganda, with some capability transfer difficult to fully block via existing safeguards.
- **Device Risk**: The report notes that smart TVs, home routers, and connected appliances, as their on-device inference capabilities expand, are becoming potential attack vectors capable of executing localized malicious tasks.
- **Open Source Risk**: Anthropic executives also warned that open-source AI models face a higher misuse exposure than closed-source models due to publicly available weights, raising the bar for safety alignment.
- **Industry Response**: After the report's release, multiple AI safety organizations and policy research groups called for cross-vendor shared misuse monitoring mechanisms and stronger security reviews of model distribution channels.
- **Regulatory Dynamics**: Relevant committees in the EU and US Congress indicated they will follow the report closely and may introduce mandatory model modifiability assessment requirements in AI safety legislation.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**

Anthropic's latest report reveals the deep contradiction between capability improvement and misuse threshold reduction in AI evolution. As models rapidly gain specialized capabilities in coding, biology, and chemistry, traditional safety alignment techniques struggle to cover all modifiable paths, intensifying the race between safety guardrails and model capability. The identification of smart appliances as new attack vectors marks the extension of AI misuse risk from "digital space" to "physical space." The misuse exposure of open-source models is more structural, since once weights are public they cannot be recalled, requiring the industry to build new collaborative frameworks for model distribution, version management, and community responsibility. On the regulatory front, the EU AI Act and US executive orders are forming a global AI governance framework; cross-border compliance coordination will become a significant component of AI company operating costs. Anthropic's choice to publicly disclose these risks reflects not only a sense of responsibility as a leading firm but also the recognition that, amid tightening regulation, proactive transparency has become a key strategy for winning policymakers' and the public's trust.

**[Multiple Perspectives]**

- **Anthropic Safety Team**: Emphasizes that more capable models have larger misuse potential, calling on industry to jointly build misuse monitoring and response mechanisms.
- **Open Source Community Representatives**: Argue that open-source model safety management requires community-driven standard setting, not unilateral control by closed-source vendors.
- **Policymakers**: EU and US legislators tend to incorporate model modifiability into mandatory safety assessment criteria.
- **Enterprise Users**: Financial institutions and critical infrastructure operators express concern over rising AI misuse exposure, accelerating internal red team testing investments.
