**[Core Summary]** According to The Verge, Microsoft Xbox has reached an agreement with Take-Two Interactive, parent of Rockstar Games, securing exclusive streaming rights for Grand Theft Auto 6 (GTA 6). This means players can play one of the world's most anticipated games without high-performance hardware through Xbox cloud gaming service, posing a major challenge to traditional console hardware sales models.

**[Event Details]**
- **Agreement Core**: Xbox cloud gaming service will exclusively provide the GTA 6 streaming version, allowing subscribers to play on phones, tablets, smart TVs, and other terminals.
- **Strategic Intent**: Microsoft accelerates the expansion of the Game Pass subscription model by bringing top-tier 3A titles into the cloud gaming launch lineup, hedging against the risk of slowing console hardware sales.
- **Player Impact**: Low-barrier players can experience GTA 6 without purchasing Xbox Series X or PS5, but game graphics quality and input latency are constrained by network conditions.
- **PlayStation Impact**: Sony is clearly behind in cloud gaming layout and may be forced to accelerate acquisitions or build cloud gaming capabilities.
- **Industry Trend**: Industry observers believe this marks the paradigm shift of the console industry from "hardware competition" to "service subscription + cloud distribution".

**[Panoramic Analysis]**
**First**: Xbox securing GTA 6 cloud gaming exclusive rights is a key turning point for cloud gaming moving from "supplementary" to "mainstream." As the world's best-selling and most influential 3A game franchise, GTA's first release choosing cloud gaming over console exclusivity means content vendors are starting to believe cloud distribution can support sufficient commercial scale. This will prompt other 3A developers to reassess their distribution strategies, and cloud gaming may evolve from optional to mandatory.
**Second**: The competitive logic of the console hardware track will be redefined. Sony and Nintendo may be forced to accelerate cloud gaming layout in the next 12 months: Sony may acquire cloud gaming companies or deepen cooperation with telecom operators; Nintendo may continue its differentiated "lightweight cloud gaming + featured hardware" route. Xbox, with GTA 6, locks in a "3A title + cloud subscription subscription" dual moat.
**Third**: The shift of business model from "one-time hardware sales" to "continuous subscription revenue share" will profoundly impact the cooperative game between console manufacturers, telecom operators, and ISPs. Cloud gaming requires low-latency, high-bandwidth network support; operators may gain new B2B monetization opportunities. At the same time, game revenue share ratios, data localization, cross-platform account interoperability will become new focus points.
