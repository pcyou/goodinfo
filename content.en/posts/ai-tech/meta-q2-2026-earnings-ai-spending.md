## Core Summary

Meta Platforms released its Q2 FY2026 earnings after market close on July 29. While revenue grew, net profit failed to meet Wall Street expectations, triggering a sharp market reaction. Following the report, Meta stock plunged nearly 10% in after-hours trading, marking one of its largest single-day declines recently. Investors expressed serious concerns about the company's massive capital expenditure plans for artificial intelligence, questioning when these investments would translate into actual returns.

## Event Details

According to Business Insider, Meta's Q2 revenue reached $42.3 billion, up 22% year-over-year, beating analyst estimates of $41.5 billion. However, adjusted earnings per share came in at $5.91, below the expected $6.12. This marks Meta's second consecutive quarter of earnings misses, indicating that its core advertising business growth is slowing.

More concerning to the market was Meta's guidance for the upcoming quarter. The company expects Q3 revenue between $43 billion and $45 billion—while still growing, the pace has clearly decelerated. More importantly, Meta announced it would significantly increase AI infrastructure investment, with 2026 capital expenditure projected at $45-50 billion, far above the $38 billion the market had previously anticipated.

CEO Mark Zuckerberg emphasized during the earnings call that AI represents the company's most important strategic direction for the next decade. He stated the company is building "next-generation AI infrastructure," including large-scale data centers and custom chips to support generative AI services and metaverse experiences. However, investors have lost patience with this "invest first, monetize later" model.

## Panoramic Perspective

Meta's earnings miss and stock plunge reflect the "AI reality check" the tech industry is undergoing. Over the past two years, market expectations for AI were pushed to historic highs, with investors willing to pay premiums for AI concepts. However, as multiple companies enter AI investment cycles, markets now demand to see actual business returns rather than just technological visions.

Meta's predicament is typical. As the world's largest social media platform, Meta possesses massive user data and rich application scenarios—theoretically the ideal candidate for AI commercialization. Yet the company has lagged in generative AI deployment, lacking both the enterprise market depth of Microsoft and the search and cloud service dominance of Google.

The deeper issue is that Meta's AI strategy contains inherent contradictions. On one hand, the company needs massive investment to build AI infrastructure; on the other hand, its core advertising business faces dual pressures from macroeconomic headwinds and privacy policy changes. This "burning cash at both ends" model appears particularly fragile in the current high-interest-rate environment.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Pessimistic View**: JPMorgan analysts believe Meta's AI investment return timeline may be longer than expected. Unlike Microsoft and Google, Meta lacks an enterprise customer base, and its AI monetization path isn't clear. The $45 billion capital expenditure will severely squeeze free cash flow, increasing financial risk.

**Optimistic View**: Some long-term investors believe Meta's short-term challenges don't diminish long-term value. The company's 2 billion daily active user data represents precious assets for AI training—once the right commercialization model is found, the explosive potential could exceed expectations. Additionally, while Reality Labs continues losing money, the metaverse's long-term potential shouldn't be ignored.

**Industry Observer View**: Tech industry analysts point out that Meta's difficulties may signal the beginning of an "AI bubble" deflation. Investors are reassessing tech companies' AI strategies—those lacking clear monetization paths will face valuation resets. This could trigger a new round of tech stock adjustment.
