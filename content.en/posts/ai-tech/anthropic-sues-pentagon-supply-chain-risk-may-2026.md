## AI Company Takes Pentagon to Court

Anthropic has formally filed a lawsuit against the US government, challenging the Pentagon's designation of the company as a "supply chain risk." The legal action marks an escalating conflict between AI companies and the military over technology ethics and usage restrictions.

### Background of the Dispute

Earlier this year, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei went public with concerns that powerful AI tools could be used by defense agencies to conduct mass domestic surveillance and deploy fully autonomous weapons of war. In response, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth moved within days to label Anthropic a "supply chain risk," meaning the company was deemed too dangerous for use in government settings.

### The Core of the Contract Dispute

The Pentagon recently signed new and expanded AI agreements with eight technology companies — Google, OpenAI, Amazon, Microsoft, SpaceX, Oracle, Nvidia, and startup Reflection — allowing AI technology to be used for any "lawful operational use." Notably absent from these agreements is Anthropic.

Anthropic had refused to accept the "any lawful use" language in its own contract, which is believed to be the direct trigger for the conflict. The company expressed concerns that such broad authorization could be misused for military operations and domestic surveillance.

### The Legal Challenge

Anthropic's legal challenge to the "supply chain risk" ruling is expected to go to court in September. The company argues that the Pentagon's decision constitutes undue retaliation and lacks sufficient legal basis.

Meanwhile, Anthropic's tools — including a version of its Claude chatbot — remain in use across many US government and defense agencies. As the first AI company to be deployed for classified work, Anthropic still holds a significant position in government AI applications.

### Industry Impact

The dispute appears to have opened the door for other AI companies to work more closely with the government and military. OpenAI was the first to ink a new deal with the Pentagon in late February, following the Anthropic row. Google's Gemini will also be used to handle government work at a classified level for the first time.

Previously, hundreds of Google employees, including many from DeepMind — the unit responsible for much of the company's AI model development — sent a letter to CEO Sundar Pichai urging the company not to deepen its work with the government.

### Positions of Both Sides

The Pentagon stated that partnering with multiple companies would help avoid "vendor lock" and ensure diversity in military AI capabilities. "Access to a diverse suite of AI capabilities from across the resilient American technology stack will give warfighters the tools they need to act with confidence and safeguard the nation against any threat," the Pentagon said in a statement.

Anthropic maintains its ethical stance, arguing that AI companies need clear boundaries and restrictions regarding military applications.
