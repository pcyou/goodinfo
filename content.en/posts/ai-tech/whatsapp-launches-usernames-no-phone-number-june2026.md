## WhatsApp Launches Usernames — Chat Without Sharing Phone Numbers

**[Core Summary]** Meta's WhatsApp has officially rolled out username functionality, enabling users to create unique usernames that others can use to initiate chats without knowing their phone numbers. The move marks a significant privacy upgrade for the world's largest messaging app, affecting over two billion users globally.

### Event Details

According to BBC and The Verge, the username feature will roll out to users globally over the coming weeks. Users will be able to create and customize a username in the app's settings, which will replace the phone number as the primary identity marker.

Previously, WhatsApp's account system was entirely tied to phone numbers — to reach someone on WhatsApp, you needed their phone number. This created privacy friction in many scenarios: e-commerce transactions, social media interactions, and temporary collaborations all required users to expose their private contact details to strangers or unfamiliar contacts.

With the new feature, users can choose to communicate exclusively through usernames, keeping their phone numbers hidden from other users. The existing phone-number-based contact discovery mechanism will remain available, but usernames will become an optional alternative.

The Verge reports that users will also be able to reserve specific usernames ahead of the feature's full launch.

### Analysis

The significance of WhatsApp's username feature goes beyond a simple product update. It represents a paradigm shift from "mapping real-world relationships" to "controllable social boundaries" in the evolution of messaging platforms.

Since its founding, WhatsApp's core philosophy was "use phone numbers instead of usernames," which contrasted with earlier instant messaging tools like ICQ and MSN Messenger. This design was hugely successful in the early smartphone era — phone numbers provided natural uniqueness and discoverability.

However, as WhatsApp evolved from a "close-contacts messaging tool" to an "all-scenario social platform," the drawbacks of phone number binding became increasingly apparent. The new feature addresses this by giving users control over who can find and contact them.

From a competitive standpoint, Telegram introduced usernames years ago, and Signal recently followed suit. WhatsApp's move is both a natural product evolution and a defensive response to competitors.

### Perspectives

**Privacy advocates** welcomed the feature. An EFF researcher commented: "Decoupling phone numbers from social discovery is a sign of messaging app maturity. Users should have the right to decide who can contact them and how they can be found."

**Some users and analysts** expressed concern that usernames could enable new forms of harassment — similar to the unsolicited DM problem on Twitter and Instagram. They urged WhatsApp to strengthen message filtering and blocking features alongside the username launch.

**Business observers** noted that the change could impact WhatsApp's business model. Phone numbers are central to WhatsApp's identity verification and spam prevention systems, and introducing usernames may increase the challenge of managing fake and bot accounts.
