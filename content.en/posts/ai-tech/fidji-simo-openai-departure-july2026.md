## Core Summary

According to joint reports from TechCrunch and CNBC, OpenAI Chief Operating Officer Fidji Simo announced on July 9 that she will leave the company's daily management position and transition to a consultant role. As a core member of OpenAI's executive team, Simo's departure is considered one of the company's most important personnel changes recently and may have a profound impact on the company's commercialization strategy.

## Event Details

Fidji Simo joined OpenAI in 2024, having previously served as CEO of application company Instacart. Upon joining OpenAI, she was entrusted with the responsibility of driving the company's commercialization, overseeing key business areas including product operations, enterprise cooperation, and market expansion.

According to OpenAI's official statement, Simo's departure is a "personal career choice," and she will transition to an advisory role, continuing to provide strategic advice to the company. The company stated it is searching for a new head of operations but has not yet announced a successor.

Sources reveal that Simo's departure is related to internal disagreements at OpenAI regarding the pace of commercialization. As a publicly listed company, OpenAI faces profitability pressure from investors, while Simo was believed to favor a more prudent product iteration strategy, creating tension with some board members' more aggressive commercialization demands.

OpenAI's CEO thanked Simo for her contributions in an internal email and emphasized that the company will continue to pursue a "responsible AI commercialization" strategy.

## Panoramic Perspective

Fidji Simo's departure reveals the deep contradictions faced by the artificial intelligence industry in its rapid commercialization. During OpenAI's transformation from a non-profit research institution to a for-profit enterprise, finding balance between technological innovation, commercial returns, and social responsibility has always been a core challenge.

Simo's background represented OpenAI's attempt to introduce mature enterprise management experience. Her successful experience taking Instacart public was once seen as an important asset for OpenAI's commercialization. However, the particularity of the AI industry lies in the fact that technological breakthrough speed far exceeds traditional product iteration cycles, requiring companies to maintain extremely high flexibility and room for error.

From a broader perspective, OpenAI's personnel changes reflect the white-hot talent competition in the entire AI industry. Top AI talent is in short supply, and executive mobility remains high. Simo's next move will become a market focus, and her choice may influence other AI companies' talent strategies.

For investors, this personnel change may raise concerns about strategic continuity in the short term. But in the long run, whether OpenAI can build an institutionalized operational system that doesn't rely on individual stars is the key to determining whether it can maintain its leadership position.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Internal Perspective**: OpenAI employees have mixed reactions to Simo's departure. Some engineers believe she focused too much on short-term commercial metrics at the expense of core R&D values, while business teams worry that new leadership may change existing enterprise cooperation models.
