## Core Summary

CNBC recently conducted a real-world comparison of Tesla's Full Self-Driving system and Rivian's Autonomy+ system. Test results show significant differences between the two systems in ride smoothness, scenario adaptability, and user experience, with each having distinct advantages. Tesla's system demonstrates stronger decision-making in complex road conditions, while Rivian's system excels in ride comfort.

## Event Details

According to CNBC, reporters test-drove vehicles equipped with Tesla's Full Self-Driving system and Rivian R1S with Autonomy+ across various road conditions. Testing covered city streets, highways, complex intersections, and other scenarios.

Test results show Tesla's Full Self-Driving system has stronger decision-making capabilities in complex road conditions, more accurately identifying traffic signs, pedestrians, and other vehicles while making appropriate driving decisions. In highway scenarios, Tesla's system is more decisive in lane changes and overtaking, though actions can be somewhat aggressive in certain situations.

Rivian's Autonomy+ system excels in ride comfort. The system's acceleration, deceleration, and steering operations are smoother, providing a better passenger experience. However, in complex intersections and construction zones, Rivian's system is relatively slower in decision-making, sometimes requiring driver intervention.

The two systems also differ in user experience. Tesla's system provides richer driving information displays, allowing drivers to understand the system's decision logic in real time. Rivian's system prioritizes simplicity with more concise information presentation.

## Panoramic Perspective

The real-world comparison between Tesla's Full Self-Driving and Rivian's Autonomy+ reflects two different development paths in current autonomous driving technology. Tesla employs a vision-only approach, relying on cameras and neural networks for environmental perception and decision-making; Rivian uses multi-sensor fusion, combining cameras, radar, and ultrasonic sensors.

From a technology evolution standpoint, Tesla's vision-only approach has cost advantages but demands higher algorithm and data processing capabilities. Rivian's multi-sensor fusion approach offers better redundancy and safety but at higher cost. Both approaches have merits, and which becomes mainstream remains to be seen.

For consumers, autonomous driving system choice depends not only on technological advancement but also on actual usage scenarios, personal driving habits, and trust in the technology. Tesla's system better suits users pursuing technology and active driving experiences, while Rivian's system better serves users prioritizing ride comfort and safety.
