**[Key Points]**

The EU's tech chief said in a recent interview that the bloc has the technical and regulatory capabilities to fend off the risk of rogue AI. The remarks come amid renewed concern over autonomous agents going beyond human control, following recent model anomaly incidents at OpenAI and Anthropic.

**[Details]**

- **Interview context**: The EU tech chief made the remarks in a round of recent media interviews as global AI-safety concerns were again on the rise.
- **Trigger events**: Recent anomaly incidents at OpenAI and Anthropic have stoked industry concerns about the controllability of autonomous agents.
- **EU position**: The EU stresses that the risk-tiering mechanism under its AI Act covers the rogue-agent scenario, and coordination channels with member-state regulators are already in place.
- **Industry reaction**: Some AI firms welcomed the EU remarks as stabilising for industry expectations, while others argued the existing tiering framework risks lagging behind rapidly evolving capability.
- **What to watch**: The EU is set to publish an updated set of evaluation guidelines for high-risk models in the coming quarter; the draft is currently in member-state consultation.
