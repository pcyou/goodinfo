## Event Summary

Europe has competitive battery technology reserves in several niche segments, but the overall industry chain still faces strong dominance by Chinese manufacturers. Focusing on next-generation battery chemistries, solid-state batteries and recycling, Europe has recently launched a number of industrial policies and investment plans in an attempt to build a more resilient local supply chain. Multiple industry observers point out that subsidies alone cannot make up for the gap in scale and manufacturing experience, and that the key lies in whether Europe can form stable coordination among policy, investment and talent training.

Looking at recent developments, European policymakers are increasingly inclined to view the battery industry as one of the key pillars of energy security and industrial competitiveness, and related support tools have extended from single subsidies to coverage of the entire industry chain. However, to truly close the gap with Chinese manufacturers in the short to medium term, Europe still needs to achieve more concrete breakthroughs in raw material security, manufacturing process maturity and downstream application coordination.
