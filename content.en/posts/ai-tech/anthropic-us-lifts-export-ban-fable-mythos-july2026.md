## Core Summary

The US government has lifted its export ban on two advanced AI models — Fable and Mythos — developed by Anthropic. The models were abruptly suspended in June over security concerns but are now cleared for global supply after meeting new safety conditions. This policy reversal marks a significant shift in US AI export control strategy.

## Details

According to BBC and WIRED, the Trump administration has formally notified Anthropic that its flagship AI models Fable and Mythos are no longer subject to the previous export restrictions. The models were suspended in mid-June after intelligence agencies raised concerns they could be exploited by hacker organizations for sophisticated cyberattacks.

Anthropic stated it has reached a new security agreement with the government, including enhanced usage monitoring, international user vetting, and real-time security audit systems. These measures convinced authorities that export risks have been reduced to acceptable levels.

Previously, Anthropic had publicly criticized the suspension, calling it "opaque and damaging to American AI companies' international competitiveness." The CEO said the ban "gave competitor nations an opportunity to fill the market gap."

## Analysis

This episode highlights the core tension in US AI governance: balancing national security with commercial competitiveness.

From a security perspective, the dual-use nature of advanced AI models is increasingly evident. These models can drive scientific research and commercial innovation, but may also be exploited by malicious actors.

From an industry perspective, the cost of the export ban was substantial. Anthropic lost hundreds of millions in overseas contracts during the suspension, with some customers already moving to competitors. The deeper impact lies in how policy uncertainty may shake international confidence in American AI products.

From a geopolitical angle, the reversal also reflects strained ally relations. European and Asian partners have expressed frustration with the US's "ban then release" approach.

Anthropic welcomed the decision as "a victory for rational regulation," while security researchers warned that relaxed controls could let advanced technology fall into wrong hands.
