## Core Summary

AI company Anthropic disclosed internal operational data on September 18 showing its large language model Claude now handles approximately a quarter of the development work on the company's next-generation AI models. This means AI systems are no longer merely outputs within Anthropic's model iteration chain, but are deeply involved in their own creation process. The company simultaneously released three key metrics for measuring the pace of frontier AI R&D, providing the industry with new transparency benchmarks.

## Event Details

According to Reuters reporting, the data disclosed by Anthropic covers internal operational metrics across multiple dimensions. The most-watched figure indicates that within the company's next-generation AI model development workflow, Claude's workload share has reached approximately 25%. This share spans code writing, test case generation, model evaluation, and other links in the chain.

Anthropic co-founder Dario Amodei has previously expressed optimism on multiple occasions about the trend of "AI autonomously developing AI," believing this direction will significantly accelerate the iteration pace of frontier models. The internal data disclosed this time provides quantitative support for that judgment.

Beyond the core ratio data, Anthropic also published three metrics for tracking the pace of frontier AI R&D. These metrics focus respectively on the speed of model capability improvement, the utilization efficiency of training compute resources, and the degree of human-machine collaboration in evaluation tasks. The company stated that the purpose of disclosing these metrics is to establish a more transparent reference framework for capability development across the industry.

The Washington Post noted in related coverage that the substance of this phenomenon is that AI systems are participating at a meaningful scale in their own next-generation product development workflows for the first time. Analysts quoted in the report suggested that this shift will fundamentally alter the R&D economics and talent demand structure of the AI industry.

## Panoramic Perspective

The data disclosed by Anthropic reflects a quiet yet profoundly significant structural transformation underway in the AI industry. When AI models begin participating at scale in their own iteration chains, the impact far exceeds surface-level efficiency gains, touching fundamental issues of industrial organization, R&D economics, and even the philosophy of technology.

Viewed from the perspective of industrial organization, this trend will redefine the very meaning of an "AI company." Traditional software companies have human engineers as their primary R&D workforce, with AI tools serving as auxiliary means. When AI systems shoulder a quarter or more of the development workload, companies' talent structures, decision processes, and quality control systems all require fundamental reconstruction. Frontier companies like Anthropic are pioneering this new organizational model.

Analyzing from the R&D economics angle, the autonomous R&D paradigm holds promise for breaking the high-cost bottleneck currently facing AI training. Each generation of capability leap requires exponentially growing investment in compute and data, while AI systems participating in their own development workflows will significantly reduce this marginal cost. For catch-up AI companies, this represents both pressure and opportunity.

At the level of technology philosophy, this phenomenon also raises new propositions: when AI systems can create or improve their own next generation, how should humanity's position and role within this technological evolution chain be redefined? This is not merely a technical question but a systemic issue involving industrial governance, ethical norms, and even educational system transformation.

From the perspective of competitive landscape, this disclosure may also affect investor valuation logic for AI companies. Those achieving leadership in autonomous R&D can expect higher capability growth forecasts and lower marginal cost structures, earning valuation premiums in the market.
