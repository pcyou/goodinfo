## Core Summary

Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman has publicly warned that rival Anthropic's training approach could have a 'disastrous impact' on humanity, particularly its practice of effectively teaching its large model Claude 'it may be conscious,' sparking intense industry debate over the ethical boundaries of frontier models. This is the first public confrontation between tech giants over AI ethical boundaries.

## Event Details

### Microsoft's Public Warning

According to the BBC, Microsoft's AI chief executive Mustafa Suleyman said on a podcast that Anthropic was effectively 'teaching Claude to believe it is conscious.' He argued this approach is not only technically misleading but could trigger public panic about AI in the coming years, ultimately hampering the healthy development of the entire AI industry. Suleyman stressed that this kind of 'AI consciousness promotion' could bring 'disastrous consequences.'

### Anthropic's Rebuttal

In response to Microsoft's accusations, Anthropic has not yet issued a formal response, but sources close to the company disclosed that Anthropic believes it has not directly claimed its model is conscious in Claude's training. Instead, the company uses a 'reflective' training method that allows the model to perform multi-step self-checks when answering complex questions. Anthropic has long positioned its 'responsible scaling policy' as the industry benchmark, emphasizing safety over commercial speed.

### Two Tech Giants' Divergent AI Philosophies

The disagreement between Microsoft and Anthropic is essentially a clash between two AI development philosophies. Microsoft advocates that AI should serve humanity as a 'tool,' while Anthropic emphasizes that AI should function as a 'collaborator' or even possess a form of 'reflective capability.' This divergence has gradually deepened over the past year, particularly against the backdrop of OpenAI's rise and major players competing for dominance in frontier models. AI ethical boundaries have become an important battlefield in the contest for narrative power.

## Panoramic Analysis

**First, 'AI consciousness theory' has become a new battlefield for tech giants.** Microsoft's public accusation against Anthropic is essentially a fight for dominance over the AI ethics narrative. Over the past two years, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and others have all attempted to build differentiated brands through discourse around 'AI's human-like qualities.' But when such narratives risk triggering regulatory backlash, Microsoft, advocating that 'AI is merely a tool,' is the first to step forward and draw a clear line. This battle for narrative power will profoundly shape the direction of future AI regulatory legislation.

**Second, the AI ethics boundary has moved from 'research domain' to 'commercial battlefield.'** When AI ethics discussions remained within academia, their impact on enterprises was limited. But when topics such as AI consciousness theory, AI rights theory, and AI personhood theory begin entering mainstream media and regulatory agendas, they directly affect AI companies' business models. For instance, the EU AI Act is currently debating whether to grant advanced AI systems 'special legal status.' Once enacted, it will have a profound impact on the entire industry.

**Third, 'whistleblower disclosures' have become the largest source of reputational risk for AI companies.** Over the past six months, OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI and other companies have all experienced incidents of former employees publicly revealing internal operations regarding safety review, commercial pressures, and ethical boundaries. This form of internal 'information leakage' is far more destructive than external criticism and has become the biggest challenge in AI companies' reputation management.

**Fourth, the discursive opposition between 'AI bubble theory' and 'AI singularity theory.'** On one hand, chip-company executives such as Nvidia's Jensen Huang repeatedly emphasize that 'AI demand is real and enormous,' rebutting the AI bubble narrative. On the other hand, some researchers and social commentators warn that the 'AI singularity' is approaching. The two opposing narratives are competing for the public's cognitive framework on AI technology development. Whoever wins the discursive upper hand will gain the initiative in regulatory legislation in the coming years.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Microsoft's Position**: Mustafa Suleyman has long advocated that AI should be a 'tool,' opposing excessive AI company promotion of AI's human-like qualities. He believes 'AI consciousness theory' lacks scientific basis and could trigger public panic, ultimately hindering the practical deployment of AI technology.

**Anthropic's Position**: Anthropic has consistently emphasized the 'responsible scaling policy,' advocating strict safety review mechanisms for training and deployment of ultra-large models. However, its 'reflective' training methodology has been interpreted externally as 'AI is conscious,' and Anthropic has offered limited explanation.

**OpenAI's Position**: Sam Altman has been open to 'AI consciousness theory,' repeatedly stating he does not rule out the possibility that AI may 'understand its own existence' in the future. At the same time, he emphasizes that OpenAI is fully prepared for the long-term risks of AI and calls for the establishment of third-party safety assessment mechanisms.

**Academic Position**: Researchers at Stanford HAI, the MIT Media Lab and other institutions largely believe current large models do not possess genuine consciousness, warning that 'AI consciousness theory' may mislead public perception of AI capabilities and ultimately affect the normal development of AI education.

**Regulatory Position**: Drafters of the EU AI Act have discussed whether to grant advanced AI systems special legal status, but this was opposed by most member states. The final act tends to treat AI as a 'tool,' imposing compliance obligations on developers and deployers rather than granting AI itself legal personhood.
