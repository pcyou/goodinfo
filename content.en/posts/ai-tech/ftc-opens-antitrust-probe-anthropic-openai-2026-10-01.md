## FTC Opens Antitrust Probe Into AI Giants Including Anthropic and OpenAI

### Core Summary

According to Reuters, the U.S. Federal Trade Commission has formally launched an antitrust investigation into several leading AI companies including Anthropic and OpenAI. The probe will focus on training data sourcing, partnership arrangements with cloud infrastructure providers, and potential market dominance in the enterprise AI market. This marks the first systematic U.S. regulatory scrutiny of frontier AI companies and signals a fundamental shift from an innovation-first approach to a more balanced innovation-plus-regulation framework.

### Event Details

The Reuters report indicates that the FTC's investigation will cover multiple companies with significant market influence in the generative AI space. The scope of the probe extends well beyond the AI models themselves, encompassing upstream data sources, partnership terms with cloud infrastructure suppliers, and pricing behavior in the enterprise AI market.

The agency is paying particular attention to potentially exclusive arrangements between leading AI companies and cloud service providers. While such partnerships provide AI companies with access to massive compute resources, they may also restrict other companies from obtaining comparable infrastructure on equal terms, thereby creating hidden barriers to competition.

In parallel, the FTC is also examining the compliance of AI companies in using copyright-protected data during model training. Several media organizations have already filed lawsuits against AI companies, alleging unauthorized use of news articles, books, and other protected content for training purposes. These disputes are expected to interact with the broader antitrust investigation.

Industry observers note that the scope of this investigation signals a notable shift in U.S. regulatory attitude toward the AI industry. While AI has delivered substantial productivity gains, regulators now believe that when a handful of companies come to control critical models and compute, proactive regulatory intervention becomes necessary.

### Panoramic Analysis

The FTC's decision to open an antitrust probe into leading AI companies marks a significant milestone in the evolution of global tech governance. Over the past decade, antitrust scrutiny of big tech has focused primarily on mature sectors such as search engines, operating systems, app stores, and social networks. AI, as an emerging industry, presents a different challenge: its market structure is not yet fully settled, and the timing and boundaries of regulatory intervention have become a central point of contention.

From an industry-structure perspective, the investigation could create ripple effects across three layers. First, at the model layer, leading companies will need to handle training data compliance and partnership arrangements with greater care, which may slow the release cadence of new models. Second, at the compute layer, AI companies that are deeply integrated with hyperscale cloud providers may need to reassess their infrastructure strategies to avoid being deemed exclusionary. Third, in the enterprise AI market, pricing, exclusivity terms, and vertical penetration will all come under regulatory review.

From a broader macro view, this antitrust action is forming a transatlantic regulatory convergence alongside the AI legislation advancing through the U.S. Congress and the European Union's AI Act. Though the three jurisdictions take different approaches, they are all attempting to answer the same fundamental question: as AI becomes embedded in the infrastructure of economy and society, are traditional antitrust tools still sufficient, or is a new regulatory framework required?

A deeper implication is that this investigation may reshape AI company strategy. For years, leading AI companies have followed a "scale first, comply later" expansion logic. The signal from this investigation is that regulators are prepared to intervene prospectively, before companies fully consolidate market dominance, which will push the entire industry toward a new equilibrium of "expansion within a compliance framework."

### Multi-Perspective Comparison

Supporters of the investigation argue that the AI industry already exhibits clear signs of market concentration: a small number of companies control disproportionate resources in foundation models, compute, and enterprise channels. Without timely regulatory intervention, a winner-take-all structure similar to that of the social media era may emerge. Supporters also point out that scrutiny of training data sourcing and partnerships can help establish fairer data usage norms across the AI sector.

Cautious voices emphasize that antitrust investigations should avoid over-suppressing AI innovation. AI is still an evolving technology, and premature or excessive regulation may push key research and development resources toward jurisdictions with lighter oversight, potentially weakening the United States' overall competitive position. Some industry groups warn that antitrust standards should fully account for the unique characteristics of the AI industry, rather than mechanically applying traditional antitrust tools to frontier technologies.
