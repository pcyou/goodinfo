## Core Summary

Nvidia announced the formation of a 37-member AI security alliance aimed at establishing security assessment frameworks and industry standards for AI systems. Notably, the three most influential companies in AI—Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic—were not included in the alliance roster. This absence immediately sparked industry-wide concerns about the alliance's representativeness and actual influence, also revealing the increasingly complex competitive landscape within the AI industry.

## Event Details

According to tech media reports, Nvidia stated in an official announcement that the alliance will focus on AI system security assessment, vulnerability detection, and risk mitigation strategies. Alliance members span semiconductor manufacturers, cloud computing service providers, cybersecurity firms, and traditional industry giants, but deliberately excluded the core companies currently dominating large language model development.

Nvidia's Chief Security Officer stated in the announcement that the alliance was formed based on a "pragmatic security cooperation" philosophy, aiming to provide actionable security standards for AI system deployment. He emphasized the alliance welcomes all qualified companies to join, including those not yet participating.

However, the absence of Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic sparked widespread speculation. Analysts noted these three companies currently dominate large language models and generative AI, with their security standards and practices directly influencing the entire industry's direction. Without their participation, standards developed by the alliance may lack industry representativeness.

Sources indicate Google and OpenAI had previously received invitations but declined to join due to disagreements over the alliance's governance structure and decision-making mechanisms. Anthropic has not publicly commented on the matter.

## Panoramic Perspective

The deeper logic behind Nvidia's move lies in its attempt to transform from a hardware supplier to a core coordinator in the AI industry ecosystem. As the absolute dominant player in the global AI chip market, Nvidia's GPUs are the core infrastructure for training and running large language models. By leading the security alliance, Nvidia not only consolidates its hub position in the industry chain but also attempts to gain influence over the critical issue of AI security.

From an industry competition perspective, there's a subtle competitive relationship between Nvidia and Google. Google is not only a large language model developer but also directly challenges Nvidia's AI hardware market dominance through its custom Tensor Processing Unit chips. While OpenAI and Anthropic primarily rely on Nvidia chips, their independent research capabilities in LLM security make them reluctant to accept security standards led by a hardware vendor.

This incident also reflects the fragmentation trend in AI security governance. Currently, multiple competing AI security frameworks exist globally, including OECD guidelines, the EU AI Act, and various national regulatory policies. Nvidia's alliance may further exacerbate standard fragmentation, creating greater compliance challenges for enterprises.

From a broader perspective, AI security is not just a technical issue but a geopolitical one. Different countries and companies have significantly different understandings and priorities regarding AI security, and reaching consensus among diverse interests will be the core challenge facing this alliance.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporters** argue that as the core AI infrastructure supplier, Nvidia has a unique perspective to coordinate cross-enterprise security cooperation. The alliance's 37 members cover the complete industry chain from chip design to end applications, and this broad representativeness helps develop more practical security standards. Some cybersecurity experts note the current AI security field lacks unified standards, and the alliance's formation could fill this gap.

**Critics** focus on the absence of core companies. A Stanford University AI Institute researcher noted that without Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic, alliance standards will struggle to cover large language models—the most important AI application area. A former Google AI ethics lead criticized the alliance as more of a business strategy for Nvidia to consolidate market position rather than a genuine public interest initiative for security cooperation.

**Industry observers** note Nvidia's move aligns with its recent intensive push into AI security. The company has previously invested heavily in AI security research and established relationships with multiple governments. The alliance's formation marks Nvidia's strategic extension from technology R&D to industry governance.
