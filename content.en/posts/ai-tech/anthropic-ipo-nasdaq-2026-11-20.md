## Key Facts

- **Listing details**: Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share, above the expected $70–$75 range, raising $8.1 billion and valuing the company at $1.5 trillion.
- **Market reaction**: Shares rose 12% on the first day of trading, pushing the market capitalisation above $1.6 trillion.
- **Scale**: The offering is the largest tech IPO in the US since 2024 and the second-largest ever after Nvidia's listing.
- **Use of proceeds**: Funds will be used to expand compute infrastructure, hire top talent and develop next-generation large language models.
- **Shareholder structure**: Amazon is the largest external investor with approximately 15%; Google and Salesforce also hold significant stakes. Founders Dario and Daniela Amodei together hold over 20% of voting rights.
- **Business profile**: Founded in 2021, Anthropic's Claude series of large language models has gained wide adoption in the enterprise market. The company claims its models are superior in safety and reliability.
- **Financials**: Revenue was approximately $4.5 billion in 2025, up 230% year-on-year, but the company reported a net loss of around $800 million and is not yet profitable.

## Event Details

Artificial intelligence company Anthropic began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANTH on Friday. The IPO priced at $83 per share, raising $8.1 billion and giving the company a post-money valuation of $1.5 trillion. This makes it one of the most valuable newly public companies globally, trailing only tech giants such as Nvidia and Microsoft.

The strong debut reflects investor confidence in enterprise AI demand. Unlike consumer-facing AI products, Anthropic's primary customers are large corporations that use its Claude models via API subscriptions. This B2B model provides more predictable revenue streams, though growth is slower than viral consumer apps.

Amazon, as both a strategic investor and cloud provider, holds about 15% of the company. Other major shareholders include Google and Salesforce. The Amodei siblings, who co-founded Anthropic after leaving OpenAI, retain significant control through their combined 20% voting stake.

## Competing Views

**Optimists see validation of long-term AI infrastructure value.** Supporters argue that Anthropic's high valuation reflects market confidence in enterprise AI adoption. Unlike traditional internet companies, Anthropic's revenue comes from large corporate clients with stable, predictable spending. Continued backing from strategic investors like Amazon is viewed as a positive signal.

**Sceptics question bubble risks.** Critics point out that the $1.5 trillion valuation far exceeds current revenue capacity, implying a price-to-sales ratio in the hundreds. If AI commercialisation slows or stronger competitors emerge, the stock could face significant correction. The company is not yet profitable and depends on continued financing to sustain operations.

**Industry watchers focus on competitive dynamics.** Analysts note that going public gives Anthropic more resources to compete with OpenAI, Google DeepMind and others. However, training costs for large models continue to rise, and compute bottlenecks may constrain growth. Regulatory risk is another uncertainty, with Europe and the US strengthening oversight of AI companies.

## Panoramic Analysis

Anthropic's listing is not just a single company's capital event but a milestone marking the maturity stage of the AI industry. It shows that capital markets have shifted from scepticism to recognition of generative AI's commercial potential, albeit built on extremely high valuation expectations.

From an industrial perspective, this IPO reveals three trends in AI infrastructure investment. First, compute is power: the core support for Anthropic's valuation is its computing resources and model capabilities, not traditional user scale or advertising revenue. Second, enterprise-first strategy: unlike consumer AI products, Anthropic focuses on providing API services to enterprise clients. This B2B model grows more slowly but offers stronger customer stickiness and lower churn. Third, safety differentiation: as model capabilities converge, Anthropic positions "Constitutional AI" and safety alignment as its core selling point, aiming to build competitive advantage in an increasingly regulated environment.

However, structural risks lurk behind the high valuation. First, the pace of technological iteration is extremely fast; today's leading model could be surpassed within six months, meaning the company must continuously invest huge R&D budgets to stay competitive. Second, customer concentration risk: if a few large clients contribute most revenue, the loss of any one client would significantly impact finances. Third, regulatory uncertainty: the EU AI Act and potential US federal legislation could change industry rules and increase compliance costs.

Key developments to watch include whether Anthropic can demonstrate a path to profitability in the next earnings season, whether its main cloud partner Amazon AWS will offer more favourable compute pricing, and whether competitors will squeeze its market share through price-cutting strategies. If the company can prove its high valuation has a solid financial foundation, it will encourage more AI start-ups to go public; otherwise, it could trigger a reassessment of the entire AI sector.
