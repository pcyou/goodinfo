**[Core Summary]**

According to a Hacker News item on Oct. 17, the official OpenCode account confirmed that its system had been hacked and some user credentials leaked, triggering wide discussion in the developer community.

**[Event Details]**

- **Disclosure**: The official OpenCode account posted on social media confirming that its system had recently been hacked and some user credentials leaked.
- **Scope**: The company has not yet disclosed the specific number or geographic distribution of affected users, but has advised all users to immediately reset passwords and enable multi-factor authentication.
- **Response**: The platform has revoked some suspicious session tokens, is conducting a comprehensive review of logs and system components, and is working with external security teams on forensic analysis.
- **Community Reaction**: The incident has triggered wide discussion on Hacker News and other tech communities, with developers focused on AI coding tools' practices around credential storage, third-party dependencies and supply-chain security.
- **Next Steps**: OpenCode said it will publish a full incident report after the review concludes, disclosing the intrusion path, scope of impact and remediation measures.

**[Panoramic Perspective]**

The OpenCode incident reflects the new "tool supply-chain security" challenge in the context of the rapid adoption of AI coding tools. AI development tools typically hold high privileges — the ability to read and write code, call cloud-side models and execute terminal commands — so that a leaked credential or token can have far greater impact than a typical SaaS application. In the short term, developers will need to re-examine permission boundaries, key management and MFA configuration when choosing AI coding tools. In the medium and long term, AI tool vendors need to establish stricter third-party dependency review, anomaly detection and least-privilege models to counter increasingly targeted supply-chain attacks.
