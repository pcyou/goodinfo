## Core Summary

According to Reuters and Barron's, South Korean memory chipmaker SK Hynix has seen its stock decline sharply following its secondary listing on Nasdaq. The company had just set a record for the largest foreign company US listing at $26.5 billion, with shares surging 17 percent on the first day. However, market sentiment reversed within days as investors began reassessing return timelines and profit expectations for AI memory chip investments.

## Event Details

Barron's reports that SK Hynix shares experienced a "plunge" following the Nasdaq listing, with "diminishing earnings optimism" according to Reuters analysis. The core investor concern: while AI demand for high-bandwidth memory continues growing, cyclical chip price fluctuations could compress profit margins.

Notably, SK Hynix priced its July 10 Nasdaq listing at $189 per share, reaching a high of $221 on the first day. But in subsequent trading sessions, shares declined steadily, reflecting broader market reassessment of semiconductor sector valuations.

Analysts point to multiple factors: overall tech sector pressure with AI concept stocks facing profit-taking after major gains; the storage chip industry's inherent cyclicality making investors cautious about current high valuations; and price competition pressure from rivals Samsung and Micron.

## Panoramic Analysis

SK Hynix's sharp stock reversal reflects a deep contradiction in the current AI investment boom: markets remain confident about long-term AI infrastructure demand, but patience for short-term returns is waning.

From an industry cycle perspective, the memory chip sector has historically exhibited strong cyclicality. High-bandwidth memory (HBM), as a core AI server component, has experienced demand explosion and price surges over the past two years. However, historical experience suggests high-boom chip cycles often accompany overcapacity and price crashes. Investors now worry whether SK Hynix and peers' massive expansion plans could lead to oversupply within two to three years.

From a valuation logic perspective, SK Hynix's Nasdaq listing valuation already fully reflected market premium for its HBM technology leadership. When valuations "perfectly price in" optimistic expectations, any below-expectation signal—whether demand slowdown, intensified competition, or technology route changes—can trigger sharp stock adjustments.

From a broader perspective, SK Hynix's stock volatility may signal the entire AI investment cycle entering a "reality check" phase. Over the past two years, AI concept enthusiasm drove major chip stock gains, but as investment return periods approach, investors are more rationally assessing actual profitability.

## Multiple Perspectives

Optimists argue the stock pullback is merely short-term technical adjustment, not changing AI memory chip long-term fundamentals. They note Nvidia and other AI chip giants' order backlogs continue expanding, and HBM supply-demand gaps remain difficult to close short-term.

Pessimists warn that memory chip cyclicality risks are seriously underestimated by markets. Citing historical data, they note every chip industry high-boom cycle has ended with overcapacity and price collapse—the current investment boom may repeat this pattern.

Korean domestic analysts focus on geopolitical factors. SK Hynix maintains important production bases in Wuxi and Dalian, China. US-China tech competition uncertainty could affect its capacity layout and cost structure.
