## Summary

TechCrunch and CoinDesk reported on September 12 that OpenAI CEO Sam Altman publicly stated that, given the current series of developments in AI safety, taking the company public in 2026 would be an ill-advised choice. He emphasized that the company should focus at this stage on advancing frontier model safety evaluations and the construction of governance frameworks, rather than devoting substantial energy to IPO compliance, disclosure, and short-term earnings pressure. This is one of Altman's clearest official statements on the OpenAI IPO timeline, and is being read by the market as a signal that the company will not push forward with an IPO in the near term.

## Details

In the interview, Altman said that given everything happening with AI safety, going public right now would be ill-advised. He noted that a public listing would create pressure on multiple levels, including disclosure requirements, quarterly earnings, and shareholder short-term return expectations, which could undermine the company's ability to maintain a long-term, steady cadence of AI safety evaluation. CoinDesk, citing the remarks, noted that OpenAI's management tends to assess the timing of a listing only after the safety governance framework matures further and the market reaches a more stable consensus on frontier AI risks.
