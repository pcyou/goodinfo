## Core Summary

According to Reuters, Bloomberg and other major outlets, Apple's upcoming annual launch event will be hosted by John Ternus, who formally became chief executive earlier in September. The flagship reveal will be Apple's first foldable iPhone, priced around USD 2,000 and targeted at the premium market. The event is Ternus's first major product unveiling since assuming the chief executive role, and it is also Apple's first move into the foldable smartphone category since the original iPhone debut in 2007. Apple's decision to enter the foldable market at this moment is widely seen across the industry as a full reshaping of the global premium smartphone landscape and a strategic push to defend Apple's leadership at the very top of the market.

## Event Details

**Headline Information about the Launch Event**: Apple's annual launch event, themed "Surprise and Shine", will be hosted personally by new CEO Ternus and will centre on the reveal of Apple's first foldable iPhone. According to multiple tech outlets, the device will adopt a book-style folding design, opening to a screen close to a small tablet while folding down to a conventional phone form factor. Bloomberg has reported that the device will be priced around USD 2,000, making it one of the most expensive iPhones Apple has ever shipped, going head to head with Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold series and Google's Pixel Fold at the high end of the category.

**Product Technical Details**: According to multiple leaks, the first foldable iPhone will deploy several cutting edge technologies across display, hinge and imaging. On the display side, the inner screen is expected to use an Apple-commissioned flexible panel with industry leading refresh rate and colour performance; the outer screen will retain the conventional iPhone experience when the device is closed. On the hinge side, Apple has developed a custom folding mechanism designed to minimise the visibility of the crease and to extend the product's service life, which is one of the main reasons the development cycle for this device was unusually long.

**Price Positioning and Market Strategy**: Both Bloomberg and The Wall Street Journal have reported that the foldable iPhone is expected to start around USD 2,000, a notable step up from the current Pro Max line, although still below some luxury custom phones on the market. Apple is positioning the device as an "ultra premium" flagship aimed primarily at high net worth buyers who want the latest technology, at professionals who need a large mobile canvas for work, and at users seeking a differentiated experience. Market analysts see this price positioning as a reflection of Apple's read on the "premium and differentiated" trend in foldables and as a sign that Apple wants to avoid the mid-market price war that Samsung and others have engaged in.

**Strategic Significance of Ternus Hosting the Event**: This is the first major product launch Ternus has led since formally taking over as Apple's chief executive on 1 September 2026. Bloomberg notes that Ternus, who rose through Apple's hardware engineering ranks, has long been a key figure behind Apple's hardware product development and has deep familiarity with new form factor products such as the foldable iPhone. Handing him the podium for this milestone launch is widely read as a deliberate signal that hardware innovation remains the company's central strategy.

**Pre-Event Hype**: Hours before the event, new Apple CEO Ternus shared a mysterious teaser video on social platform X, sparking widespread speculation among tech enthusiasts and the press. The video did not directly reveal product details, but its style and length matched Apple's traditional pre-event teaser pattern. Multiple tech outlets, including 9to5Mac and MacRumors, have reported that several iPhone 18 Pro camera features have been spotted in code, including improved sensors, a new lens configuration and more professional shooting modes.

**Industry Competitive Landscape**: Apple's decision to enter the foldable market at this moment is closely tied to the rapid growth of the global foldable smartphone market. Counterpoint Research data shows that global foldable smartphone shipments have grown rapidly over the past two years, with Samsung, Huawei, Google, Xiaomi, OPPO and others having launched multiple generations of foldable products. Apple, as the leader of the global premium smartphone market, has long been absent from foldables, an absence widely viewed as a notable gap in its product line. The launch of the first foldable iPhone is broadly seen as Apple's strategic response to competitors that have dominated the foldable category for years.

**Supply Chain and Capacity Information**: Bloomberg has reported that initial production of Apple's first foldable iPhone will be relatively constrained, mainly due to yield limits on foldable panels and hinge assemblies. Apple has established exclusive partnerships with multiple supply chain partners to secure a stable supply of critical components. Market analysts expect foldable iPhone supply to be tight at launch, with wait times for some configurations stretching to several weeks or even months.

## Full Picture

The launch of Apple's first foldable iPhone is a landmark event in the history of the global smartphone industry, with implications that go far beyond any single product debut.

From an industry development perspective, foldable smartphones have evolved from a concept product into a mainstream category over the past decade. Since Samsung launched the first commercial foldable phone in 2019, it has poured tens of billions of dollars into research and development and built a relatively mature supply chain and consumer mind share. Huawei, Google, Xiaomi, OPPO and others quickly followed with multiple generations of foldable products, creating a relatively intense competitive landscape. Apple, as the leader of the global premium smartphone market, has long been absent from foldables, ceding the first mover advantage in this emerging category. The debut of Apple's first foldable iPhone marks the formal arrival of the "Apple era" in foldables, and is set to reshape the competitive landscape, consumer perception and supply chain structure of the global foldable market.

From a product form factor perspective, the launch of the foldable iPhone will further accelerate the diversification of smartphone form factors. For more than a decade, the dominant smartphone form has been the "slab with a large screen". Foldables broke that monopoly by offering "small screen portability plus large screen experience" in one device. Apple's entry will accelerate the transition of foldables from "novelty" to "mainstream" and is likely to push the entire industry towards bigger breakthroughs in screens, hinges, battery life and application adaptation. At the same time, the launch may have a degree of cannibalisation effect on the iPad line, since some of the "large screen mobile productivity" use cases that the iPad currently serves may be partially absorbed by the foldable iPhone.

From a business model perspective, the high pricing strategy of the foldable iPhone reflects Apple's strategic push into the "ultra premium" segment. The USD 2,000 price tag positions the device as a "luxury-grade technology product", primarily targeting high net worth buyers who are not price sensitive. This strategy is consistent with Apple's broader push to grow services revenue in recent years: use differentiated hardware to lock in high net worth users, then monetise through App Store, Apple Music, iCloud, Apple TV+ and other recurring services. As Apple's "most expensive iPhone" to date, the foldable iPhone is expected to become a key vehicle for further penetration of the Apple services ecosystem.

From a technology evolution perspective, the foldable iPhone is the concentrated result of Apple's long term investments in flexible displays, precision mechanics, power management and other technical fields. The core technical challenge for a foldable phone is "how to keep the display intact through repeated folding", which touches flexible display panels, hinge structures and protective layers. Apple, as one of the world's biggest spenders on technology R&D, has accumulated deep expertise in these areas. The launch marks the conversion of years of accumulated technology into a mass produced, commercially viable product and showcases Apple's evolution from "chip maker" to "complete hardware solution provider".

From a competitive landscape perspective, the launch will have a major impact on Samsung, Huawei and other rivals. Samsung, the long time leader in foldables, has built deep expertise in product iteration, consumer mind share and channel coverage, but its share is concentrated mainly in the mid-to-high end. Apple's entry will directly target Samsung's high end foldable users and create a competitive moat through the stickiness of the Apple ecosystem. Huawei, while leading foldables in China, has relatively limited influence globally. Google, Xiaomi, OPPO and vivo will need to seek breakthroughs through product differentiation, pricing strategy and ecosystem building.

From a consumer perception perspective, the launch will significantly lift mainstream awareness and acceptance of foldable phones. Foldables have long been seen by some consumers as a niche or "gadget" product, with low market penetration. Apple, as the bellwether of the global consumer electronics industry, generates intense discussion and media coverage for every new product it releases, which in turn lifts consumer awareness of the category. The foldable iPhone launch is expected to significantly expand the user base for foldable smartphones and drive rapid development of the foldable application ecosystem.

From a supply chain perspective, the launch will be a major tailwind for the global foldable supply chain. Apple, as one of the world's largest consumer electronics brands, has supply chain scale and influence that rank at the very top of the industry. The mass production of the foldable iPhone will drive demand growth in screens, hinges, batteries, cameras and other key components, and provide important business opportunities for display panel makers such as Samsung Display, LG Display and BOE. At the same time, the high specification requirements of the foldable iPhone will push upstream suppliers to step up R&D investment, lift technical capability and drive the broader upgrade and capacity expansion of the foldable supply chain.

From a long term impact perspective, the launch may open a new era of smartphone form factor innovation. Once foldables mature, the industry may further explore more diverse form factors such as rollable, stretchable and multi fold displays. As the industry leader, Apple's product direction often shapes the trajectory of the entire industry. The foldable iPhone launch will inspire more manufacturers to step up investment in new displays, precision mechanics and human computer interaction, pushing the entire industry to evolve further.

## Multi-Party Viewpoints

**Apple management** is highly anticipatory about the event. New CEO Ternus, by hosting the launch personally, is sending a clear signal that hardware innovation remains the company's core strategy. Apple management believes that the company's combined advantages in hardware design, silicon, ecosystem and brand equity will allow the foldable iPhone to quickly establish leadership in the premium foldable segment.

**Industry analysts** hold a cautiously optimistic view on the foldable iPhone's market prospects. Supporters argue that the company's brand power and ecosystem stickiness will drive rapid adoption among high end users and expand the overall foldable market; sceptics argue that the USD 2,000 price tag may limit market penetration and that initial production constraints will weigh on early market performance. Counterpoint Research and other market research firms forecast that the foldable iPhone could ship in the millions of units in its first year and become the leader of the premium foldable segment within three years.

**Competitors** have reacted differently to Apple's entry. Samsung, the long time foldable leader, is expected to respond with more aggressive pricing, faster product iteration and broader channel coverage. Huawei will continue to hold its foldable leadership in China while exploring global expansion. Google, Xiaomi, OPPO and vivo are likely to seek market space through differentiated positioning and more competitive pricing.

**Tech media** coverage has broadly focused on product details, pricing and market impact. Bloomberg, The Wall Street Journal, The Verge and other major tech outlets have provided extensive coverage and deep analysis of the event's potential market impact. Some tech observers believe that the foldable iPhone launch will further intensify competition in the premium smartphone market and may produce spillover effects into the mid and low end segments.

**Consumers** are divided. Supporters view the foldable iPhone as the next direction of smartphone form factor innovation and are willing to pay a premium for it; sceptics worry about durability, repair costs and application adaptation and are hesitant about the USD 2,000 price tag. Market research shows that around thirty percent of existing iPhone users have expressed clear purchase interest in the foldable iPhone, a higher figure than for users of competing foldable brands.

**Supply chain partners** have broadly welcomed the launch. Multiple critical component suppliers expect to obtain important business opportunities from foldable iPhone mass production and will further step up investment in flexible displays, precision mechanics and other areas. Supply chain sources note that initial supply of foldable iPhone components may be tight, but supply and demand are expected to balance within six months of launch as capacity ramps.
