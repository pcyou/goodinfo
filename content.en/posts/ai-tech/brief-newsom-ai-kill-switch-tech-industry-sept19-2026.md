## Core Summary

California Governor Gavin Newsom has proposed introducing a so-called AI "kill switch" mechanism to address potentially uncontrollable behavior of AI systems in critical scenarios. The mechanism would allow regulators to suspend or restrict AI operations under certain conditions and is viewed by the industry as one of the most direct state-level regulatory attempts targeting frontier AI technology in the United States, triggering extensive discussion among tech companies, academia, and civil society organizations.

## Event Details

According to Bloomberg, Governor Newsom has stated on several public occasions that the pace of capability advancement in frontier AI systems has outstripped the adaptive capacity of existing regulatory frameworks, and that California therefore needs to explore new regulatory tools including a "kill switch" mechanism. The core idea is that when an AI system is deemed to pose an unacceptable risk to public safety, consumer rights, or social stability, regulators would have the authority to require the relevant companies to suspend or restrict the system's operations.

Newsom's office indicated that possible legislative pathways for the mechanism could include convening a special session of the California legislature, issuing an executive order, or pushing related regulatory bills. Specific details have not been fully disclosed, but his team stressed that the mechanism design needs to balance risk prevention with innovation encouragement.

On the industry side, several major tech companies based in California have expressed a cautious attitude toward the "kill switch" mechanism, arguing that overly broad regulatory authority may dampen innovation in the AI field. Academia broadly supports strengthening AI regulation but holds differing views on the feasibility of the "kill switch" as a specific tool. Several AI ethics experts noted that the design of the mechanism should be grounded in clear criteria and transparent procedures to avoid regulatory power abuse.
