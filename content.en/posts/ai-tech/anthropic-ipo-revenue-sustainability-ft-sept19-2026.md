## Core Summary

According to a report by the Financial Times, some investors are warning that AI startup Anthropic, even if it successfully completes an IPO, may struggle to sustain rapid revenue growth due to high training costs and enterprise customer paid-conversion rates falling short of expectations, putting the company financial sustainability under pressure.

## Event Details

According to views from multiple hedge funds and growth-equity investors cited by the Financial Times, Anthropic has completed multiple high-valuation funding rounds over the past two years, but the compute and data costs corresponding to unit revenue remain significantly higher than those of traditional software companies. Investors are particularly focused on three aspects: first, paid-conversion rates among top enterprise customers, meaning the migration ratio from trial accounts to paid subscription accounts; second, gross margin of vertical industry solutions, especially customized deployments for high-compliance industries such as finance and healthcare; and third, the marginal impact of model iteration cycles on training costs, namely whether training of new-generation models will significantly raise infrastructure spending.

The report also notes that Anthropic current valuation relies primarily on expectations of future rapid revenue growth rather than current financial performance. If market tolerance for revenue growth declines after the IPO, the stock price could experience significant volatility. The report also mentions that Anthropic management has emphasized in several public settings over the past quarters the increase in the share of enterprise-grade API revenue, attempting to shift capital-market attention from consumer-grade products to a more stable B2B revenue structure.

## Full Picture

Investor warnings about Anthropic post-IPO revenue sustainability in fact reflect a tension that widely exists between valuation and commercialization at current AI startups. Analysis at three levels is worth developing.

First, on valuation logic, AI startup valuations over the past two years have relied heavily on expectations of exponential future revenue growth. Anthropic, as a leading foundation-model company, has valuation multiples far higher than traditional SaaS companies. But unlike traditional SaaS, AI companies have a marginal cost structure closer to the infrastructure industry — every additional inference call brings real compute overhead. This cost structure makes the transmission chain between revenue growth and profit growth far longer and more uncertain than for traditional software.

Second, on enterprise commercialization, leading AI companies such as Anthropic and OpenAI are simultaneously facing two opposing pressures: on the one hand, enterprise customer procurement budgets for AI capabilities are still growing rapidly, especially in high-value industries such as finance, healthcare, and law; on the other hand, the commoditization speed of AI capabilities is accelerating. Open-source models and alternative offerings from smaller vendors are gradually weakening the pricing power of leading AI companies. This dual pressure means whether enterprise-grade AI revenue can truly become a stable cash-flow source still needs to be validated by key data over the coming quarters.

Third, on capital markets, if Anthropic post-IPO stock price performance falls short of expectations, it may have a chain reaction on the valuation system of the entire AI startup sector. Investment institutions may reassess the valuation reasonableness of OpenAI, xAI, Mistral, and other companies, in turn affecting the financing rhythm of the entire AI industry. This transmission chain has already been reflected in some AI funding rounds in 2024-2025, and IPO market performance may become the trigger for a new round of valuation correction.

## Comparison of Viewpoints

Investor position: Some hedge fund and growth-equity investors have stated explicitly that a reasonable valuation multiple for Anthropic post-IPO stock should be closer to that of a high-quality SaaS company rather than a high-growth infrastructure company. This means the market is willing to pay a premium for certainty cash flow rather than solely for future growth expectations.

Anthropic official position: Company management has stated on multiple occasions that enterprise-grade API revenue is rapidly migrating toward becoming the dominant share of overall revenue, and gross margin of vertical industry solutions is also showing an improving trend. But management has simultaneously acknowledged that the cost of training new-generation foundation models remains a significant capital expenditure.

Industry analyst view: Industry analysts are divided on Anthropic long-term outlook. Optimists argue that leading AI companies still have a deep moat in the enterprise market, especially in the dimensions of data security, compliance certification, and industry know-how accumulation. The cautious camp warns that the speed of AI technology iteration may cause the current moat to be rapidly diluted within the next two to three years.
