## Core Summary

The Trump administration announced on June 23 a loan package totaling $17.5 billion to support the construction of ten large nuclear reactors across the United States. This represents the largest direct federal financial support for the nuclear energy sector in recent years, marking a major strategic shift in US energy policy toward nuclear power.

## Event Details

The Department of Energy officially unveiled the details of the loan program. The funds will be distributed through the DOE's Loan Programs Office to support multiple energy companies in building next-generation large light-water reactors at various sites across the country. The projects receiving loan support are spread across multiple states, covering the full industrial chain from reactor design and equipment procurement to construction.

This is a core component of the administration's energy dominance strategy. Under pressure from climate change, nuclear energy's unique advantages as a zero-carbon baseload power source have regained attention. Unlike intermittent renewables such as wind and solar, nuclear reactors can provide round-the-clock stable power output, which is critical for meeting the explosive electricity demand from data centers and the AI industry.

The Energy Secretary stated that the ten new reactors are expected to come online over the next eight to ten years, with a total generating capacity of approximately 12 gigawatts, enough to power nearly ten million homes.

## Analysis

The strategic significance of this loan program extends far beyond simple energy investment. First, it reflects US energy security anxiety amid surging electricity demand from AI and data centers. Tech giants have already signed nuclear power purchase agreements, and large-scale federal intervention will further accelerate nuclear energy's return to the American energy landscape.

Second, from a geopolitical perspective, heavy US investment in nuclear energy also reflects competition with China and Russia in the global nuclear export market. Over the past decade, China and Russia have dominated global reactor exports. If the US wants to reassert energy leadership through nuclear technology, it must first rebuild domestic nuclear industrial capacity and talent.

However, the project faces significant challenges: nuclear plant construction's history of cost overruns, the political stalemate over nuclear waste disposal, and persistent public concerns about nuclear safety.

## Perspectives

**Supporters (energy industry and tech sector)**: This is a critical step for ensuring US power supply security and achieving carbon neutrality goals. Nuclear is the only baseload power source that can replace fossil fuels at scale.

**Skeptics (environmental groups and some lawmakers)**: Concerned the loans may burden taxpayers, noting nuclear construction has a history of severe cost overruns and delays, and the nuclear waste problem remains unsolved.

**International energy analysts**: Believe this will stimulate a global nuclear investment revival, but caution the US must simultaneously invest in next-generation technologies like small modular reactors, or risk falling behind China and Russia in new nuclear technology.
