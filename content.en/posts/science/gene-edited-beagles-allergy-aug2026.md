## Gene-Edited Beagles May Offer Future Option for Dog Allergy Sufferers

**[Core Summary]** American researchers have used CRISPR gene-editing technology to modify beagles, successfully reducing expression levels of major proteins that trigger human allergies. This breakthrough research, published in the latest scientific journal, offers potential for tens of millions of dog allergy sufferers worldwide to coexist with dogs in the future. The study has also sparked discussions about animal ethics and the boundaries of genetic modification.

### Event Details

According to AP News, this research was conducted jointly by multiple leading US research institutions. The scientific team precisely edited genes responsible for producing Can f 1 and Can f 2, the two major allergens in dogs. These proteins are primarily found in dog saliva, dander, and urine, and are the core triggers for approximately 90% of human allergic reactions to dogs.

The research team used CRISPR-Cas9 technology to genetically modify beagle embryos. The resulting gene-edited dogs showed approximately 95% reduction in allergen protein expression. Preliminary testing revealed that patients allergic to these dogs' dander experienced significantly reduced or completely eliminated allergy symptoms after contact with the experimental dogs.

One of the research leaders, a veterinary genetics professor at UC Davis, stated: "This is the first demonstration that gene-editing technology can substantially reduce canine allergens. Our goal is not to create 'perfect pets' but to allow allergy sufferers to enjoy the companionship of dogs."

### Panoramic Perspective

This research marks an important application breakthrough in gene-editing technology for companion animals. Since its introduction in 2012, CRISPR technology has demonstrated enormous potential in agriculture, medicine, and basic research, but its application to pet genetic modification remains cutting-edge exploration.

From a market demand perspective, approximately 10-20% of the global population is allergic to dogs, with a significant proportion unable to own pets due to allergy symptoms. In the US alone, over 30 million people report dog allergies. If gene-edited hypoallergenic dogs can be commercialized, it would create a multi-billion dollar emerging pet market.

However, this technology has also sparked widespread ethical controversy. Animal rights organizations point out that genetic modification of healthy animals may pose unforeseen health risks and violates the principle of "not interfering with animals' natural state." An ASPCA spokesperson stated: "We understand the suffering of allergy patients, but the long-term health impacts of gene-edited animals remain unclear."

Additionally, the regulatory framework for gene-edited pets has not yet been established. The USDA and FDA are coordinating on regulatory authority for such products, with preliminary guidelines expected within the next 1-2 years.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Supporting View**: The American Academy of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology (AAAAI) president stated this research opens entirely new directions for allergy treatment. "If safety is verified, gene-edited hypoallergenic dogs could become a revolutionary solution for allergy sufferers."

**Ethical Concerns**: Animal ethicist Professor Peter Singer noted that while alleviating human suffering is a legitimate goal, "we must ensure genetic modification does not harm the animals themselves. Beagles as an experimental breed have already endured too much and should not become subjects of genetic modification."

**Industry Response**: The pet industry holds cautious optimism about this technology. The American Pet Products Association (APPA) stated that if the technology matures and regulations are clear, the hypoallergenic pet market could become a new industry growth point. However, consumer education and technology transparency remain key challenges.
