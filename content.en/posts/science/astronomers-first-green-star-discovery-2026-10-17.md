**[Core Summary]**

An international team of astronomers from multiple countries announces the first-ever discovery of a star in the Milky Way showing distinct green spectral features, pointing to a new high-resolution spectroscopic technique that reveals a stellar surface chemical anomaly. The discovery opens new observational windows for stellar evolution, surface chemistry, and exoplanet search research, and places new technical demands on global observatories, space telescopes, and spectroscopic data processing pipelines.

**[Event Details]**

- **Research team**: The discovery was made by an international team of astronomers from multiple observatories and research institutions across North America, Europe, and Asia; the related paper has been submitted to a peer-reviewed journal and a dedicated talk was delivered at the International Astronomical Union annual meeting.
- **Observation method**: The team used a new-generation high-resolution spectrograph to collect multi-band fine spectroscopy on the candidate star and cross-validated the results with synchronous observations from multiple large telescopes worldwide, with total spectral collection time exceeding one hundred hours.
- **Green feature**: The star's spectrum shows an anomalous emission peak in the visible green band at an intensity significantly higher than that of ordinary stars of comparable temperature and metallicity; by comparing stellar evolution models with laboratory atomic spectral data, the team pinned down a surface chemical reaction not previously included in standard models.
- **Surface chemistry**: The green spectral feature originates from a high-temperature chemical reaction on the stellar surface involving metal oxides and hydrocarbons; this reaction is extremely rare on ordinary stellar surfaces but is significantly amplified in the high-pressure, high-radiation environment of this star.
- **Follow-up plan**: The team has applied to multiple large observatories and space-telescope operators for additional observation time, planning full-band fine-spectral follow-up of the star over the next six months and a search for possible exoplanets around it.
