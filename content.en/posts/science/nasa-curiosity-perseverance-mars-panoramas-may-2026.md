## 📰 Body

On May 1, 2026, Space.com reported that NASA's two Mars rovers — Curiosity and Perseverance — have each captured stunning 360-degree panoramas of the Martian surface. These panoramic images not only showcase the breathtaking scenery of the Red Planet but also reveal its distinctive appearance across different geological periods.

### Curiosity's Panorama

The Curiosity rover captured its 360-degree panorama at a location called "Nevado Sajama" within Gale Crater. This site is located at the base of Mount Sharp and represents a key stop in Curiosity's exploration of Mars's geological history.

The panorama reveals rich details of the layered sedimentary rocks within Gale Crater. These rock layers record billions of years of Martian geological history, including the existence of ancient lakes and the transition of Mars's climate from wet to dry.

NASA scientists noted that rock samples from the Nevado Sajama area show clear signs of water erosion, providing yet another compelling piece of evidence that liquid water once existed on Mars.

### Perseverance's Panorama

Meanwhile, the Perseverance rover captured a spectacular panorama within Jezero Crater. Unlike Curiosity's location, Perseverance's surroundings are significantly older, dating back approximately 3.5 billion years.

Jezero Crater is believed to be an ancient river delta, where scientists hope to find signs of ancient microbial life. Perseverance's panorama showcases the fine structures of delta sediments, as well as the topographical features of surrounding cliffs and ridges.

### Scientific Missions of the Two Rovers

While both rovers are dedicated to exploring Mars's geological history, their scientific objectives differ:

- **Curiosity** (landed 2012): Primarily studies the geological and climate history of Gale Crater, assessing whether Mars once had conditions capable of supporting microbial life
- **Perseverance** (landed 2021): Focuses on finding direct evidence of ancient life and collecting samples for future Mars sample return missions

### "Time-Traveling" Comparison

USA Today described the two sets of panoramas as a "time-traveling" comparison. Curiosity explores relatively "younger" Martian geological layers (approximately 3 billion years old), while Perseverance confronts a much older Martian surface (approximately 3.5 billion years old). By comparing the two panoramas, scientists can gain a more comprehensive understanding of Mars's evolution over billions of years.

### Sample Return Mission

A key task for Perseverance is collecting Martian rock and soil samples and sealing them in titanium tubes. These samples are planned to be returned to Earth for detailed analysis in a future joint NASA-ESA mission. This will be the first time humans bring Martian samples back to Earth, potentially providing definitive answers to the mystery of Martian life.

### Future Outlook

As both rovers continue their exploration journeys, scientists' understanding of Mars continues to deepen. NASA plans to launch a crewed Mars mission in the 2030s, and data from these two rovers will provide invaluable reference for future Mars exploration.
