## Core Summary

NASA officially announced on August 19 that it has abandoned its rescue plan for the Swift space telescope. Launched in 2004, the telescope has operated well beyond its designed two-year lifespan for over 21 years. During its mission, Swift detected more than 2,000 gamma-ray bursts, making significant contributions to astronomical research.

## Event Overview

According to the Associated Press, the Swift telescope has experienced multiple system failures in recent years, including gyroscope problems and communication equipment degradation. NASA engineers attempted remote repairs but ultimately determined the telescope could not be restored to normal operation.

Swift's primary scientific achievement was detecting and studying gamma-ray bursts—the most violent explosions in the universe. The telescope could automatically reorient within seconds of detecting a burst, providing precise coordinates for ground-based telescopes, greatly advancing high-energy astrophysics.

NASA stated that while the Swift mission has ended, its scientific data will continue to provide valuable resources for astronomical research. Its successor is being planned and is expected to launch within the next decade.
