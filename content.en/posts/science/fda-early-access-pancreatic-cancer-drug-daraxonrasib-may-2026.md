## 📰 Body

On May 1, 2026, The New York Times reported that the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has granted expanded access approval for darxonrasib, a new pancreatic cancer drug. The decision brings fresh hope to patients battling pancreatic cancer, one of the most difficult cancers to treat.

### The Breakthrough

Darxonrasib, developed by Revolution Medicines, is an oral targeted drug designed to attack KRAS gene mutations. KRAS mutations are prevalent across multiple cancer types, appearing in over 90% of pancreatic cancer cases. For decades, KRAS was considered an "undruggable" target — darxonrasib's development has changed that landscape.

Reuters reported that the FDA's expanded access approval allows eligible patients to use the drug outside of clinical trials, providing a pathway for those who may not otherwise have access to experimental treatments.

### Clinical Data

According to published clinical trial data, darxonrasib has shown encouraging efficacy in some advanced pancreatic cancer patients. The Washington Post noted that the FDA's decision "grants early access to a promising drug for one of the worst cancers."

USA Today reported that the FDA's expedited review of darxonrasib reflects the drug's potentially transformative value in pancreatic cancer treatment. The five-year survival rate for pancreatic cancer has long remained below 10%, making it the cancer type with the poorest prognosis among major malignancies.

### Medical Community Response

A physician at UW Health told WKOW that the breakthrough advancement in pancreatic cancer treatment is "exciting." Darxonrasib's emergence provides clinicians with an entirely new therapeutic option.

### Significance

Analysts believe darxonrasib's approval marks an important step forward for targeted therapy in pancreatic cancer. If larger-scale clinical trials can confirm its efficacy, the drug could become a key component of standard pancreatic cancer treatment regimens.
