## Core Summary

Lake Powell, a critical reservoir in the American West, has fallen to its lowest level ever recorded. As a key node in the Colorado River system, this decline poses serious threats to water supply and power generation for downstream states.

## Event Details

According to The New York Times, the water level at Lake Powell has dropped below all previous records. Located on the border of Utah and Arizona, it is the second-largest artificial lake in the US, vital for water supply and electricity across southwestern states.

The primary cause is prolonged drought in the Colorado River basin, compounded by climate change reducing snowfall and increasing evaporation.

## Broader Perspective

This milestone signals deeper structural issues in Western water management. The Colorado River Compact of 1922 was based on unusually wet period data, overestimating available water. The crisis forces states to reconsider allocation policies and invest in conservation technology.
