## Core Summary

Spaceflight Now reported on September 13 that SpaceX completed the 700th launch of its Falcon rocket family in the early hours of the day from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida, sending the final three O3b mPOWER medium-Earth-orbit satellites for Luxembourg-based operator SES into orbit, formally closing out the multi-year medium-Earth-orbit satellite internet upgrade program. The Falcon family has crossed from maiden flight to 700 missions in just over a decade, averaging more than 60 launches per year and becoming the most frequently launched and most reusable rocket series in commercial spaceflight. The success not only cements SpaceX's lead in the reusable launch and commercial launch services markets, but also gives SES a fully upgraded capability to deliver differentiated services to government, maritime, aviation and telecom operator customers around the world.

## Event Details

**Mission profile**. The mission used a previously flown Falcon 9 vehicle that lifted off from Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in the early morning of September 13, U.S. Eastern Time. About eight minutes after liftoff, the first-stage booster landed successfully on a droneship, while the second stage deployed the three O3b mPOWER satellites into the planned medium-Earth-orbit insertion trajectory. The payload fairing was also recovered by a separate recovery vessel after re-entry, further lowering the marginal hardware cost of the mission.

**Closing out the O3b mPOWER program**. The three satellites launched on this mission are the final nodes of SES's O3b mPOWER constellation. The constellation has been deployed since 2022, with the goal of providing low-latency, high-throughput satellite internet services to government, maritime, aviation, energy and telecom operator customers worldwide. With the final batch now in orbit, SES will be able to deliver gigabit-class bandwidth with millisecond-level latency across the globe, including across oceans, polar regions and remote land areas that are difficult to reach with terrestrial fiber or geostationary satellites.

**Milestone significance for the Falcon family**. The original Falcon 1 reached orbit successfully for the first time in 2008. Falcon 9, which debuted in 2010, has now logged more than 550 missions. Together with Falcon Heavy, the Falcon family has become the most frequently launched and most reusable rocket series in commercial spaceflight over the past decade-plus. Through consistent first-stage booster recovery, fairing reuse and a streamlined launch cadence, SpaceX has dramatically lowered the unit launch cost, steadily expanding its share of the commercial, civil and military launch markets.

**SES's upgraded differentiated capability**. As one of the world's largest satellite operators, SES has long operated both geostationary communication satellites and the O3b medium-Earth-orbit constellation. With the O3b mPOWER upgrade complete, SES can extend its services from traditional broadcast distribution into cloud connectivity, maritime broadband, government emergency communications, aviation internet and other high-value segments, sharpening its competitive differentiation against terrestrial telecom operators, Amazon's Project Kuiper and the UK-based OneWeb low-Earth-orbit constellation.

**Implications for the global satellite internet landscape**. SpaceX's Starlink currently dominates the low-Earth-orbit satellite internet market, while Amazon's Kuiper, OneWeb and China's GW constellation are accelerating their own deployments. O3b mPOWER takes a medium-Earth-orbit route, positioning itself with differentiated high-bandwidth, low-latency services aimed primarily at enterprise and government customers. Industry observers expect a multi-layer orbital constellation mix to reshape the service architecture and business model of global satellite internet in the years ahead.

## Full Perspective

SpaceX's 700th Falcon launch and the final delivery of SES's O3b mPOWER satellites look like a routine commercial mission, but they are in fact a microcosm of three converging milestones: the global commercial space industry, reusable launch technology and the satellite internet sector. Their ripple effects will be felt for years to come.

**Reusable launch technology has moved from engineering breakthrough to industry norm**. More than a decade ago, reusable rockets were still experimental ideas within the space industry, with major uncertainties around recovery success rates, fairing refurbishment cost and launch cadence. Today, SpaceX has not only achieved reliable first-stage booster recovery at sea and on land, but has also used fairing salvage, fast-turnaround refurbishment and other measures to compress the marginal hardware cost per launch to a fraction of what traditional rockets require. This shift has directly lowered the capital threshold for commercial satellite operators to deploy large constellations and has made dedicated satellite internet and Earth observation capabilities affordable to more mid-sized countries and enterprises.

**The industrial chain of commercial spaceflight is rapidly specializing**. SpaceX spent more than a decade proving that a "rocket company + satellite operator + customer" business model can run reliably. Today, a complete industry chain has formed worldwide, comprising launch service providers, satellite manufacturers, ground equipment suppliers, operators and end customers. SES, as a traditional satellite operator, has built a long-term partnership with SpaceX on the launch side while competing and cooperating with telecom operators and cloud providers at the network and service layers. This "vertical division of labor plus horizontal coopetition" pattern has become the defining organizational form of global commercial spaceflight.

**Multi-layer orbital constellations are becoming the mainstream satellite internet architecture**. Early satellite internet was dominated by geostationary communication satellites, offering wide coverage but limited bandwidth and latency. In recent years, low-Earth-orbit constellations such as Starlink have been deployed at scale, while medium-Earth-orbit constellations such as O3b mPOWER have been upgrading rapidly. Over the next several years, global satellite internet is likely to take shape as a multi-layer hybrid architecture combining low-Earth-orbit wide coverage, medium-Earth-orbit high bandwidth, geostationary broadcast distribution and tight integration with terrestrial 5G/6G, with satellites in different orbits playing differentiated roles depending on the application.

**The convergence of satellite internet and terrestrial networks is reshaping the global digital infrastructure map**. O3b mPOWER is primarily aimed at high-value customers such as governments, maritime fleets, aviation, energy operators and telecom carriers, and its significance lies in providing reliable connectivity to the transoceanic vessels, cross-border energy projects, emergency response scenarios and multinational enterprises that terrestrial fiber and mobile networks struggle to reach. As capabilities of this kind expand, the path to closing the global digital divide will shift from "laying fiber everywhere" toward "space-based plus ground-based collaboration," with demonstration effects likely to appear first in emergency communications, industrial internet and cross-border finance.

**The national security attributes of commercial spaceflight are strengthening significantly**. Major countries are already sourcing large portions of their communications, Earth observation and navigation satellite capabilities from commercial operators. SES's O3b mPOWER services have long served NATO, the U.S. Department of Defense and several European governments. SpaceX's Falcon rockets have also carried a large share of missions for the U.S. Department of Defense, the National Reconnaissance Office and allied militaries in the UK, Canada and South Korea. The dual commercial and strategic character of this latest mission reflects the increasingly tight coupling between commercial spaceflight and national security.

## Multiple Viewpoints

**SpaceX** says the 700th Falcon launch is just a routine node on the company's long-term roadmap. The core goal remains to push the marginal cost of reusable rockets even lower and to use Starship to lift per-launch payload capacity by an order of magnitude. The company also thanked SES for its long-standing trust across multiple launch campaigns.

**SES management** noted that the final batch of O3b mPOWER satellites in orbit means the company can now offer end-to-end, gigabit-class satellite connectivity to government, maritime, aviation, energy and telecom operator customers, further consolidating its differentiated position in the multi-layer orbital satellite communications market. SES also said it will continue to cooperate with SpaceX, Amazon and the European Space Agency on launch vehicles, ground networks and end-user terminals.

**The European Space Agency and several European industry bodies** believe that the successful deployment of O3b mPOWER provides critical infrastructure support for Europe in satellite internet, emergency communications and defense communications. Multiple institutions have stressed that the European Union and ESA should further increase investment in satellite communications, Earth observation and space situational awareness in the next phase, to avoid falling behind in the global competition across multi-layer orbital constellations.

**The U.S. Space Force and the National Reconnaissance Office** have recently indicated they will continue to expand cooperation with commercial space companies, outsourcing more launch missions, Earth observation data and communications capabilities to mature operators such as SpaceX, SES and Amazon, in order to reduce unit cost and improve responsiveness.

**Executives and analysts at competing constellations such as Amazon's Project Kuiper, OneWeb and China's GW constellation** note that O3b mPOWER has a first-mover advantage in differentiated medium-Earth-orbit services, but low-Earth-orbit constellations still lead in coverage density, terminal cost and consumer-grade user experience. Over the next several years, multiple operators are expected to compete more intensely through multi-layer orbital combinations, cross-constellation roaming and differentiated pricing.

**The International Telecommunication Union and global digital infrastructure research bodies** generally agree that the coordinated deployment of multi-layer orbital constellations such as O3b mPOWER, Starlink, Kuiper and OneWeb will, within the next five years, give billions of people and industrial users who previously lacked stable internet access a new set of connectivity options. Multiple research bodies have noted that the deep integration of satellite internet with terrestrial 5G/6G networks will become a key indicator for measuring the maturity of global digital infrastructure.
