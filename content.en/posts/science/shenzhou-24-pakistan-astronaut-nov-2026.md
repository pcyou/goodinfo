## Core Summary

A Long March-2F carrier rocket lifted off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center at 8:18 a.m. Beijing time on November 25, placing the Shenzhou-24 crewed spacecraft into its designated orbit. The China Manned Space Agency declared the launch a complete success. The crew comprises commander Tang Hongbo, flight engineer Wu Yi, and payload specialist Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a Pakistani Air Force pilot and the first foreign astronaut ever to fly on a Chinese mission. Ali is also set to become the first non-Chinese national to board the Tiangong space station, staying in orbit for roughly five to ten days before returning aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft.

## Event Details

The mission is China's 18th crewed spaceflight and its 12th astronaut assignment to Tiangong. About ten minutes after liftoff, the spacecraft separated from the rocket and entered its designated orbit, with the agency confirming all three astronauts were in good condition.

Tang Hongbo has flown on two previous missions and becomes the first astronaut to board Tiangong three times. Flight engineer Wu Yi is a first-time flyer. Ali previously served as a pilot in the Pakistani armed forces and underwent training in China, including supervised work at Chinese space facilities.

Under the plan, Tang and Wu will spend six months aboard the station alongside Zhu Yangzhu from the Shenzhou-23 crew, which launched in May. Zhu has been studying how the human body adapts to long periods in microgravity. Ali's short-term payload work includes a photosynthetic cyanobacteria experiment and studies of how radiation and microgravity affect seeds. The crew will carry out more than 90 scientific experiments and technology tests during their mission.

Beijing and Islamabad signed a spaceflight cooperation agreement in February 2025, after which two Pakistani candidates were selected and sent to China for training. Ali and Khurram Dawood both reached the final selection stage, and the crew was announced only the day before launch.

## Broad Perspective

The significance of this flight extends well beyond the headline of a first foreign astronaut. It represents a structural shift in China's space strategy. Tiangong, whose three modules were assembled in low Earth orbit in late 2022, has always been operated as a strictly national laboratory with three-person crews rotating every six months. Carrying a foreign astronaut marks the facility's emergence as a genuinely international research platform.

From an engineering standpoint, the difficulty far exceeded a routine launch. Admitting a foreign astronaut to Tiangong required reconciling life-support systems, emergency evacuation procedures, crew coordination training, and medical support protocols with international practice. Arranging Ali's brief stay and early return was deliberate risk management, allowing the first partnership to be validated at low cost before committing to longer joint missions.

The deeper implications sit in the geopolitical and industrial sphere. The comprehensive strategic partnership between China and Pakistan, signed in 2025, is now expanding from defence and infrastructure into high-technology domains such as spaceflight. With no indigenous launch capability, Pakistan gains access to orbit through this arrangement, while China opens a new channel for technology export. For other spacefaring nations in South Asia, this offers a third path alongside the American and Russian systems.

Notably, the decision has also raised questions about intellectual property and orbital resource allocation. Station modules are a finite resource. Experiment scheduling, crew training capacity, and data-sharing rules will all require explicit arrangements among participating states. Who gets access, when, and who owns the resulting data are the core questions that international space cooperation will now have to answer.

## Multiple Perspectives

**China Manned Space Agency** said all technical parameters met design specifications, stressed that the station remains dedicated to making scientific achievements benefit all of humanity, and expressed willingness to cooperate with countries worldwide on the peaceful use of outer space.

**Pakistani officials** framed the mission as a major milestone for the country's space capabilities. Before liftoff, Ali told reporters he was carrying the name of Pakistan on his shoulder along with the hopes of 250 million people.

**Western aerospace observers** have noted the substantive shift in China's posture on international cooperation, while cautioning that it may also serve as a diplomatic instrument. Some see China positioning itself as a hub for global crewed spaceflight during the transition after the International Space Station retires.

**Industry analysts** focus on commercial implications, arguing that opening Tiangong to international partners will support commercial crew and spacecraft-docking services, and could reshape competitive dynamics in the global space industry over the coming decade.
