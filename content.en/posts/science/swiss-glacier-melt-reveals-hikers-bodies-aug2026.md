## Core Summary

A glacier in the Swiss Alps has continued to melt due to climate warming, recently exposing the bodies of two hikers who went missing decades ago. This discovery once again highlights the profound impact of global climate change on alpine environments and has sparked discussions about glacier retreat rates and mountain safety.

## Incident Details

According to BBC reports, glaciers in the Valais Alps of southern Switzerland have shown abnormal retreat recently. Local guides discovered two human bodies during routine patrols, which were later identified as two hikers who went missing in the area decades ago.

Swiss authorities stated that as summer temperatures continue to rise, glaciers in the Alps are melting at unprecedented rates. Over the past decade, Swiss glaciers have lost approximately 10% of their volume, with some lower-altitude glaciers facing the risk of complete disappearance.

Forensic experts are conducting further identification of the remains to confirm identities and notify families. Local authorities noted that such discoveries have become increasingly frequent in recent years, as glacier melt continues to uncover historical mysteries.

## Panoramic Perspective

The accelerated retreat of Swiss glaciers is a microcosm of global climate change. According to data from the Swiss Federal Office of Meteorology and Climatology, the Alps are warming at twice the global average rate. This not only means new safety risks for mountaineers and local communities—glacier melt can trigger secondary disasters such as landslides and rockfalls—but also has profound implications for Europe's water resources, ecosystems, and tourism industry.

From a scientific perspective, glaciers are the "memory" of Earth's climate system. Their disappearance not only means permanent changes to natural landscapes but also the loss of vast amounts of climate history data. Scientists are racing against time to study glacier ice cores, attempting to extract precious climate records before they melt completely.

From a policy perspective, the Swiss government has incorporated glacier protection into its national climate strategy. However, to achieve the 1.5-degree Celsius warming target set by the Paris Agreement, global greenhouse gas emissions need to be halved by 2030. A significant gap remains between current reduction commitments and actual pathways.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Scientific Community**: Glaciologists at ETH Zurich note that Alpine glacier retreat has exceeded even the most pessimistic model predictions, calling for strengthened climate action.

**Local Government**: Valais officials stated they are developing adaptation strategies, including enhanced mountain safety monitoring, adjusted climbing routes, and economic support for affected tourism communities.

**Mountaineering Community**: Experienced climbers warn that climate change is altering traditional climbing conditions in the Alps, and mountaineers need to update their risk assessment methods.
