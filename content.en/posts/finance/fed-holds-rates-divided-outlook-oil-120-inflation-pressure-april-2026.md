# Federal Reserve Holds Rates Steady as Internal Divisions Deepen Amid $120+ Oil

The Federal Reserve announced on April 29 that it would keep the federal funds rate unchanged, a widely anticipated decision that came against the backdrop of escalating Iran-related tensions and surging oil prices. This meeting marked the final policy gathering chaired by Jerome Powell, and it also revealed deepening divisions within the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) regarding the future trajectory of monetary policy.

## Rate Decision and Policy Statement

The Fed maintained the federal funds rate target range at its current level, in line with market expectations. However, the policy statement unusually acknowledged divisions among committee members — some leaning toward considering rate cuts if inflation continues to moderate, while others worry that energy price shocks from the Iran conflict could reignite inflationary pressures.

Powell stated at the post-meeting press conference that the Fed is "closely monitoring" the impact of geopolitical risks on energy prices and overall inflation, emphasizing that the current policy stance is "appropriate" but future adjustments will depend on incoming economic data.

## Powell Confirms He Will Remain as Fed Governor

Following the meeting, Powell confirmed that he will continue to serve as a Federal Reserve Governor even after his term as Chair concludes. This announcement quelled earlier market speculation that he might leave the Fed entirely once his chairmanship ends.

The New York Times reported that Powell said, "I will continue to serve the Federal Reserve as a governor," meaning he will remain involved in future monetary policy decisions, albeit no longer in the chairman's role.

## Oil Above $120, Inflation Pressures Mount

As the Fed announced its decision, international oil prices continued their climb. Driven by Iran's extended blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, Brent crude surpassed the $120 per barrel mark, reaching its highest level since 2022.

The sustained rise in oil prices poses a dual challenge to the U.S. economy: pushing up consumer inflation expectations on one hand, and increasing transportation and production costs for businesses on the other. Morningstar analysis notes that as the oil shock persists, the probability of a rate cut in 2026 is "rapidly vanishing."

## Market Reaction

U.S. stocks showed mixed performance following the Fed's decision. The Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped slightly, while technology stocks remained relatively resilient, supported by earnings season results. Investors are weighing the Fed's cautious stance against ongoing geopolitical risks.

In the bond market, U.S. Treasury yields saw limited movement after the decision, but market pricing indicates that investors have significantly reduced their expectations for Fed rate cuts in the remainder of 2026.

## Outlook Ahead

Analysts widely believe the Fed faces a delicate balancing act: managing upside inflation risks from oil shocks while avoiding overtightening that could damage economic growth. Powell's continuation as a Governor provides policy continuity, but the trajectory of the Iran conflict and oil price developments remain critical variables shaping future monetary policy.
