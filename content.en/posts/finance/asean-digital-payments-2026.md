## Core Summary

China, Japan, South Korea, and ASEAN member countries have agreed to establish a dedicated working group on cross-border digital payments and digital asset regulation. This decision stems from a meeting held in Uzbekistan in May this year, where countries identified cross-border digital payments as a new area of cooperation. Finance ministers and central bank governors are expected to further discuss this matter at the Nagoya meeting in May 2027.

## Event Details

According to a statement released on Thursday, finance and central bank deputies from ASEAN+3 member countries met in Kyoto, Japan this week to review progress in implementing guidance previously established by ASEAN+3 finance ministers and central bank governors.

A major focus of the meeting was technological innovation, with discussions centering on cross-border payments and regulatory approaches to crypto assets. The deputies agreed to establish the AMRO Cross-Border Digital Payments Program and a dedicated working group to support further policy dialogue.

### Cooperation Background

The Kyoto decision resulted from a meeting held in Uzbekistan in May, where the group identified cross-border digital payments as a new area of cooperation. At that meeting, the group instructed deputies to establish modalities by the end of 2026 to deepen policy discussions on retail and wholesale payment connectivity, as well as regulatory approaches to stablecoins.

The group stated in its May declaration: "Building on local currency-based payment systems, technological innovation in cross-border payments, such as tokenization, should be pursued in consistency with ongoing global policy discussions."

### Technical Research Directions

According to a job posting published last week by AMRO seeking a consultant on cross-border retail payments, the initiative may cover research into a range of use cases, including:

- Interoperability of retail cross-border payments
- Connectivity of wholesale payment systems
- Regulatory frameworks for stablecoins and Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDC)
- Payment infrastructure based on blockchain technology

### Regional Significance

This cooperation marks deep integration of major Asian economies in the digital finance sector. China, Japan, and South Korea, as the three major East Asian economies, plus the ten ASEAN countries, cover nearly one-third of the global population and economic output. By establishing unified cross-border digital payment standards, the region expects to reduce transaction costs, improve payment efficiency, and enhance its voice in the global financial system.

## Comprehensive Perspective

This cooperation among ASEAN+3 countries on cross-border digital payments and cryptocurrency regulation reflects the broader trend of global financial systems transitioning toward digitalization. As traditional cross-border payment systems (such as SWIFT) face issues of low efficiency and high costs, new payment infrastructure based on blockchain and distributed ledger technology is becoming a focus of exploration for various countries.

From a geopolitical perspective, this cooperation also reflects Asian countries' efforts to seek greater autonomy in the financial sector. By establishing independent regional digital payment networks, these countries can reduce their dependence on the US dollar-dominated international payment system and enhance their ability to respond to international financial volatility.

For the cryptocurrency industry, a unified regulatory framework will help eliminate policy uncertainty and promote compliant innovation. In particular, the development of stablecoins and Central Bank Digital Currencies will provide more efficient and transparent settlement tools for cross-border trade and investment.

However, this process also faces challenges. Legal differences among countries in areas such as data privacy, capital controls, and anti-money laundering need to be coordinated, and the unification of technical standards requires time and resource investment. Furthermore, balancing innovation with risk prevention and control will be a core issue that the working group needs to address.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Chinese Government Position**: China has been actively promoting digital currency research and application, with digital yuan pilots covering multiple cities. Participation in this cooperation helps China play a larger role in shaping regional digital finance standards while demonstrating its leadership in financial technology.

**Japanese and South Korean Positions**: Both countries have relatively mature frameworks for cryptocurrency regulation. Participation in this cooperation helps promote their experience to the regional level while creating favorable conditions for their domestic enterprises to enter the Southeast Asian market.

**ASEAN Countries' Position**: ASEAN countries vary significantly in economic development levels and acceptance of digital payments. Countries like Singapore and Thailand are more advanced in financial technology, while others are in early stages. Cooperation helps narrow the digital divide and promote regional economic integration.

**Industry Response**: FinTech companies have welcomed this cooperation, believing that a unified regulatory framework will reduce compliance costs and promote innovation. Traditional financial institutions remain cautious, concerned that digital currencies may disrupt their business models.

**International Observation**: Institutions such as the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank are monitoring this development, believing that regional digital payment cooperation may provide new ideas for global financial governance. However, they also remind countries to be mindful of preventing systemic financial risks.
