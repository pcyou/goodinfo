## Core Summary

IBM stock suffered a historic crash on July 14, plunging over 25% in a single day—the largest decline in the company's history since its founding in 1911. The immediate trigger was IBM's second-quarter earnings preannouncement showing AI business growth far below market expectations. This event not only devastated investor confidence but also raised deep questions about legacy tech giants' ability to transform in the AI era.

## Event Details

According to Forbes, IBM released a disappointing earnings preannouncement before market open, revealing its AI division Watsonx revenue growth was only 8%, far below Wall Street's expected 25%. CEO Arvind Krishna acknowledged in a statement that enterprise customer adoption of AI solutions has been slower than expected, particularly in heavily regulated industries like finance and healthcare.

Following the announcement, IBM shares plunged over 25% within 30 minutes of market open, with trading volume five times the daily average. Analysts noted this decline reflects not just disappointment with IBM specifically, but broader anxiety across the legacy tech industry about the AI race.

## Analysis

IBM's predicament reflects the structural challenges facing legacy tech giants in the AI era. Unlike AI winners such as Nvidia and Microsoft, IBM's AI strategy has overly relied on enterprise solutions—a market characterized by long decision cycles, high customization demands, and regulatory barriers. Watsonx, IBM's flagship AI product, despite its technical strength, has struggled to compete with consumer-grade AI products from OpenAI and Google.

From an industry evolution perspective, IBM's case reveals an important trend: AI technology commercialization is not a linear process. Enterprise customers' attitudes toward AI are shifting from "blind enthusiasm" to "rational evaluation," demanding clear return on investment rather than being swayed by technology concepts alone. This shift poses severe challenges for companies relying on "AI premium" valuations.

IBM's difficulties have also sparked discussion about an "AI bubble." While AI technology itself has revolutionary potential, not all companies will profit from this technological revolution. The market is undergoing a painful value reassessment process, distinguishing true AI winners from companies merely riding the "AI concept."

## Multiple Perspectives

**Bear Case**: Notorious short-selling firm Citron Research released a report stating IBM's AI strategy has failed and the company should consider spinning off or selling its Watsonx business. The report argues IBM's lag in the cloud computing era is irreversible, and AI investments are merely "painting on a sinking ship."

**Bull Case**: Some long-term investors believe IBM's drop is overdone. They point out IBM still has strong foundations in enterprise AI markets, particularly in hybrid cloud and edge computing. The company's robust cash flow and dividend payments provide a safety cushion for long-term investors.

**Industry Analysts**: Gartner's Research VP stated IBM's difficulties are not isolated but a microcosm of the entire enterprise AI market. Many legacy IT vendors face similar challenges in AI transformation: mismatch between technical capabilities and market demands.

**IBM Official Response**: A company spokesperson emphasized that AI transformation is a long-term process and short-term volatility shouldn't overshadow the company's advantages in technical foundations and customer relationships. Krishna stated the company will adjust strategy to focus more on high-value industry solutions.
