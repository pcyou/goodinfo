## Core Summary

European defense AI company Helsing has completed an $1.8 billion funding round, reaching an $18 billion valuation, according to CNBC. The company, often described as Europe's answer to Anduril Industries, specializes in AI-powered defense solutions for NATO member states. This marks one of the largest funding rounds in European defense tech history, underscoring Europe's accelerated push for defense modernization amid rising geopolitical tensions.

## Event Details

Founded in 2021 and headquartered in Munich, Germany, Helsing was established by former Palantir executives. The company's core products include AI-based battlefield awareness systems, autonomous drone defense platforms, and military intelligence analysis tools. Its clients include defense ministries of multiple NATO members, including Germany, the UK, and France.

According to sources familiar with the matter, the round was led by several European sovereign funds and tech investment firms. The capital will be used to expand R&D teams, establish new production facilities in Germany and the UK, and accelerate joint deployments with NATO.

Notably, Helsing's valuation has grown nearly 10x in just two years. In 2024, the company was valued at approximately $2 billion; today it has jumped to $18 billion, making it a benchmark enterprise in European defense tech.

## Panoramic Perspective

Helsing's funding success reflects a fundamental shift in European defense strategy. Since the Russia-Ukraine conflict erupted in 2022, European defense budgets have continued to rise, but traditional defense giants (such as Rheinmetall and BAE Systems) have lagged far behind their American counterparts in AI and autonomous systems innovation.

Anduril Industries' success in the US (valued at $30 billion in 2025) provided a reference template for Europe: leveraging Silicon Valley-style rapid iteration to militarize commercial AI technology. Helsing's rise indicates Europe is closing this gap, but challenges remain—internal EU ethical debates over military AI, fragmented procurement processes across member states, and competition with US defense tech firms are all obstacles Helsing must overcome.

From a macro perspective, defense tech is becoming a new hotspot in global capital markets. As geopolitical risks rise, the boundary between traditional defense contractors and emerging tech companies is increasingly blurred. Helsing's high valuation is precisely a microcosm of this trend.
