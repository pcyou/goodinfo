## Revolut Receives Conditional OCC Approval, Steps Closer to US Bank Charter

**[Core Summary]** On September 3, 2026, European fintech giant Revolut announced it has received conditional approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), a critical step toward obtaining a US national bank charter. Once finalized, Revolut will be able to offer full banking services in the United States, marking the London-based fintech's formal entry into the US market to compete with domestic players like SoFi and Chime.

### Details

According to The Block, Revolut announced on September 3 that it has received conditional approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), a key prerequisite for obtaining a US national bank charter. The OCC is a bureau of the US Treasury Department that regulates all national banks.

Revolut CEO Nikolay Storonsky stated: "Receiving conditional approval from the OCC is a significant milestone in Revolut's journey. The United States is the world's largest financial market, and we look forward to bringing a better financial services experience to American users."

Revolut still needs to satisfy several conditions set by the OCC, including capital adequacy requirements, compliance infrastructure, and consumer protection measures, before receiving final bank charter approval. The process could take several months.

Founded in 2015, Revolut currently has over 45 million users globally, offering multi-currency accounts, international money transfers, cryptocurrency trading, and other services. The company was valued at $33 billion last year, making it one of the highest-valued fintech companies in the world.

### Panoramic Perspective

The strategic significance of Revolut's entry into the US banking market lies in representing a "reverse invasion" of European fintech companies into traditional American banking. Over the past decade, American fintech companies led the world in mobile payments and digital banking. However, European fintech companies, with their stricter regulatory environment and more international user base, are developing unique competitive advantages.

From a regulatory perspective, the OCC has become increasingly open to fintech companies applying for bank charters in recent years. This trend reflects American regulators' recognition of the "fintech plus banking" hybrid model. Traditional banks rely on branch networks, while fintech companies can deliver services at lower costs through mobile apps and AI technology.

From a competitive landscape analysis, Revolut will face intense competition entering the US market. SoFi obtained its bank charter in 2022, and digital banks like Chime and Varo are also expanding rapidly. However, Revolut's differentiated advantage lies in its strong international remittance capabilities and multi-currency support, which is particularly attractive to users with cross-border needs.

Challenges are equally significant. The US banking regulatory environment is complex with significant differences between state regulations. Additionally, American users have high loyalty to domestic brands, and European brands have limited recognition in the US market. Revolut will need to make substantial efforts in brand building, compliance investment, and localized operations.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Industry Analysts**: A CB Insights analyst believes Revolut's conditional OCC approval "sends an important signal: US regulators are embracing the banking trend in fintech." The analyst predicts more fintech companies will apply for bank charters in the next two years.

**Competitor Response**: A SoFi spokesperson said on social media they "welcome new competitors," while emphasizing SoFi has "deep user base and comprehensive compliance infrastructure" in the US market. Chime stated it will continue focusing on deepening its presence in the domestic market.

**Investor Sentiment**: Following the announcement, Revolut's valuation expectations in the secondary market rose approximately 8%. Multiple analysts raised their valuation estimates, believing a US bank charter will unlock significant growth potential.

**User Feedback**: American Revolut users generally welcomed the news. A British professional working in New York said: "I've been using Revolut for UK-US transfers. If it becomes a US bank, the experience will definitely improve."

**Regulatory Expert View**: A Georgetown University financial regulation professor noted that OCC approval "doesn't mean deregulation." On the contrary, obtaining a bank charter means stricter capital requirements and compliance oversight. "It's a double-edged sword: greater business freedom comes with greater regulatory responsibility."
