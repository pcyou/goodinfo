## Markets Ignore Geopolitical Risk: US Stocks Hit New Highs During Iran War

Despite ongoing geopolitical tensions, US equities have shown remarkable resilience. With the Iran war now entering its second month and the Strait of Hormuz blockade pushing oil prices higher, the S&P 500 continues to trade near record levels — a divergence between market performance and geopolitical risk that has sparked widespread debate.

### Why Is the Stock Market Ignoring the War?

CNBC analysis suggests investors have already priced in concerns about the Iran conflict. While the market experienced sharp volatility at the war's outset, attention has since shifted back to corporate earnings and economic growth data. Additionally, Federal Reserve interest rate expectations continue to provide support for equities.

Investopedia posed the question directly: "The Iran War Isn't Over, So Why Are Stocks Back Near Record Highs?" The analysis suggests market pricing reflects expectations that the conflict will conclude in the near term, combined with confidence in the underlying resilience of the US economy.

### Oil Prices and Economic Risk

However, the risks have not disappeared. The New York Times reported that investors are bracing for the economic repercussions of the Iran war. The ongoing Strait of Hormuz blockade continues to affect global oil supply, and further oil price increases could pressure consumer spending and corporate margins.

A leading economist told Yahoo Finance that the stock market has become "increasingly disconnected from the economy." Historical data shows that oil shocks typically lead to economic slowdowns, and current market optimism may not fully reflect this risk.

### Outlook

In the near term, market direction will depend on the evolution of the Iran situation and upcoming economic data releases. If the conflict escalates further or oil prices break through key resistance levels, equities could face correction pressure. Conversely, if diplomatic negotiations make progress, market optimism could strengthen further.
