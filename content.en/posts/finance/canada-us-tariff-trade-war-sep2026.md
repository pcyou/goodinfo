## Canada Imposes Fresh Round of US Tariffs as Trade War Enters Prolonged Phase

**[Core Summary]** Canada new round of retaliatory tariffs on US goods took effect on September 8, covering dairy products, perfumes, golf clubs, furniture, and dozens of other categories. This is the Canadian side's direct response to the Trump administration's recent tariff threats and represents a watershed moment in the US-Canada trade dispute as it shifts from short-term friction to long-term confrontation. Canadian officials have openly stated that the trade war will enter a "prolonged battle" phase, with deep reshaping of the bilateral trade landscape ahead.

### Latest Developments

**Tariff specifics:** According to the list published by the Canadian Department of Finance, the new round of tariffs covers approximately 12.5 billion US dollars of US imports, primarily including dairy products (additional 25 percent tariff), wines and spirits (25 percent), perfumes and cosmetics (25 percent), household appliances (25 percent), furniture and home goods (25 percent), golf clubs and outdoor goods (25 percent), among other categories. Some agricultural products and industrial intermediates are also included.

**Policy background:** The new round of tariffs was triggered directly by the Trump administration's recent tariff escalation on Canadian steel and aluminum products and the threat of a ban on Bombardier aircraft entering the US market. The Canadian side argues that US tariff measures violate core provisions of the USMCA agreement and therefore takes reciprocal countermeasures. Prime Minister Carney stated clearly at a press conference that Canada "will not be coerced and will respond firmly with equivalent measures to every escalation."

**US response:** Trump posted on social media warning that if Canada does not "immediately withdraw the tariffs," the United States will impose a new round of tariffs on Canadian automobiles and parts. The White House spokesperson said that President Trump has "lost patience" with Canada's "long-term exploitation" of the United States. The Office of the US Trade Representative has already begun the procedure for a new 301 investigation.

**Market reaction:** The Canadian dollar depreciated slightly by about 0.3 percent against the US dollar after the new tariffs took effect, and major North American stock markets reacted relatively mildly to the escalation of trade friction. Canadian dairy and alcohol importers have begun adjusting their supply chains, and some US exporters are scrambling to find alternative markets. The Toronto Dow Jones Index fell 0.5 percent on the day, reflecting market concerns about the long-term nature of the trade war.

**Industry impact:** The US dairy industry has become one of the most severely impacted sectors by this round of tariffs. Farmers in dairy belts such as Wisconsin and New York have expressed strong dissatisfaction through industry associations, demanding that the federal government provide subsidies or launch market diversification support programs. Canadian consumers are turning to domestic dairy and European imports, and the market share of US brands in the Canadian retail market is expected to decline significantly.

### Comprehensive Analysis

The continued escalation of the US-Canada trade dispute has gone beyond the scope of bilateral tariff games and is having a profound impact on the process of North American regional economic integration.

From a North American supply chain perspective, the United States and Canada are among the most closely integrated bilateral trading partners globally, with daily cross-border trade in goods and services exceeding 3 billion US dollars. Prolonged tariff friction will force businesses to reassess their "nearshoring" strategies. Some Canadian companies originally dependent on the US market may be compelled to shift toward Asian or European markets, while some US companies may see their Canadian market share eroded by European and Mexican competitors.

From an inflation impact perspective, the cost of tariffs will ultimately be borne mainly by consumers. US consumer spending on Canadian dairy products, timber, and other sectors may rise by 2 to 5 percent, while Canadian consumer spending on US appliances and furniture may see similar increases. Inflation data in both countries may face new upward pressure, further compressing central banks' room for monetary policy maneuver.

From a geopolitical perspective, the escalation of trade disputes is prompting Canada to accelerate trade diversification. The Carney government has recently conducted consultations with multiple trading partners including the European Union, the United Kingdom, and ASEAN, aiming to reduce dependence on the US market. If this "de-Americanization" trend continues, it will have a structural impact on the North American regional economic landscape.

From a political impact perspective, the Trump administration's strategy of using tariffs as a foreign negotiation tool has triggered strong political pushback in Canada. Mainstream Canadian public opinion broadly supports reciprocal countermeasures, believing that "succumbing to US pressure would harm national sovereignty." This public opinion base makes it difficult for the Canadian government to make substantive concessions in negotiations with the United States.

From a global trading system perspective, the US-Canada trade dispute occurs against the backdrop of stalled WTO reform and challenges to multilateral trading rules. As traditionally core members of the Western camp that adhere to multilateral trading rules, the continued escalation of bilateral friction between the two countries will have a negative impact on the global trade governance system and may further exacerbate the trend of global trade fragmentation.

### Multi-Stakeholder Perspectives

**The Canadian government** maintains a firm position. Prime Minister Carney has clearly stated on multiple public occasions that Canada will respond to every US tariff escalation with a "calm but firm" posture. He emphasized that Canada will not compromise on sovereignty and core interests and will reduce dependence on the US market by expanding cooperation with other trading partners.

**The US government** adopts a tough stance. The Trump administration insists that the United States has long been "unfairly treated" by Canada in trade, and that tariff measures are only a reasonable means to correct this imbalance. White House officials hinted that if Canada does not make substantive concessions, the United States may roll out harsher tariff measures in coming weeks.

**Canadian business community** reactions are divided. Industries severely impacted such as dairy and wine broadly support the government's countermeasures, but some manufacturing companies dependent on the US market express concerns about prolonged trade friction. A Canada-US Chamber of Commerce survey shows that over 60 percent of Canadian companies hope both sides can quickly return to the negotiating table to resolve differences through dialogue.

**The US business community** mostly opposes tariff escalation. Industry organizations such as the US Chamber of Commerce and the National Association of Manufacturers issued a joint statement warning that tariff escalation will damage the market position of US companies in Canada and may trigger a comprehensive restructuring of North American supply chains. Some US retailers have begun lobbying Congress to restrict the president's tariff powers through legislation.

**Financial market analysts** generally adopt a cautious stance. Economists at Toronto-Dominion Bank believe that the prolonged nature of the trade dispute will reduce Canada's 2026 GDP growth rate by 0.3 to 0.5 percentage points. Morgan Stanley reports suggest that tariff escalation may slow the pace of Fed rate cuts and have ripple effects on global capital markets.

**International organizations** express concern over trade friction. The WTO Director-General issued a statement calling on both sides to resolve differences through multilateral frameworks and avoid unilateral measures that could trigger global trade fragmentation. The IMF warns that prolonged tariff games will constitute a significant drag on global economic growth.
