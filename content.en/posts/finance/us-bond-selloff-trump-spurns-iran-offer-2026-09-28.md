## US Bond Market Selloff Deepens as Trump Spurns Iran Offer Stocks Slip

### Core Summary

Bloomberg and CNBC report that the US Treasury selloff has deepened, with the 10-year yield climbing to multi-year highs. In parallel, the Trump administration has publicly rejected Iran's latest diplomatic overture, layering geopolitical risk on top of rate pressure and pushing US stocks lower at Monday's open. Investor focus has broadened from a single rates factor to a multi-factor repricing of the fiscal outlook, inflation expectations and Middle East risk, driving a phase of asset allocation and risk-preference adjustment.

### Event Details

According to Bloomberg and CNBC, the Treasury market has extended its selloff over the past week, with the 10-year yield pushing to multi-year highs and the long end of the curve under particular strain. Market observers cited by the outlets attribute the move not to a single trigger but to a confluence of forces: a reassessment of the US fiscal trajectory and deficit outlook, a re-evaluation of the Fed policy path and renewed concern about inflation stickiness.

In parallel, the Trump administration has publicly rejected a recent Iranian diplomatic proposal conveyed through intermediaries. The US position is that the Iranian offer fails to address the core disagreements between the two sides. The rejection is being read by markets as a fresh signal that Middle East geopolitical risk is on the rise again. Combined with elevated rates, the move is weighing on global risk assets at the start of the week.

US equity benchmarks opened lower across the board, with sector performance mixed. CNBC's live coverage noted that sentiment has been shaped by both the yield move and a downward revision to corporate earnings expectations, with some of the sectors that had led the rate-cut trade giving back gains notably. Traditional safe-haven assets such as gold saw renewed inflows, and the dollar index held in a high range.

### Panoramic Analysis

From the perspective of US fiscal dynamics, the Treasury selloff reflects a market repricing of long-run fiscal sustainability. With the 10-year yield at multi-year highs, investors are demanding a larger term premium to compensate for fiscal deficit and inflation risk. If the selloff extends, the move will feed through into mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs and the federal government's debt rollover bill, ultimately constraining the room for expansionary activity.

From the perspective of geopolitics and energy markets, the US rejection of Iran's diplomatic offer raises the probability of a near-term escalation in Middle East tensions. The security of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, the compliant pathways for Iranian crude exports and global energy-price volatility are all set to return to the center of market attention. The divergent performance of energy stocks is the most direct expression of a market repricing the geopolitical risk premium.

From the perspective of global asset allocation, the combination of elevated US yields and rising geopolitical risk complicates the rotation between dollar assets, safe havens and emerging-market assets. Gold has seen renewed inflows, signaling that institutional and high-net-worth investors are stepping up defensive positioning against systemic risk; at the same time, several emerging-market currencies and assets are under clear pressure from dollar strength.

From the perspective of policy coordination, this episode highlights how much harder it has become to align fiscal, foreign and monetary policy. The Fed must balance inflation and employment mandates, the Treasury must finely manage debt issuance and market expectations and the diplomatic team must protect strategic interests abroad. When coordination falters, markets reprice quickly.

### Multi-Perspective Comparison

The White House and the Treasury have emphasized the resilience of the US real economy, describing the bond move as a "cyclical adjustment" and reiterating that the administration will stay the course on its fiscal and foreign policy, arguing that a normalization of long-end yields is part of a healthy market clearing.

Fed watchers are focused on the tension between inflation stickiness and labor-market resilience. Some see the current rate level as close to restrictive and see limited room for further upside; others warn that if fiscal and geopolitical risks intensify, the Fed may have to maintain a hawkish stance for longer than markets currently price.

Wall Street strategists are clearly divided. Some believe the recent adjustment is largely complete and recommend adding to high-quality assets on dips; others prefer to wait for clearer directional signals from the geopolitical and rates backdrop before raising exposure.

Emerging-market central banks and policymakers are watching the spillover into capital flows and currency stability. Several emerging economies have already intervened in FX markets or adjusted policy rates to cushion the impact of dollar strength and rising global rates, underscoring how tightly linked global financial conditions have become.
