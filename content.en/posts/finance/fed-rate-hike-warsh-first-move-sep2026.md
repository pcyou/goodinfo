## Core Summary

Reuters reported on September 14 that the Federal Reserve under Kevin Warsh is heading into its first key rate decision of his tenure, and markets widely expect the meeting to formally launch a rate hike. The decision is a major directional signal for global capital markets, foreign exchange, emerging market capital flows and international commodity prices. Warsh has repeatedly voiced concern about inflation stickiness in public settings, and the market currently expects a hike of around 25 basis points. If the hike expectation is confirmed, it will mark an important directional turn for the Fed after several years of relative accommodation and could trigger a chain reaction across global financial markets.

## Event Details

**Main drivers of the hike expectation**: According to Reuters interviews with multiple Wall Street analysts and Fed watchers, the hike expectation rests on four main pillars. First, U.S. core inflation has stayed persistently above the Fed's 2% long-run target in recent months. Second, the U.S. labor market is slowing but wage growth remains relatively resilient. Third, Warsh has repeatedly warned in public that cutting rates too soon would be a historic mistake, and the market reads him as a clear hawkish signal. Fourth, the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield has stayed near 5% recently, reflecting strong bond market concern over inflation stickiness.

**Policy framework adjustments under Warsh**: Since taking over as Fed Chair, Warsh has pushed adjustments at multiple layers. In communications, Warsh leans toward more direct and more hawkish language on policy, which the market reads as a recalibration of forward guidance. On policy tools, Warsh has shown a tendency to accelerate balance sheet normalization, in contrast to his predecessor's gradual approach. On internal governance, Warsh has pushed for tighter coordination of regional Fed bank president remarks to reduce the market's over-reading of policy disagreements inside the FOMC.

**Expected cross-market reactions**: If the Fed confirms the hike, global capital markets are expected to react on multiple dimensions. In FX, the dollar index should extend its recent strength, while emerging market currencies may continue to come under pressure. In rates, the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield may move higher, with a spillover into global bond markets. In equities, financials may benefit from wider net interest margins in the short term, while growth and real estate exposed sectors may suffer from higher funding costs. In commodities, gold may come under short-term pressure from higher real rates, while geopolitical risk premium could provide support.

**Pressure on emerging market capital flows**: The dollar hike cycle has historically been a focal point for emerging market capital flows. In this cycle, Argentina, Turkey, Indonesia and South Africa — all with large external debt and relatively thin FX reserves — may face the most pronounced capital outflow pressure. The IMF's recent Global Financial Stability Report has already flagged the vulnerability of emerging markets during dollar hike cycles.

**Potential impact on crypto markets**: The short-term effect of a dollar hike on Bitcoin and other crypto assets is two-sided. Higher real rates reduce the attractiveness of risk-free assets and may push some money out of crypto into dollar assets, while a stronger dollar may suppress dollar-denominated crypto prices. However, the long-term trend in crypto will still hinge on fundamentals such as the regulatory framework, technological progress and institutional adoption, and the short-term shock from the hike expectation is limited.

## Panoramic Analysis

The Fed's hike expectation is not only a major turn for U.S. monetary policy but also a key directional signal for the global financial system over the next 12 to 24 months. The symbolic significance and actual impact of Warsh's first hike goes far beyond the surface magnitude of 25 basis points.

**Global monetary policy cycle synchrony faces new challenges**. Over the past several years, the monetary policy cycles of the world's major central banks — the Fed, ECB, BoE and BoJ — have stayed largely coordinated, with relatively close timing on hikes and cuts. This time, the Fed may move ahead of the ECB and BoE, breaking that synchrony and reshaping global capital flow patterns. Emerging market central banks will face harder choices: follow the Fed to defend their currencies or stay accommodative to support domestic growth?

**Macro implications of the 5% threshold on the 10-year Treasury yield**. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield has stayed near 5% in recent months, and that is the important market backdrop for the hike expectation. A 5% long-bond yield materially raises the rolling refinancing cost of U.S. government debt and creates new pressure on fiscal sustainability. At the same time, this yield level has profound implications for global bond markets, equity valuations and real estate funding costs. If the hike lands and the 10-year yield moves higher, it could trigger a systematic repricing of global asset prices.

**The global capital flow effect of rising dollar asset relative attractiveness**. Dollar hike cycles usually raise the relative yield of dollar assets and pull global capital back into them. The impact on the global capital flow pattern is multi-dimensional: emerging markets may face capital outflow pressure, currency depreciation and asset price pressure; on the other hand, a stronger dollar may compress the international competitiveness of U.S. exporters and aggravate global trade imbalances. Overall, the global spillover from a dollar hike cycle tends to be asymmetric — the hit to capital-importing economies (emerging markets) is usually larger than the upside to the capital-exporting economy (the U.S.).

**The game between inflation stickiness and monetary policy credibility**. The core logic of this hike expectation is the Fed's concern about inflation stickiness. If inflation fails to recede meaningfully after the hike, the Fed faces a credibility test — is it willing to bear the cost of economic deceleration or even a short recession to bring inflation down? History shows that the Fed's reaction function to inflation stickiness is often non-linear: once it confirms stickiness, it may run a more aggressive consecutive hike than the market expects. The risk of policy overshoot is the systemic risk source global financial markets need to guard against over the next 12 to 18 months.

**Crypto as an alternative asset and the policy game space**. In a dollar hike cycle, the crypto market faces a two-way game between short-term pressure and long-term gain. In the short term, the hike expectation may suppress the price performance of Bitcoin and other major crypto assets. But over the long term, the policy shock to the traditional financial system may drive more institutions and individuals toward crypto as an alternative allocation. At the same time, the gradual maturation of the crypto regulatory framework will provide a clearer compliance channel for large-scale institutional capital. Overall, the crypto market's performance in the hike cycle will increasingly be driven by fundamentals like regulation and institutional adoption rather than by the liquidity cycle alone.

## Comparison of Multiple Viewpoints

**The Fed and Warsh**'s official position leans toward emphasizing data dependence and policy flexibility. Warsh said in a recent public speech that the Fed will make its rate decision meeting by meeting based on incoming economic data and will not pre-commit to a specific path. But the market widely reads this as hawkish guidance because Warsh has already voiced concern about inflation stickiness many times.

**Wall Street's major investment banks** are split on the hike. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lean toward a 25bp hike but stress that the forward guidance in the policy statement may matter more than the hike itself. JPMorgan and Citi are more cautious, arguing the Fed may choose to hold steady to observe more data.

**The U.S. Treasury and the White House** have stayed relatively low-key on the hike, avoiding direct interference with Fed independence. But the Treasury Secretary has publicly stressed that the Fed should keep all policy tools on the table, which the market reads as implicit support for the hike.

**The ECB and the BoE** have adopted a relatively cautious wait-and-see stance recently. The ECB President said at the latest press conference that euro area inflation has come down significantly but still has upside risks, and that the ECB will coordinate cautiously with the Fed. The BoE faces a more complex environment — having to deal with both inflation stickiness and growth slowdown.

**Emerging market central banks** will respond in a divided way. Brazil and Mexico, with relatively well-anchored inflation, may choose to hold steady. Argentina and Turkey, with bigger inflation pressures, may be forced to passively follow the hike to defend their currencies. Asian emerging economies such as South Korea and Indonesia may continue to stay relatively accommodative to support exports and growth.

**The IMF** in its latest World Economic Outlook has issued a clear warning about the global spillover from a dollar hike cycle and called for stronger international policy coordination to avoid systemic financial turbulence in emerging markets.

**The crypto industry** is divided on the hike expectation. Bitcoin advocates lean into the digital gold narrative, arguing that the hike cycle will further reinforce Bitcoin's long-term value as an inflation hedge. Regulators pay more attention to the maturation of the crypto regulatory framework and the rise in institutional adoption during the hike cycle.
