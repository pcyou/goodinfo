## Asian Tech Stocks Slump as Korea's KOSPI Plunges 8%, Triggering Circuit Breakers

### Core Summary

The global technology stock sell-off continues to spread, with Asian markets bearing the brunt. South Korea's KOSPI index plunged 8% in a single day, triggering circuit breakers for the third time this week to prevent panic selling. Meanwhile, S&P 500 futures also declined notably, signaling a sharp deterioration in global risk appetite.

### Event Details

According to BBC and Bloomberg, Asian stock markets faced massive selling pressure in Thursday trading, with technology stocks hit hardest. South Korea's KOSPI index fell as much as 8%, led by chipmaker stocks, forcing the exchange to halt trading multiple times to stabilize market sentiment.

The Korean stock crash is closely tied to cyclical adjustments in the global semiconductor industry. Multiple analytical firms note that concerns about slowing AI chip demand growth, combined with geopolitical tensions, have triggered mass investor exodus from the technology sector.

S&P 500 futures declined in tandem, suggesting US markets may extend losses at the open. Nasdaq futures fell even more sharply, with heavyweight tech stocks under severe pressure.

Japan's Nikkei 225 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng Tech Index also declined to varying degrees, presenting a broad-based Asia-Pacific selloff.

### Panoramic Analysis

The deeper causes of this tech stock sell-off deserve careful analysis. First, after a two-year surge in AI concept stocks, valuations have reached historic highs — any fine-tuning of growth expectations can trigger violent corrections. Second, major central banks maintaining higher-for-longer interest rate environments has exceeded market expectations, increasing financing cost pressure on high-valuation tech stocks.

As a core node of the global semiconductor industry, South Korea's stock market volatility serves as a bellwether. The trajectory of chip giants like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix will directly affect pricing expectations across the global chip supply chain.

From a macro perspective, this selloff may signal an accelerating style rotation from "growth" to "value" stocks. Investors are reassessing the sustainability of high-growth expectations in the current interest rate environment.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Bearish view**: Some analysts believe the tech correction has just begun, and an AI bubble burst could trigger deeper market corrections, reminiscent of the 2000 dot-com crash.

**Bullish view**: Other market participants argue this is a healthy technical correction — quality tech companies' fundamentals haven't fundamentally changed, and current declines create long-term positioning opportunities.

**Policymaker perspective**: South Korean financial regulators are closely monitoring market dynamics and will take stabilization measures if necessary. Central banks worldwide are also assessing the impact of financial market volatility on the real economy.
