## Core Summary

Global bond markets are experiencing significant turmoil, with bond yields surging across countries from the US to Japan, reflecting deep investor concerns about persistent inflation and expanding fiscal deficits. This market shift not only affects traditional financial assets but also poses new tests for the hedging properties of digital assets like Bitcoin. Market participants are reassessing the performance of various assets in a high-interest-rate environment.

## Event Details

According to Reuters reports, global bond markets faced massive sell-offs on the 18th, driving yields significantly higher. US 30-year Treasury yields rose to their highest level since 2007, while Japan's 10-year government bond yield also hit multi-year highs. This synchronized sell-off across markets reflects widespread investor concern about the global macroeconomic outlook.

Key factors driving this bond sell-off include: persistent inflationary pressures, large-scale fiscal stimulus programs by governments worldwide, and expectations of central bank monetary policy normalization. Markets worry that even as economic growth slows, inflation may remain elevated for an extended period, forcing central banks to maintain tighter policies for longer.

Bitcoin and other crypto assets have also been affected. While some proponents argue Bitcoin is "digital gold" that can preserve value during inflationary periods, recent market performance shows Bitcoin's correlation with traditional risk assets remains relatively high. In an environment of rising global bond yields, investors tend to reduce allocation to high-risk assets.

## Panoramic Analysis

The turmoil in global bond markets reveals deep challenges facing the current macroeconomy. First, post-pandemic fiscal expansion policies are producing far-reaching effects. Governments implemented massive fiscal stimulus to combat the pandemic, pushing public debt levels to historical highs. As interest rates rise, debt servicing costs increase dramatically, creating a vicious cycle.

Second, inflation persistence has exceeded expectations. Although supply chain bottlenecks have eased, tight labor markets, energy price volatility, and geopolitical conflicts continue to support inflation. This forces central banks to strike a difficult balance between supporting economic growth and controlling inflation.

For Bitcoin, this environment presents both challenges and opportunities. On one hand, high interest rate environments increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets, potentially suppressing Bitcoin demand. On the other hand, if vulnerabilities in the traditional financial system become further exposed, Bitcoin's appeal as a "decentralized" alternative may strengthen.

In the long term, the global financial system is undergoing structural transformation. Investors need to rethink asset allocation strategies and find genuine risk hedging tools in an environment of increasing uncertainty. This may drive increased attention to alternative assets including Bitcoin.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Traditional Finance View**: Bond market analysts warn that sustained yield increases may pressure stock and real estate markets. They recommend investors increase cash and short-term bond allocations while reducing long-term asset exposure.

**Crypto Industry View**: Bitcoin proponents maintain that Bitcoin's fixed supply mechanism makes it an ideal tool against fiat currency depreciation. They point out that current sell-offs are merely short-term fluctuations, and long-term Bitcoin will continue to benefit from global monetary system instability.

**Central Bank Policy Perspective**: Monetary policymakers face a dilemma. Continuing rate hikes may suppress economic growth or even trigger recession, but premature policy easing could lead to runaway inflation. This policy uncertainty itself is an important source of market volatility.

**Emerging Market Impact**: Rising bond yields in developed countries create additional pressure on emerging markets, potentially causing capital outflows and currency depreciation. These countries may be forced to adopt more aggressive tightening policies even as their economic recovery foundations remain fragile.
