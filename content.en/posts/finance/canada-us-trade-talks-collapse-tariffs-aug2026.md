## Core Summary

Trade negotiations between the United States and Canada officially collapsed on August 22, with Washington announcing new tariffs on Canadian goods. The Canadian government swiftly responded, pledging to impose matching "dollar-for-dollar" counter-tariffs on American products. This trade confrontation marks a new phase of tension in North American trade relations, with potential far-reaching impacts on global supply chains and both economies.

## Event Details

According to The New York Times, the core disagreements center on digital services taxes and dairy market access. The U.S. Trade Representative's Office accused Canada of "unfair trade practices," claiming Canada's digital services tax discriminates against American tech companies.

Canada's Trade Minister stated at a press conference that his country had made multiple concessions, but U.S. demands were "beyond reasonable." He emphasized that Canada would adopt "precise reciprocal" countermeasures targeting politically sensitive U.S. industries.

BBC reports that Canada has published its retaliation list, covering U.S. agricultural products, manufactured goods, and some technology equipment. These measures are expected to take effect within the next 30 days.

## Panoramic Perspective

This trade conflict reflects the deeper trend of rising global trade protectionism. North America, as one of the world's largest bilateral trade relationships, has stability crucial to the global economy. This breakdown affects not only businesses in both countries but could trigger chain reactions:

First, supply chain restructuring pressures will intensify. Many multinational corporations rely on seamlessly integrated U.S.-Canada supply chains. Tariff barriers will force companies to reassess production layouts, increasing operational costs.

Second, this may set negative precedents for other trade negotiations. When U.S. trade relations with allies show cracks, other countries may adopt more hardline negotiating positions.

Third, consumers will ultimately foot the bill. Historical experience shows that tariff costs are largely passed to consumers in importing countries. Canadian and American citizens will face rising prices.

In the long term, this conflict may accelerate fragmentation of the global trading system, pushing regional trade agreements to replace multilateral mechanisms.

## Multiple Perspectives

**U.S. Position**: The U.S. Trade Representative emphasized that Canada's digital services tax "discriminates against American companies" and must be corrected. White House spokespersons stated the president has a duty to protect American workers and businesses from "unfair trade practices."

**Canadian Position**: The Canadian government maintains the digital services tax is a "sovereign tax policy" consistent with international norms. The Trade Minister pointed out that U.S. dairy subsidies are equally problematic, and negotiations should be based on "mutual respect."

**Business Community Response**: The Canadian Chamber of Commerce warned that a trade war will "hurt workers and consumers in both countries." The U.S. Chamber of Commerce called on both sides to "return to the negotiating table" to avoid full-scale trade conflict.

**Analyst Views**: Some economists believe this is negotiating tactics rather than a true breakdown, and both sides will eventually reach compromise. However, experts note that in an election year, political factors may make rational compromise more difficult.
