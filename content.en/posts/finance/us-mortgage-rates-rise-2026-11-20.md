[Brief] US Mortgage Rates Rise for First Time in Five Weeks

## Key Facts

- **Rate increase**: The thirty-year fixed mortgage rate rose to 6.85%, up 0.15 percentage points from last week's 6.70%.
- **Context**: This is the first increase after five consecutive weeks of declines.
- **Fifteen-year rate**: Also rose to 6.15%.
- **Drivers**: Higher-than-expected consumer price index data and the ten-year Treasury yield climbing to 4.35%.
- **Market impact**: Existing home sales fell 1.2% month-on-month and 8.5% year-on-year in September.
- **Outlook**: High rates may further cool the housing market, particularly affecting first-time buyers.

## Event Details

According to the latest data released by Freddie Mac, the US thirty-year fixed mortgage rate rose to 6.85% this week, up 0.15 percentage points from the previous week's 6.70%. This marks the first rebound after five consecutive weeks of decline. The fifteen-year fixed rate also rose synchronously to 6.15%. Analysts pointed out that the recently published Consumer Price Index showed persistent inflationary pressures, coupled with the ten-year Treasury yield climbing to 4.35%, jointly pushing up borrowing costs. The high-rate environment may further cool the already slowing home sales market, particularly pressuring first-time buyers. Real estate agents reported a noticeable increase in prospective buyers postponing purchase plans due to higher monthly payments. National Association of Realtors data showed that existing home sales fell 1.2% month-on-month and 8.5% year-on-year in September.

## Competing Views

**Optimists see this as short-term volatility.** Some economists noted that the rate increase may be related to seasonal factors, and mortgage rates may fall again as the Federal Reserve potentially cuts rates in December. They advise homebuyers not to panic excessively and to wait for better timing. Additionally, wage growth continues, partially offsetting the impact of rising rates.

**Cautious voices warn of long-term high-rate risks.** Other analysts believe that if inflation stickiness persists, the Fed may delay its rate-cutting cycle, keeping mortgage rates at elevated levels for longer. This would severely impact housing affordability, especially for young families. Historical data shows that when mortgage rates exceed 7%, home sales volumes typically drop significantly.

**Industry practitioners focus on market restructuring.** Real estate agents say the market is shifting from a seller's market to a buyer's market in the high-rate environment. Sellers need to adjust price expectations, while buyers have more bargaining power. Meanwhile, the proportion of adjustable-rate mortgages may rise, but this increases future interest rate risk.

## Panoramic Analysis

Changes in mortgage rates are not just financial indicators but important signals reflecting macroeconomic health. Behind the current rate rise is a tug-of-war between stubborn inflation and resilient economic growth. The Fed faces a dilemma: cutting rates too early may reignite inflation, while cutting too late may suppress economic activity.

From a real estate market perspective, high rates are reshaping the industry landscape. First, declining transaction volumes lead to reduced commission income for agents, and some small brokerages may exit the market. Second, builders face inventory backlog pressure and may clear stock through price promotions, creating downward pressure on existing home prices. Third, rental demand may rise as more people choose renting over buying, pushing up rent levels.

At the social level, the housing affordability crisis may intensify. Young families finding it harder to buy homes may postpone marriage and childbearing plans, thereby affecting demographic structures. Additionally, weakened wealth effects may suppress consumer spending as home equity growth slows.

Key indicators to watch include the Fed's policy statement at its next meeting, subsequent inflation data trends, and changes in housing inventory levels. If rates remain persistently high, policy intervention may be needed, such as expanding tax incentives for first-time homebuyers or increasing affordable housing supply.
