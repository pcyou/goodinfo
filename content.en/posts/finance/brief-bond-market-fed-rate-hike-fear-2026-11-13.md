## Key Facts

- **A shift in the dominant concern**: After tech equities sold off and Treasurys caught a safety bid, the bond market's main worry has moved from the artificial intelligence selloff to the possibility of further Federal Reserve rate hikes, which is now driving yields higher.
- **Driving force**: Expectations of additional rate hikes have become the main force pushing yields up.
- **Context**: Earlier this week the ten-year Treasury yield broke above five percent as soon as trading reopened after the holiday, with higher oil prices reinforcing rate-hike bets.

## Competing Views

**One reading treats the return of hike expectations as a return of the main line.** The inflow into government debt during the tech selloff was a tactical safety move, while the rate path is the long-term variable that determines valuations. When yields rise, long-duration assets come under more pressure than short-dated ones, so a shift from a single-sector adjustment to a broad repricing of interest rates means pressure may spill out of technology and into the rest of the the market.

**Another reading emphasises the timing of the switch.** Treasurys had benefited from the equity selloff, and that inflow was clearly substitutive; when a new concern appears and starts lifting yields, the earlier support weakens as well. The two effects combine, meaning the bond market faces a repricing of its own fundamentals rather than a pile-up of one-directional flow.

## Panoramic Analysis

The bond market's dominant line switching from tech-stock risk to monetary policy reflects a change in how investors think. The market is moving back from allocating positions to assessing the discount rate. The first is a horizontal question of asset allocation; the second is a vertical question about the value of time, and they differ in both depth and duration. When the ten-year yield broke above five percent as soon as trading reopened after the holiday, and oil prices rose alongside it, two threads reinforced each other: energy costs pushed up inflation expectations, and inflation expectations in turn constrained policy room.

The more interesting question is the transmission path of this repricing. In the previous tightening cycle, rising yields mainly affected companies through funding costs. In this cycle, a large share of entities funding heavy capital expenditure with debt are compute-related, so higher rates simultaneously damage their refinancing capacity and the valuation of their assets. That makes the bond market and the technology sector no longer two separate lines but two ends of the same thing.

Four indicators are worth watching. The wording of the next Federal Open Market Committee meeting. Whether oil holds its current level until the next inflation print. The direction of the spread between the ten-year and two-year yields. And whether new-issue spreads on high-grade investment-grade credit widen in step with Treasurys.
