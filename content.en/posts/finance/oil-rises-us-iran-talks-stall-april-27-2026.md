## Oil Prices Rise as US-Iran Peace Talks Stall

According to the BBC on April 27, international crude oil prices surged as peace negotiations between the United States and Iran hit a roadblock. Market participants are growing concerned about the rising risk of supply disruptions in the Middle East, pushing both Brent and WTI crude higher.

### Key Obstacles in Negotiations

Iran's foreign minister briefly returned to Pakistan on April 26, with Islamabad working to salvage the U.S.-Iran ceasefire talks. However, the negotiation process has encountered multiple obstacles. In an interview with Fox News, Trump stated that if Iran wishes to negotiate, it can call the United States, but did not confirm a specific timeline or framework for talks.

Meanwhile, Trump criticized CBS's "60 Minutes" program, condemning its attempt to reference an alleged "manifesto" linked to the White House Correspondents' Dinner shooting suspect. While this incident is not directly related to the U.S.-Iran negotiations, it reflects the highly polarized political atmosphere in Washington, which could affect the coherence of diplomatic decision-making.

### Market Reaction

Driven by news of the stalled negotiations, international oil prices rose sharply. Analysts note that the market is repricing the Middle East geopolitical risk premium. If the U.S. and Iran fail to reach a ceasefire agreement, shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz could be threatened — a passage that carries approximately one-fifth of global crude oil shipments.

Traders are closely monitoring the following indicators:
- Brent crude oil futures price range
- Possibility of U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) releases
- OPEC+ member countries' production policy adjustments

### Energy Market Outlook

Given that OPEC+ recently reached a production increase agreement, the fundamental outlook for international oil prices was trending toward easing. However, the sudden escalation in geopolitical risk may offset this bearish factor. Energy analysts predict that if negotiations cannot make breakthroughs in the near term, oil prices may continue to trend upward.

On the other hand, U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) data shows that domestic crude oil inventories are at seasonal lows, further limiting downside risk for oil prices.

### Impact on Global Markets

Rising oil prices not only affect the energy sector but could also transmit to the broader financial market through inflation expectations. The Federal Reserve's latest meeting minutes suggested a possible rate cut cycle starting in June, but if oil price increases lead to a rebound in inflation expectations, the monetary policy path may face adjustment pressure.

Meanwhile, the European Central Bank maintained interest rates unchanged, with Lagarde warning that core inflation in the Eurozone remains "sticky." Rising oil prices could further reinforce this assessment, making the ECB's rate cut path even more cautious.
