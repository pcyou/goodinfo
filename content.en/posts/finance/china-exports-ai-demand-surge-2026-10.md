**[Core Summary]**

China's customs data shows September exports surged significantly, driven by strong demand for AI-related products, posting a recent high. This trend highlights the continued competitiveness of Chinese manufacturing in the global supply chain amid the technology industry's shift toward AI infrastructure.

**[Event Details]**

- **Data Highlight**: September exports grew significantly above market expectations year-on-year, with mechanical, electrical, and high-tech products as the main contributors.
- **Drivers**: AI compute expansion has driven sharp increases in exports of servers, chip components, and high-speed connectors, with procurement by overseas tech giants continuing to rise.
- **Market Structure**: ASEAN, EU, and Latin American markets showed strong performance, partially offsetting the slowdown in exports to the US.
- **Currency Impact**: RMB weakness against USD has provided some support for export competitiveness, though profit margins remain pressured by raw material price volatility.
- **Policy Response**: The Ministry of Commerce stated it will continue to promote foreign trade structural optimization, with a focus on supporting exports of high-tech and green low-carbon products.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**

The strong resilience shown by Chinese exports under AI-driven demand reflects two key features of the global tech supply chain restructuring: first, the AI infrastructure investment cycle has a very significant pull-through effect on hardware demand, with server, storage, and networking equipment exports becoming new growth drivers; second, Chinese manufacturing remains competitive in components and supporting segments, particularly in cost-sensitive and fast-response products. However, geopolitical uncertainty, tariff frictions with the US, and export controls on certain advanced chips remain potential constraints. Over the medium to long term, whether Chinese exports can sustain high growth depends on forming new competitive advantages in advanced chip manufacturing, AI end-products, and green technology, rather than relying solely on scale and cost advantages.

**[Comparative Perspectives]**

- **Official Position**: The Ministry of Commerce emphasizes continued foreign trade structural optimization, with the share of high-tech product exports steadily rising.
- **Exporters**: Orders are ample but profit margins are under pressure, hoping for exchange rate stability and lower raw material costs.
- **Overseas Buyers**: Some tech companies continue to advance procurement diversification, but cannot fully decouple from Chinese supply chains in the short term.
- **Economists**: Believe AI-driven export growth may not be sustainable long-term, cautioning against policy and geopolitical risk-induced pullbacks.
