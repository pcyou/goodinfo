## Core Summary

Evergrande Group founder Xu Jiayin has been sentenced to life imprisonment for multiple economic crimes, marking the judicial reckoning phase of China's real estate debt crisis. This verdict is not only a legal response to the collapse of the Evergrande empire but also a profound reflection on China's high-leverage real estate expansion model.

## Event Details

According to multiple international media reports, Evergrande Group founder Xu Jiayin was sentenced to life imprisonment for various economic crimes. Evergrande was once one of China's largest real estate developers, with debt reaching approximately $300 billion when the crisis erupted in 2021, making it the largest corporate debt default in global history.

Xu's sentencing signals that the Chinese government's rectification of the real estate sector has entered deep water. Since the Evergrande crisis erupted in 2021, China's real estate market has remained sluggish, with multiple major developers defaulting on their debts. The government has maintained a "housing is for living, not speculation" policy stance while pushing for industry deleveraging.

## Panoramic Perspective

This verdict has far-reaching implications for China's and global real estate markets. First, it sends a clear signal: the Chinese government will not tolerate the accumulation of systemic financial risks, and even former industry giants must pay the price for violations. Second, this event accelerates the consolidation and transformation of China's real estate sector, with future industry focusing more on stable operations rather than blind expansion.

From a global perspective, the Evergrande incident and its founder's sentencing also remind international investors to pay attention to policy risks and regulatory environment changes in the Chinese market. For foreign institutions seeking Chinese real estate investments, this verdict means reassessing risk exposure and compliance requirements.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporting View**: Many analysts believe this verdict is a necessary legal action that helps restore market confidence and prevent moral hazard from spreading. It demonstrates the Chinese government's ability and willingness to handle violations by large enterprises.

**Cautious View**: Some market observers worry that overly harsh penalties may stifle entrepreneurial innovation, especially given current economic downward pressure. They suggest finding a balance between punishing violations and protecting entrepreneurial spirit.

**International Perspective**: International media generally view this verdict as reflecting the Chinese government's determination to rectify financial order, but also its potential impact on the real estate market recovery process.
