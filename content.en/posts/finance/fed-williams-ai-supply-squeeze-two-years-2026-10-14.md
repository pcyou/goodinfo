**[Core Summary]**

New York Federal Reserve President John Williams publicly stated on October 14 that supply chain bottlenecks driven by AI infrastructure investment are expected to persist for more than two years and pose structural upward pressure on inflation. He reaffirmed that the Fed may still need to raise interest rates once more this year to maintain price stability.

**[Event Details]**

- **Core Statement**: Williams stated in a public speech that power, chip, and data center construction bottlenecks driven by AI-related demand will be difficult to alleviate within at least the next two years, potentially continuing to pressure goods and service prices.
- **Policy Signal**: Williams reaffirmed his previous stance that the Fed may still need to raise rates once more this year to address AI-driven inflation stickiness.
- **Background Factors**: Major US tech companies have substantially increased AI infrastructure investment this year, intensifying power demand and semiconductor supply chain tightness, triggering market concerns about inflation rebound.
- **Market Reaction**: After Williams's remarks, US Treasury yield edged up slightly, reflecting a mild increase in market expectations of continued Fed tightening.

**[Panorama]**

Williams's remarks signal that the Fed's high-level attention to AI technology's macroeconomic impact has entered mainstream policy discussion. The demand structure driven by AI infrastructure investment differs fundamentally from demand shocks in traditional economic cycle: it stems more from productivity enhancement potential rather than consumption-driven factors, making its inflationary impact more structural. This means that even as overall demand is suppressed by high interest rates, bottleneck in AI-related supply chains may independently push up certain goods and service prices, challenging the effectiveness of central bank monetary policy. In the medium-to-long term, AI-driven supply tightness may force central banks globally to reassess the reasonableness of neutral interest rate levels and inflation target frameworks. Meanwhile, the scale of AI infrastructure investment is massive and concentrated in a few companies, potentially leading to increased financial market risk concentration and asset price bubble formation risks, requiring continuous attention from regulator. Williams's comments show the Fed has recognized that AI's economic impact is no longer confined to specific industries, but may become a key variable influencing overall macroeconomic trends.

**[Multiple Perspectives]**

- **Williams's Position**: Emphasizes that AI-driven supply tightness has structural characteristics, calling on the Fed to maintain tightening bias to address potential inflation stickiness.
- **Some Economists' View**: Believe AI technology will ultimately deliver substantial productivity gains, helping suppress inflation over the long term, expressing concerns about excessively tight policy.
- **Market Participant View**: Some investors worry that continued rate hikes may excessively restrain US economic momentum and exacerbate financial market volatility risks.
