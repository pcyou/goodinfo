**[Core Summary]**

US equities fell across the board, with the Nasdaq composite down more than 2% in its worst session since July. NBC News reported that money rotated into Treasuries and yields eased, an unusual combination in which risk assets and bonds move the same way. Market participants attributed the drop to two overlapping threads: growing doubt about the payback on AI capital spending, and an oil-price rise tied to renewed tension in the Middle East.

**[Event Details]**

- **Index moves**: The Nasdaq lost more than 2%, its worst day since July. The S&P 500 and Dow fell alongside it, and the session was described as broadly lower rather than a structural divergence.
- **Sector detail**: Funds focused on AI stocks fell considerably harder than the index, which points to pressure on crowded growth positioning rather than an even distribution of selling.
- **Flows**: Treasuries rallied and yields fell on the same day. Stocks and bonds rarely rise together, and the combination normally signals a downgrade to growth expectations alongside an easing in policy-rate expectations.
- **Triggers**: Two things landed together. Large technology companies reported results and the market raised its bar for capital spending, while statements on possible military action against Iran pushed oil higher and revived inflation concerns.
- **Read-through**: Equipment and component names weakened in sympathy, and a supplier's disclosure about supply-chain constraints erased an earlier semiconductor-equipment rebound.

**[Wider Perspective]**

What makes this session unusual is that the two drivers are structurally unrelated yet arrived on the same tape. Ordinarily a valuation reset and a geopolitical shock arrive in sequence, which gives markets room to process one before the other. But when technology stocks are being asked to justify payback on spending while oil simultaneously lifts the inflation path, the two reinforce each other: lower valuations compress risk appetite, and lower risk appetite pushes capital toward cash-like instruments, which raises the real cost of funding. That is precisely the mechanism behind same-day gains in both stocks and bonds.

The distribution of the selling matters just as much as its size. Reporting indicates that vehicles weighted toward AI stocks declined noticeably more than the broad market, which tells us capital was not leaving equities altogether but trimming the most crowded position in them. That is a materially different event from the correction of two years ago. That episode followed a valuation expansion, when holders generally had earnings beneath them. This one questions the marginal efficiency of capital spending directly, in other words it asks when the investment converts into cash flow. For assets underwritten by discounted distant returns, that question is far more damaging than a simple earnings miss.

A second detail deserves attention: the spillover into semiconductor equipment. An equipment maker's disclosure about supply constraints erased an earlier sector rebound. That suggests the transmission is running from demand toward supply. Once the market starts questioning the investment rhythm of downstream customers, order visibility at the supplier becomes the first thing questioned in turn, and the doubt propagates back up the chain. It also explains why chip designers and equipment names moved in different directions within the same session.

Over a longer horizon this drawdown is a test of the sustainability of the AI infrastructure narrative built over the past two years. The verdict rests on three observable inputs: how efficiently capital spending actually converts, where the bottleneck sits in power and land for data centres, and how sensitive the financing structure is to the rate environment. The first two determine whether demand persists and the third determines whether the valuation can hold. A sustained divergence in any one of them would reclassify this session from a routine correction into a repricing of the whole framework.
