## Key Summary

International oil prices surged past the $90 per barrel mark during Asian trading on July 20, reaching their highest level since late 2024. The direct trigger for this price spike is the ongoing escalation of the US-Iran military conflict and the resulting concerns about shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz. Analysts warn that if the conflict expands further, oil prices could test the psychological $100 threshold.

## Event Details

According to The New York Times, Brent crude futures touched $91.3 per barrel in overnight trading before settling around $90.5. US West Texas Intermediate crude also climbed above $88 per barrel.

The price surge comes against a backdrop of deteriorating US-Iran military confrontation. US Central Command confirmed that two American service members were killed in recent Iranian missile strikes, with several others wounded. These represent the first US military casualties since the conflict began.

The Strait of Hormuz, as the world's most critical oil transit chokepoint, handles approximately 20 million barrels per day, roughly one-third of global seaborne oil trade. Markets are concerned that if Iran takes extreme measures to block the strait, it could trigger severe disruption to global energy supply chains.

## Panorama Analysis

The breach of $90 oil is more than a numerical milestone; it exposes the deep fragility of the global energy security architecture. First, it reveals that the world economy remains heavily dependent on Middle Eastern oil, despite rapid renewable energy development. Oil's dominance in transportation and petrochemicals is difficult to displace in the short term.

Second, the oil price surge will create cascading effects: rising transportation costs fuel inflation expectations, increased manufacturing costs compress corporate profits, and reduced consumer spending power could drag on economic growth. For developing countries reliant on oil imports, this energy price shock could worsen debt burdens and social instability.

Third, this crisis may accelerate the global energy transition. In a high oil price environment, the economic advantages of electric vehicles, hydrogen, nuclear energy, and other alternatives become more pronounced. Governments may increase clean energy investment, and oil-consuming nations will reassess energy diversification strategies to reduce dependence on single regions.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Optimistic View**: Some analysts argue the oil price spike is a short-term emotional reaction, with actual supply-demand fundamentals unchanged. US strategic petroleum reserve release capacity, OPEC spare production capacity, and weakening global demand due to economic slowdown will all cap further price increases.

**Pessimistic View**: Geopolitical risk experts warn of escalation risks. Iran could retaliate through proxy wars, cyberattacks, or strikes on Gulf state oil facilities. Any escalation could trigger more severe supply disruptions.

**Neutral View**: The International Energy Agency has called for restraint from all parties, emphasizing that the global oil market still has sufficient buffer capacity to handle the current crisis. The agency stated it will closely monitor developments and coordinate strategic reserve releases if necessary.
