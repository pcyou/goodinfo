## Core Summary

Tech giant IBM saw its stock plunge over 25% in a single session after issuing a second-quarter earnings warning. The company cautioned that massive corporate investment in artificial intelligence is crowding out budgets for traditional software and services, pressuring core business revenue. The announcement triggered a broad selloff in the technology sector.

## Details

In its after-hours earnings warning, IBM stated that despite strong AI-related demand, customers are reallocating IT budgets as they accelerate AI deployments, cutting spending on traditional software licenses and consulting services. The company now expects Q2 revenue to fall below prior analyst expectations.

Following the announcement, IBM shares dropped over 25% in after-hours trading. Reuters reported the decline was pushing IBM toward its worst single-day performance in history. The broader tech sector also suffered, with multiple software stocks declining.

## Analysis

IBM's predicament reveals a deep contradiction: while AI transformation offers new growth opportunities, it may erode traditional revenue sources in the short term. Massive corporate spending on AI infrastructure (GPU procurement, cloud services) is squeezing other IT spending categories. This "crowding out effect" affects not just IBM but potentially the entire enterprise software industry.

More broadly, IBM's case may signal a structural adjustment underway in the tech sector. Over the past two years, markets have assigned extremely high valuations to AI concept stocks, but actual commercialized revenue remains concentrated among a few top companies (NVIDIA, Microsoft). For companies like IBM with a base in consulting and traditional software, the pain of AI transformation may be more severe than expected.

Investors need to reassess the benefit sequence across different segments of the AI investment cycle. Hardware infrastructure providers benefit first, while monetization at the application and consulting layers may lag by years.

## Perspectives

**Bears**: Some analysts believe IBM's warning reflects a common problem across the enterprise software industry. Morgan Stanley downgraded IBM, saying the AI budget crowding-out effect could persist for several quarters.

**Bulls**: Others argue the market reaction is excessive. IBM's AI business (including the watsonx platform) continues to grow rapidly and will compensate for traditional business declines over time. Some value investors believe the 25% drop has fully priced in the bad news, making the valuation attractive.

**Industry observers**: Tech analysts note IBM is not unique. Other companies with a heavy traditional IT services base (Accenture, Cognizant) may face similar pressure. AI transformation's reshaping of the industry landscape is just beginning.
