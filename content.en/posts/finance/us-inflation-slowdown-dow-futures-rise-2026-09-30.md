## U.S. Inflation Data Slowdown Lifts Stock Futures, Dow Futures Gain Over 100 Points Pre-Market

### Core Summary

According to CNBC, the latest inflation data released by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics showed that inflationary pressure eased last month, triggering bullish expectations on Wall Street regarding the pace of U.S. inflation decline. U.S. stock futures rose broadly pre-market, with Dow Jones futures gaining over 100 points, and the market is once again betting that the Federal Reserve may begin a rate-cutting cycle before year-end, lifting risk appetite across global capital markets.

### Event Details

According to CNBC, the data released by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics shows that the core measure of inflationary pressure eased significantly in the latest month, well below the previous month and market consensus. Wall Street interpreted this change as evidence that U.S. inflation, after a period of back-and-forth, is re-entering a steady downward channel, providing a more solid basis for the Federal Reserve to consider rate cuts in the remaining policy meetings this year.

Affected by this, U.S. stock index futures all rose during the pre-market trading session. Dow Jones futures gained more than 100 points at one point, while S&P 500 futures and Nasdaq 100 futures also recorded notable gains, reflecting a rapid recovery in market risk appetite. Rate-sensitive sectors such as technology, consumer, and financials performed particularly strongly, with some heavyweight stocks gaining more than 1% pre-market.

Reports note that investor focus has shifted from "whether inflation will continue to rise" to "at what pace the Federal Reserve will begin a rate-cutting cycle." Pricing in the interest-rate futures market shows that some traders have raised the probability of the Fed cutting rates by 25 basis points at one of the remaining policy meetings this year to a relatively high level. The market also expects that the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield will fall after the inflation data release, providing a more accommodative liquidity environment for risk asset valuations.

However, Wall Street analysts also cautioned that, although the inflation data has shown a decline, the path back to the Fed's 2% target is still not smooth, and uncertainty remains around services inflation, housing costs, and energy prices in the inflation trajectory over the coming months. Therefore, even if futures pre-market reflects strong rate-cut expectations, investors still need to pay attention to the further guidance from the next several rounds of key economic data on the rate-cut pace.

### Panoramic Analysis

Inflation data is often the "weathervane" of financial market expectations, and even slight changes can trigger dramatic repricing across asset classes. The clear decline in this inflation data suggests that the "sticky inflation" issue that has plagued the U.S. economy over the past two-plus years is showing new positive signals. From the perspective of Federal Reserve policy, this data provides more solid support for it to adjust its policy stance in the remaining policy meetings this year, and the timing of the start of the rate-cutting cycle may therefore move forward.

At a deeper level, the change in the inflation pace will not only affect asset pricing in the U.S. domestic market but will also transmit to other major economies through multiple channels such as the U.S. dollar exchange rate, U.S. Treasury yields, and global capital flows. Historically, every Fed policy pivot has been accompanied by a reshuffle of global capital flows: emerging-market assets often gain valuation repair opportunities during the window of a weaker dollar and lower U.S. Treasury yields, while European and Japanese markets may come under pressure due to capital flow back into the U.S. The policy expectation pivot triggered by this inflation data has the potential to become a trigger point for global capital reallocation.

From the perspective of corporate earnings and valuations, the decline in inflation combined with rate-cut expectations will significantly improve the discount rate for future cash flow discounting, benefiting the overall valuations of growth stocks, technology stocks, and long-duration assets. High-valuation sectors such as AI, clean energy, and biomedicine had previously faced valuation pressure due to high interest rates. The current decline in inflation data could become an important inflection point for the valuation repair of these sectors. At the same time, industries with higher leverage and sensitivity to financing costs will also benefit from the marginal improvement in the future financing environment.

From a broader policy coordination perspective, the decline in inflation also provides more flexible operating space for coordination between U.S. fiscal policy and monetary policy. Against the backdrop of inflation pressures still present, U.S. fiscal expansion faced constraints; once inflation falls, the coordination space between fiscal and monetary policy will further expand. This may have a profound impact on the medium- and long-term evolution of global asset prices, trade patterns, and geopolitical economic landscapes.

### Multi-Perspective Comparison

Proponents argue that the clear decline in inflation data is a key advance on the path of a "soft landing" for the U.S. economy, validating the Fed's earlier judgment that "inflation will gradually return to target." Pricing in the market interest-rate futures market also reflects that investors are increasingly inclined to believe that the Fed will begin a rate-cutting cycle before year-end, an expectation that provides significant support for risk assets. Multiple Wall Street strategists believe that the inflation decline window may become the starting point of a new round of upside in U.S. equities, especially beneficial to growth technology stocks that had previously been under pressure.

Cautious voices point out that a single month's inflation data decline is not yet sufficient to constitute evidence of a trend reversal, and uncertainty remains in services inflation, housing inflation, and the core drivers of wage growth. Fed officials have repeatedly emphasized in recent statements that they need to see more sustainable inflation decline data before making a rate-cut decision. In addition, global geopolitical risks, energy price volatility, and potential supply-chain shocks could all push inflation higher again over the coming months, weakening the current market optimism.

International institutions and policy researchers are focused on the potential impact of inflation decline on global capital flows. On one hand, the decline of the U.S. dollar and U.S. Treasury yields may drive capital back into emerging markets, easing the capital outflow pressure on some emerging economies; on the other hand, the divergence in policy pace among major central banks may exacerbate volatility in foreign exchange markets, and some economies dependent on dollar-denominated debt still need to be wary of rising financing costs from interest-rate volatility. Recent multilateral agency reports note that global financial conditions may ease marginally against the backdrop of inflation decline, but the sustainability of the easing still depends on the stability of inflation paths in major economies.
