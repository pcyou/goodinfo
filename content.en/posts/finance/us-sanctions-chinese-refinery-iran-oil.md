## US Expands Iran Oil Sanctions, Targets Chinese Independent Refinery

The US Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) announced a new round of sanctions on April 25, targeting a Chinese independent refinery—commonly known as a "teapot" refiner—alongside approximately 40 shipping companies and vessels involved in operating Iran's shadow fleet.

According to CNBC, the sanctions represent the latest escalation in Washington's campaign to pressure Iran's economy. In its statement, OFAC alleged that these entities facilitated Iran's efforts to circumvent international restrictions on its oil exports through a complex network of financial and shipping arrangements. The sanctioned Chinese teapot refinery was accused of being a major long-term buyer of sanctioned Iranian crude, while the shipping companies allegedly used tactics such as disabling vessel tracking systems and frequently changing ship flags to conceal the true origins of oil shipments.

This sanctions action marks a significant expansion of Washington's reach into Iran's oil export network. Previously, US sanctions had primarily focused on Iranian domestic entities and a limited number of third-country enterprises. The direct sanctioning of a Chinese refinery could intensify existing trade tensions between Washington and Beijing.

Analysts note that as the world's largest crude importer, China's independent refineries play a crucial role in global oil procurement. The sanctions may force these companies to adjust their purchasing strategies and could create ripple effects throughout the global crude supply chain.

Iran has not yet issued an official response to this latest round of sanctions. Market analysts expect short-term volatility in international oil prices, though the overall impact will depend on whether companies in other countries continue to engage in oil trade with Iran.
