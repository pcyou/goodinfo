### Key Facts

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said naval mines had prevented three tankers from passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and traders reassessing Middle East supply risks pushed oil prices higher.

### Wider Perspective

Mines are a low-cost asymmetric tool whose impact extends well beyond the military dimension: once a key route is viewed as mined, shipowners divert or halt voyages, and the effective loss of capacity can multiply well beyond the direct incidents. With the strait carrying close to a third of seaborne crude, any real reduction in transit efficiency transmits rapidly through freight, insurance and inventory channels to end prices. How far prices move now depends on demining progress and escort arrangements restoring shipowner confidence, which in turn requires actual military de-escalation rather than unilateral statements.
