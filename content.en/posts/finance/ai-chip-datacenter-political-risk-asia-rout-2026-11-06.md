## Key Facts

Asian technology stocks tumbled on Friday, tracking a sharp sell-off on Wall Street overnight that pushed the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite into correction territory. South Korean memory chipmaker SK Hynix fell 10.44% and Samsung Electronics shed 6.35%, while LG Innotek and LG Display dropped 7.02% and 4.45% respectively. Japan's Advantest fell 4.99%, Tokyo Electron slid more than 5%, SoftBank Group tumbled 10.83%, Kioxia Holdings fell 8.72% and Renesas Electronics declined 5.74%. Taiwan's TSMC slid 4.69%, and on the Chinese mainland both the STAR Market and the ChiNext Artificial Intelligence Index dropped more than 4%.

- **Overnight backdrop**: the Nasdaq Composite sank 5.5%, closing more than 10% below its recent peak, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged 12.2% as investors questioned the sustainability of the artificial intelligence investment boom and the returns on enormous data-centre spending.
- **The immediate trigger**: investors worried that the Democrats' sweeping victory in the US midterms could bring tighter restrictions on data-centre development, slowing demand for AI chips and the infrastructure around them.
- **The European exception**: European equities were broadly lower but technology was among the least-hit sectors, with losses of around 0.25% in morning trading. Dutch lithography maker ASML rose about 4% and ASMI rose about 3%.
- **Trading structure**: the rout was amplified by position unwinding, stop-loss selling and elevated volatility, according to Timothy Moe, chief Asia-Pacific equity strategist at Goldman Sachs, who added that potential political opposition to US data-centre construction could affect demand and profits for Asian AI hardware suppliers.
- **The political backdrop**: technology companies had already warned about local and voter backlash against data centres before this sell-off. Meta warned that election backlash could make AI expansion harder, and OpenAI said it would stop using non-disclosure agreements for data-centre projects, while state-level clean-energy and permitting debates regained momentum.

## Event Details

Moe's framing was blunt. If there is concern about the speed and magnitude of buildout in the United States, then that assumption goes directly into question about everything downstream of it, and the market's tendency is always to shoot first and ask questions later. The comment matters because it locates the repricing in expected order books rather than in current demand, since none of the reporting points to any change in actual shipments during the week.

That is also what makes the European divergence important. ASML and ASMI sell lithography systems into fabs regardless of where the racks that consume the output eventually sit, so a reduction in the expected pace of US data-centre construction does not reduce the expected pace of fab construction in the same way it reduces expected memory purchases. The two European names rose while Asian memory and foundry names fell by single digits to low double digits, and the pattern maps cleanly onto customer location rather than onto the semiconductor cycle.

The political channel runs through several layers at once. Federal legislation would set the baseline, but the binding constraint on a large data-centre project is usually state and municipal: zoning, power interconnect queues, water use and local tax treatment. It is also where voter sentiment is expressed, and the recent results gave local campaigns a clearer mandate than they had before. Several states with new legislative control are pushing clean-energy and siting agendas that could complicate approvals, and the companies that have already gone public on their expansion plans are the ones whose capital spending guidance now carries the most policy risk.

## Competing Views

The bearish case rests on the location of demand. If the expected speed and scale of construction inside the United States becomes uncertain, every order assumption built on it must be revisited, and since the market prices that kind of assumption before it prices realised shipments, the correction can outrun any actual change in order intake.

The counterargument is that the political resistance has so far stayed at the level of statements. No restrictive statute has been enacted, and the objection to data-centre siting has not yet produced a single withdrawn project of the scale that would justify repricing a global supply chain. Bulls read the same evidence differently: a company is unlikely to walk away from a multi-billion-dollar build, and political resistance typically shortens a timeline rather than cancels it.

A third reading emphasises what did not fall. European equipment names rose on the same day. That the market separated the two rather than selling both suggests it is discriminating between business models, not rejecting an entire narrative about the build-out. Whether that discrimination is sophisticated positioning or an over-correction will only become clear when guidance for the next quarter is published.

## Panoramic Analysis

The most instructive feature of this session is not the 12.2% fall in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index but the split between Europe and Asia. Equipment makers rose while memory and foundry names fell, which means the market was not pricing a semiconductor downturn. It was pricing the geography of demand. Suppliers sell into fabs, and where the racks ultimately sit does not change how many fabs get built. Suppliers of memory and advanced foundry capacity sell into the deployment itself, and that deployment now carries a location risk that did not exist in the pricing models of the past three years.

The deeper consequence is that data centres have entered the price structure as a political variable rather than a financial one. For years the standard forecasting framework was straightforward: hyperscaler capital spending was a function of those companies' own balance sheets, the supply of advanced chips, and the availability of power, so an analyst needed to track a handful of earnings calls and a few supply bottlenecks. Under that framework a state zoning decision was noise. Under the new one it carries roughly the same information value as a fab expansion announcement. That is a paradigm shift in how the chain gets modelled, and it is the single most durable implication of this session.

Two consequences follow that are not yet fully priced. The first is that geographic redundancy in the supply chain stops being a cost question and becomes a risk question. Concentrating memory capacity in a handful of locations was rational when the objective was amortisation; now the same concentration represents a single shared political exposure, which helps explain why Kioxia and SK Hynix were among the sharpest decliners. The second is that second-tier suppliers get repriced in the opposite direction: a vendor whose revenue does not depend on US hyperscale customers can now trade at a relative premium precisely because its customers are not exposed to the political table in question, which is what the European strength reflects.

One clarification is worth making, because it is easy to misread. This session's move came after the Nasdaq had already entered correction territory, meaning the artificial intelligence trade was already in drawdown and the political news amplified an existing adjustment rather than causing it. The flip side is equally important. When a trade is fragile, any incremental headline carries greater marginal damage. The same message that might have been worth two points three months ago was worth ten today. Whether the market is currently repricing fundamentals or simply liquidating crowded positioning will not be clear until the next round of capital spending guidance is published.

Three indicators deserve monitoring: whether state legislatures write data-centre siting and permitting into statute rather than resolution during the 2027 sessions, whether hyperscaler capital spending guidance shows any softening in language, and whether memory and foundry suppliers begin disclosing structural shifts in where their customers are building.
