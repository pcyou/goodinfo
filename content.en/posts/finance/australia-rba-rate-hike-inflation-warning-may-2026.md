## 📰 Main Story

The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) announced another interest rate hike on Tuesday, making it one of the few major central banks still tightening policy in the current global cycle. The bank warned explicitly in its policy statement that inflation is likely to remain above target for an extended period, citing escalation in the Middle East as a key risk factor.

### Middle East Conflict Fuels Inflation Expectations

The RBA noted in its statement that domestic inflation has picked up materially since the second half of 2025. Tensions in the Middle East -- particularly around the Strait of Hormuz -- have driven sharp increases in fuel and commodity prices, further compounding imported inflation pressures.

As one of the world's major resource exporters, Australia faces a dilemma amid surging energy prices: on the one hand, higher mineral and energy export revenues benefit the trade balance; on the other, elevated oil prices directly push up domestic transportation, production, and living costs, eroding consumer purchasing power.

### Uncertain Rate Path

The RBA governor told reporters that the central bank would "closely monitor" the impact of Middle East developments on the global economy and commodity markets but declined to give clear guidance on the future interest rate trajectory. Market analysts believe that if the Hormuz Strait passage crisis persists, the RBA may be forced to continue raising rates in the coming months.

The Australian dollar strengthened slightly following the rate decision, while equity markets weakened, reflecting investor concerns about growth prospects in a higher-rate environment.

### Global Economic Spillovers

Australia's rate hike further underscores the spillover effects of the Middle East conflict on the global economy. The European Central Bank and the Federal Reserve have both adjusted their monetary policy outlooks due to energy price volatility. For Australia, which is heavily reliant on international trade, the dual pressure of slowing global growth and elevated energy prices will present an increasingly complex policy landscape.
