## SpaceX Stock Hits New All-Time Low as AI Capex Jumps in Q2

SpaceX's stock price has plummeted to a record low following the release of its first quarterly earnings report, which revealed massive capital expenditure plans for artificial intelligence infrastructure. The sharp decline reflects growing investor concern over the company's aggressive AI spending strategy and its potential impact on profitability.

The aerospace and electric vehicle conglomerate reported that AI-related capital expenditures surged dramatically in the second quarter, as CEO Elon Musk pushes to integrate advanced AI capabilities across Tesla's autonomous driving systems, SpaceX's satellite operations, and xAI's language models. Musk told investors that people were "underestimating" his company's AI ambitions.

### Key Details

According to Yahoo Finance and the Wall Street Journal, SpaceX's stock dropped to historic lows after releasing its first quarterly earnings report since going public. The report showed the company significantly increased AI infrastructure investment in Q2, including data center construction, GPU procurement, and AI research team expansion.

In the earnings call, Musk stated that investors are "underestimating" SpaceX's AI potential. He emphasized that the company is deeply integrating AI technology across multiple business segments including Tesla autonomous driving, SpaceX satellite operations, and xAI language models. However, this aggressive AI investment strategy has not been well received by the market.

Analysts point out that SpaceX's AI capital expenditure far exceeded expectations, which will severely compress profit margins in the short term. While AI technology has broad long-term prospects, the massive upfront investment has made many investors uneasy. The Dow Jones Industrial Average continued to rise that day, but SpaceX declined against the trend, highlighting market division over its AI strategy.

### Industry Impact

The SpaceX stock plunge reflects a core contradiction in the current AI investment boom: the tension between technological vision and short-term financial performance. Musk has always been known for his grand technological visions, from electric vehicles to space exploration to artificial intelligence, he consistently bets on long-term disruptive technologies. However, capital markets' tolerance for patience is declining.

This event also reveals the enormous cost of AI infrastructure investment. Building data centers, procuring high-performance GPUs, and recruiting top AI talent each require astronomical funding. For a company like SpaceX with multiple business lines, advancing multiple AI projects simultaneously means resource dispersion and multiplied financial pressure.

From a broader perspective, SpaceX's predicament is not unique. Many tech companies face similar choices: conservative investment to maintain short-term profits, or aggressive spending to seize opportunities in the AI era? Musk's answer is clear — he chooses the latter, but whether the market will give him enough time to prove this decision correct remains unknown.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Pro-Musk view**: Some long-term investors believe AI is the most important technological transformation of the next decade, and current investments will yield rich returns in the future. "Musk has been questioned many times in history but ultimately proved himself right," said a tech fund manager.

**Financial concern view**: Wall Street analysts are generally cautious about SpaceX's AI spending plans. "Massive capital expenditure will severely compress profit margins; investors need to see a clear return timeline," a Morgan Stanley analyst pointed out.

**Neutral observation view**: Others argue that SpaceX's diversified business structure provides some buffer for its AI investment. "Even if AI projects lose money in the short term, Tesla and SpaceX's core businesses can still support company operations."
