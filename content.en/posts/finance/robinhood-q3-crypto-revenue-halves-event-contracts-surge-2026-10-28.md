## Key Facts

- Total revenue for the quarter ending September 30 was 1.27 billion dollars, a 12 percent increase from a year earlier.
- Transaction-based revenue was 867 million dollars, up 19 percent from a year earlier but below the Wall Street estimate of 879 million.
- Transaction revenue growth was driven primarily by a more than 500 percent increase in event contracts revenue. Options revenue grew 31 percent to 399 million and equities revenue rose 41 percent to 121 million.
- Those gains were partially offset by cryptocurrency revenue, which fell 55 percent to 120 million.
- Net income fell to 468 million dollars, or 51 cents per share, from 556 million dollars, or 61 cents a share, a year earlier.
- The chief financial officer, Shiv Verma, told CNBC that event contracts on crypto movements did well in the quarter, offering investors another way to express a view on crypto despite the slump in prices and spot trading.
- Net deposits on the platform totalled 15.7 billion dollars for the quarter, equal to a 17 percent annualised growth rate based on total platform assets at the end of the second quarter.
- Over the past year, net deposits totalled 71 billion dollars, representing 21 percent growth compared with total platform assets at the end of the third quarter of 2025.
- The company continues to diversify into options, event contracts, wealth management and subscriptions as it expands its brokerage app into a broader financial services platform.
- Shares fell about 4 percent in after-hours trading as investors digested the result.

## Competing Views

The bullish case emphasises that net deposits are still growing at a double-digit rate. Fifteen-point-seven billion dollars of new money in the quarter means client funds keep arriving, while event contracts and options have become income lines that do not depend on a single market. The decline in crypto trading was offset by a new contract product, which says users did not leave the platform but changed how they express a view, and that validates stickiness.

The bearish case notes that transaction revenue came in below expectations and net income fell year on year, while a halving of crypto revenue shows the most profitable market activity receding. The valuation narrative depends on the idea of a financial super-app, and a super-app whose revenue mix remains heavily exposed to volatile trading runs into a ceiling quickly if the crypto market stays weak for long.

A middle reading puts the weight on the flow of money rather than the income statement. Net deposits grew at seventeen percent while net income fell, which says the company is trading current profitability for durable balances. Acquisition costs are being covered out of current profit, and this is a company that treats the balance sheet as the product. The market has simply not yet paid a premium for that.

## Panoramic Analysis

This quarter offered an unusually clean controlled experiment: when the underlying asset stops working, what is left of a trading platform?

The answer arrived in two versions inside the same release. Cryptocurrency revenue halved from its prior-year level to 120 million dollars, while event contracts, which package the very same crypto price action into a different tradable form, grew more than fivefold. Volatility itself never disappeared. It moved out of spot trading and into a contract product. So the quarter does not demonstrate that the company has escaped dependence on the crypto market. It demonstrates that when spot activity contracts, the company can reprice and resell the same volatility through a different wrapper. That is resilience, and it is also a warning, because the wrapper carries regulatory uncertainty while the asset does not.

That regulatory uncertainty is not hypothetical. Event contracts sit inside a category whose jurisdictional status is currently being litigated, with the commodity futures regulator's dispute over one prediction market operator still before the courts. A revenue line that grows more than fivefold in a year while its own legal standing remains undetermined is not an annuity. Any model that extrapolates the prior quarter's growth rate will fail on this line before it fails anywhere else, and the failure will arrive as a binary rather than a gradual slowdown.

The deposits are the more durable thread. Fifteen-point-seven billion dollars of net inflows at a 17 percent annualised rate, and 71 billion over the trailing year, mean the platform is still net attracting customer capital while its trading revenue falls. Capital gathering and revenue generation have now decoupled, which is a genuinely different business model, and also the signature of a company that treats the balance sheet as the product rather than the transaction. It earns on the scale and stickiness of balances rather than on the spread of a trade.

That is where the real crack in this quarter sits. Net income fell while net deposits rose. This is a company trading current profitability against durable balances, and the two are moving in opposite directions. The market has chosen to doubt the first and is not yet paying for the second. Whether that judgement is fair depends on whether balances gathered this quarter convert into new revenue lines, or only into more deposits to sit and earn. If it is the latter, then 15.7 billion dollars buys a bigger balance sheet rather than a better business.
