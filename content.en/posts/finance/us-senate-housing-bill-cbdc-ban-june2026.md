## Core Summary

The US Senate passed a comprehensive housing supply bill with an overwhelming 85-5 vote. The legislation aims to lower housing costs and restrict Wall Street investors from purchasing single-family homes, while notably including a provision that bans the Federal Reserve or any federal agency from issuing a central bank digital currency (CBDC) or similar digital assets through the end of 2030.

## Event Details

According to The Washington Post and The Block, the Housing Supply Act received broad bipartisan support. Key provisions include:

**Housing Affordability Measures**: The bill allocates billions of dollars to increase affordable housing supply while implementing the first-ever restrictions on large institutional investors purchasing single-family homes.

**CBDC Ban Provision**: The most closely watched provision explicitly prohibits the Federal Reserve from issuing a central bank digital currency. The ban extends through the end of 2030 and covers any form of federal government digital liability instrument.

**Reactions**: The crypto industry welcomed the bill passage. Supporters argue the CBDC ban leaves room for private digital asset innovation. Critics worry that lacking federal digital currency infrastructure could put the US at a disadvantage in global digital payments competition.

## Panoramic Analysis

The bill reveals the deep connection between housing policy and digital financial regulation in the US. Housing affordability has become one of the most pressing economic issues, while the CBDC ban reflects America traditional vigilance against government power expansion.

From a macro perspective, the US choice to legislatively ban CBDCs contrasts sharply with China and the EU, which are actively developing central bank digital currencies. This regulatory competition could reshape the global digital finance landscape.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporters**: Coin Center stated the bill protects financial freedom and prevents excessive government intrusion into personal payment choices.

**Critics**: Former Federal Reserve officials warn that a blanket CBDC ban could put the US behind in international digital dollar competition.

**Middle Ground**: Some economists suggest accelerating real-time payment system upgrades alongside the CBDC ban to balance privacy protection with payment efficiency.
