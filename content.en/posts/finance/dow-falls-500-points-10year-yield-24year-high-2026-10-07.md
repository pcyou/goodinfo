**[Core Summary]**
CNBC, The Wall Street Journal and Yahoo Finance reported that US stocks sold off broadly on Tuesday, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropping more than 500 points and the US 10-year Treasury yield climbing to its highest level since 2008. The 30-year yield simultaneously hit a fresh 24-year high, pushing mortgage rates higher and amplifying market concerns over sticky inflation and fiscal deficits.

**[Event Details]**
- **Index Action**: The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed down more than 500 points, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite moving in the same direction, pulling the S&P 500 back from recent record highs.
- **Treasury Yields**: The 10-year Treasury yield touched approximately 4.94%, the highest level since 2008. The 30-year Treasury yield simultaneously hit a fresh 24-year high, weighing on the pricing of long-duration assets.
- **Mortgage Rates**: The 30-year fixed mortgage rate followed long-end yields higher, hitting its highest level in nearly three years and directly raising the monthly payment burden for American homebuyers.
- **Market Logic**: Sticky inflation, widening fiscal deficits and persistent geopolitical uncertainty continue to weigh on sentiment. The market has pushed back expectations for the Federal Reserve's rate-cut path, with capital flowing back from risk assets into long-dated Treasuries.
- **Sector Performance**: Tech stocks came under pressure against a backdrop of rising rates, with previously high-flying AI-related names seeing notable profit-taking.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**
**First**, the simultaneous rise in the 10-year and 30-year Treasury yields is the core signal of this correction. The 10-year yield breaking through 4.9% means the risk-free rate has repriced to levels last seen before the global financial crisis, while the 30-year hitting a 24-year high further compresses long-duration valuations. This reflects a dual repricing of "US fiscal sustainability" and "sticky inflation" by investors.

**Second**, rising long-end rates will transmit to the real economy through three channels: higher mortgage rates dampening housing demand, higher corporate financing costs curbing capital expenditure, and higher returns on household savings dampening consumption willingness. The 30-year mortgage rate hitting a near three-year high today shows that the real-estate chain is the first segment under pressure.

**Third**, this correction will also test the AI narrative. Supported by AI policy tailwinds and resilient corporate earnings, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq had traded at elevated levels for an extended period. Any unexpected rise on the rates end makes the tension between "high valuations" and "high financing costs" explicit. In the short term, the market will continue to oscillate around the Fed's policy path and long-end yield pricing; in the medium term, whether this correction breaks the valuation anchor of the AI narrative will be key to watching the next stage of style rotation.

**[Comparative Perspectives]**
- **CNBC live coverage**: Positioned the selloff as a "rate-driven pullback," emphasizing long-end yields at multi-year highs as the core catalyst.
- **WSJ analysis**: Argued the move reflects downward revisions to expectations for the speed of inflation easing and a reassessment of fiscal-deficit monetization risk.
- **Yahoo Finance observation**: Pointed out that the 30-year mortgage rate hitting a near three-year high is directly affecting US housing market activity and represents an early sign of transmission to the consumer side of the economy.
- **Some strategist views**: Tended to view the pullback as a "healthy correction," arguing that the fundamentals have not materially deteriorated and the long-term investment thesis is unchanged.
- **Bearish view**: Cited long-end yields breaking through key thresholds, arguing the correction could extend into a wider sector rotation.
