## Core Summary

According to multiple media reports, OpenAI investors have approached the company about a new funding round that could value the company at up to $1.5 trillion, with disclosed ranges between $1.2 trillion and $1.5 trillion. The valuation would surpass any previous OpenAI funding round and rank it among the world's most valuable private tech companies. The funding is expected to close before the IPO, meaning OpenAI is laying the groundwork for its eventual public market debut.

## Event Details

### Valuation Range Disclosed by Media

CNBC first reported that OpenAI investors have been in contact with the company about a new funding round. The New York Times subsequently indicated that OpenAI is considering a new round at a $1.5 trillion valuation, while the Financial Times reported a slightly lower figure of $1.2 trillion. Combining the disclosures, OpenAI's new round valuation is between $1.2 trillion and $1.5 trillion, with the final amount to be determined based on investor response.

### Strategic Purpose of the Funding

Sources familiar with the matter revealed that OpenAI's new round has three main purposes: first, to provide capital support for continued large-scale AI infrastructure, including data centers, chip procurement, and R&D investment; second, to pave the way for a potential IPO and ensure an optimal pre-listing valuation; third, to introduce strategic investors, including sovereign wealth funds, large tech companies, and financial institutions, to further strengthen the corporate governance structure.

### Global Valuation Ranking

At a $1.5 trillion valuation, OpenAI would become the world's most valuable private tech company, surpassing ByteDance (around $400 billion), SpaceX (around $400 billion), and Stripe (around $95 billion). OpenAI would also become one of the few private companies in the world with a valuation exceeding $1 trillion, comparable to Saudi Aramco's pre-listing valuation.

## Panoramic Analysis

**First, the 'AI funding bubble' has become a new focal point of discussion.** OpenAI's $1.5 trillion valuation is already close to the market caps of mature tech giants like Apple and Microsoft, meaning the AI sector's valuation has caught up with traditional tech leaders. This 'valuation catch-up' configuration has triggered concerns about an 'AI bubble,' with some investors arguing that AI company valuations have severely deviated from fundamentals and could see significant corrections in the future. But supporters argue that AI, as a 'general-purpose technology,' has long-term value far beyond short-term valuation volatility.

**Second, the OpenAI IPO is likely to land between 2027 and 2028.** From a strategic perspective, the real purpose of OpenAI's new round is to prepare for the eventual IPO. Given the capital-intensive nature of AI infrastructure construction and the exponential growth in AI compute demand, OpenAI needs sustained capital market support through a listing. It is widely expected in the industry that OpenAI's IPO will land between 2027 and 2028, potentially becoming one of the largest IPOs in global tech history.

**Third, sovereign wealth funds are becoming a key force in AI funding.** Sources familiar with the matter revealed that Middle Eastern sovereign wealth funds such as Abu Dhabi Investment Authority and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) may participate in OpenAI's new round. This reflects that the 'AI race' has transcended competition between tech companies and become a capital game between nations. Oil-rich Middle Eastern countries are trying to occupy strategic initiative in the AI era through capital deployment.

**Fourth, AI corporate governance structures are undergoing profound changes.** OpenAI was originally founded as a non-profit organization and transformed into a 'capped-profit company' in 2019. With the expansion of funding scale and the approach of an IPO, OpenAI is undergoing a transition from 'mission-driven non-profit' to 'profit-mission driven.' This transition has raised concerns among some researchers and former employees that OpenAI may gradually deviate from its original intention of 'safely developing AI.'

## Multiple Perspectives

**OpenAI's Position**: OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar has repeatedly emphasized that the company is currently well-capitalized and that the new funding is 'preparing for future growth,' not 'addressing short-term capital pressure.' She stressed that OpenAI's 'capped-profit' structure will continue at the time of IPO, ensuring a balance between the company's mission and commercial interests.

**Investor Position**: Investors involved in this round's negotiation generally hold a 'highly optimistic' view of OpenAI, believing that AI as a 'general-purpose technology' has long-term value far beyond short-term valuation. But some institutional investors have expressed 'caution' about the rapid valuation rise, fearing that if the IPO underperforms, it will trigger a valuation correction across the entire AI sector.

**Microsoft's Position**: As OpenAI's largest strategic investor and cloud service provider, Microsoft has a 'welcome' attitude toward the new funding. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella has previously stated that Microsoft is willing to continue supporting OpenAI's expansion under the premise of 'protecting its own interests.'

**Competitor Position**: Competitors such as Anthropic, xAI, and Google DeepMind have valuations far below OpenAI, but are growing rapidly. Anthropic's previous funding round valued it at approximately $183 billion, while xAI is at approximately $200 billion. This 'tiered competition' configuration could trigger a new round of AI funding competition.

**Regulatory Position**: The US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), among others, are 'paying close attention' to large-scale AI funding. Once OpenAI initiates its IPO, regulators will conduct strict reviews of its information disclosure, valuation reasonableness, and related-party transactions.
