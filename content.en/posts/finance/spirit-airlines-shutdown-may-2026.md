Spirit Airlines, one of America's largest ultra-low-cost carriers, officially ceased operations on May 1, 2026, in what has become the largest airline bankruptcy event in recent US aviation history.

## The Final Hours

According to CNBC, Spirit's final flight took off amid emotional farewells from staff. "Godspeed my friend" — the parting words from employees — captured the end of more than two decades of operations for the airline.

Spirit was known for its ultra-low base fares and à la carte pricing model, where passengers paid separately for virtually everything beyond a seat. The airline was devastated by the pandemic and, despite multiple attempts to save itself through mergers and restructuring, ultimately could not reverse its decline.

## Impact on Passengers

CNN has provided detailed guidance for affected passengers:

- **Ticket holders**: Passengers with existing Spirit Airlines tickets need to contact their original booking channel for refunds or rebooking. Those who purchased tickets with credit cards may be eligible for credit card refund protection.
- **Alternative arrangements**: Affected passengers will need to rebook on other airlines, but several carriers have announced special discounts for Spirit's displaced travelers.
- **Compensation**: With Spirit no longer operating, traditional flight delay or cancellation compensation may not be available.

## Industry Context

Spirit's collapse is the latest chapter in the ongoing turbulence of the US aviation industry's post-pandemic recovery. While overall air travel demand has returned to pre-pandemic levels, the ultra-low-cost carrier business model faces multiple headwinds:

1. **Rising fuel costs**: Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East have driven sustained volatility in international oil prices, severely impacting carriers that depend on low-cost operations.
2. **Labor cost increases**: Pilot and maintenance staff shortages across the aviation industry have pushed wages higher.
3. **Intensified competition**: Major airlines have introduced basic economy products, eating into the ultra-low-cost carriers' market space.

## Industry Impact

Spirit's exit means further consolidation in the US domestic aviation market. The airline held approximately 5% of the US domestic flight market, and its departure may lead to higher fares on certain routes, particularly in secondary cities where Spirit had been most competitive.

Analysts note that this event has also sparked broader discussions about aviation industry antitrust regulation and market competition.
