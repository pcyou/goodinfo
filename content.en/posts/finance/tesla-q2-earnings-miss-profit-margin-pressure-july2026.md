## Core Summary

Tesla reported Q2 2026 earnings on July 22, with adjusted EPS of $0.52, below the $0.60 consensus. Revenue reached $25.5 billion, up 8% year-over-year, but free cash flow turned negative at -$1.2 billion. Shares fell approximately 4% in after-hours trading.

## Event Details

### Auto Margins Under Pressure

Tesla's automotive gross margin fell to 16.5% in Q2, down 2.3 percentage points year-over-year, driven by global price cuts and intense competition in China. Deliveries reached 466,000 vehicles, up 6% year-over-year, but per-vehicle profitability continues to narrow.

### Free Cash Flow Turns Negative

More concerning for investors, free cash flow swung to negative $1.2 billion from positive $1.5 billion a year earlier. The company attributed the shift to continued investment in Texas and Berlin Gigafactories, plus increased AI project spending.

### Energy Business Shines

The energy division delivered $3.2 billion in revenue, up 45% year-over-year, with energy storage deployments reaching 6.5 GWh, a 78% increase.

## Analysis

Tesla's earnings illustrate the strategic transition challenge facing the EV industry as it moves from hyper-growth to mature competition. Musk's pivot toward AI and robotics represents a fundamental redefinition of Tesla's identity from automaker to AI and energy technology company.

## Perspectives

**Bull case**: ARK Invest CEO Cathie Wood maintains her $3,000 price target, arguing the AI transition is correct and Robotaxi will open a new growth curve.

**Cautious case**: Morgan Stanley analysts note declining auto margins with AI revenue not yet materializing, suggesting near-term stock pressure.

**Bear case**: Some hedge fund managers worry Tesla is losing its EV leadership while facing high uncertainty in AI returns.
