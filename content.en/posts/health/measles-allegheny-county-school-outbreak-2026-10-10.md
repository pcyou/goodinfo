**[Key Points]**

Pennsylvania's Allegheny County health authorities have confirmed a measles case at a local school and have launched contact tracing along with a vaccination catch-up effort. Officials warn that the US measles outbreak is accelerating this year, with multiple states reporting new cases, and they flag declining vaccination coverage as the main driver.

**[Details]**

- **School detection**: A measles case was confirmed at the school this week; the school has notified parents and activated its response plan.
- **Agency response**: The county health department is conducting contact tracing, organising catch-up MMR vaccinations and coordinating with surrounding medical resources.
- **Outbreak context**: Multiple US states have reported new measles cases this year; officials link the spread to regional declines in school-age vaccination coverage.
- **Federal guidance**: The CDC has issued updated guidance urging unvaccinated individuals to obtain MMR catch-up vaccinations promptly.
- **Public attention**: The incident has reopened debate in the US over school-level outbreak policy and the efficiency of public-health communication.
