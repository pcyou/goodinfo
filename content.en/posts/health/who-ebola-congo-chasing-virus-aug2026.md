## Core Summary

The World Health Organization (WHO) has issued an urgent warning that in the Democratic Republic of Congo's Ebola outbreak, health workers are "chasing" the virus's spread, but the epidemic is expanding faster than containment responses. Additionally, WHO confirmed that this Ebola outbreak actually began months before it was officially declared.

## Event Details

According to BBC, WHO officials publicly stated that current Ebola infection rates remain high, indicating authorities face enormous challenges in controlling the epidemic. Contact tracing, isolating confirmed patients, and safe burials—these core containment measures—are constrained by insufficient resources and security conditions.

ABC News reports that WHO confirmed the Ebola outbreak in Congo has been spreading longer than officially announced. This means the virus has been circulating undetected in communities for months, increasing containment difficulty.

Eastern Congo has long experienced armed conflict, and health workers face security risks when carrying out missions, with some areas completely inaccessible.

## Panoramic Perspective

Since Ebola virus was first discovered in 1976, it has broken out multiple times in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The severity of this outbreak lies in the superposition of multiple factors: first, security conditions in conflict zones limit health workers' freedom of movement; second, local communities have insufficient trust in health interventions, with some residents refusing to cooperate with containment measures; third, the virus's hidden transmission chains make early detection difficult.

From a global public health perspective, Ebola's fatality rate ranges from 50% to 90%. Although transmission routes are relatively limited (primarily through bodily fluid contact), control is extremely difficult in areas with weak health infrastructure. The international community needs to increase aid to Congo, including providing vaccines, protective equipment, and human resource support.

Alarmingly, climate change and expanding human activity ranges may expose previously localized infectious diseases to larger-scale transmission risks. The global health security system needs to establish more sensitive early warning mechanisms to address future infectious disease threats.

## Multiple Perspectives

**World Health Organization**: Admits containment speed is lagging behind virus spread, calling for increased international aid.

**Congo Health Authorities**: Face dual challenges of security and health, with armed conflict seriously hindering epidemic prevention work.

**International Public Health Experts**: Recommend adopting rapid vaccination strategies with new vaccines and strengthening community engagement to improve containment cooperation.
