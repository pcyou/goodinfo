## Core Summary

US Secretary of Health and Human Services Robert F. Kennedy Jr. is challenging a decades-old regulatory loophole that allows certain chemicals to legally exist in the American food supply. This move signals a potential major shift in US food safety policy.

## Event Details

According to The Washington Post, Kennedy is targeting the FDA's "Generally Recognized As Safe" (GRAS) system. This system allows food manufacturers to self-determine certain additives as safe substances without formal FDA approval.

Critics point out that this loophole has resulted in thousands of chemical substances being used in food processing without adequate safety assessments. Kennedy has stated he will push for legislative reform requiring all food additives to undergo independent third-party scientific evaluation with complete safety data made public.

The American food industry has reacted strongly. Industry associations warn that excessive regulation could lead to rising food costs and stifled innovation. However, consumer rights organizations express support, arguing that the public has the right to know what chemicals are in their food.

## Panoramic Perspective

Kennedy's move reflects his long-standing critical stance toward the food industry. As a vaccine skeptic and organic food advocate, his appointment itself sparked widespread controversy in the scientific community. However, the issue of food safety regulatory gaps does exist and transcends partisan divides.

From a global perspective, the European Union adopts a stricter "precautionary principle" in food additive regulation, requiring all additives to receive prior approval from the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA). In comparison, America's GRAS system does appear lenient.

If successfully implemented, this reform will have far-reaching impacts on the American food industry. On one hand, stricter regulation may raise production costs, leading to food price increases; on the other hand, it could also spawn new markets for testing, certification, and alternative additives. For consumers, improved transparency helps make more informed dietary choices.

Notably, this issue carries political sensitivity in an election year. The food industry is an important source of political donations, while consumer protection is an effective topic for winning public support. Whether Kennedy can withstand pressure and push through substantive reform remains to be seen.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Consumer Rights Organizations**: Support reform, emphasizing that public health should take priority over corporate profits, demanding fully transparent ingredient disclosure.
