## Core Summary

Uganda has successfully discharged its last Ebola patient, a "moment of joy" according to WHO that marks effective control of the outbreak in the country. However, this good news contrasts sharply with the dire situation in neighboring DR Congo, where Ebola cases have exceeded 2,000 with over 754 deaths. WHO Director-General warns the virus is spreading faster than it can be tracked.

## Event Details

According to the BBC, Uganda has successfully discharged its last Ebola patient, a achievement WHO has called a "moment of joy." Uganda's epidemic prevention work has yielded significant results through strict contact tracing, rapid isolation, and community engagement, successfully containing the virus transmission chain.

However, in stark contrast to Uganda's success, the Ebola outbreak in eastern DR Congo is spreading out of control. According to AP News, Congo's Ebola cases have surpassed 2,000, with 754 deaths. More concerning, additional healthcare workers have begun striking, further weakening epidemic prevention capabilities.

WHO Director-General Tedros warned that Ebola virus transmission in eastern Congo has outpaced tracking capabilities, with the outbreak "spreading faster than we can contain." The Economist magazine bluntly stated "the Ebola epidemic is getting out of control."

## Panoramic Analysis

The stark contrast between Uganda and Congo reveals critical factors in public health governance. Uganda's success was not accidental but built on strong government execution, adequate international assistance, and effective community mobilization. In contrast, eastern Congo faces multiple challenges including prolonged armed conflict, weak medical infrastructure, and public distrust of vaccines.

From a global public health security perspective, Congo's uncontrolled outbreak poses a serious threat. Ebola virus is highly contagious, and if not effectively controlled, could spread to neighboring countries and even broader regions. International assistance needs to be significantly strengthened, particularly in healthcare worker training, protective equipment supply, and vaccine development.

This event also highlights the inequity in global health governance systems. Developed countries have abundant resources and technology for infectious disease response, while developing countries often face funding shortages and infrastructure deficits. Establishing a more equitable and effective global health emergency response mechanism is key to preventing future pandemics.

## Perspectives

**WHO** congratulated Uganda on its achievement but expressed serious concern about the Congo outbreak. Tedros called on the international community to increase assistance to Congo to prevent further deterioration.

**Public health experts** note Uganda's experience is worth emulating. Rapid response, transparent communication, and community engagement are the three pillars of Ebola control. However, Congo's complex security environment makes these measures difficult to implement effectively.

**Humanitarian organizations** worry Congo's outbreak could trigger a larger humanitarian crisis. Healthcare worker strikes mean more patients cannot receive timely treatment, further increasing virus transmission risk.
