## Core Summary

According to The New York Times, a new study has found that over 1,700 HIV treatment facilities worldwide have been forced to close after the US government significantly cut foreign medical aid funding. Researchers warn this trend is having "severe and devastating" impacts on global HIV prevention efforts, with millions of patients' lives at risk.

## Event Details

### Research Data

The study, conducted by an international team of public health experts, covered multiple key prevention regions across Africa, Asia, and Latin America. Data shows that since the US aid cuts, treatment centers dependent on American funding have ceased operations one after another, leaving many patients unable to access necessary antiretroviral treatment.

### Regional Impact

Sub-Saharan Africa has been most severely affected, as the region is home to approximately two-thirds of all people living with HIV globally. Multiple countries have reported significant increases in treatment interruption rates, with some areas seeing virus testing capacity decline by more than 50 percent.

### Expert Warnings

The World Health Organization and former officials from the US President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief have issued urgent warnings, stating that treatment interruptions could lead to a significant increase in AIDS-related deaths and increased virus transmission risk. They emphasize that interrupting antiretroviral treatment can also lead to the emergence of drug-resistant virus strains.

## Panoramic Perspective

This event highlights the fragility of the global health aid system. The US has long been the largest single donor for global HIV prevention, and its policy changes have profound impacts on the global public health landscape. Aid cuts not only affect direct treatment services but also undermine the international public health infrastructure and cooperative networks built over many years.

From an economic perspective, the interruption of HIV prevention and treatment will bring higher long-term costs. Untreated HIV patients experience reduced work capacity and increased family burden, negatively impacting economic development in developing countries. Research shows that every dollar invested in HIV prevention generates approximately four dollars in economic returns.

At the international relations level, this policy change is affecting US diplomatic relations with developing countries. Multiple African national leaders have publicly expressed dissatisfaction, arguing this violates America's commitment to global health security. Some countries are beginning to seek new cooperative relationships with other donors.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US Government Position**: The White House stated that aid policy adjustments were made to improve efficiency, emphasizing focusing resources on more sustainable projects. Officials stated other countries should bear more responsibility.

**Public Health Community Response**: Global health experts have strongly criticized the policy, saying it will lead to an increase in preventable deaths. Doctors Without Borders has called for urgently filling the funding gap.

**Affected Countries' Stance**: Health ministers from multiple African countries issued a joint statement urging the US to reconsider the aid cut decision. They warned this would undermine decades of prevention achievements.

**Congressional Response**: Some bipartisan members of Congress have expressed concern about the aid cuts, calling for a reassessment of policy impacts. However, there is currently no clear legislative action on Capitol Hill to restore funding.

**Patient Group Voices**: HIV patient rights organizations report that treatment interruptions are creating a humanitarian crisis. Many patients have been forced to interrupt treatment regimens they have maintained for years.
