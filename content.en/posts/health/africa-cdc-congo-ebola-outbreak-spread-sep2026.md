## Core Summary

The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention has issued an urgent warning that the ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has significantly exceeded the original epicentre and is spreading at an accelerated pace in several neighbouring areas. The agency, in coordination with the World Health Organization, is calling on the international community to urgently provide additional funding, personnel and medical supplies to contain the risk of the outbreak evolving into a cross-border public health crisis. The warning suggests that the DRC may be facing its most severe Ebola outbreak in recent years.

## Event Details

**Africa CDC's Urgent Warning**: In its latest outbreak notification, the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention clearly stated that the Ebola virus strain currently circulating in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo has broken through the boundaries of the original epicentre, and new confirmed and suspected cases have emerged in multiple adjacent health zones. Citing multiple epidemiological indicators including the geographic distribution of cases, transmission chains and characteristics of community transmission, the notification emphasised that the speed of the outbreak's spread has outpaced the rhythm that the existing response resources can handle, and that coordinated regional response is urgently needed.

**Key Epidemiological Indicators of the Current Outbreak**: According to information disclosed by multiple international public health agencies, the current outbreak first emerged in a community in eastern DRC, and due to differences between the virus strain and previous outbreaks as well as frequent population movement in the affected areas and limited medical capacity, the outbreak faced a high risk of community transmission from the very beginning. As the outbreak expands outward, the distribution of new cases shows a multi-point scattered pattern, posing significant challenges to epidemiological investigation and contact tracing.

**Multiple Challenges Facing Outbreak Response**: The World Health Organization and its partners clearly pointed out in their latest notifications that the response to the current outbreak faces multiple challenges. First, frontline healthcare workers in the affected areas have long been overloaded, and in some regions morale is low due to unpaid salaries and insufficient personal protective equipment; second, long-standing security conflicts in the affected areas make some villages difficult to access, affecting the progress of vaccination and case isolation work; and third, global public health funding being dispersed across multiple crises has led to insufficient dedicated support for the DRC outbreak compared to expectations.

**Response Actions by Professional Forces**: Africa CDC has activated regional emergency coordination mechanisms, dispatching epidemiologists and field coordinators to the DRC, and coordinating with the WHO, UNICEF, Médecins Sans Frontières and other agencies on the deployment of vaccine stockpiles, mobile laboratories and community mobilisation teams. At the same time, relevant agencies are working with the DRC Ministry of Health to jointly advance a ring vaccination strategy, that is, emergency Ebola vaccination of close contacts around known cases to interrupt the transmission chain.

**Funding Shortfall Issues Highlighted Once Again**: Multiple international agencies involved in the response have warned that one of the key bottlenecks in the response to the current outbreak is the lack of dedicated funding. The WHO has pointed out on multiple occasions recently that global public health emergency funding has long been in short supply, and that this year multiple epidemic hotspots are simultaneously consuming limited resources, leading to relatively limited attention and support received by the DRC outbreak. Africa CDC has also called on member states and partners to increase flexible and rapidly disbursable financial support.

**Potential Risk of Cross-Border Spread**: The Democratic Republic of Congo borders multiple African countries, and cross-border population movements are frequent. Epidemiological experts warn that if the outbreak cannot be effectively contained at the original epicentre, the possibility of cross-border spread cases in the coming weeks will continue to rise. Once such a scenario emerges, it will pose a severe test to the public health systems of the entire Central African region, and may once again trigger discussions on a global public health emergency.

## Panoramic Perspective

The repeated emergence of Ebola outbreaks in the Democratic Republic of Congo reveals the long-standing structural shortcomings of global public health governance. On the one hand, since the Ebola virus was first discovered in 1976, it has experienced dozens of outbreaks in Central Africa, each of which relies on highly specialised field response capabilities; on the other hand, because most of the affected areas are located in regions with weak infrastructure, scarce medical resources and complex security situations, international aid often struggles to form effective intervention within the golden window period. The fact that the current outbreak is spreading faster than expected is precisely the result of these shortcomings being amplified in practice.

From a broader perspective, the current outbreak also poses a challenge to the global public health funding allocation mechanism. When multiple epidemic hotspots simultaneously consume limited international emergency resources, how the decision-making mechanism prioritises among different crises, how to ensure that funds can be rapidly disbursed to the front line, and how to balance short-term emergency response with long-term capacity building will all directly affect the final trajectory of the outbreak. It can be expected that the experiences and lessons from the response to this outbreak will be repeatedly cited in subsequent policy discussions at Africa CDC, the WHO and among multiple donors, and may drive further adjustments to the global public health emergency architecture.
