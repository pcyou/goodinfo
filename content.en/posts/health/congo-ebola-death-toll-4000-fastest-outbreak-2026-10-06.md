**[Core Summary]**
Congo's current Ebola outbreak has now killed more than 4,000 people, making it the fastest-spreading Ebola outbreak ever recorded. France has announced it will scale up medical assistance to the region, and the WHO is warning that cross-border transmission risks continue to rise, urging the international community to step up its response.

**[Event Details]**
- **Scale of Outbreak**: Since the outbreak began in August of last year, the DRC has reported more than 12,000 confirmed and suspected Ebola cases, with deaths surpassing 4,000 and a case fatality rate of roughly 35%.
- **Geographic Spread**: The epicenter lies in North Kivu and Ituri provinces, bordering Uganda, Rwanda, and South Sudan; in the past week at least three suspected cross-border cases have been reported in Uganda.
- **International Response**: France's Health Minister announced an additional €15 million in aid to the WHO to deploy mobile laboratories and vaccine cold chains in the outbreak zone; the UK and Germany have pledged vaccine stockpiles.
- **Strain on Local Health System**: Hospitals in the epicenter are overwhelmed, six health workers have died, and ongoing armed conflict has repeatedly interrupted vaccination campaigns.

**[Panoramic Perspective]**
**First**, the current outbreak has eclipsed the 2014-2016 West African outbreak in spread, becoming one of the worst Ebola episodes ever recorded. The core reason is geography: the epicenter lies in an active conflict zone, making it difficult to deploy vaccines and medical teams consistently, while local vaccine hesitancy has repeatedly caused the critical window for breaking transmission chains to be missed.

**Second**, the risk of cross-border spread is rising. North Kivu shares direct borders with Uganda and Rwanda, where thousands of traders and refugees cross each week. If the outbreak breaches border controls, it will place an enormous strain on health systems across East Africa. The WHO has raised the regional risk level to "very high."

**Third**, France's decision to expand aid reflects how "African health security" is climbing the European foreign-policy agenda. Repeated outbreaks of Ebola, mpox, and other viruses have made Europe realize that infectious-disease control cannot wait for outbreaks to reach its own shores — intervention at the source is far cheaper than dealing with the consequences later.

**Multiple Perspectives**
- **Senior DRC Health Official**: Blamed part of the spread on "ongoing destruction of medical corridors by anti-government armed groups" and called on UN peacekeeping forces to help protect health workers.
- **WHO Director-General**: Told reporters in Geneva the outbreak has "not yet peaked" and urged the international community to immediately honor pledges of funds and vaccines.
- **French Health Ministry**: Announced the new aid package, stressing that "health security begins beyond Europe's borders" and committing to sustained investment in African disease control.
- **Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF)**: Criticized vaccine inequity, noting that the DRC outbreak zone has received only 35% of globally available doses.
- **U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**: Listed eastern Congo as a "Level 3" travel advisory, recommending that non-essential travelers postpone trips.
