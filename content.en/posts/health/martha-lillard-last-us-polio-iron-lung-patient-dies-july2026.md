**[Core Summary]** Martha Lillard, the last polio patient in the United States to rely on an iron lung for survival, passed away recently in Oklahoma at the age of 78. Her life spanned the era from the devastating polio epidemics to the near-total eradication of the disease through vaccination. Her death marks the end of an era in medical history.

**[Event Details]**

According to the Associated Press, Martha Lillard died peacefully at a medical facility in Oklahoma. She contracted polio as a child, which left her fully paralyzed, and she had depended on an iron lung to breathe since her youth. The iron lung is a negative-pressure ventilator that mechanically assists breathing, and it was one of the most critical life-saving devices during the mid-20th century polio epidemics.

Lillard was the last person in the United States still using an iron lung, and her story had drawn national attention. Although modern medicine has developed more advanced respiratory support equipment, she chose to continue using the iron lung, considering it the most familiar and comfortable way for her to live. Her resilience and optimism inspired countless people and brought greater awareness to the profound impact polio had on her generation.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**

Polio was once one of the most feared diseases of the 20th century. Before the development of vaccines, hundreds of thousands of people worldwide were infected each year, leading to paralysis or death. In 1955, Dr. Jonas Salk's inactivated polio vaccine (IPV) was introduced, followed by Albert Sabin's oral polio vaccine (OPV) in the 1960s. Widespread vaccination campaigns led to a dramatic decline in polio cases globally.

By 2026, polio has been eradicated in most countries, with wild virus transmission remaining only in a few areas such as Afghanistan and Pakistan. The Global Polio Eradication Initiative (GPEI), launched in 1988, has reduced global cases by over 99.9%. Martha Lillard's life was a living testament to this journey from epidemic to victory.

**[Multiple Perspectives]**

Public health experts view Lillard's passing as "the closing of an era," while emphasizing that her story underscores the importance of vaccination. The polio survivor community expressed deep condolences, calling her "a symbol of resilience and hope." Medical ethicists have also noted that Lillard's choice to continue using the iron lung sparked discussions about the balance between patient autonomy and medical technological progress. Her family expressed hope that her story would encourage more people to appreciate the achievements of modern medicine and support global vaccination efforts.
