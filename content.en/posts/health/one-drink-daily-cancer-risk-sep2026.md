## Core Summary

According to CNN, a large-scale study by the American Cancer Society (ACS) has found that consuming even just one alcoholic drink per day may significantly increase the risk of developing and dying from multiple types of cancer. The research covers liver cancer, breast cancer, esophageal cancer, colorectal cancer, and other types, with impacts extending far beyond previous understanding.

## Event Details

Published in CA: A Cancer Journal for Clinicians, the study analyzed long-term health data from hundreds of thousands of participants. Researchers found that even light drinkers (no more than one standard drink per day) had a significantly higher cancer mortality risk than non-drinkers.

The data shows that alcohol-related cancer burden extends well beyond the liver. Breast cancer, esophageal cancer, colorectal cancer, oral cancer, and laryngeal cancer are all significantly associated with alcohol consumption. Notably, the study found no "safe drinking level" — risk increases with consumption, but even the lowest level carries a measurable risk increment.

The ACS chief scientist emphasized that the study's importance lies in quantifying the risk of low-level drinking. Many public health guidelines previously defined "moderate drinking" as no more than one to two drinks per day, but the latest evidence suggests cancer risk is already significantly elevated within this range.

The World Health Organization has classified alcohol as a Group 1 carcinogen, alongside tobacco and asbestos. However, alcohol consumption is deeply rooted in global culture, and many countries' public health guidelines have not fully reflected the latest scientific evidence.

## Panoramic Perspective

This study has far-reaching implications for global public health policy. Governments may need to revisit alcohol consumption guidelines. Countries like Canada have already updated their guidelines to state clearly that "no amount of alcohol is safe," consistent with the latest findings.

From a socioeconomic perspective, the alcohol industry faces new challenges. The global alcoholic beverage market exceeds $1.5 trillion. If consumers reduce drinking due to health concerns, it will significantly impact the supply chain. Alcohol advertising and marketing may face stricter regulatory restrictions.

For China, where alcohol culture plays an important role in social interactions, this study suggests that public health authorities need to strengthen education about alcohol risks, particularly promoting healthy drinking concepts in business entertainment and social settings.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Research Team**: The lead authors emphasized that the goal is not to frighten the public but to provide accurate scientific information to help people make informed choices. They recommend that non-drinkers need not start, and those who drink should reduce intake.

**Industry Response**: International alcohol industry associations respect scientific research but note that moderate drinking has shown potential cardiovascular benefits in some studies. They emphasize responsible drinking rather than blanket abstinence recommendations.

**Public Health Experts**: Most experts support stricter alcohol control, noting that alcohol causes approximately 3 million deaths globally each year. Increased taxation, advertising restrictions, and raising minimum drinking ages are effective interventions.
