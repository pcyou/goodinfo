## [Brief] Measles Outbreak in US Amish Community Forces Some Residents to Rethink Vaccination

The New York Times reports that a measles outbreak in the US Amish community has shown signs of rapid spread through under-vaccinated populations. Some residents are reconsidering vaccination under the pressure of the spreading disease and are seeking ways to receive vaccine information that respect their faith and community norms. The report noted that the trend reflects the potential impact of outbreak pressure on the vaccine views of certain groups.

Public health officials cited in the report noted that measles is one of the most contagious viruses known, and unvaccinated individuals face a very high risk of infection once exposed. Officials warned that low vaccination rates may exacerbate the spread of the outbreak, and that severe complications can occur in children, pregnant women and people with weaker immune systems.

The report also noted that public health authorities are working with community leaders to deliver vaccine information through home visits, community clinics, religious venues and other channels. Multiple medical and public health experts stressed that vaccine trust building must be grounded in respect for community faith and culture, must provide accurate medical information in a transparent and accessible way, and must avoid stigmatizing any group.
