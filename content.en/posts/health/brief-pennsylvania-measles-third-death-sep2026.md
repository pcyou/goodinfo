## Summary

The Washington Post reported on September 13 that a Pennsylvania woman has died of measles-related complications, with the local coroner officially confirming the cause of death. The case is the third death reported in the state's current measles outbreak, drawing further attention from US public health authorities to vaccination coverage, grassroots disease control capacity, and the responsiveness of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention system. NBC News and The Guardian have followed up with parallel reports, noting that the CDC is facing growing professional scrutiny over the pace of its response amid concurrent outbreaks in multiple states.

## Details

According to The Washington Post, the woman who died was from Pennsylvania. The local coroner has completed the cause-of-death determination and notified state health authorities and the family. Local public health officials say an epidemiological investigation has been launched to comprehensively review the patient's recent exposure history, vaccination history, and clinical care records. Pennsylvania's current measles outbreak has continued to spread since early summer, with multiple community transmission cases and severe hospitalizations reported. The Guardian notes that state public health authorities have strengthened coordination with federal disease control agencies following multiple infant deaths and have called on children and high-risk groups who have not yet been vaccinated to get their shots as soon as possible.

NBC News focuses on the growing questioning from public health experts and frontline clinicians about the pace of the CDC's response amid concurrent outbreaks in multiple states. Multiple experts said in interviews that the CDC's recent response speed on data release, testing guidance, and vaccine supply coordination has slowed, which may be related to uncertainties brought about by the agency's internal budget and personnel adjustments. The topic has become one of the focal points in US public health policy discussions.
