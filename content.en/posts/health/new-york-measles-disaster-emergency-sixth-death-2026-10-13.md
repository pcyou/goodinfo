**[Core Summary]**

New York State officially declared a 'disaster' emergency on October 13 due to the continuing spread of a measles outbreak that has now caused a sixth death. State health officials warned that persistently low vaccination coverage in some communities — below the herd immunity threshold — is the main driver of the outbreak, and the state government will launch emergency measures including mandatory vaccination and community screening.

**[Event Details]**

- **Outbreak Scale**: Since the start of the outbreak, New York State has reported more than 480 confirmed measles cases, concentrated in several northern and western counties with predominantly religious communities.
- **Death Toll**: The most recent death was an unvaccinated child, bringing the total death toll in this outbreak to 6, drawing intense attention to childhood vaccination rates.
- **Mandatory Measures**: The state government announced it will activate a temporary mandatory vaccination order requiring all unvaccinated residents in outbreak-concentrated areas to complete vaccination within 30 days, or face legal consequences including restrictions on access to public places.
- **Resource Commitment**: New York State will allocate approximately $200 million to expand vaccine procurement, community screening, epidemiological investigation, and public outreach, and will establish a 24-hour hotline to provide vaccination consultation for parents.

**[Panorama]**

The escalation of the New York measles outbreak to a 'disaster' emergency reflects the public health risks posed by the structural decline in vaccination coverage in parts of the United States. Over the past decade, influenced by religious beliefs, individual liberty notions, and misconceptions about vaccine side effects, MMR vaccination rates in some communities have fallen below 85%, well below the 95% herd immunity threshold. This decline has not only allowed measles, a 'basically controlled' infectious disease, to make a comeback, but also planted the seeds for potential outbreaks of other vaccine-preventable diseases such as pertussis and diphtheria. From a public health policy perspective, this incident may prompt multiple US states to reconsider non-medical exemption clauses and expand the scope of mandatory vaccination. However, mandatory vaccination orders also face legal challenges and community resistance at the implementation level, which may trigger a new round of policy debate on the balance between individual liberty and public health.

**[Multiple Perspectives]**

- **State Health Department**: Emphasizes that vaccination is the most effective way to protect children and communities, urging parents to arrange vaccinations for their children as soon as possible.
- **Public Health Experts**: Note that the outbreak is the inevitable result of continued declines in vaccination rates and warn that more outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases may occur in the future.
- **Religious and Civil Liberties Groups**: Express reservations about mandatory vaccination orders, arguing that education and voluntary vaccination should be prioritized to raise coverage.
