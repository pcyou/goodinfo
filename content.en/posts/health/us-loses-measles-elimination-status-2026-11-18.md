The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has confirmed that the United States has officially lost its measles elimination status, maintained for 26 years, due to record-high case numbers. This public health setback has raised expert concerns about declining vaccination rates and the impact of anti-vaccine movements.

According to latest data, the US has reported over 500 measles cases this year, the highest level since 1991. Previously, the United States had been recognized by the World Health Organization as a measles-eliminated country since 2000, meaning local transmission chains had been broken.

The CDC Director pointed out that declining vaccination rates are the primary cause. In recent years, influenced by misinformation, some parents have refused to vaccinate their children against measles, creating gaps in herd immunity barriers. Particularly in certain communities, vaccination rates have fallen below the 95% threshold needed to maintain herd immunity.

This status loss means the US must reimplement stricter entry quarantine measures and strengthen domestic epidemic monitoring. Health authorities urge the public to complete vaccinations promptly, especially residents planning international travel.

**Panoramic Perspective**

The US losing its measles elimination status is a warning-sign public health event. As one of the countries with the richest medical resources globally, America's regression in basic infectious disease control reflects deeper social issues.

From a public health perspective, this incident highlights the severity of vaccine hesitancy. In the social media era, misinformation spreads far faster than scientific knowledge dissemination. Anti-vaccine movements exploit parental concerns about children's health, spreading unverified risk warnings that lead some populations to abandon effective protective measures.

From a policy analysis standpoint, America's federal system creates variations in vaccination requirements across states. Some states allow exemptions based on personal or religious beliefs, leaving institutional loopholes for disease transmission. In contrast, many European countries and Australia adopt stricter mandatory vaccination policies, maintaining higher herd immunity levels.

For China, this incident reminds us of the importance of continuously consolidating immunization program achievements. Although China has achieved significant measles control results, increased population mobility and frequent international exchanges bring importation risks. Strengthening border quarantine, improving vaccination coverage in remote areas, and combating false health information are all tasks requiring sustained investment.

**Multiple Perspectives**

Medical experts express serious concern. An American Academy of Pediatrics spokesperson stated that measles is a highly contagious disease, and unvaccinated children face serious health risks including pneumonia, encephalitis, and even death. Restoring elimination status requires coordinated nationwide vaccination promotion efforts.

Public health policy researchers point out that relying solely on voluntary vaccination is insufficient to address current challenges. They suggest learning from Australia's approach, linking vaccination to welfare distribution, or imposing additional taxes on unvaccinated families to increase vaccination rates.

On the other hand, individual freedom advocates oppose mandatory measures. They believe parents have the right to decide their children's medical choices, and the government should not over-intervene. This view is common in conservative communities and is an important reason for regional differences in vaccination rates.

International health organizations call for enhanced cooperation among countries to share best practices. The World Health Organization stated that global measles cases have been rising in recent years, requiring concerted international efforts to reverse this trend.
