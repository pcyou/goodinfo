## Core Summary

US-Iran nuclear talks have paused to attend the funeral of Iranian Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. President Trump claims progress has been made, with mediators reporting that both sides have narrowed their positions. Khamenei's body will lie in state at Tehran's Grand Mosalla on Friday ahead of days-long funeral events.

## Event Details

According to CBS News, US-Iran nuclear talks paused after "progress" was made to attend the funeral of Iran's Supreme Leader. President Trump and mediators both claimed positive outcomes from the negotiations, though specific details were not disclosed.

Iranian state media confirmed that Khamenei's body will lie in state at Tehran's Grand Mosalla on Friday. The funeral for the Supreme Leader, who was assassinated in February, will last several days, during which Iran enters a national mourning period.

During the pause, both delegations have left the negotiation site. Mediators indicated that while positions have narrowed on some issues, significant disagreements remain on core questions such as the future of the nuclear program and sanctions relief timelines.

## Panoramic Perspective

The interruption of US-Iran nuclear talks reflects the complexity of Middle Eastern geopolitics. On one hand, the Trump administration seeks to resolve the Iran nuclear issue through diplomacy, avoiding military conflict; on the other hand, Iran's domestic political landscape is in a sensitive transition period following the assassination of its Supreme Leader, with the new leadership needing to balance between hardliners and pragmatists.

In the long term, even if a deal is eventually reached, its implementation will face multiple challenges. Technical supervision of Iran's nuclear program, phased arrangements for sanctions relief, and behavioral constraints on regional allies (such as Hezbollah in Lebanon and the Houthis in Yemen) all require effective verification and enforcement mechanisms.

Additionally, the Middle Eastern context of these talks cannot be ignored. Israel's concerns about Iran's nuclear facilities, geopolitical considerations from Saudi Arabia and other Gulf states, and the roles of Russia and China will all influence the final agreement's achievement and implementation.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US Position**: The Trump administration emphasized progress in talks, suggesting Iran may make concessions on nuclear program limitations. White House spokespeople stated the US maintains a "zero nuclear weapons" goal but is willing to achieve it through phased agreements.

**Iran Position**: The Iranian delegation insisted on comprehensive sanctions relief and refused to include ballistic missile programs in negotiations. Iranian state media emphasized that any agreement must respect Iran's "sovereign rights."

**Mediator Assessment**: Mediators from Oman and Qatar indicated progress on technical issues but noted more political compromise is needed. They called on both sides to exercise restraint and avoid letting national sentiment during the funeral period affect negotiations.

**International Observation**: The UN Secretary-General called on both sides to seize the diplomatic opportunity, emphasizing "the Middle East doesn't need another war." The EU foreign policy chief stated that if talks fail, the EU is prepared to impose "more forceful sanctions."
