**[Core Summary]** According to CNBC and multiple media reports, Lithuania's parliament voted by majority to pass the first round of votes on lifting the constitutional nuclear weapons ban. This is a historic step toward Lithuania becoming a nuclear-weapon state, prompting strong opposition from Moscow. NATO and the EU have expressed high alertness on regional nuclear proliferation risks, while the UN reaffirms nuclear non-proliferation principles.

**[Event Details]**
- **First Round Vote**: Lithuania's parliament voted by majority to pass the first round of votes on lifting the constitutional nuclear weapons ban, with the issue moving to the second round of deliberation. Under Lithuania's legislative procedures, a constitutional amendment requires two rounds of voting before being submitted to a national referendum.
- **Official Argument**: The Lithuanian government has argued that the country's national security environment has undergone significant changes due to the Russia-Ukraine conflict and the adjustment of surrounding military forces, making nuclear weapons a legitimate one of sovereign tools.
- **Moscow Response**: The Kremlin and the Russian Foreign Ministry issued strongly-worded statements, warning that "any attempt to lift the nuclear weapons ban will trigger cascading setbacks in regional security systems," and hinted at raising objections at the UN Security Council level.
- **Allies' Stance**: NATO and the EU expressed "high alertness," stating that nuclear non-proliferation is the cornerstone of decades of international security and must not be shaken.
- **UN Position**: The UN Secretary-General's spokesperson reiterated that nuclear non-proliferation remains a "non-negotiable" principle of the international community, urging all parties to strictly fulfill existing treaty obligations.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**
**First**: This event is a clear signal of structural loosening in the global nuclear non-proliferation system. The Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT) currently faces three major pressures: first, the "repairability" assessment of nuclear arsenals by emerging technologies such as AI; second, the modernization of nuclear arsenals by nuclear-weapon states themselves; third, declining identification of "nuclear-free zones" among emerging countries. If Lithuania's motion is ultimately implemented, it will further impact the system's credibility.
**Second**: European nuclear deterrence and NATO nuclear sharing mechanisms will be forced to adjust. NATO nuclear sharing arrangements are the "last line of defense" of the European security architecture. Once Lithuania proposes "lifting nuclear weapons" and receives tacit approval from some countries, related countries may reopen NATO nuclear sharing negotiations, and the original "security trust" will be impacted.
**Third**: Global energy, supply chains, and financial risk pricing will gradually be affected by nuclear proliferation themes. The linkage between nuclear proliferation and energy security and financial asset prices will be reassessed, and high-sensitive assets in regional leading index funds may become precautionary properties.

- **NATO Secretary-General**: Nuclear non-proliferation is the unshakable cornerstone of global security, calling on all parties to "listen carefully."
