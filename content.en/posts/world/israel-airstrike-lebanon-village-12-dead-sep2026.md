The Israeli military launched airstrikes on a village in southern Lebanon on September 7, killing at least 12 people and injuring multiple others. The strikes targeted what the military described as Hezbollah military facilities, but civilian casualties drew widespread international attention and condemnation.

The Israel Defense Forces announced on September 7 that it had carried out precision strikes on a Hezbollah military site in southern Lebanon. The Israeli military claimed that the site was used to plan rocket attacks against Israeli northern communities. However, casualty figures released by the Lebanese Ministry of Health showed that the strikes killed at least 12 civilians, including women and children. Lebanese caretaker Prime Minister Najib Mikati issued a statement strongly condemning Israel's "barbaric acts" and called for immediate international intervention. The UN Special Coordinator for Lebanon issued a statement expressing "deep concern" over civilian casualties and called on all parties to "immediately cease fire." Egypt, Jordan, Qatar, and other countries also issued statements calling for avoiding further escalation. Air raid sirens sounded in multiple Israeli northern communities on the same day, intensifying regional tensions.

The incident reflects the structural dilemma of the Israel-Lebanon border conflict. Although the two sides reached a ceasefire agreement in 2024, core disputes over the existence of Hezbollah's armed forces, the demand for the return of northern residents, and settlement issues have never been resolved. Israel responds to border threats with a "targeted elimination" strategy, while Lebanon, due to its vastly inferior military strength, struggles to achieve effective countermeasures — this asymmetric conflict makes civilian casualties the norm. The incident also reflects that against the backdrop of continued Gaza conflict, the Israel-Lebanon border has become a new "secondary front" in the Middle East, and its spillover effects may further exacerbate regional instability.
