**[Core Summary]**

According to Fortune, The New York Times and Reuters, Alberta — home to the world's fourth-largest oil reserves — is set to hold a first-step secession referendum, marking a new political stage for the province's separatist movement.

**[Event Details]**

- **Key Milestone**: The upcoming Alberta referendum is described by separatist supporters as a "procedural first step," intended to lay the legal and political groundwork for subsequent formal independence negotiations.
- **Background Disputes**: Alberta has had persistent disagreements with the federal government in recent years over energy policy, carbon taxes and interprovincial pipeline projects; some residents are dissatisfied with the pace of the federal energy transition.
- **Energy Stakes**: Alberta holds the world's fourth-largest oil reserves, with oil sands as the province's economic backbone; any independence process would directly affect the policy environment, export corridors and investment outlook for the industry.
- **Federal Response**: Canada's federal government and most mainstream parties stress national unity, saying they will handle the referendum outcome in accordance with constitutional and legal procedures while hoping to resolve differences with Alberta through dialogue.
- **Next Steps**: After the vote, political negotiations between Alberta and the federal government, interprovincial energy agreements and possible constitutional discussions will become the focus of outside attention.

**[Panoramic Perspective]**

Alberta's separatist momentum is not merely a regional political event but a tangible manifestation of changing demands from "resource-rich provinces" in the context of the global energy transition. Under the multiple pressures of carbon reduction, clean-energy investment and shifting global oil-and-gas demand, resource-rich provinces tend to seek greater autonomy at the policy and fiscal level — a demand seen across several federal countries. In the short term, Canada's federal government must find a new balance between safeguarding national unity and responding to local demands. In the long run, changes in the political demands of resource-rich regions will directly shape the internal governance structure of federal countries during the energy transition.
