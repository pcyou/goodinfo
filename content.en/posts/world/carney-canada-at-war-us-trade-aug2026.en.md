## Core Summary

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney delivered his strongest diplomatic language yet on August 22, publicly declaring that Canada is "at war" with the United States in trade matters. Carney accused the Trump administration of "attacking" Canada's economic sovereignty through unilateral tariff impositions, stating that Canada will take all necessary measures to defend its national interests. This statement elevates what was previously a trade dispute to the level of national security, triggering widespread international concern about the future of the North American alliance.

## Event Details

### Carney's "War" Declaration

According to The New York Times, Carney stated at an emergency press conference in Ottawa: "When your largest trading partner launches an economic attack against you, this is no longer just a trade dispute—this is war." He accused the United States of conducting a series of "hostile actions" over the past 72 hours, including the sudden announcement of 50% comprehensive tariffs, threats to withdraw from the North American Free Trade Agreement framework, and targeted sanctions against key Canadian industries.

Carney emphasized that Canada will not succumb to "economic blackmail" and has activated countermeasure plans. He revealed that the Canadian government is evaluating retaliatory tariffs on U.S. agricultural products, energy, and technology services, while also considering filing a complaint with the World Trade Organization.

### White House's Strong Response

President Trump quickly fired back on social media, calling Carney's remarks "highly inappropriate" and "ungrateful." He claimed that the United States has been "taken advantage of by Canada for years" and is now simply "taking back what belongs to us." Trump also threatened that if Canada doesn't "return to the negotiating table with good faith," the U.S. will raise tariff levels to 75%.

The U.S. Trade Representative's Office subsequently issued a statement accusing Canada's digital services tax of "discriminating against American businesses" and demanding that Canada make "substantive concessions" on dairy market access.

### Market Reaction and Global Impact

Following the announcement, the Canadian dollar fell 2.3% against the U.S. dollar, and the Toronto Stock Exchange's main index dropped over 1.5%. U.S. futures markets also showed volatility, with agricultural and energy sectors leading the decline.

An International Monetary Fund spokesperson stated that this trade conflict "poses a serious threat to the North American and global economy," calling on both sides to resume dialogue as soon as possible. The EU Trade Commissioner issued a statement expressing "deep concern" over "economic confrontation between allies."

## Panoramic Perspective

The deeper implications of this trade conflict extend far beyond bilateral economic relations. From a geopolitical standpoint, the fact that Canada and the U.S.—founding NATO members and closest allies—have descended into a state of "economic war" marks an unprecedented fracture in the Western alliance system established after World War II. This rift could be exploited by other major powers, reshaping the global balance of power.

From an economic structure perspective, North American industrial chains have become highly integrated over decades. The automotive industry is a typical example—production of a single vehicle may involve dozens of cross-border movements among the U.S., Canada, and Mexico. A tariff war will force companies to undergo painful cost restructuring, with consumers ultimately bearing the burden. According to estimates by the Peterson Institute for International Economics, 50% tariffs would increase annual household spending in the U.S. by $2,000 to $3,000.

The more profound impact lies in the shaking of the international rules system. When the world's largest economy bypasses WTO mechanisms to take extreme unilateral measures, it is effectively undermining the foundation of the multilateral trade order. This could trigger a "demonstration effect," encouraging other countries to follow suit with unilateralism, ultimately leading to fragmentation of the global trade system.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Views supporting Carney's position** argue that a strong stance is necessary in the face of economic coercion. Former Canadian Foreign Minister Chrystia Freeland stated: "Appeasement has never worked. Prime Minister Carney is defending Canada's dignity and sovereignty." This position enjoys cross-party support in Canada, with polls showing 78% of Canadians support the government's tough response.

**Critical voices** warn of the risks of escalating confrontation. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce issued a statement saying "there are no winners in a trade war," calling on both sides to "remain calm and return to rational negotiations." Some Canadian business leaders have also privately expressed concern that excessive toughness could provoke the U.S. and lead to more severe economic consequences.

**Centrist views** suggest seeking third-party mediation. Mexican President López expressed willingness to serve as an "honest broker" to facilitate trilateral dialogue. The EU also hinted it might provide diplomatic support. This position holds that in a highly interconnected global economy, bilateral confrontation will only result in mutual losses, and multilateral coordination is the solution.
