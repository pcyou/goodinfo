## Mali Defense Chief Killed as Armed Groups Seize Towns and Military Bases

According to the Associated Press on April 27, Mali's defense minister was killed during a large-scale coordinated operation by armed groups that seized multiple towns and military bases. This is one of the most severe security incidents Mali has faced in recent years, posing a significant challenge to the military junta's governance legitimacy.

### Events Unfold

The attack is closely linked to the massive coordinated armed operation that began on the evening of April 26. Reports indicate that multiple armed organizations launched synchronized attacks across the country. Explosions and continuous gunfire erupted in the capital Bamako, with fighting spreading to strategic locations including Kati, Gao, and Kidal. During the course of the conflict, the defense minister was killed.

### Rise of an Armed Alliance

The Azawad Liberation Front (FLA) and Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin (JNIM), a group linked to al-Qaeda, formed a rare alliance during this operation. FLA spokesperson Mohamed Elmouloud Ramadan confirmed to the BBC, "We have been carefully planning this operation for a long time, and it was conducted in alliance with JNIM."

This coalition between Tuareg separatist forces and jihadist groups marks a significant shift in the Sahel's security landscape. Analysts note that while the two types of armed groups have cooperated before, a coordinated operation of this scale is unprecedented.

### Military Response and Situation Assessment

The Malian military claims to have "repelled" the armed groups, stating that "hundreds" were killed. However, the AP was unable to independently verify this claim. There are indications that fighting continues in some areas, with armed groups having taken control of multiple town entrances and key infrastructure.

According to reports, Malian government army barracks in Kidal and Gao came under siege but have not yet fully fallen. Ramadan stated, "We are in Kidal, and it has not been fully occupied. There are still remnants of the Malian army and Russian mercenaries there."

### International Concerns

The U.S. State Department's Bureau of African Affairs strongly condemned the attacks, stating it "stands with the Malian people and government in this act of violence." The UK Foreign Office advised against all travel to Mali following the attacks and confirmed that Bamako International Airport has been temporarily closed.

The capital has implemented a three-night curfew (21:00 to 06:00), checkpoints have been established on roads leading to the airport, and all flights were temporarily suspended.

### Implications for the Junta

Mali is currently ruled by a military junta led by General Assimi Goïta, who came to power through a 2020 coup, promising to restore security and repel armed groups. However, following the withdrawal of UN peacekeepers and French forces, the junta turned to Russian mercenaries, yet the security situation has continued to deteriorate.

The defense minister's death will have far-reaching implications for the stability and international credibility of the country's military government. Analysts warn that if the government cannot effectively control the situation, the Sahel region could face an even broader security crisis.
