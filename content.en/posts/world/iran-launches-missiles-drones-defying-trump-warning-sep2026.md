## Iran Launches Missiles and Drones Defying Trump Warning as Middle East Crisis Escalates

**[Core Summary]** According to The Washington Post, Iranian armed forces launched missiles and drones at US military targets on September 2, 2026, in a massive retaliatory strike that openly defied President Trump's stern warnings. This is the second round of military exchanges following a US strike that killed four people at a wedding party, marking an unprecedented escalation in direct US-Iran military confrontation.

### Latest Developments

Iranian state media confirmed that armed forces fired multiple ballistic missiles and drones at US military facilities in the Middle East. The statement emphasized that the strikes were a legitimate self-defense response to "American aggression" and warned that any further US military action would face even more intense retaliation.

Notably, this strike operation proceeded despite explicit warnings from President Trump just hours earlier. Trump had vowed to impose "severe strikes" on Iran and hinted that the US possessed "unprecedented military options." Iran's decision to proceed with the attack under these circumstances is interpreted by analysts as Tehran demonstrating its strategic resolve.

### Military Situation Analysis

Iran's arsenal in this operation included medium-range ballistic missiles and suicide drones, with strike ranges covering multiple US military bases in the Gulf region. US Central Command has activated air defense systems for interception, though the effectiveness and specific losses remain unclear.

Meanwhile, the USS Abraham Lincoln aircraft carrier, after completing its Middle East deployment, has docked near Pattaya beach in Thailand. This deployment adjustment is seen as the US maintaining strategic deterrence in the region while reducing direct confrontation risks.

### Comprehensive Analysis

The escalation of US-Iran military confrontation has profound and multi-dimensional impacts on global geopolitics.

**From a strategic perspective**, Iran's decision to proceed with attacks despite Trump's clear warning sends a clear strategic signal: Tehran will not back down under military pressure. This "offensive defense" strategy is both a product of domestic political pressure—the wedding casualties sparked strong anger in Iranian society—and a necessary move to demonstrate resolve to regional allies. For the US, facing escalation of overseas military operations during an election cycle creates equally significant political pressure, forming a bilateral "hardline trap."

**From an energy security perspective**, the safety of passage through the Strait of Hormuz faces new severe challenges. Approximately one-fifth of global oil trade passes through this critical waterway, and any shipping disruption would have immediate impacts on global energy prices. Brent crude oil futures jumped more than 3% in Asian morning trading.

**From an international order perspective**, the "normalization" of direct US-Iran military confrontation is reshaping Middle East security architecture. The traditional proxy conflict model is being replaced by direct state-to-state confrontation, meaning both miscalculation risks and escalation probabilities are significantly increasing. UN Security Council mediation efforts face enormous difficulties, as both sides view this confrontation as defending core interests.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Iranian officials** characterize the strike operation as "exercise of legitimate self-defense rights," emphasizing that targets were strictly limited to military facilities, and calling on the international community to condemn "American aggression." Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson stated that Tehran remains open to diplomatic solutions, but only if the US stops military provocations.

**The US side** faces difficult choices. The Trump administration must balance demonstrating a strong stance with maintaining regional stability. Congressional parties disagree on the scope of presidential military authorization, with some lawmakers demanding the White House immediately submit a military action report to Congress.

**International observers** are widely concerned that both sides are falling into a "retaliation cycle" trap. Each military action triggers an even larger response from the other side, potentially leading to uncontrollable full-scale conflict. China, Russia, and the EU have all called for maximum restraint and resolution of differences through dialogue.

**Gulf Arab states** are in a state of high tension. Saudi Arabia and the UAE worry that conflict expansion could affect regional stability and global energy supplies, and are actively mediating to prevent the spread of hostilities.
