## Core Summary

French President Emmanuel Macron has publicly warned that Russia's "hybrid attacks" against Europe are significantly escalating, spanning cyber sabotage, disinformation and interference with critical infrastructure. The remarks signal a major shift in Europe's strategic threat assessment toward Moscow and suggest that the EU may roll out more systematic responses across security, defense and sanctions policy.

## Event Details

According to the BBC, Macron stated in a recent public address that Russian "hybrid attacks" against Europe are intensifying, taking forms well beyond conventional military action—including cyber intrusions, disinformation campaigns, disruption of critical infrastructure and political pressure exerted through proxies.

Macron stressed that these operations are characterized by "persistence, low cost and high deniability," and that their cumulative effect is now materially threatening European social stability and political decision-making. He called on EU member states to build tighter coordination in intelligence sharing, cyber defense and critical infrastructure protection, and to consider expanding sanctions on Russian-linked entities and individuals.

In recent months, multiple European countries have disclosed suspected Russian-linked sabotage operations, including cyberattacks on power grids, telecoms networks and government agencies, as well as disinformation campaigns targeting elections and public discourse. France, Germany and the UK have all raised their cyber threat levels and rolled out countermeasures.

Analysts note that Macron's remarks carry significant political weight. Against the backdrop of continued EU divisions over aid to Ukraine, his emphasis on the practical danger of hybrid threats is an attempt to forge a stronger common position within the bloc and lay the groundwork for the next phase of defense and security policy coordination.

## Full Picture

Macron's latest remarks are both an objective response to a changing European security environment and a reflection of Europe's deeper strategic recalibration toward Russia. The implications extend well beyond a single speech.

**First, Europe's threat perception has expanded from "conventional military" to "full-spectrum hybrid."** For years, Europe's risk assessment toward Moscow centered on border military movements and energy dependence. Macron's remarks show that this framework has significantly broadened—cyberspace, the information environment, critical infrastructure and even migration are now considered part of the security threat picture. This shift will likely drive greater European investment in intelligence, cyber defense and civil protection.

**Second, intra-EU security coordination mechanisms are set to accelerate.** Hybrid threats are inherently cross-border, and no single country can respond effectively on its own. Macron's call points to faster EU progress on intelligence sharing, joint response mechanisms and critical-infrastructure protection legislation. This process will reshape the balance between national sovereignty and supranational governance within the bloc.

**Third, information warfare and the opinion space will become the new European security battleground.** Hybrid attacks, with their deniability and low cost, are particularly suited to information environments. The EU may move toward more systematic policies on fact-checking, platform accountability and media literacy to curb disinformation's interference with elections and public decision-making—a trend that will also shape global internet-governance rulemaking.

## Multiple Perspectives

**France and the hawkish camp:** The French government has continued to push the EU toward a tougher line on Russia, advocating wider sanctions and broader protection of European societies. Macron's remarks are a continuation of that trajectory, viewed as a key moment for France's leadership on EU security.

**Central and Eastern European states:** Countries such as Poland and the Baltic states, which have long been highly sensitive to Russian threats, broadly welcome tighter EU coordination on hybrid threats and see Macron's comments as a necessary acknowledgment of real risk.

**Some Western European states and dialogue advocates:** While strengthening defenses against Russia, several Western European countries still prefer to keep some diplomatic channel open to avoid further escalation. They worry that over-emphasizing "hybrid threats" could entrench a full-spectrum confrontation with Russia with unpredictable consequences.

**Academic and think-tank views:** Multiple European think tanks argue that hybrid attacks are inherently "below the threshold of war" and persistent, requiring long-term systematic response rather than reactive measures after major incidents. Some scholars have called for a dedicated EU "Hybrid Threat Response Center" that would coordinate intelligence, cyber and civil-protection resources.
