## Core Summary

BBC reports that the Trump administration has recently barred several major US media outlets' reporters from entering the White House, with press credentials belonging to CNN, MS NOW, and Politico journalists having been confiscated. The affected outlets have confirmed the news on their own platforms and indicated their reporting teams will face restricted access to White House coverage. The action is widely viewed as the latest escalation point in the strained relationship between the US executive branch and major news organizations.

## Event Details

According to related reports, the reporters whose credentials were revoked are primarily responsible for daily White House coverage, including tracking the president's schedule, attending cabinet briefings, and covering foreign policy press conferences. The affected media companies stated they have filed legal appeals with the White House, requesting the restoration of the reporters' press access.

The White House has not yet issued a complete official statement explaining the specific reasons for this credential revocation, although some officials hinted that the action is related to "inaccurate content" in recent specific reports. Multiple media organizations and press freedom advocacy groups quickly spoke out, warning that this move may violate the First Amendment's protection of press freedom and questioning whether the executive branch is using press access as a tool of political retaliation.

Legal analysts note that while the issuance and revocation of White House press credentials falls within executive discretion, if proven to be targeted retaliation based on reporting content, the action could face judicial challenge. It remains unclear whether this action is a temporary measure or a long-term policy shift, with all parties awaiting further clarification from the White House.
