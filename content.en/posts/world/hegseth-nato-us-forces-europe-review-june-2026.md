## Core Summary

US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth publicly lambasted NATO allies for failing to meet defence spending commitments and announced a comprehensive review of US military deployments in Europe. The statement triggered strong reactions from European allies, marking one of the most serious tests of transatlantic security relations since the end of the Cold War.

## Event Details

According to NBC News, Hegseth expressed strong dissatisfaction with the defence spending levels of several European allies during NATO-related meetings in Brussels. He noted that despite the 2014 Wales Summit commitment to raise defence spending to 2% of GDP, multiple member states have still failed to meet the target.

Hegseth announced that the Pentagon would launch a full review of US military deployments in Europe, assessing whether the current military presence aligns with American strategic interests. The review covers bases and force configurations in Germany, Italy, Poland and other countries. This statement was widely interpreted as a signal that the US may further reduce its European military footprint.

European defence and foreign officials expressed serious concern. Germany's defence minister responded that Europe understands US concerns but emphasised that transatlantic cooperation is more important than ever in the current security environment. France's foreign ministry called on NATO allies to resolve differences through dialogue rather than public pressure.

## Panoramic Analysis

The deeper context behind Hegseth's tough stance is the continued shift of US strategic focus toward the Indo-Pacific region. In Washington's view, the enormous costs of the Iran war and the strategic priority of competing with China make maintaining Cold War-scale European military deployments increasingly cost-ineffective. American calls for European allies to bear more of their own defence costs have escalated from private diplomatic discussions to public pressure.

For Europe, this situation is both a crisis and a catalyst. Europe's long-standing security dependence on the US has led to severely lagging defence capability development. Signals of potential US troop reductions are forcing the EU to accelerate its "strategic autonomy" agenda. European Defence Fund initiatives and Permanent Structured Cooperation mechanisms are expected to receive greater political support and funding.

However, European defence integration faces numerous practical obstacles: fragmentation of national defence industries, inconsistent procurement standards, and differences in political will all make building a European defence system independent of NATO unrealistic in the short term. The more likely path is for Europe to assume greater responsibility within the NATO framework while gradually building independent operational capabilities.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US position**: Washington believes European allies have long been "free-riding," failing to meet fair fiscal obligations while the US bears global security costs. A comprehensive troop review is a necessary means of pressuring allies.

**European moderates**: Led by Germany, moderates acknowledge the legitimacy of insufficient defence spending and advocate responding to US concerns through clear spending increase timelines while maintaining NATO unity.

**European hardliners**: Voices advocating strategic autonomy, particularly from France, believe US pressure proves Europe must build security architecture independent of Washington and should accelerate European defence integration.

**Russian perspective**: Moscow welcomes the deepening of transatlantic rifts, believing this creates greater strategic space for Russia in the European direction.
