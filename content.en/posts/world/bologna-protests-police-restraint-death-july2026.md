## Core Summary

Mass protests have erupted in the northern Italian city of Bologna after a Moroccan-born man, Abderrahim Fakir, died while being forcibly restrained by two police officers. Several thousand people took to the streets demanding "justice for Fakir," with a smaller group clashing with police overnight, leaving 64 officers injured. Prosecutors in Bologna have opened a manslaughter investigation into the officers and medical workers present at the scene.

## Event Details

The incident occurred in Bologna's working-class Pilastro neighborhood. Police were called on Sunday morning after a neighbor reported Fakir "behaving erratically" and kicking garage doors. Officers attempted to calm the situation but then used pepper spray.

A video circulating on Italian media shows two policemen sitting on Fakir as he lies face down, crying out for them to stop. A third non-police person pins his legs down while emergency workers stand by. About two minutes into the video, Fakir stops struggling. The officers tie his wrists and ankles, and when one realizes Fakir is no longer moving, he slaps him twice on the cheek. By the time an ambulance worker approaches, Fakir is clearly unconscious.

## Reactions

Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said it was imperative that the truth surrounding his death be investigated, but "nothing can justify violence against law enforcement." Bologna Mayor Matteo Lepore also expressed concern over the incident.

Fakir's sister Khadija told Italian media: "You can't die like this. They killed him mercilessly, and I want the truth." Several eyewitnesses told media that while Fakir appeared to be in distress, he did not threaten anybody and was clearly unarmed.

## Broader Analysis

This incident has once again pushed the issue of excessive police force in Europe into the public spotlight. In recent years, from the Floyd case in the United States to the Nahel case in France, similar incidents have repeatedly caused social division and institutional reflection. Italy, as the EU's third-largest economy, has long faced questions from human rights organizations about the transparency and accountability of its law enforcement system.

The deeper implications of this event are twofold: on one hand, it tests the balance between the Meloni government's right-wing coalition's support for law enforcement and public expectations of judicial fairness; on the other, Bologna, as a traditional left-wing stronghold in Italy, may see this protest's scale and intensity become a catalyst for national political change.

At the societal level, the trust crisis between immigrant communities and law enforcement continues to intensify across many European countries. Finding the balance between maintaining public safety and protecting civil rights has become a governance challenge that European governments can no longer avoid.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Pro-law enforcement view**: Conservative media and political figures emphasize that officers must quickly control situations when responding to reports of "erratic behavior" to protect public safety. They argue the protesters' violent actions are unacceptable, and PM Meloni's statement that "violence cannot be justified" has received broad support from right-wing circles.

**Critical view**: Human rights organizations and left-wing political forces point out that the video shows Fakir was fully controlled and no longer resisting when officers continued suppression measures that ultimately led to his death—clearly constituting excessive force. They question why officers slapped him after he stopped struggling and demand full release of body camera footage.

**Legal expert view**: Italian legal professionals believe prosecutors' manslaughter investigation is the correct first step, but whether convictions can be secured depends on forensic findings—specifically whether Fakir's death resulted from positional asphyxia, drug reaction, or other factors.
