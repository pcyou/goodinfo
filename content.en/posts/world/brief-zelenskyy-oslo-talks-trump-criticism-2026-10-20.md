**[Key Facts]**

Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy arrived in Norway on October 20 and was due to meet Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Stoere and President of Finland Alexander Stubb in Oslo, with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner also taking part. The Norwegian prime minister said in a statement that the aim was to put diplomatic efforts on a more constructive path.

**[What We Know]**

- **Meeting arrangements**: Zelenskyy arrived in Norway on Tuesday and was due to meet the Norwegian and Finnish leaders in Oslo later in the day, with US envoys joining the discussions. The Norwegian prime minister said Ukraine requested that the US envoys attend.
- **US position**: Responding to criticism, Trump said waiving sanctions against Russia had put Ukraine on the “wrong side of history,” adding that Zelenskyy “doesn't know what he's talking about” and is a leader who should have had the war settled long ago.
- **Shifting backdrop**: The United States has recently eased some pressure on Russia, which invaded Ukraine in 2022, opening a narrow window for diplomatic contact.

**[Wider Perspective]**

Holding the talks in a third country at Ukraine's request signals that Kyiv is still trying to preserve a diplomatic channel through a multilateral framework. With the US applying public pressure and Ukraine dependent on outside support, the practical scope for agreement in Oslo is limited, but the signalling value is not: Norway and Finland's willingness to keep hosting dialogue suggests European capitals have not closed the diplomatic option. The interests of the three parties diverge, and the presence of US envoys points to an agenda likely covering ceasefire terms, sanctions arrangements and Ukrainian security guarantees. Whether anything concrete emerges will depend on how much runway remains before the electoral cycle.
