## Core Summary

The US House of Representatives passed a historic bill on July 14 to make Daylight Saving Time permanent, eliminating the need to adjust clocks every spring and fall. This legislative achievement ends America's century-long "time switch" debate with broad bipartisan support. The bill now moves to the Senate for consideration.

## Event Details

According to CNN, the bill passed by voice vote, demonstrating strong bipartisan consensus. Supporters argue that permanent daylight saving time reduces traffic accidents, improves public health, conserves energy, and provides more shopping time for retailers.

This issue has been debated in the US for decades. Since 2020, over 30 states have passed legislation for permanent daylight saving time at the state level, but federal uniform action has been stalled. In 2022, the Senate unanimously passed similar legislation, but it languished in the House. This House passage marks the bill just one step away from becoming law.

## Analysis

Implementing permanent daylight saving time will have far-reaching effects on American society. From a public health perspective, research shows that the spring "spring forward" hour is associated with a 24% increase in heart attack risk, while the fall rollback leads to reduced evening daylight, correlating with rising seasonal depression rates. Permanent daylight saving time means longer evening daylight year-round, benefiting outdoor activities and community interaction.

From an economic perspective, retail and leisure industries are the biggest beneficiaries. Longer evening daylight directly stimulates consumption—people are more inclined to shop, dine, and participate in outdoor activities after work. The US Chamber of Commerce estimates permanent daylight saving time could generate approximately $4 billion in additional retail revenue annually.

However, this change also faces criticism. Some health experts point out that permanent daylight saving time means darker winter mornings, potentially affecting early workers and students. The agricultural sector has traditionally opposed daylight saving adjustments because livestock biological clocks struggle to adapt to artificial time changes.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporters**: Bill sponsors emphasize that eliminating twice-yearly clock changes reduces sleep disorders, traffic accidents, and heart attack risks. The retail association expressed support, believing longer evening daylight will stimulate consumption.

**Opposition**: Some health experts warn darker mornings in winter may affect student safety. Agricultural stakeholders worry about adverse effects on livestock. Some parent groups expressed concern that delayed daylight could affect children's school commute safety.

**International Experience**: Russia implemented permanent daylight saving time in 2011 but restored standard time in 2014 due to strong public dissatisfaction with dark winter mornings. This precedent provides important reference for American policymakers.
