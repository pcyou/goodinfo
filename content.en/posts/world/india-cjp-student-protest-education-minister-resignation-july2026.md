## Summary

India has witnessed its largest student protest movement in years. The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP)-led demonstrations, initially sparked by exam paper leak scandals, rapidly spread into a nationwide youth political movement. Under sustained pressure from massive protests, India's education minister has resigned, and CJP subsequently announced a pause in demonstrations. The event is regarded as one of the most significant grassroots challenges to the Modi government in recent years.

## Details

According to BBC News, the CJP youth movement was initially triggered by exam paper leaks. Multiple state-level civil service and qualification exams in India were found to have compromised questions, sparking outrage among young students and job seekers.

The CJP movement, organized primarily through social media, quickly expanded from local protests to a national movement. Tens of thousands of young people took to the streets demanding investigations into the exam leaks and education equity reforms. Protests unfolded simultaneously across multiple states, significantly disrupting traffic and public order in some areas.

Facing escalating protests, India's education minister ultimately announced resignation. CJP then declared a pause in protests but stated it would continue monitoring the government's education reform commitments. Political analysts note this is the most threatening grassroots movement the BJP led by Modi has faced in recent years.

## Analysis

The far-reaching impact of this student movement extends well beyond education. First, it reveals Indian youth's deep anxiety about the job market and examination system — millions compete annually for limited civil service positions, and exam fairness directly affects social mobility. Second, CJP's decentralized organizational model demonstrates a new form of youth political participation in the social media era, difficult for traditional party machines to absorb or suppress.

For the Modi government, this event sounds an alarm. Despite BJP's dominance in national elections, grassroots discontent can rapidly coalesce into political pressure on specific issues. While the education minister's resignation was damage control, if exam system reforms fail to deliver substantive progress, protests are likely to resurge.

## Multiple Perspectives

**CJP supporters** view the movement as a legitimate resistance against systemic injustice. Exam leaks are not merely administrative failures but a reflection of elite monopolization of public resources. They praise CJP's nonviolent pressure strategy.

**The Indian government and BJP supporters** emphasize the government's swift response, noting the education minister's resignation demonstrates responsibility. Some officials have hinted at political manipulation behind the protests.

**Independent analysts** point out that CJP's real test lies in whether it can translate street protests into sustained policy influence. Indian history offers many examples of large student movements that dissipated into political compromise, but this movement's broader social base warrants close attention.
