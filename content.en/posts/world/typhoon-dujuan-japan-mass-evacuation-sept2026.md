## Core Summary

Powerful Typhoon Dujuan has struck Japan with force, the BBC reported, with authorities urging millions of people to evacuate urgently to avoid the severe impacts of the storm. The typhoon's landfall is among the more serious natural disasters Japan has faced in recent years, posing a severe test of Japanese people's safety of life and property, the normal operation of the economy and society, and the effective functioning of the disaster prevention and mitigation system. The event will also draw international attention to the trend of increased frequency of extreme weather events in the context of climate change.

## Event Details

**Intensity and Scope of Impact**: According to the BBC, Typhoon Dujuan struck Japan with strong intensity, affecting multiple regions. The report noted that the typhoon brought multiple types of extreme weather, including strong winds, heavy rainfall, and storm surges, which had a significant impact on transportation, electricity, water supply, and communications infrastructure across multiple regions of Japan. Several prefectures and cities issued the highest-level evacuation advisories, requiring residents in the relevant areas to evacuate urgently to safe locations. The report noted that the evacuation advisories issued by the relevant authorities involved millions of people, reflecting the severity and potential scope of the typhoon's impact.

**Evacuation Advisories and Disaster Response**: The report noted that the Japan Meteorological Agency and local governments issued early warning information before the typhoon's landfall, and gradually raised warning levels as the typhoon's impact expanded. Several prefectures and cities issued evacuation advisories to residents in high-risk areas, including low-lying areas, riverbanks, and areas prone to geological hazards, based on meteorological forecasts and disaster prevention plans. Japan's disaster prevention and mitigation system is characterised by refined early warning, multi-level response, and broad public participation, and the response process once again reflects Japan's institutional advantages and organisational capabilities in the field. Disaster prevention analysts noted that the rapid response and broad mobilisation by relevant authorities played an important role in protecting the safety of life.

**Infrastructure and Economic-Social Impact**: The landfall of Typhoon Dujuan had a significant impact on Japan's infrastructure and economic-social operations. Multiple railway lines were suspended, multiple flights were cancelled, and several expressways were temporarily closed, causing widespread disruption to Japan's domestic transportation network. Power supply, water supply systems, and communications networks in multiple regions were affected to varying degrees, with some areas experiencing specific problems such as power outages and water supply interruptions. Many businesses, shops, and schools were temporarily closed, causing inconvenience to the daily lives and work of Japanese citizens. Economic analysts noted that the specific impact of the typhoon on Japan's economy and society remains to be assessed through specific follow-up, but in the short term it will cause a clear shock to economic activity in the relevant regions.
