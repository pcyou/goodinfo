## Core Summary

US Vice President JD Vance has cancelled his planned trip to Switzerland for the next phase of US-Iran peace negotiations, marking a significant setback in diplomatic efforts. Meanwhile, military confrontation between Israel and Hezbollah in southern Lebanon has escalated dramatically over the past 24 hours, with both sides reporting heavy casualties. These developments pose a direct challenge to the US-Iran peace agreement signed just two days ago.

## Event Details

According to NBC News, Vance was scheduled to travel to Switzerland for technical-level negotiations following the US-Iran peace deal, but the trip was cancelled due to the rapidly deteriorating situation in Lebanon. The White House has not announced a new timeline for talks.

Lebanon's Health Ministry reported that Israeli airstrikes in southern Lebanon have killed at least 18 people, with multiple civilian facilities damaged. The Israel Defense Forces confirmed that four soldiers were killed in exchanges of fire with Hezbollah, marking the largest single loss of life for Israeli forces in recent weeks.

Israel maintains that its military operations against Hezbollah are independent of the US-Iran peace framework. An IDF spokesperson stated that strikes against Hezbollah will continue until the group no longer poses a threat to Israel's northern border. Hezbollah has previously rejected any terms of the US-Iran agreement.

Iran has stated that the US bears responsibility for Israel's actions, arguing that Washington has failed to constrain its ally's behavior in violation of the spirit of the peace agreement.

## Analysis

The escalation exposes a fundamental gap in the US-Iran peace deal: it addresses direct bilateral confrontation but does not cover parallel conflicts involving Israel and non-state armed groups like Hezbollah. From a geopolitical perspective, Vance's cancellation signals weakening US control over the regional situation, with various powers exploiting the perceived power vacuum.

For global markets, this uncertainty directly increases oil price volatility risks. While the Strait of Hormuz has reopened under the deal, further escalation in Lebanon could give Iran pretext to restrict passage again.
