## Orbán Exits Hungarian Parliament: The End of an Era

Viktor Orbán, Hungary's former prime minister, formally announced on April 25 that he will not accept his seat in the National Assembly. This comes after his Fidesz party suffered a historic defeat in the recently concluded parliamentary elections, ending more than a decade of uninterrupted rule.

According to the BBC, Orbán communicated his decision to senior party officials shortly after the election results were confirmed. Analysts view this move as a fundamental shift in Hungary's political landscape—since 2010, Orbán has been the most influential figure in Hungarian politics, presiding over a series of controversial reforms that included weakening judicial independence, tightening media controls, and adopting a hardline stance on immigration.

In this election, the opposition coalition won by a decisive margin, with voters expressing deep frustration over economic stagnation, persistent inflation, and strained relations with the European Union. In his victory speech, the opposition leader pledged to steer Hungary back toward mainstream European values.

Orbán's decision to step down drew significant international attention. The European Union welcomed the development and reiterated its commitment to supporting Hungary's democratic transition. Analysts suggest that Orbán's choice to forego his parliamentary seat may reflect an effort to avoid an uncomfortable dynamic in an opposition-controlled assembly, while also leaving room for future political maneuvering.

Hungary's new government is expected to be formed within weeks, and its foreign policy—particularly regarding relations with the EU, Russia, and China—will be closely watched by the international community.
