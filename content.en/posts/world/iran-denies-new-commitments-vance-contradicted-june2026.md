## Core Summary

Less than 24 hours after US Vice President Vance announced major progress in US-Iran talks and claimed Iran agreed to resume international nuclear inspections, Iran foreign ministry publicly stated that Tehran made no new commitments. This rare public contradiction casts doubt on what appeared to be a breakthrough diplomatic outcome.

## Event Details

According to BBC and CBS News, after concluding direct talks with Iran delegation in Geneva, Switzerland, Vance told reporters that negotiations achieved great progress and that Iran had agreed in principle to resume IAEA inspections of its nuclear facilities under terms similar to the Obama-era nuclear deal framework.

However, Iran foreign ministry subsequently issued a statement carefully distancing itself from Vance claims. The statement emphasized that Iran made no new commitments during the Geneva talks and expressed willingness to continue dialogue on the basis of equality and mutual respect.

## Panoramic Analysis

The public contradiction between the two sides reveals the fragile nature of the current diplomatic process. Vance eagerness to announce a breakthrough may stem from domestic political considerations. Iran denial reflects Tehran bottom-line thinking on nuclear issues.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US Position**: The White House characterized Vance Geneva trip as constructive engagement, emphasizing technical-level consensus on a principled framework for inspections.

**Iran Position**: Tehran credited its own diplomatic wisdom for the outcomes, insisting any arrangement resulted from equal consultation and denying unilateral concessions.
