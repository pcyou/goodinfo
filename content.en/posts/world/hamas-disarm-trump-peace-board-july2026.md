## Core Summary

A senior Hamas official has told the BBC they have agreed to the Board of Peace's plan. Israel has not commented. This breakthrough could bring a turning point to the years-long Middle East conflict, with the international community responding with cautious optimism.

## Event Details

BBC News reports that a senior Hamas official revealed in a BBC interview that the organization has agreed to accept the plan proposed by the "Board of Peace." This marks the first time since the conflict began that Hamas has explicitly signaled willingness to accept an international mediation proposal.

The "Board of Peace" is an international mediation body initiated by former US President Trump, aimed at achieving lasting peace in the Middle East. Its members include several former heads of state and diplomatic experts who have conducted intensive consultations with all parties over the past months.

The Hamas official stated that conditions for agreeing to disarm include ensuring reconstruction funding for Gaza, lifting the blockade on the territory, and establishing an international monitoring mechanism. However, he emphasized that specific details still require further negotiation.

The Israeli government has not yet issued an official response. Previously, Israel has consistently insisted on Hamas's complete disarmament as a prerequisite for any peace agreement. Analysts believe Hamas's statement may open a window for restarting negotiations, but the final agreement still needs to overcome numerous obstacles.

International reactions are mixed. The UN Secretary-General's spokesperson welcomed any development that helps ease tensions, the EU foreign policy chief called on all parties to seize this "historic opportunity," while some Middle Eastern countries remain in wait-and-see mode, awaiting Israel's formal response.

## Panoramic Perspective

Hamas's agreement to disarm marks a potential major turning point in the Middle East peace process. This decision stems from multiple converging factors: the ongoing humanitarian crisis has placed Gaza in unprecedented distress, international pressure continues to mount, and the regional power structure is undergoing profound changes.

From a strategic perspective, the Board of Peace's involvement offers a new pathway beyond traditional mediation channels. The Middle East policy framework formed during the Trump administration is continuing in new forms, while regional cooperation among countries like Saudi Arabia and the UAE on maritime alliances has laid foundations for broader security architecture.

However, disarmament is only the first step. Gaza's reconstruction requires massive funding and long-term commitments — estimates suggest infrastructure repair alone needs tens of billions of dollars. Whether international aid promises materialize, whether monitoring mechanisms function effectively, and whether all parties can build basic trust will determine whether this breakthrough translates into lasting peace.

The deeper impact lies in how this development could reshape the Middle East's security paradigm. If successful, it would provide a replicable mediation model for other conflict zones; if it fails, it could deepen divisions and make peace prospects even more distant. Historical experience shows the Middle East peace process is full of twists and turns, but every breakthrough deserves to be cherished.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Hamas Position**: Emphasizes that agreeing to disarm is based on humanitarian considerations and responsibility for Gaza's civilian welfare, but requires the international community to fulfill reconstruction commitments and lift the blockade.

**Israel's Stance**: Currently maintaining silence, but previous position clearly demands complete disarmament. Observers believe there may be divisions within the Israeli government, and the final response will reflect complex political considerations.

**US Role**: The Trump administration has re-engaged Middle East affairs through the Board of Peace, attempting to leave a diplomatic legacy. This move is both a continuation of traditional Middle East policy and a new experiment.

**International Reaction**: The UN and EU welcome the development but remain cautious, emphasizing the need to see concrete actions. Regional countries are divided — some actively supportive, others watching and waiting.
