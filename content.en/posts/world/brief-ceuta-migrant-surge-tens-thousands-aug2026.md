## Summary

Tens of thousands of migrants have crossed from Morocco into Spain's North African enclave of Ceuta in a massive surge. Spain has criticized what it called the "selfish" response of some EU member states, highlighting deep divisions within the bloc on migration policy.

## Details

BBC News reported that the unprecedented flow of migrants into Ceuta has prompted a strong reaction from the Spanish government, which is calling for a more coordinated European approach to managing the crisis.

Ceuta, one of Spain's two North African enclaves, has long been a crossing point for migrants attempting to reach Europe. The scale and speed of this latest influx has raised concerns about border security and the sustainability of current EU migration frameworks.

## Perspectives

Spanish officials emphasize that migration burdens must be shared more equitably across the EU, rather than falling disproportionately on border nations. Some other EU members, however, favor stricter border controls and increased returns, reflecting the ongoing policy divide within the bloc.
