## Core Summary

Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson formally announced his resignation on September 17, ending his tenure leading the moderate coalition government. The political change follows a recent parliamentary election in which the center-right governing coalition failed to maintain its majority, with the left-wing opposition bloc led by Social Democrat Magdalena Andersson poised to form the next government. The result marks a significant shift in Sweden's political landscape.

## Event Details

According to BBC and Guardian reporting, Swedish parliamentary election results showed the left-wing party bloc winning a majority of seats in this vote. Following the results, Kristersson submitted his resignation to the Speaker of Parliament in accordance with convention, clearing the way for the new government formation.

As Moderate Party leader, Kristersson has served as Swedish Prime Minister since 2022, during which time he led a minority government. Over the past few years, he has overseen Sweden's historic decision to apply for NATO membership and pushed through a series of tough policies on counterterrorism and public security. However, slowing economic growth and divisions over immigration issues ultimately undermined the governing coalition's popular support base.

Magdalena Andersson briefly served as Sweden's first female Prime Minister from 2021 to 2022 and is now expected to return to office. Her comeback will bring clear policy adjustment signals, particularly a more proactive stance on social welfare, climate change, and EU affairs.

The specific formation process of the new government has now commenced. According to Swedish constitutional procedure, the motion to nominate a new prime minister will be submitted by the Speaker, with Parliament scheduled to vote in the coming days. Upon passage, Andersson will formally form her cabinet.

Notably, Sweden's far-right party performed strongly in this election, in contrast to the trend of political spectrum rightward shift observed in other Nordic countries in recent years. This phenomenon will have profound impacts on the new government's governing stability and policy direction.

## Panoramic Perspective

This Swedish political landscape change occurs against the broader backdrop of profound adjustments in European political ecology. In recent years, the rise of far-right parties in France, Germany, the Netherlands, and other countries has continuously rewritten traditional political maps, while Nordic countries have long been regarded as a stronghold of social democracy.

Kristersson government's fall reflects several deep trends: first, immigration and security issues have risen to core election-determining topics in Sweden, with traditional parties' positions increasingly divided; second, climate policy and energy transition costs have become middle-class concerns, influencing centrist voters' preferences; third, the Nordic welfare state model is facing dual pressures from aging populations and globalization competition, with traditional left-right divisions no longer fully covering voter demands.

The new government formation will face complex political coordination challenges. Andersson needs to reach compromises with potential coalition partners across multiple dimensions including domestic affairs, foreign policy, and security. Meanwhile, though the far-right party has not entered government, it possesses strong parliamentary influence and will pose continuous constraints on the new government's legislative agenda.

From a European geopolitical perspective, the Swedish political change may affect the country's role positioning within the NATO framework. During Kristersson's government, Sweden completed its historic NATO accession process, but domestic divisions over NATO military commitments have not been eliminated. The new government's foreign policy direction is closely watched, particularly against the backdrop of the continuing Russia-Ukraine conflict.

The deeper impact lies in the European integration process. Though Sweden is a eurozone member, it has maintained a cautious stance on multiple EU topics. If the new government more actively promotes the EU integration agenda, it will produce demonstration effects on the overall Nordic regional position.
