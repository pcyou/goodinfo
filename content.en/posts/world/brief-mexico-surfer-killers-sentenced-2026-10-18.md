**[Core Summary]**

According to The New York Times and Fox News on Oct. 17, a Mexican court has sentenced three men to more than 158 years each for their roles in the killings of US and Australian surfers on Mexico's coast.

**[Event Details]**

- **Verdict**: The Mexican court sentenced the three defendants to more than 158 years each; the cumulative sentencing reflects the gravity of the case in terms of cross-border impact and the nature of the crime.
- **Background**: The victims were US and Australian citizens who were abducted while surfing along Mexico's coast; their remains were discovered in subsequent investigations, and the case previously drew wide international media coverage.
- **Judicial Significance**: Mexico's judicial authorities view the verdict as a landmark case against organized crime, emphasizing the strict prosecution of major cases involving foreign nationals.
- **Cross-Border Reaction**: The relevant families and diplomatic agencies in the US and Australia expressed concern about the verdict and reiterated their support for overseas citizen safety and cross-border judicial cooperation.
- **Going Forward**: The episode again focuses outside attention on the operation of tourist safety, diplomatic protection and cross-border judicial cooperation mechanisms in parts of Mexico's coastal region.

**[Panoramic Perspective]**

The verdict sends a clear judicial signal that major cases involving foreign nationals will face severe prosecution. However, the episode also focuses outside attention on the long-standing challenges Mexico's coastal areas face in tourist safety, policing capacity and judicial efficiency. In the short term, the verdict may serve as a deterrent and lift local tourism and foreign-resident communities' safety expectations; in the long run, Mexico's continued investment in coastal governance, policing modernization and cross-border judicial cooperation will be the key variable determining whether overseas tourist and investor confidence can recover substantively.
