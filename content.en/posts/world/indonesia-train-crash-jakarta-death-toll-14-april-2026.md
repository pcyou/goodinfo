The death toll from a train collision on the outskirts of Indonesia's capital Jakarta has risen to 14, the head of the national rescue agency confirmed on April 28, 2026. Rescue operations are ongoing, with workers continuing efforts to extract people trapped in the wreckage.

The accident occurred on Monday (April 27) when two passenger trains collided on tracks in the Jakarta suburbs. Preliminary investigations suggest the crash may be related to a signal system failure, though the exact cause remains under investigation. The collision severely damaged multiple carriages, presenting significant challenges for the rescue operation.

Indonesia's National Search and Rescue Agency stated that a large number of personnel and equipment have been deployed to the accident scene. Due to the severe deformation of some carriages, rescuers have had to use specialized cutting tools to access the interiors. Several trapped passengers have been successfully extracted and transported to nearby hospitals for treatment.

Indonesia, an archipelago nation of 270 million people, relies heavily on rail transportation as a key mode of transit. In recent years, the Indonesian government has been pushing for the modernization of its railway system, but some sections of track and infrastructure remain relatively outdated, with ongoing safety concerns.

The Indonesian Presidential Palace has expressed concern over the accident and ordered relevant departments to make every effort in treating the injured and investigating the cause. The Ministry of Transportation has also announced a nationwide safety inspection of the railway system to prevent similar incidents in the future.

The accident has drawn public attention to Indonesia's railway safety standards. Analysts point out that with accelerating urbanization and growing transportation demands, Indonesia urgently needs to increase investment in railway infrastructure and improve overall transportation safety.
