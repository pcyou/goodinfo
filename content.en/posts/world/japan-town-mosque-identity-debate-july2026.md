## Core Summary

According to the BBC, a Japanese town has sparked heated debate and protests from some residents over plans to build its first mosque. This incident reflects the deep structural challenges facing Japanese society amid demographic changes: as Japan's economy becomes increasingly dependent on foreign labor, how to accommodate and integrate different cultures and religions has become an unavoidable social issue.

## Event Details

This Japanese town has long been known for its homogeneous social structure, with the vast majority of residents being Japanese and practicing Shinto or Buddhism. When a group of foreign workers proposed plans to build a mosque, some local residents expressed strong opposition.

Concerns from opponents include: the mosque construction might change the community's traditional character; different religious customs might conflict with local lifestyles; and the increase in foreign population might strain community resources. Some residents organized petition drives calling on local government to reconsider the mosque construction plans.

Supporters of the mosque argue that foreign workers have made important contributions to the local economy and community development and should enjoy the right to freedom of religious belief. They point out that Japan faces severe labor shortages, particularly in construction, agriculture, and nursing, where foreign workers are essential to keeping these industries running.

The local government has maintained a cautious stance amid the controversy, stating it will process construction applications according to law while calling on all parties to resolve differences through dialogue. Local officials say balancing community harmony with respect for multiculturalism is a challenge that requires careful consideration.

Nationwide, the number of foreign workers in Japan has grown steadily in recent years. According to Japan's Ministry of Justice, foreign workers in Japan have exceeded two million, reaching a record high. As the foreign population increases, religious diversity issues are gradually emerging, with growing demand for mosques and prayer spaces.

## Panoramic Perspective

This mosque controversy reflects the deep structural challenges facing Japanese society. Japan is one of the most ethnically homogeneous developed countries in the world, with virtually no large-scale immigration for a long period. However, with accelerating population aging and declining birthrates causing severe labor shortages, Japan has had to gradually open its foreign labor market.

This shift has brought profound sociocultural impacts. Japanese society has traditionally taken pride in its single ethnic culture, with highly unified social norms and lifestyles. The influx of foreign workers brings differences in language, religion, diet, habits, and customs, posing unprecedented tests for Japanese society's inclusiveness and adaptability.

From an economic perspective, foreign workers are increasingly vital to Japan's economy. Multiple industries are heavily dependent on foreign labor, and without it, Japan's economic growth and social functioning would face greater difficulties. However, the tension between economic needs and sociocultural adaptation has become a challenge Japanese policymakers must confront.

From an international comparison, Japan's approach contrasts sharply with European countries. European nations introduced foreign workers on a massive scale after World War II but failed to effectively address integration issues, leading to social division and rising extremism. Japan appears to be trying a different path, but the mosque controversy shows that even small-scale religious and cultural differences can trigger social tension.

In the long term, Japan needs to establish a more inclusive multicultural framework while maintaining social cohesion. This involves not only legal and policy adjustments but also shifts in social attitudes. How to embrace diversity while respecting traditional culture will be an important challenge Japan faces in the coming decades.

## Multiple Perspectives

Residents opposing the mosque construction primarily focus on preserving community traditions and lifestyles. They are not entirely opposed to foreigners but believe the mosque might bring unforeseen changes to the community. Some residents expressed concern that prayer calls and religious activities might affect daily life.

The foreign worker community emphasizes that freedom of religious belief is a fundamental human right that should be respected. They point out they have contributed to local economic and social development and deserve equal rights with locals. The mosque serves not only as a religious space but also as an important venue for community exchange and mutual support.

Japanese intellectuals and human rights organizations generally support religious freedom, believing the mosque controversy reflects Japan's need for greater openness and inclusiveness. They call on the government to develop clear multicultural policies to help foreign workers integrate into society.

Business leaders take a pragmatic approach, arguing Japan must face the reality of labor shortages and properly treating foreign workers is essential for maintaining economic competitiveness. They recommend the government strengthen cross-cultural education and community exchange programs to reduce misunderstandings and biases.
