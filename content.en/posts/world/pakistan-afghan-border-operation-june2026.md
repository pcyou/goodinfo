## Summary

Pakistan's military announced a major ground operation with air support along the Afghan border, killing 29 militants. Pakistan's information minister confirmed the operation targeted extremist group hideouts in the tribal areas along the Pakistan-Afghanistan border. This is one of the largest security operations in the border region in recent months.

## Details

According to the Associated Press, Pakistani military intelligence identified multiple militant camps in the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa border area after weeks of reconnaissance. The operation launched in the early hours of June 28, with ground forces advancing under cover of attack helicopters and drones to strike confirmed targets.

Pakistan's Information Minister Ataullah Tarar told a press conference in Islamabad that the killed militants belonged to multiple banned extremist groups, including factions linked to the Afghan Taliban. He said security forces also seized large quantities of weapons, ammunition, and explosives.

The Dawn newspaper, citing security sources, reported the operation's success was aided by recently enhanced border surveillance, including new checkpoints and drone patrol networks. Some militants may have fled across the border into Afghanistan before the operation began, and Pakistan has raised the matter with Afghan authorities.

## Analysis

The large-scale operation reflects the deteriorating security situation in Pakistan. Terrorist attacks have increased significantly this year, particularly in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan provinces. Pakistan has long accused the Afghan Taliban government of failing to honor its commitment to prevent any group from using its territory to threaten other countries' security.

From a geopolitical perspective, Pakistan's high-profile announcement serves both to address domestic security concerns and demonstrate counterterrorism resolve to the international community — important for maintaining IMF aid programs and Western security cooperation.
