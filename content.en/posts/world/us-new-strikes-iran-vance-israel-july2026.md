## Core Summary

The United States has launched a new wave of military strikes against Iran, with explosions reported in multiple locations across the country. In a remarkable development, Vice President JD Vance publicly accused Israel of "manipulating" American public opinion to prolong the Iran conflict, sending shockwaves through Washington and raising questions about the future of US-Israel relations.

## Event Details

According to BBC reporting, explosions have been heard across Iran as the US initiates another round of military action. This follows previous US airstrikes on Iranian targets and the reinstatement of a naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz.

In an unprecedented move, Vice President Vance issued a statement accusing Israel of manipulating American public opinion to keep the US engaged in military action against Iran. This represents an extraordinary public criticism of America's closest Middle Eastern ally by a sitting vice president.

Vance's comments have sparked intense debate in Washington. Supporters argue he has voiced an uncomfortable truth, while critics condemn his statement as "irresponsible" and potentially damaging to US-Israel relations. The White House has not issued an official response to Vance's remarks.

US Central Command stated it is monitoring the situation closely but provided no additional details about the new strikes.

## Panoramic Analysis

The significance of this event extends beyond the military dimension to reveal a deepening rift within American foreign policy circles. Vance's public accusation against Israel is virtually unprecedented in the history of US-Israel relations, reflecting a growing divide between the "America First" faction and traditional pro-Israel establishment voices.

From a strategic perspective, Vance's statement suggests the existence of two competing approaches to Iran within the US government: one advocating continued pressure aligned with Israeli interests, and another favoring diplomatic engagement. This internal contradiction, if perceived by adversaries, could undermine America's negotiating position in the region.

On the military front, the continuation of strikes indicates that the US has not slowed its operational tempo despite Iran's previous retaliatory actions. However, sustained military operations are consuming significant resources, and the security situation in the Gulf region is becoming increasingly complex. Global energy markets are closely monitoring the Strait of Hormuz, through which approximately 21 million barrels of oil pass daily.

The broader implications extend to regional geopolitics. The public rift between US and Israeli positions may prompt Gulf states including Saudi Arabia and the UAE to reassess their strategic positioning, while Iran may seek to exploit the divergence for diplomatic gains.

## Multiple Perspectives

Domestic US reaction to Vance's statement has been sharply divided. The "America First" wing of the Republican Party largely supports Vance, arguing the US should not be drawn into endless Middle Eastern conflicts by allies. Traditional foreign policy circles, however, have expressed alarm, suggesting the remarks damage trust with America's most important ally.

Israel has not yet issued an official response to Vance's accusations. Israeli officials have consistently characterized their influence on American public opinion as "legitimate diplomatic activity."

Iranian state media has interpreted Vance's statement as "evidence of awakening conscience within the American establishment," while reiterating calls for dialogue-based dispute resolution.

International observers note that Vance's remarks may reflect more than personal opinion, potentially signaling an ongoing policy debate within the US government. If this debate ultimately leads to a substantive shift in US policy toward Israel, the Middle East geopolitical landscape could face its most significant transformation in decades.
