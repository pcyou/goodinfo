## Core Summary

According to the Financial Times, Ukrainian President Zelenskyy is considering dismissing the Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces of Ukraine. This decision comes amid escalating domestic protests, reflecting deep divisions within Kyiv's leadership over war strategy and military command.

## Event Details

The Financial Times, citing multiple sources familiar with the matter, reported that President Zelenskyy is dissatisfied with the current battlefield situation and is evaluating the possibility of replacing the top military commander. The report indicates significant disagreements between the Presidential Office and military leadership on strategic direction, particularly regarding defense deployments and resource allocation.

Meanwhile, domestic protests in Ukraine continue to escalate. Demonstrations demanding improved treatment for soldiers and faster counteroffensive progress have erupted in multiple cities. Veterans and families of fallen soldiers have taken to the streets to express dissatisfaction with the government's management of the war.

The Ukrainian Presidential Office has not issued an official comment on the report. A Defense Ministry spokesperson stated that adjustments to military command fall within the president's authority, but there are currently no such plans.

## Panoramic Analysis

This personnel change rumor reveals the deep governance challenges Ukraine faces in a prolonged war. From a political science perspective, civil-military relations during wartime are a core issue of national governance. When military command and political leadership diverge on strategic judgment, how to maintain civilian control of the military while respecting military professionalism becomes a key test of leadership wisdom.

From historical experience, replacing military commanders during wartime is often a double-edged sword. On one hand, new commanders may bring fresh strategic thinking and stronger execution; on the other hand, frequent personnel changes can disrupt command continuity and morale. During World War II, Stalin frequently replaced generals in the early stages of the war, only stabilizing the command system after generals like Zhukov gained full trust.

From the perspective of Ukraine's domestic political ecology, the escalation of protests reflects public anxiety about war progress. After more than two years of conflict, Ukrainian society is being tested by "war fatigue." How to maintain public support while preserving strategic patience is the core challenge facing the Zelenskyy government.

Additionally, this incident reflects Western donor countries' concerns about Ukraine's battlefield progress. The United States and other NATO countries are closely monitoring decision-making trends in Kyiv, as the stability of military leadership directly affects the effectiveness of Western military assistance.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Views Supporting Dismissal**: Some Ukrainian parliamentarians and military commentators believe the current battlefield stalemate requires new leadership thinking, and replacing the commander could break bureaucratic inertia and inject new vitality.

**Voices Opposing the Change**: Military insiders and defense experts warn that replacing the top commander during wartime could cause command confusion and give Russia an opportunity. They emphasize that current problems lie not in individual capability but in insufficient resources and an unfavorable strategic environment.

**International Observer Perspectives**: Western think tank analysts point out that this controversy reflects the normal operation of Ukraine's democratic system under war pressure—political leadership has the right to evaluate and adjust military strategy, but must carefully balance professionalism and political considerations.

**Divided Public Opinion**: On Ukrainian social media, public opinion is sharply divided. Some support the president making decisive decisions, while others worry this move will increase internal instability.
