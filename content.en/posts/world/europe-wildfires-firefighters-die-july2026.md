## Core Summary

Wildfires in southern Europe continue to worsen. Two firefighters tragically died while battling blazes on the Greek island of Crete, while France's Gironde region sees thousands of hectares of forest destroyed and authorities urgently evacuate multiple villages. The combination of high temperatures, strong winds, and drought creates severe firefighting challenges. Weather services warn that fires may become even more uncontrollable in the coming days.

## Event Details

According to BBC News, a severe wildfire broke out on the Greek island of Crete on July 29, with strong winds fueling rapid fire spread and forcing authorities to urgently evacuate multiple villages. Two firefighters died during rescue operations, with several others injured. Greek civil defense authorities have requested EU aerial firefighting support.

France's southwestern Gironde region faces equally severe fire conditions. Over 3,000 hectares of forest and shrubland have been consumed by the blaze, with approximately 1,500 residents evacuated. Local fire departments deployed over 200 firefighters and multiple aerial firefighting aircraft. One volunteer firefighter told the BBC: "This is our forest, and we must protect it." However, some residents refuse to evacuate, complicating rescue efforts.

Northern Spain has also reported multiple fire incidents, with authorities deploying over 500 emergency personnel. The EU Civil Protection Mechanism has been activated to coordinate cross-border firefighting support among member states.

## Panoramic Perspective

The early arrival and abnormal intensity of this European wildfire season highlights the profound impact of climate change on the continent. First, the Mediterranean region is experiencing unprecedented heatwave cycles, with extended summer high temperatures and reduced precipitation pushing vegetation dryness to critical levels, making it highly susceptible to large-scale fires. Second, traditional firefighting strategies face challenges—strong winds cause fire spread rates to far exceed expectations, putting firefighters' safety at greater risk.

From an economic perspective, direct losses from wildfires include forest resources, agricultural output, and tourism revenue. France's Gironde region is a famous wine-producing area; if the fire spreads to vineyards, it will deal a heavy blow to the local economy. Additionally, smoke pollution from fires affects air quality and could trigger public health crises, particularly for patients with respiratory diseases who face higher risks.

In the long term, Europe needs to reassess forest management strategies and urban planning standards. Measures such as increasing firebreaks, improving tree species selection, and establishing early warning systems urgently need advancement. Meanwhile, EU-level climate adaptation policies require more specific financial support and implementation mechanisms, rather than remaining at the commitment stage.

## Multiple Perspectives

**French Government Response**: France's Ministry of Interior stated it has mobilized national firefighting resources to support affected areas and called on the public to strictly comply with evacuation orders. President Macron expressed condolences to the families of deceased firefighters on social media, calling them "heroes of the nation."

**Greek Emergency Services**: Greece's Civil Protection Minister noted that this year's fire season started nearly a month earlier than usual, with fire intensity far exceeding historical averages. Authorities have implemented nationwide fire bans and strengthened regulations on outdoor fires.

**Environmental Organizations**: The World Wildlife Fund European Division issued a statement criticizing insufficient investment by governments in forest fire prevention. The organization noted that climate change impacts have been warned by the scientific community for years, but policy response speeds have lagged far behind.

**Meteorological Expert Analysis**: European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts data shows that southern Europe's summer temperatures this year are 3-5 degrees Celsius above historical averages, with precipitation reduced by over 40%. Experts warn that if greenhouse gas emission trends remain unchanged, such extreme fire events will become more frequent and severe.
