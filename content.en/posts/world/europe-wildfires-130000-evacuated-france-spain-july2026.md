## Core Summary

According to BBC News, the wildfire crisis in southern Europe has escalated dramatically over the past 24 hours. More than 130,000 people have been evacuated across France and Spain, with France's Gironde department around Arcachon Bay experiencing the most severe conditions. This marks the largest natural disaster event in Europe this summer, prompting the European Union to fully activate its cross-border rescue mechanism.

## Event Details

Wildfires in southern and southwestern France have spread rapidly since earlier this week. Gironde department is among the hardest-hit areas, with extensive pine forests and camping grounds around Arcachon Bay engulfed by flames. French authorities report that consecutive days of extreme heat and strong winds have caused fires to spread faster than anticipated. Over 80,000 people have been evacuated in France alone, including many tourists on vacation.

Eastern Spain's Catalonia and Valencia regions face equally severe fire conditions. Spanish emergency services report that strong winds and dry vegetation have made fires difficult to control, with residents and tourists from multiple coastal resort areas transferred to safety. Spain's total evacuees have exceeded 50,000.

European Commission spokesperson confirmed that the Union Civil Protection Mechanism has been fully activated. Italy, Greece, and Portugal have dispatched firefighting aircraft and ground rescue teams to assist France and Spain. Germany and the Netherlands have also pledged additional aerial firefighting support.

The French President and Spanish Prime Minister each delivered televised addresses, urging citizens to remain calm and comply with evacuation orders. Both countries have requested additional support at the EU level.

Meteorological departments warn that extreme heat will persist in southern Europe over the coming days. While winds are expected to weaken, humidity remains extremely low, keeping fire risk at "extreme" levels.

## Panoramic Perspective

This European wildfire crisis once again highlights the profound impact of climate change on continental-scale disaster risks. Mediterranean regions are experiencing increasingly frequent extreme heat and drought conditions, creating ideal conditions for large-scale wildfires. Scientific models show that southern Europe's "fire season" is significantly lengthening, with traditionally safer spring and autumn periods now showing high-risk windows.

From a disaster management perspective, this event exposes ongoing challenges in the EU's cross-border rescue coordination mechanisms. Although the Civil Protection Mechanism has been legally established for years, rapidly allocating limited aerial firefighting resources remains challenging when multiple countries are simultaneously affected. Response experience from this crisis will likely drive the EU to further strengthen joint disaster response capacity.

Economically, the impact on tourism and agriculture cannot be ignored. Tourism is the economic pillar of southwestern France and eastern Spain, and large-scale evacuations and infrastructure damage will bring huge economic losses. The insurance industry expects to face billions of euros in claims.

## Multiple Perspectives

The French government emphasizes it has mobilized all available national firefighting resources and thanked EU partner countries for support. However, opposition parties criticize the government for slow response in early warning systems and early response, arguing evacuation orders were issued too late.

Spain's Catalonia regional government points out that the central government should provide more aerial firefighting resources. Madrid responds that it has done its utmost to coordinate national rescue efforts.

The European Commission praises member states' solidarity but acknowledges the existing aerial firefighting fleet is insufficient to handle such large-scale simultaneous fires. Environmental organizations call for the EU to invest more resources in fire prevention and climate adaptation measures, rather than relying solely on post-disaster rescue.
