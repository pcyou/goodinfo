# Trump Says Iran Wants Blockade Lifted as Negotiations Remain Deadlocked

US President Donald Trump stated on April 28, 2026, that Iran has reached out to Washington requesting the lifting of the naval blockade on its ports. However, despite signals of contact, multiple experts warn that formal negotiations remain stalled, with global energy markets facing the risk of "long-term disruptions."

## Trump's Statement

Trump told reporters at the White House: "Iran has contacted us. They want us to lift the blockade." He framed this as a potential sign of thawing relations, suggesting it could reflect the possibility of a "cherished bond" between the two nations.

However, Trump did not confirm whether the US would take immediate action to lift the blockade. Analysts noted that the statement may be a negotiating tactic, designed to pressure Iran into making concessions on key issues.

## The Stalled Negotiations

Despite Trump's positive signals, the reality on the ground is far more complex. According to multiple sources, formal US-Iran negotiations are currently "stalled." The two sides remain deeply divided on several core issues:

1. **Nuclear Program**: The scope and limitations of Iran's nuclear program remain one of the biggest points of contention.
2. **Sanctions Relief**: Iran is demanding comprehensive removal of economic sanctions, while the US insists on a phased, conditional approach.
3. **Regional Security**: The security landscape in the Middle East, particularly Iran's military presence in neighboring countries, is also a focal point of the negotiations.

## Economic Impact: Oil Prices Continue to Rise

With the US-Iran standoff ongoing, international oil prices have continued to climb. The US naval blockade of Iranian ports has severely disrupted Middle Eastern oil exports, tightening global energy supplies.

Energy analysts warn that if the blockade persists, it could lead to "long-term supply disruptions" — a scenario that would not only hamper global economic growth but could also exacerbate inflationary pressures. Meanwhile, China's electric vehicle industry is positioning itself to capitalize on the crisis — BBC reported that China's EV sector is seeing new opportunities amid the global fuel scramble.

## International Reaction

The international community is closely monitoring the US-Iran situation. German Chancellor Merz has publicly criticized the US military action against Iran, prompting Trump to publicly rebuke him, saying Merz "doesn't know what he's talking about" and defending the necessity of the Iran campaign.

The United Nations and humanitarian organizations have also issued warnings, urging all parties to resolve the dispute through diplomatic channels and prevent further escalation of the conflict.
