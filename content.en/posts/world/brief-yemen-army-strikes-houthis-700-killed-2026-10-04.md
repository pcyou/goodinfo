Al Jazeera reports that Yemen's government-aligned army has launched hundreds of airstrikes and ground operations against Houthi-held territory, killing approximately 700 fighters. The Yemeni army frames the operation as a key step toward retaking critical western areas and weakening Houthi control over Red Sea shipping lanes; several key infrastructure and military sites have been destroyed.

The campaign runs alongside Saudi planning for a large-scale offensive against the Houthis and Washington's current decision not to join, suggesting the Yemeni government is racing to expand battlefield gains before US posture becomes clearer. The Houthis have not publicly acknowledged the large-scale losses but their official media has characterised the Yemeni-government action as "illegal aggression" and pledged a reciprocal response.

International humanitarian organisations have raised concerns over civilian casualties and infrastructure damage in western Yemen, urging all parties to comply with international humanitarian law and protect civilians and civilian objects. Red Sea shipping, global energy transit and broader Middle East security dynamics remain highly attentive, with fears that further escalation could spill into global shipping and energy flows. Al Jazeera's analysis notes that Yemen's civil war is shifting from Houthi-led offense to multi-axis counter-attack, and that Red Sea shipping safety and Yemen's internal humanitarian situation are likely to face severe tests in the coming period.
