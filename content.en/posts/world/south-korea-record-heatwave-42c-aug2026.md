## Record Heatwave Hits South Korea as Temperatures Soar to 42.5C

**[Core Summary]** South Korea experienced its most severe heatwave on record on August 5, with temperatures in some areas soaring to 42.5 degrees Celsius, breaking historical records. The extreme heat has caused at least 19 deaths and thousands of heatstroke cases. The government issued the highest-level heat alert, urging residents to reduce outdoor activities.

### Event Details

According to BBC reports, the Korea Meteorological Administration confirmed this heatwave as the strongest since records began in 1907. Major cities including Seoul, Daegu, and Busan all exceeded 40 degrees, with Daegu recording an extreme 42.5C.

South Korea's Ministry of Health and Welfare reported that the heatwave has caused at least 19 deaths from heat-related illnesses, with over 3,000 people seeking medical treatment for heatstroke and dehydration. The deceased are mostly elderly over 65 and outdoor workers. Seoul City has opened all 2,000+ cooling centers and dispatched mobile medical teams to check on elderly living alone.

The Presidential Office announced activation of emergency response mechanisms, requiring all levels of government to suspend non-essential outdoor activities, switch schools to online classes, and adjust construction site working hours. Surging electricity demand caused brief power outages in some areas, with Korea Electric Power Corporation urging citizens to conserve energy.

### Panoramic Perspective

This extreme heatwave is another warning signal of climate change in Northeast Asia. Meteorologists note that the Western Pacific subtropical high has strengthened abnormally, overlapping with high-temperature systems in Japan and eastern China, causing unprecedented sustained heat on the Korean Peninsula. This "heat dome" phenomenon traps warm air in the lower atmosphere, creating a continuous warming cycle.

From a public health perspective, South Korea's highly aged society (population over 65 exceeds 18%) makes extreme heat a severe threat to elderly groups. Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency data shows over 80% of heat-related deaths are elderly. This exposes the dual risks of urban heat island effects and demographic changes — high-density cities lack sufficient green space and ventilation corridors, while elderly living alone lack timely assistance.

Economically, the high heat has caused significant impacts on agriculture, manufacturing, and tourism. The National Agricultural Cooperative Federation reports that rice pollination disruption and vegetable yield reductions have led to 15-20% agricultural price increases. Semiconductor factories face capacity pressure from surging cooling demands, with Samsung and SK Hynix wafer fabs recording historic electricity usage.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Meteorology Community View**: Seoul University climate science professor Kim Min-jun noted such extreme heat events will become more frequent in coming decades. "We are moving from 'once in a century' to 'once a decade' or even 'annual' new normal. Adaptation measures must shift from emergency response to systematic planning."

**Policy Criticism**: The opposition Democratic Party criticized the government's slow response, noting uneven distribution of cooling facilities and insufficient support for rural and low-income communities. The party spokesperson stated: "Climate change is not a future threat but a current crisis. The government needs a long-term adaptation strategy, not repeating emergency patterns annually."
