## Core Summary

According to BBC, the Bald Range wildfire in British Columbia, Canada continues to rage out of control, spreading over 53 square miles (approximately 137 square kilometers). This wildfire has caused one death and forced mass resident evacuations. Local officials warn residents to "brace for the worst" as the fire continues to spread under strong winds and dry conditions.

## Background

British Columbia has frequently suffered from wildfires in recent years, with climate change-induced drought and extended hot seasons considered the main causes. The 2017 and 2018 fire seasons set records, and this year appears to be heading toward another severe wildfire season.

## Broader Perspective

Western Canada's wildfire problem has become a visible manifestation of climate change. As global temperatures rise, forest dryness increases, wildfire seasons lengthen, and fire intensity and frequency both rise. This not only threatens resident safety but also damages forest ecosystems and carbon sink functions, creating a vicious cycle. Strengthening forest management, investing in early warning systems, and improving community fire prevention capabilities have become urgent priorities.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Government Response:** Provincial government has mobilized substantial firefighting resources and requested federal support.

**Expert Analysis:** Climate scientists note such extreme wildfire events may become more frequent in the future, requiring long-term adaptation strategies.
