## Key Facts

Germany and France are jointly advancing a trade tool against the Chinese market, evaluating to bar China's access to key European initiatives. The terms span export controls, investment review, critical asset procurement review, and across other dimensions. This marks a major shift in EU strategic-autonomy posturing toward China economic policy.

## What We Know

- **Policy tools**: Germany and France are considering a combination of trade tools including export controls, investment review, and critical asset procurement review.
- **Focus areas**: Key technology areas including AI, quantum technology, semiconductors, clean energy, and biotech.
- **EU stance**: The European Commission president has publicly expressed support for relevant tools, with cautious analysts judging EU internal policy detail divergences remain.
- **China reaction**: Chinese Ministry of Commerce spokesperson calls on the EU to comply with WTO rules and avoid protectionist measures.

## Analysis

**First**, the Germany-France joint move on China trade tools marks a new phase in EU economic policy toward China. Moving from the previous passive WTO-rules-dependent approach to a more strategically proactive policy combination.

**Second**, from a geopolitical dimension, the background for this tool is European concern over its own declining industrial competitiveness, wariness of China's rapid advances, and consensus on supply chain diversification. A German joint action capitalizes on this strategic window.

**Third**, for global supply chains, European tools will accelerate global supply chain further bifurcation. On one hand, European enterprises will face re-examination in the Chinese market; on the other, Chinese enterprises will face policy uncertainty in the European market.

**Fourth**, for US-China relations, this tool means both sides need to find balance between strategic autonomy and win-win cooperation. Short-term, it may trigger bilateral trade friction escalation; long-term, it will push US-China economic governance into a new phase.

**Fifth**, from a medium to long-term impact perspective, the launch of EU trade tools against China may prompt more cooperation between US and Europe in third-party markets. Multiple emerging economies may become carriers for deepened cooperation between the two sides, while also facing new alignment pressures.
