## U.S. Missile Stockpiles Critically Low: 80% of Key Interceptors Depleted, Commanders Warn

**[Core Summary]** According to an exclusive CNN report, the U.S. military has consumed nearly 80% of its key missile interceptor stockpiles in Middle East and Ukraine conflicts. Multiple military commanders have warned Congress that current reserve levels are "dangerously low," severely undermining America's ability to handle multi-front conflicts. This revelation has sparked intense debate on Capitol Hill regarding defense industrial capacity and military spending allocation.

### Event Details

Citing multiple anonymous military sources, the report indicates that inventories of Patriot air defense interceptors, THAAD anti-missile interceptors, and Standard-3 (SM-3) sea-based interceptors have fallen to dangerous levels. Over the past two years, the U.S. has provided substantial air defense weapons aid to Ukraine while simultaneously addressing threats from Iran and its proxies in the Middle East, causing precision-guided munitions consumption rates to far exceed replenishment rates.

The U.S. Secretary of Defense acknowledged in closed-door hearings that the defense industrial base faces production bottlenecks and critical component supply chain constraints. Major contractors like Lockheed Martin and Raytheon have announced expansion plans, but new capacity will take 18-24 months to become fully operational.

### Panoramic Perspective

This incident exposes a core contradiction in modern warfare: high-precision weapon systems are expensive and time-consuming to produce, while actual combat consumption rates far exceed peacetime estimates. The U.S. military's strategic commitment to maintaining both European and Middle Eastern fronts is testing the limits of its logistics support system.

At the strategic level, the missile inventory crisis may reshape U.S. global military deployment logic. The Pentagon must reassess its traditional planning baseline of "fighting two wars simultaneously," instead considering more selective ally support strategies. This has cascading effects on security commitments to NATO's eastern flank and Indo-Pacific allies, while also prompting allied nations to accelerate their own defense industrial development.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Military Position**: Joint Chiefs of Staff members emphasize the need for immediate defense budget increases to replenish stockpiles and expand production capacity, otherwise the U.S. faces a "strategic deterrence vacuum."

**Congressional Hawks**: Advocate for significant cuts to foreign aid, prioritizing homeland defense needs. Some lawmakers criticize "bottomless pit" aid to Ukraine as damaging U.S. military readiness.

**Congressional Doves**: Argue the root problem lies in defense industry monopoly and inefficiency, advocating for more commercial competition mechanisms to boost production rather than simply increasing budgets.

**Industry Response**: Major contractors state they are expanding production at full capacity, but emphasize the government needs to provide long-term procurement commitments to support investment confidence, noting that short-term surge production is unrealistic.
