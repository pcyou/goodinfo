President Donald Trump posted a map on social media on September 7 suggesting that New Mexico be renamed "New America." The proposal immediately sparked fierce controversy in the United States, with Democrats criticizing it as "political theater" while some Republicans remained silent.

Trump posted the map on Truth Social with the caption, "This is one of the most beautiful and historically significant regions in our nation's history." The renaming proposal triggered polarized reactions on social media. Supporters saw it as a tribute to local indigenous culture and the heritage of America; critics argued that Trump lacks the constitutional procedures to push for state renaming and that this distracts from substantive issues. New Mexico Governor Michelle Lujan Grisham issued a statement saying, "New Mexicans do not need the President to tell us what to call ourselves."

The incident reflects the continued rise of social media's influence on presidential decision-making in the context of U.S. political polarization. State renaming is a constitutional issue requiring approval by both the state legislature and the federal Congress, making this proposal more of a political gesture and an opinion-mobilization tool. Trump's practice of initiating policy issues directly through social media is reshaping the boundaries of American political communication and raises deeper concerns about whether constitutional procedures are being "bypassed."
