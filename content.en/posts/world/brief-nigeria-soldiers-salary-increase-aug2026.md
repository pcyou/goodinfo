## [Brief] Nigeria's President Orders 80% Salary Increase for Junior Soldiers

**[Core Summary]** Nigerian President Bola Tinubu announced on August 5 an 80% salary increase for junior soldiers to address recruitment and retention crises in the security forces. The move comes as the military fights on multiple fronts against Boko Haram, ISIS West Africa branch, and other armed groups.

### Event Details

According to BBC reports, President Tinubu announced the decision at a military conference in Abuja. The salary adjustment will benefit approximately 60,000 junior soldiers, with estimated annual fiscal expenditure increasing by about $150 million. The president stated: "Our soldiers risk their lives protecting the country; they deserve compensation matching their sacrifice."

Nigeria's security forces have faced severe challenges in recent years. Low pay, poor conditions, and high risk have led to rising soldier attrition rates, with some units experiencing desertion. Military analysts note that low salaries are also a factor contributing to corruption and low morale.

### Panoramic Perspective

This decision reflects the severity of the security situation in West Africa. Nigeria has been fighting Boko Haram and its affiliate ISIS West Africa Province (ISWAP) since 2009, with the conflict causing tens of thousands of deaths and millions displaced. Additionally, the country faces multiple security threats including northern kidnapping ransoms, central farmer-herder conflicts, and southern oil region militant activities.

From an economic perspective, an 80% salary increase represents a major fiscal commitment given Nigeria's current economic conditions. The country is experiencing high inflation (over 30%) and currency depreciation pressure, with limited government fiscal space. However, military experts argue that without improving soldier compensation, security force combat effectiveness will further decline, and the ultimate cost could be higher.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Military Response**: The Nigerian Military Union welcomed the move but demanded that mid-level officers also be included in the salary adjustment. Union spokesperson stated: "This is a step in the right direction, but we need comprehensive compensation reform."

**Critical Voices**: Opposition parties pointed out the government should prioritize solving equipment and training issues rather than only raising salaries. "Soldiers need bulletproof vests, modern weapons, and adequate training; otherwise, salary increases only treat symptoms, not root causes."
