## Core Summary

The Grand Canyon National Park area in Arizona was hit by sudden flash floods, leaving at least 20 hikers missing. The flash floods, described by witnesses as "terrifying," swept through the canyon in an extremely short time, posing enormous challenges to rescue operations. Currently, multiple rescue teams have rushed to the scene, but search and rescue progress has been slow due to complex terrain and severe weather conditions. The incident once again highlights the threat of extreme weather events to public safety.

## Event Details

According to BBC and NBC News reports, the Grand Canyon area experienced severe flash floods on Sunday afternoon local time. Witnesses described the floods as coming extremely suddenly, with water levels rising rapidly within minutes, leaving many hikers unable to evacuate in time.

The Grand Canyon National Park Service stated that at least 20 people have been confirmed missing, and rescue operations are underway. The US National Park Service, Arizona Emergency Management Department, and local fire departments have dispatched multiple rescue teams to the scene.

However, rescue operations face multiple challenges. First, the Grand Canyon's complex terrain means many areas can only be reached by helicopter or professional climbing teams. Second, continued rainfall puts rescuers at risk of secondary flash floods. Additionally, some missing persons may be trapped in narrow areas deep in the canyon, increasing search and rescue difficulty.
