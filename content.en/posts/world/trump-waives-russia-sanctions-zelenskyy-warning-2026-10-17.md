**[Core Summary]**

U.S. President Donald Trump signed an executive order on October 17 bypassing a Congress-passed Russia sanctions bill named for the late Senator Lindsey Graham, citing "national interests." Ukrainian President Zelenskyy immediately warned the decision amounted to "rewarding Putin" and would extend and intensify the Russia-Ukraine war. The bill was set to take effect in 11 days, and Trump simultaneously waived tariffs on the largest buyers of Russian oil, triggering bipartisan congressional criticism and concern from European allies.

**[Event Details]**

- **Executive order content**: Trump on October 17 signed an executive order formally waiving the new Russia sanctions measures under the so-called Graham Act, a bill named for the late Republican Senator Lindsey Graham that was scheduled to take effect automatically in 11 days. Trump invoked "national interests" in the order to suspend implementation, and simultaneously waived tariffs on the largest buyers of Russian oil.
- **Zelenskyy's response**: Ukrainian President Zelenskyy publicly stated that Trump's waiver decision amounted to "rewarding Putin," would extend Russia's war on Ukraine, and significantly undermine the effect of U.S. military and financial aid to Kyiv. Zelenskyy warned that the sanctions waiver would give Moscow greater fiscal and military resources and inflict greater harm on Ukrainian civilians and front-line soldiers.
- **Bipartisan congressional pushback**: The bill was championed by the late Republican Senator Graham and enjoyed broad bipartisan support; Trump's executive order bypassing Congress triggered sharp dissatisfaction from both Republican and Democratic lawmakers. Multiple senators said they would pursue new legislative means to restore the sanctions and consider curbing the White House's unilateral discretion in foreign policy.
- **European allies' concern**: NATO members Britain, France, Germany, and Poland expressed "serious concern" over the U.S. sanctions waiver, fearing it would weaken the G7's overall pressure campaign on Russia and disrupt the transatlantic sanctions coordination mechanism. EU foreign-affairs officials have requested urgent consultations with the U.S. State Department on Russia policy coordination.
- **Energy-market reaction**: The decision to waive tariffs on the largest buyers of Russian oil triggered short-term volatility in international crude prices, with Asian buyers quickly resuming some Russian crude purchases, while the EU and Britain maintained their import caps and price-cap mechanisms on Russian oil products.
- **Background timeline**: Trump has publicly questioned the effectiveness of Russia sanctions on multiple occasions this year and criticized European aid levels to Ukraine at various junctures. The waiver decision marks a further shift in U.S. Russia policy from "maximum pressure for talks" toward "conditional engagement," creating an obvious divergence from the bipartisan mainstream position in Congress.

**[Panoramic Perspective]**

Trump's waiver of Russia-related sanctions marks the entry of U.S. Russia strategy into a new phase of uncertainty. In the short term, the substantive impact of the executive order is limited — the EU and Britain still maintain their own Russia sanctions and price cap, and U.S. firms actively avoid the Russian market due to compliance risk, so actual purchases of Russian oil and gas are unlikely to rebound significantly. In the medium to long term, **so the waiver sends a clear signal: the United States no longer treats "maximizing pressure on Russia" as its top priority and has elevated conflict management in Ukraine and great-power competition management to a higher strategic level; this adjustment will directly affect NATO internal cohesion, Ukraine's battlefield resupply, and the global energy-trade pattern.** Lacking a U.S. reciprocal action, the EU is likely to accelerate a mechanism that uses the proceeds of frozen Russian sovereign assets to support Ukraine's reconstruction, partially filling the aid shortfall that the United States may create at the fiscal level.

The deeper tension lies in the fatigue toward the Ukraine issue within the U.S. domestic political landscape. Trump's waiver decision is not only a public challenge to the bipartisan establishment but also a direct response to his voter base's "anti-war, anti-foreign-intervention" demands. **so over the next six months, the scale and pace of U.S. aid to Ukraine will be shaped more by the domestic political cycle than by actual battlefield needs in Ukraine; this variable will force Ukraine and European allies to accelerate the construction of long-term aid and reconstruction-financing mechanisms that do not depend on the United States.** At the same time, Russia may use this window to accelerate battlefield advances and explore partial detente with Europe through diplomatic channels; the international community must closely watch Moscow's coordinated moves on the battlefield and diplomatic fronts.
