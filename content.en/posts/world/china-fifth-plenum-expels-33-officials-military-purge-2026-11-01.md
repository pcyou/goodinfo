## Key Facts

- China's ruling party concluded its most important meeting of the year, the Fifth Plenum, confirming the expulsion of 33 senior party members as the anti-corruption campaign continues to widen.
- The meeting opened on Monday and closed on Thursday, with discipline and inspection as its central theme.
- Of the 376 full and alternate central committee members originally named in 2022, only 279 attended. More than a quarter were absent, and many of them are believed to be under investigation.
- Among those expelled are Zhang Youxia, formerly China's most senior military general, and Liu Jianchao, a senior diplomat previously tipped to be the next foreign minister.
- General Zhang Shengmin, a key player in the anti-corruption campaign, was promoted to the Politburo, which consists of more than 20 of the party's top officials.
- Analysts describe the military leadership as almost completely purged over the last three or four years, with Zhang Shengmin the notable exception who both survived and was elevated.
- The plenum is read as a signal ahead of next year's party congress, in which a fourth term for the top leader is widely speculated.

## Competing Views

**Neil Thomas, a senior fellow on Chinese politics at the Asia Society Policy Institute**, reads the meeting as organisational preparation. He argues that the focus of this plenum suggests the top leader is unlikely to take risks on Taiwan, the South China Sea or disputes with the United States until he feels more comfortable with the party he leads, which makes the meeting relevant to how outsiders assess Beijing's posture.

**Joseph Torigian, a professor of Chinese studies at American University**, emphasises the political signal instead. He says the meeting sends another message amid wide speculation about a fourth term, and that its effect is to ensure everyone is on the same page before the congress.

**The absence rate itself** offers a third reading. One analyst says the meeting reveals how much work the top leader thinks the party still has to do to become a more effective, loyal governing institution. A committee with more than a quarter of its members absent, many of them under investigation, is a direct measure of the state of that work rather than an interpretation of it.

**The military case** illustrates the depth and direction of the campaign. One analyst notes that the military leadership has been almost completely purged in the last three or four years, yet one figure not only survived but was promoted into the Politburo, which reinforces the importance of the appointment as a statement about who is trusted.

## Panoramic Analysis

The most overlooked and most informative number at this meeting is the absence rate. Of 376 full and alternate central committee members named in 2022, only 279 attended, leaving more than a quarter of the body absent. In a committee that sits higher and shrinks with every tier of the hierarchy, the absence of one in four is not a temporary gap in a few posts. It is the signature of a large-scale vacancy in senior personnel, and it converts discipline and inspection from a policy agenda into a measurable organisational condition.

That is also why reading this plenum as a routine milestone does not hold. In the preview stage it was widely treated as a transitional meeting whose subject was discipline. What actually pushed that agenda further was the structure of absence itself. Personnel decisions are normally made before a plenum and merely confirmed inside it. Placing that confirmation in the formal communique gives the removals the highest available endorsement, and makes the scale of the campaign something the outside can check rather than take on trust.

The direction of promotion is a second under-read signal. A general who has long participated in the campaign moving into the Politburo connects the position of the enforcer with the position of the decision maker. And the description of the military leadership as almost completely swept points to reconstitution rather than simple removal: when almost every seat at the top of the military leadership changes within a few years, the result is not the continuation of an old order but the establishment of a new one.

That points to the meeting's significance on a longer time horizon. Read together, the theme and the absence structure suggest the real purpose of the discipline campaign was not any particular case but the preparation of a controllable organisation for next year's congress, so that personnel arrangements at the congress itself are no longer constrained by factions or by cases still under investigation. This is what is meant by the caution about taking risks: a leadership whose internal cohesion is not fully settled leans conservative in its external posture by default.

Three indicators matter for outside observers. Whether the absent members are replaced before the next congress. What portfolio the newly promoted general takes on inside the Politburo. And whether the centre of gravity of the campaign moves further down into the party and state system at grassroots level. A directional change in any one of these will reveal the real policy priority earlier than the communique ever will.
