## Core Summary

A U.S. federal judge delivered a landmark ruling on August 22, determining that the Trump administration's visa freeze on 75 countries violates U.S. law and ordering the immediate rescission of the executive order. The judge sharply criticized Secretary of State Rubio's policy as having "Orwellian" characteristics, stating that its "discriminatory approach based on nationality violates the fundamental principles of U.S. immigration law." This ruling is seen as a significant judicial check on executive branch immigration policy and could reopen doors for millions of visa applicants from affected countries.

## Event Details

### Core Content of the Ruling

According to NPR, the presiding judge detailed the policy's illegality in an 87-page opinion. The judge noted that the State Department "failed to provide reasonable national security justification" when implementing the visa freeze and "did not follow the public comment procedures required by the Administrative Procedure Act." More seriously, the policy "was based on nationality rather than individual risk assessment," constituting systematic discrimination against citizens of specific countries.

The opinion particularly emphasized that these 75 countries include several long-term U.S. allies, such as Costa Rica, Greece, and certain Southeast Asian nations. The judge questioned: "If these countries do not pose security threats, then a comprehensive ban based on nationality lacks legal basis."

### White House and State Department Response

President Trump quickly issued a statement after the ruling was announced, calling the judge's decision "absurd and dangerous" and stating the government would "immediately file an appeal." He accused the judge of "interfering with the President's constitutional power to protect the nation" and hinted at seeking Supreme Court intervention.

Secretary of State Rubio stated on social media that the State Department "respects judicial independence" but "strongly disagrees with the legal logic of this ruling." He emphasized that the visa freeze policy was "based on detailed security assessments" aimed at preventing "potential threats from entering the United States."

### Reactions from Affected Countries

The ruling triggered widespread celebration in affected countries. Costa Rica's Foreign Ministry issued a statement expressing "relief" at "the return of justice" and calling on the U.S. State Department to resume visa services as soon as possible. Iran's Foreign Ministry took a cautious stance, stating it would "observe the U.S. government's actual implementation actions."

The American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU), an immigrant rights organization, issued a statement calling it "a victory for common sense and the rule of law." The organization's chief attorney stated: "This ruling confirms a fundamental principle—immigration policy cannot be built on discrimination and prejudice."

## Panoramic Perspective

The far-reaching implications of this ruling extend well beyond the single policy itself. From a constitutional perspective, it redraws the boundaries between executive and judicial power in the immigration domain. The Trump administration attempted to expand executive discretion citing "national security," while the court's ruling makes clear that such power is not without limits. This precedent could provide legal precedent for future challenges to other immigration restriction measures.

From a diplomatic standpoint, the 75-country visa ban has seriously damaged U.S. relations with multiple nations. Many citizens from banned countries have legitimate business, educational, and family ties in the United States, and the ban has led to numerous family separations and lost business opportunities. Some countries have even considered implementing reciprocal restrictions on U.S. citizens, causing lasting damage to America's international image and soft power.

The economic impact is equally significant. According to estimates by the Migration Policy Institute, the visa ban has cost the U.S. tourism industry approximately $8 billion in revenue, caused higher education institutions to lose over 15,000 international students, and reduced annual tuition income by about $600 million. The technology sector has also been affected, with numerous H-1B visa applicants rejected due to nationality, exacerbating labor shortages in certain fields.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Views supporting the ruling** consider it an important victory for the rule of law. Harvard Law School professor Amy Wall noted: "This ruling confirms that even in the national security domain, executive power must be constrained by law. Collective punishment based on nationality violates the fundamental spirit of American law." Civil rights organizations praised the ruling, calling it "an important milestone in the fight against discriminatory policies."

**Views supporting the government's position** emphasize the priority of national security. The immigration reform organization FAIR issued a statement saying the ruling "undermines the President's ability to protect America." The organization believes that "preventive measures are necessary" given that terrorist threats remain severe in certain regions. Some members of Congress also expressed concern, arguing that courts "should not interfere with national security decisions."

**Centrist views** call for seeking a balanced approach. Former State Department officials suggest the government could achieve security objectives without implementing a comprehensive ban, through "strengthening individual case review" and "improving risk assessment mechanisms." This position holds that both national security protection and maintenance of America's open image and rule of law traditions are necessary.
