## Core Summary

Iran has launched large-scale retaliatory attacks on US military bases across multiple countries in the Middle East. Explosions were heard across Iran overnight, and neighboring Gulf states began reporting attacks. This marks the latest dramatic escalation in US-Iran tensions, following US strikes on Iranian targets and a naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz.

## Event Details

According to BBC reporting, Iran launched overnight retaliatory strikes against US bases in the Middle East, with multiple Gulf states reporting attacks. Explosions were heard across Iran, and the situation deteriorated rapidly over several hours.

This follows the US launching a new round of airstrikes on Iran and restoring its naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump had previously warned Tehran to "behave," but Iran chose a tit-for-tat confrontation path.

US Central Command stated it was assessing the attacks and reserved the right to respond further. No official reports of US casualties have been confirmed, but multiple bases were reportedly hit by missiles and drones.

The involvement of Gulf states complicates the situation further. These countries had previously tried to maintain balance between the US and Iran but now face the reality of conflict spreading to their territories.

## Panoramic Analysis

The spiraling escalation of US-Iran conflict is pushing the Middle East to the edge of full-scale confrontation. Iran's decision to launch direct retaliatory strikes on US bases marks a strategic shift from "strategic patience" to "active deterrence." The deeper reason lies in Tehran's assessment that continued concession would only invite greater pressure.

From a military perspective, the US maintains dozens of bases across the Middle East, distributed in Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, and the UAE. While these bases have layered defenses, questions remain about whether interception rates can be sustained against saturation attacks from Iran's large arsenal of ballistic and cruise missiles.

More concerning is the spillover effect. Gulf Cooperation Council members are being forced into great power competition, with their infrastructure and economic lifelines facing direct threats. Global energy markets have already reacted, with oil prices jumping during Asian trading. If the conflict persists, the 21 million barrels of crude oil transiting the Strait of Hormuz daily face serious risk.

## Multiple Perspectives

US officials emphasized that military operations aim to "protect international shipping safety" and "maintain regional stability," characterizing Iran's attacks on US bases as "unacceptable provocation." The Pentagon stated it was assessing damage and preserving response options.

Iran characterized its retaliatory operations as a "legitimate response to sovereignty violations." Tehran claimed strikes targeted only military objectives and hinted it would return to negotiations if the US stops attacks.

Gulf Cooperation Council members called for restraint from both sides, fearing conflict spread would severely damage regional economics and security. Saudi Arabia and the UAE are reportedly conducting active diplomatic mediation.

International energy market analysts warned that if the conflict disrupts Strait of Hormuz navigation, oil prices could surge above $100 per barrel, posing a major threat to the global economy.
