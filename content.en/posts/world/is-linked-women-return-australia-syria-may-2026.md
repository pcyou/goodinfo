## Background

The Australian government has confirmed that several women and children with links to the Islamic State (IS) group have returned to Australia from Syria. A group of nine women and their children has landed in Melbourne, while another woman and her child arrived in Sydney. This marks the latest development in Australia's gradual repatriation of citizens linked to IS.

## Years in Syrian Camps

These women and their children have spent years in camps in northern Syria following the military defeat of IS in 2019. Large numbers of foreign IS family members were stranded in camps in northeastern Syria, living in dire conditions amid an unstable security environment.

The Australian government had faced mounting domestic pressure to take action to repatriate its citizens. Officials stated that those returning would undergo security assessments and necessary monitoring measures.

## Security Concerns and Debate

The repatriation has sparked widespread debate within Australia. Supporters argue that these women and children deserve basic consular protection, and that many children born in camps should not bear the consequences of their parents' actions. Critics have raised security concerns, calling for strict monitoring and management of returnees.

The government emphasized that returnees will undergo comprehensive security screening, with measures applied based on assessment outcomes. Australian security agencies said they would closely monitor the individuals' subsequent activities.

## International Context

Australia is not alone in facing this challenge. Multiple Western nations, including the UK, France, and Germany, are grappling with the repatriation of their own IS-linked citizens from Syria. Some countries have taken a more cautious approach, including stripping citizenship to prevent return.

UN human rights officials have repeatedly called on governments to resettle their citizens stranded in Syrian camps, particularly children.
