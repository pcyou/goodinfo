## Summary

Europe is grappling with its most severe heatwave of the summer. After France recorded deadly temperatures, the heat dome is rapidly expanding eastward across Western Europe and is expected to reach Central and Eastern regions by the weekend. Multiple power outages across France have left thousands of households without electricity in 40-degree Celsius heat.

## Details

According to Reuters and BBC, the epicenter of this heatwave is shifting eastward from France. Southern and central France continue to endure extreme temperatures, with some areas exceeding 40 degrees Celsius. Meanwhile, power infrastructure failures under the strain of extreme heat have caused widespread blackouts.

Surging electricity demand as residents simultaneously use air conditioning and fans has placed enormous pressure on the grid. Combined with reduced transmission line efficiency in extreme heat, some areas have experienced outages lasting several hours. EDF has dispatched emergency repair crews, but restoration efforts face significant challenges in the extreme conditions.

Meteorological agencies predict the heatwave will affect broader areas of Western Europe on Wednesday, including southwestern Germany, Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg. By the weekend, the heat center may shift to Central Europe, with Poland, Czech Republic, and Austria potentially recording record temperatures.

Governments across the continent have issued heat warnings. France's Health Ministry has again urged elderly people, children, and those with chronic conditions to avoid going outside during peak heat hours, stay hydrated, and remain in cool environments. Multiple cities have opened air-conditioned cooling centers and increased patrols along waterways.

## Analysis

The persistence and intensity of this European heatwave has prompted deeper reflection on climate change impacts. Climatologists note that Europe is warming approximately 30 percent faster than the global average, making extreme heat events more frequent and severe. Long-term data from Météo-France shows that days exceeding 40 degrees Celsius have nearly doubled compared to the late 20th century.

From an infrastructure perspective, most European cities' power networks were designed in the mid-20th century without accounting for the load surges that extreme heat brings. As climate change drives up heatwave frequency, grid modernization has become an urgent priority. The International Energy Agency has previously warned that global cooling electricity demand could double by mid-century, posing unprecedented pressure on power systems.
