## Core Summary

According to BBC reports, a major fire broke out at a shoe manufacturing factory in Jinjiang City, Fujian Province, China on July 9, killing at least 28 people. Footage shows huge flames rising from a building and thick, black smoke rising into the sky. Jinjiang City, known as the "Shoe Capital of China," is an important global shoe manufacturing base, and this incident has sparked widespread attention to work safety in the manufacturing industry.

## Event Details

The fire broke out on the afternoon of July 9 local time, with the fire originating in a large shoe manufacturing factory. According to the local emergency management department, fire departments responded quickly after receiving the alarm, but due to the large amount of flammable raw materials stored in the factory, the fire spread extremely rapidly.

Footage videos circulated widely on social media, showing the entire building engulfed in flames with fire trucks operating intensively. As of now, the main fire has been basically extinguished, and search and rescue operations continue. The casualty count may be updated further.

Jinjiang City is an important manufacturing city on China's southeast coast, producing over 1 billion pairs of various shoes annually, with products exported to more than 100 countries and regions worldwide. The city is home to many well-known sports brands including Anta and Xtep, with a complete shoe manufacturing industry chain.

Following the incident, the local government activated the work safety accident emergency response plan and established an accident investigation team. The Ministry of Emergency Management has dispatched a working group to the scene to guide rescue and follow-up work.

## Panoramic Perspective

This fire incident once again highlights the severe challenges facing work safety in China's manufacturing industry. Despite significant government investment in work safety supervision in recent years, safety hazards in labor-intensive manufacturing remain prominent.

The shoe industry, due to its special production characteristics—extensive use of glue, solvents, synthetic materials, and other flammable substances—has always been a key focus of fire safety. Many small and medium-sized factories face issues such as aging fire protection facilities, obstructed safety passages, and insufficient employee safety training, all of which lay the groundwork for major accidents.

From an industry perspective, Jinjiang, as a key shoe export hub in China, may have a chain reaction effect on safety production inspections locally and nationwide. Relevant departments are expected to launch special actions nationwide to investigate safety hazards in labor-intensive manufacturing.

Notably, China's manufacturing industry is undergoing a critical transformation from "quantity expansion" to "quality improvement." Work safety is not only a humanitarian requirement but also an inherent need for industrial upgrading. Factories with low automation levels and poor working environments are more prone to safety accidents, which in turn accelerates the urgency of manufacturing automation transformation.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Official Statement**: The local government stated it will "thoroughly investigate the cause of the accident and hold those responsible accountable," and promised proper resettlement and compensation for the families of the deceased. The Ministry of Emergency Management required all industries nationwide to learn from this incident and strengthen safety hazard investigations.

**Media Commentary**: Xinhua News Agency published a commentary article pointing out that "work safety is a red line that cannot be crossed," calling on enterprises to earnestly fulfill their main responsibilities and not pursue profits at the expense of employee safety.

**Industry Reaction**: Multiple local Jinjiang enterprises stated they will immediately conduct internal safety inspections. Industry insiders point out that fire safety renovation costs for some old factories are high, and small and medium enterprises face a dilemma between operational pressure and safety investment.
