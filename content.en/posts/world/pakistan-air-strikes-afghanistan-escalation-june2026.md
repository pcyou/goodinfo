## Summary

Pakistan has conducted air strikes against multiple targets inside Afghanistan, with the Afghan Taliban government reporting dozens of casualties and condemning the action as "unforgivable aggression." This marks a major escalation following Pakistan's ground operation along the border on June 28, raising serious international concerns about stability in South Asia.

## Details

According to BBC reporting, Pakistan's military spokesperson confirmed the air strikes were in response to a series of security incidents within Pakistan's territory. Pakistan stated the targets were militant camps and logistics facilities in Afghanistan's eastern provinces, and the operation was based on precise intelligence aimed at eliminating direct threats to national security.

The Afghan Taliban government spokesperson strongly condemned the strikes at a press conference in Kabul, calling them a "blatant violation of Afghan sovereignty" and claiming dozens were killed, including civilians. The Taliban warned of "proportionate response at the appropriate time."

Notably, these air strikes came immediately after Pakistan completed a large-scale ground operation along the border. On June 28, Pakistani forces killed 29 militants in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province. The rapid succession of ground and air operations suggests Pakistan is adopting a significantly more aggressive posture on border security.

The UN Secretary-General's spokesperson has called on both sides to exercise restraint and resolve differences through dialogue. China's Foreign Ministry also expressed concern over developments, urging relevant parties to avoid actions that could further escalate the situation.

## Analysis

The sharp escalation in Pakistan-Afghanistan border security reflects a profound restructuring of South Asia's security landscape. Pakistan's consecutive ground and air strikes in a short period indicate its tolerance for cross-border militant activity has reached a new low. This strategic shift may be driven by rising domestic security pressure, as attacks on military, police, and civilians have increased in recent years, creating strong public demand for decisive action.

However, airstrikes on sovereign neighboring territory remain controversial under international law and plant seeds for long-term deterioration of bilateral relations. The Afghan Taliban government faces a dilemma: a strong military response could trigger full-scale border conflict, while silence could be criticized by domestic hardliners as weakness.

From a regional perspective, the Pakistan-Afghanistan escalation will directly affect China's push for regional connectivity projects, including the security of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor and the Trans-Afghan Railway plan. The international community needs to intensify mediation efforts to prevent both countries from sliding into full confrontation.
