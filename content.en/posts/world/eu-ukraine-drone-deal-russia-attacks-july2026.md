## Core Summary

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen visited Kyiv to sign an agreement expanding drone production with Ukraine. However, hours after the deal was signed, Russian forces launched one of the war's heaviest missile assaults on the capital, striking multiple districts. This event highlights the continued escalation of the Russia-Ukraine conflict.

## Event Details

According to Al Jazeera, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen met with Ukrainian leaders in Kyiv, where both sides signed a cooperation agreement to expand drone production. The agreement aims to enhance Ukraine's defense industrial capabilities through EU funding and technical support, accelerating large-scale drone manufacturing.

However, within hours of von der Leyen's departure from Kyiv, Russian forces launched a massive missile and drone attack on Ukraine's capital. According to JW.org, multiple districts of Kyiv were struck, including residential areas and infrastructure. This was one of the heaviest airstrikes since the war began, causing multiple casualties.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky condemned Russia's attack as an "act of terror" and called for Western allies to provide more air defense systems. He stated that the EU drone deal is "the right direction," but Ukraine needs faster and larger-scale military assistance.

The Russian Defense Ministry stated that the strikes were "precision operations" targeting Ukrainian military facilities, in response to Kyiv's refusal to engage in peace negotiations.

## Panoramic Analysis

The EU's drone agreement marks a strategic shift in Western military assistance to Ukraine from "providing weapons" to "joint production." This deep cooperation not only enhances Ukraine's defensive capabilities but also promotes European defense industry integration. In the long term, this could give rise to a more unified European defense industrial system, reducing dependence on the United States.

However, Russia's immediate large-scale airstrikes following the agreement signing clearly signal to Kyiv and Brussels that military aid cannot alter battlefield dynamics. This "fight while talking" pattern may become the new normal in the conflict, making prospects for peace negotiations even more remote.

From a technical perspective, drones have become core equipment in modern warfare. The Russia-Ukraine conflict has been called a "drone war," with both sides extensively using various drones for reconnaissance, strikes, and electronic warfare. The EU agreement will help Ukraine establish domestic drone production capabilities, which not only meets current war needs but also lays the foundation for Ukraine's post-war defense industry.

For the global geopolitical landscape, the EU's deep involvement means the Russia-Ukraine conflict is no longer just a US-Russia game, but has evolved into a broader "West versus Russia" confrontation. This may further solidify global camp divisions and increase the risk of conflict escalation.

## Multiple Perspectives

The EU emphasizes this is a concrete action to "support Ukraine's right to self-defense," demonstrating that Europe will not abandon Kyiv due to war fatigue. Von der Leyen stated that the EU will "stand with Ukraine until peace is achieved."

Russia warns that Western weapons aid will only prolong the conflict, calling this move a direct threat to Russian security. A Kremlin spokesperson stated that NATO and EU involvement is pushing Europe toward "direct confrontation."

Ukraine welcomes the aid but calls for more air defense systems. Zelensky emphasized that the drone agreement is "an important step," but Ukraine needs advanced air defense weapons capable of intercepting Russian missiles, such as Patriot systems.
