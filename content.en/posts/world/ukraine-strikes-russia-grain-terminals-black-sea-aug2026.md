## Core Summary

Ukraine has struck major Russian grain export terminals on the Black Sea coast, damaging key facilities used to ship wheat, corn, and sunflower oil to global markets. According to the BBC, grain exports from both Russia and Ukraine are falling as both countries attack each other's shipping facilities and ports.

## Details

The attacks targeted critical Russian grain export hubs on the Black Sea coast. These terminals are key nodes for Russian grain exports to Middle Eastern, African, and Asian markets. Following the strikes, some loading equipment was damaged and operations were disrupted.

Meanwhile, Russia has also intensified strikes on Ukrainian Black Sea ports. Cities like Odesa and Mykolaiv have been frequently hit by missiles and drones, with grain storage and transport infrastructure repeatedly damaged.

Data shows grain export volumes from both countries have declined significantly. Together, the two nations account for nearly 30% of global wheat exports, and the simultaneous degradation of their export capacity directly impacts global food supply-demand balance.

The UN Food and Agriculture Organization has repeatedly warned that Black Sea grain transport security is critical for global food security, particularly for Middle Eastern and North African countries that depend on Black Sea grain imports.

## Panorama Analysis

This mutual targeting of grain exports reflects the evolution of the Russia-Ukraine conflict into a broader economic war. Both sides recognize that degrading the other's grain export capacity not only damages economic revenue but also creates leverage on the international stage.

The impact on global food security should not be underestimated. World Food Programme data shows hundreds of millions face food insecurity globally, and the Black Sea region is one of the world's most important grain supply sources. When both major exporters see their capacity damaged simultaneously, international grain prices inevitably face upward pressure, with the most vulnerable countries and populations hit first.

From a geopolitical perspective, this situation also pressures Black Sea coastal nations like Turkey, which played a key role in mediating the Russia-Ukraine grain deal. But with both sides attacking port facilities, the original "Black Sea Grain Initiative" framework has effectively collapsed. The international community urgently needs new mechanisms to ensure the safety of grain transport corridors.

## Perspectives

**Russia** has described the strikes on its terminals as "terrorist attacks" and vowed to take all necessary measures to protect critical infrastructure. Moscow also accused Ukraine of trying to destabilize global grain markets by targeting civilian facilities.

**Ukraine** frames the strikes as legitimate targeting of Russia's military-economic system, arguing that Russian grain export revenue directly funds its war machine. Kyiv maintains that all targets related to the Russian economy are legitimate until the war ends.

**International grain traders** have expressed serious concern. They note that damage to export terminals affects not just current shipments but requires months to repair, meaning global grain supply tightness could persist into next year.
