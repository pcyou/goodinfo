## Core Summary

Iran's Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was laid to rest in the Imam Reza Shrine in Mashhad on July 10, concluding six days of national mourning. According to BBC reports, hundreds of thousands of mourners gathered in the streets of Mashhad for the solemn funeral. Khamenei's death marks the beginning of a crucial political transition in Iran, as the selection of a new Supreme Leader will profoundly impact Iran's domestic and foreign policy direction, particularly amid tense US-Iran relations and ongoing regional conflicts.

## Event Details

According to BBC's Chief International Correspondent, Khamenei's funeral took place in Mashhad, Iran's second-largest city and the holiest Shia Muslim site. The funeral lasted several hours, with crowds flooding the streets from across the nation.

Khamenei served as Iran's Supreme Leader since 1989, ruling for 37 years and making him the longest-serving Supreme Leader in the history of the Islamic Republic of Iran. Under his leadership, Iran navigated multiple major international crises, including nuclear negotiations, confrontations with the West, and escalating tensions with the United States and Israel in recent years.

Currently, Iran's Assembly of Experts is deliberating the election of a new Supreme Leader. According to Iran's constitution, the Assembly of Experts is responsible for selecting and supervising the Supreme Leader. Observers are closely watching who will assume this critical position and how the new leadership will address multiple challenges including confrontation with the US, regional proxy wars, and domestic economic difficulties.

## Panoramic Perspective

Khamenei's death and burial mark Iran's entry into a transitional period filled with uncertainty. From a geopolitical perspective, as a major regional power, changes in Iran's leadership will directly affect the regional balance of power. Iran-backed regional armed forces (such as Hezbollah in Lebanon, Houthi rebels in Yemen, and Shia militias in Iraq) will face strategic adjustments.

From the perspective of US-Iran relations, both sides are currently in a state of high tension. The Trump administration recently reiterated that "the ceasefire is over" while simultaneously stating it would continue negotiations. Against this backdrop, the new Iranian leadership's attitude toward the US will be a critical variable. If the new leader adopts a more hardline stance, it could escalate regional conflict risks; if inclined toward pragmatic diplomacy, it may provide a window for de-escalation.

From Iran's domestic political perspective, the transition of Supreme Leaders may trigger power redistribution. The competition between the Revolutionary Guard, moderate reformists, and the religious establishment will intensify. The livelihood difficulties under economic sanctions may also become an important factor influencing political direction.
