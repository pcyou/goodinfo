**[Summary]**

The White House announced that US President Trump, Russian President Putin, and Chinese President Xi Jinping will hold a historic trilateral meeting next week during the APEC summit in Shenzhen, focusing on global nuclear weapons reduction. This marks the first such three-way talks between the leaders of the world's largest nuclear powers.

**[Details]**

According to White House spokesperson Olivia Wales, the unprecedented meeting will take place as Trump attends the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen. She stated that the president would discuss nuclear weapons reduction, which he believes poses significant dangers due to proliferation concerns.

Iran may also be discussed during the meeting, but Ukraine is not on the agenda. Moscow and Beijing have yet to comment. Senior US officials indicated that no agreement is expected from the talks.

Trump will arrive in Shenzhen on Wednesday evening for the two-day summit and will also hold bilateral talks with Xi on Thursday—their fourth meeting since Trump returned to office in 2025.

**[Perspective]**

This trilateral meeting represents a significant shift in international geopolitical dynamics. From a historical perspective, it is extremely rare for the three nations with the largest nuclear arsenals to sit at the same negotiating table, reflecting the complex evolution of current global power structures.

From a nuclear disarmament standpoint, the Trump administration's initiative faces multiple challenges. While Russia and China remain skeptical of US strategic intentions, all three countries continue to invest in nuclear modernization. Observers note that even without concrete agreements, such high-level dialogue holds important symbolic value in easing tensions and establishing communication channels.

The meeting's location in the Asia-Pacific region underscores the area's central role in global strategy. However, the absence of Ukraine from the agenda indicates that European security issues will remain a major obstacle in US-Russia relations.

Experts generally believe that while such high-level contacts may not immediately resolve deep-seated disagreements, they provide necessary diplomatic space to prevent miscalculation and conflict escalation. How parties follow up on this meeting in the coming months will be an important indicator of international relations trends.
