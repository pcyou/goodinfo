# North Korea Adds Second Naval Destroyer as US, South Korea and Japan Hold Military Exercises

## Core Summary

According to the Associated Press, North Korea formally commissioned its second domestically produced guided-missile destroyer into active naval service on September 7. The 5,000-ton-class warship is capable of launching ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and air defense missiles. North Korean state media broadcast footage of the commissioning ceremony. Simultaneously, the United States, South Korea, and Japan are conducting the annual "Trident" joint naval exercises in waters around the Korean Peninsula, deploying more than 20 vessels including aircraft carriers and nuclear submarines. Military tensions on the peninsula continue to escalate.

## Event Details

**Technical Specifications of the New Ship**: Based on footage released by Korean Central Television, the newly commissioned destroyer bears a strong resemblance to North Korea's first destroyer, the "Choe Hyon," with a displacement of approximately 5,000 tons. It is equipped with vertical launch systems, air defense radars, and anti-ship missile launchers. The Academy of National Defense Science claimed the vessel "possesses dual capabilities for ballistic missile interception and ground attack." Military analysts believe its combat capabilities approach some metrics of South Korea's Sejong the Great-class destroyers.

**Commissioning Ceremony**: North Korean leader Kim Jong Un personally attended the commissioning ceremony and delivered a speech. He stated that the Korean People's Navy is accelerating its modernization, and the batch construction of new surface warships will enable North Korea to "counter any maritime threat in coastal waters." Kim emphasized that North Korea's defense modernization "is not aimed at any specific country" but "must be prepared to respond to all threats."

**U.S.-Japan-South Korea Exercises**: Almost simultaneously, the U.S. "Ronald Reagan" carrier strike group, South Korea's "Dokdo" amphibious assault ship, and Japan's "Izumo" helicopter destroyer are conducting joint exercises in waters east of the Korean Peninsula. Exercises include anti-submarine warfare, air defense, maritime blockade, and humanitarian rescue drills. The three nations deployed over 10,000 personnel — the largest joint drill of 2026.

**Reactions from All Sides**: South Korea's Ministry of National Defense issued a statement strongly condemning North Korea's military provocations and warning that "any act threatening South Korean national security will be met with resolute counteraction." Japan's Defense Minister stated at a press conference that "North Korea's missile and nuclear weapons development poses a serious threat to Japan's security," and that the U.S.-Japan alliance will take all necessary measures to maintain regional peace. U.S. Indo-Pacific Command emphasized that the U.S. military deployment on the Korean Peninsula is "defensive" and aimed at maintaining regional stability.

**International Community Reactions**: Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson expressed hope that all relevant parties would "remain calm and restrained and avoid taking actions that could escalate tensions." Russia's Foreign Ministry issued a statement criticizing the U.S.-Japan-South Korea joint exercises as "destructive acts to peace and stability on the Korean Peninsula." The UN Security Council has previously held multiple emergency meetings on North Korean missile tests but has failed to reach consensus due to deep divisions.

## Panoramic Analysis

North Korea's commissioning of its second destroyer, coupled with the U.S.-Japan-South Korea joint exercises, marks a new "qualitative change" phase in military dynamics on the Korean Peninsula. On the surface, this appears to be routine military development by North Korea, but it in substance reflects a profound reshaping of the Northeast Asian geopolitical landscape.

**First, North Korean naval modernization has achieved a real breakthrough**. For a long time, the Korean People's Navy has been equipped primarily with small patrol boats and submarines, lacking blue-water combat capability. The commissioning of a 5,000-ton-class destroyer means North Korea now possesses, for the first time, a surface combat platform that can "rival" South Korean main combatants. This not only enhances North Korea's coastal defense capability but also provides a technical foundation for future development of aircraft carriers and other large surface warships.

**Second, U.S.-Japan-South Korea military integration is accelerating**. The scale of these trilateral exercises, the advanced level of equipment deployed, and the combat-realistic nature of the drills have all reached the highest level in recent years. This reflects America's transformation of its Asia-Pacific alliance system from "primarily bilateral" to "multilateral coordination." The improvement in Korea-Japan relations has also provided a political foundation for this multilateral coordination. However, this trend will further stimulate North Korea to accelerate military modernization, creating a vicious cycle of "security dilemma."

**Third, the risk of a Northeast Asian arms race is rising significantly**. The commissioning of North Korea's new ship is just one microcosm of its military modernization. Simultaneously, North Korea is increasing investment in ballistic missiles, submarine-launched missiles, and nuclear weapon miniaturization. South Korea and Japan, with U.S. support, are also accelerating the deployment of THAAD systems, developing hypersonic weapons, and expanding naval combat capabilities. Without effective management of this multilateral arms race, Northeast Asia could slide into a Cold War-era "balance of terror" state.

**Fourth, the Korean Peninsula issue is increasingly being viewed as a "secondary battlefield" of great-power competition**. The Korean Peninsula issue has long transcended regional concerns, becoming a strategic intersection of the U.S., China, Russia, Japan, and South Korea. The U.S. consolidates its military presence in Asia-Pacific by strengthening the U.S.-Japan-South Korea alliance; China and Russia maintain their strategic interests by supporting North Korea and balancing against U.S.-Japan-South Korea. Every North Korean military action triggers chain reactions from regional and global powers.

From a deeper perspective, the development of the Korean Peninsula situation is, in essence, the product of the overlap of Cold War legacies and new geopolitical competition. In the absence of effective multilateral security mechanisms and a serious lack of mutual trust, any unilateral action by one side will be perceived as a threat by others, triggering more intense confrontation. The commissioning of North Korea's second destroyer and the U.S.-Japan-South Korea exercises are just another concentrated eruption of this deep-seated contradiction. To truly achieve peace on the peninsula, what is needed is not more armaments but for all parties to return to the dialogue table and address fundamental security concerns through political means.

## Multiple Perspectives

**North Korea**: The Ministry of National Defense spokesperson issued a statement calling the commissioning of the new ship "a legitimate act within North Korea's sovereignty" and that "no external force has the right to interfere." The Foreign Ministry warned that the U.S.-Japan-South Korea joint exercises are "serious war provocations" and that North Korea "will take all necessary measures to respond."

**South Korea**: The Blue House issued a statement strongly condemning North Korea's military provocation, emphasizing that South Korea "will maintain close cooperation with the United States and Japan to firmly safeguard peace and stability on the Korean Peninsula." The Ministry of National Defense announced it will accelerate deployment of the "Korean Three-Axis System," including the Kill Chain, Korean Air and Missile Defense, and the Korea Massive Punishment and Retaliation plan.

**United States**: The State Department spokesperson reaffirmed that the U.S. defense commitment to South Korea is "ironclad" and that the U.S. will "take all necessary measures" to maintain regional peace. The White House National Security Council said it is closely monitoring North Korean military activities and maintaining close coordination with allies.

**Japan**: The Prime Minister's Office issued a statement expressing "grave concern" over North Korea's military modernization and emphasized that "Japan's defense forces are entering a new era." The Ministry of Defense announced it will deploy the improved Type 12 surface-to-ship missile ahead of schedule within fiscal year 2026 to strengthen the defense of the southwestern islands.

**International Organizations and Neutral States**: UN Secretary-General António Guterres called on all parties to "exercise maximum restraint and resolve differences through dialogue." The ASEAN chair issued a statement expressing "deep concern" over Korean Peninsula tensions and calling on all parties "to avoid unilateral actions that could exacerbate tensions."
