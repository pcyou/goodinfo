## Core Summary

The United States experienced a major storm system during the Thanksgiving holiday, with strong winds and heavy rain battering the eastern and midwestern regions. The severe weather led to over 460 flight cancellations and nearly one million households losing power. New Jersey and New York declared states of emergency, and New York City's famous Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed until Saturday.

## Event Details

The so-called "Thanksgiving Storm" swept through the eastern and midwestern United States on November 26, bringing winds of up to 70 miles per hour (approximately 113 kilometers per hour) and torrential rain. According to FlightAware, a flight tracking website, 460 flights were cancelled within the US on Thursday, with an additional 2,508 flights experiencing delays.

The hardest-hit states included Pennsylvania, Maryland, Virginia, and New Jersey. By Thursday evening, nearly one million households across the country had lost power, with these four states being most severely affected. Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro stated that utility companies were working tirelessly to restore power, but energy companies in Virginia and New Jersey warned that some areas might take days to recover, as crews needed to wait for safe conditions before accessing damaged areas.

### State Responses

New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill declared a state of emergency for the entire state, with widespread flooding reported in several communities along the Jersey Shore. New York Governor Kathy Hochul also declared a state of emergency for New York City, Long Island, and Westchester County. Due to the storm, New York City's annual Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed from Thursday to Saturday.

In Maryland, strong winds and large waves led to the closure of several bridges, while Delaware's beaches suffered from seawater intrusion, with water spilling onto the boardwalk. Washington DC experienced its wettest Thanksgiving on record, with rising tidal waters flooding runways at Reagan Washington National Airport.

### Federal Government Response

President Donald Trump stated on Thursday afternoon that he would consider releasing emergency funds for the east coast facing the holiday storm. The Federal Aviation Administration had previously warned that flight delays were possible "throughout the week" due to forecasted rain and wind.

Meanwhile, the US West Coast also faces threats from severe weather. The National Weather Service has warned that a series of Pacific storms are expected to impact the western region.

## Comprehensive Perspective

The impact of this Thanksgiving storm extends far beyond a simple weather event, exposing the vulnerability of US infrastructure in the face of extreme climate conditions. As climate change leads to more frequent extreme weather events, flood control facilities, power grid systems, and transportation networks in the eastern coastal regions urgently need upgrading and reinforcement.

From an economic perspective, Thanksgiving is one of the most important consumer holidays in the US. Travel disruptions and retail activity impediments caused by the storm may negatively affect fourth-quarter economic data. It is estimated that over 80 million Americans planned to travel during this year's Thanksgiving period. Flight delays and cancellations not only affect passenger experiences but also cause direct economic losses to airlines and related service industries.

Furthermore, this event highlights the importance of emergency management systems. The rapid declaration of states of emergency and resource coordination by various states demonstrates the critical role of local governments in disaster response under the US federal system. However, the reality that power restoration may take days also reminds people that individual and community-level emergency preparedness is equally indispensable in the face of extreme weather.
