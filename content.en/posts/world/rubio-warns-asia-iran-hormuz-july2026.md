## Core Summary

According to the Associated Press, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned leaders across Asia during his tour that Iran's demands in the Strait of Hormuz, if accepted, would set a "dangerous precedent" for international shipping security. This statement underscores US concerns about energy corridor security in the Middle East and reflects escalating US-Iran tensions.

## Event Details

### Diplomatic Warning

During his multi-day Asian tour, Rubio held meetings with senior officials from Japan, South Korea, India, and other countries. According to sources familiar with the discussions, Rubio repeatedly emphasized freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz, warning that Iran's demands "concern not just regional security but the stability of global energy supply chains."

The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most critical oil transit corridors, with approximately 20 million barrels of crude oil passing through daily. Any threat to freedom of navigation in the strait would directly impact global energy markets and economic development.

### Iran's Position

Iran has previously indicated it may need to reassess its navigation policy in the Strait of Hormuz due to changing regional security conditions. Iranian officials suggested that if oil export restrictions are further tightened, Tehran might take "reciprocal measures" affecting other countries' shipping activities.

This position immediately drew significant international attention. The US and its allies believe Iran's demands essentially "weaponize" international waterways to gain negotiating leverage.

### US Response

President Trump has previously stated clearly that the US will respond "quickly and forcefully" to any hostile Iranian actions in the Strait of Hormuz. The Pentagon has also announced enhanced naval deployments in the region to ensure freedom of navigation.

Rubio's Asian tour aims to secure regional support for the US position. He stated that maintaining freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz is "a shared responsibility of the international community" requiring coordinated responses.

## Panoramic Perspective

The deeper significance of Rubio's warning lies in revealing a core contradiction in contemporary international relations: the tension between control of critical waterways and global commons governance.

**First, energy security vulnerability exposed.** Although the global energy structure is transitioning, oil and gas will remain primary energy sources in the short to medium term. The security of critical waterways like the Strait of Hormuz and the Strait of Malacca directly relates to national economic lifelines. Iran's demands expose vulnerable links in global energy supply chains.

**Second, international maritime law enforcement dilemma.** The UN Convention on the Law of the Sea establishes the "transit passage" principle, but in practice, conflicts of interest between coastal states and user states are common. When coastal states believe their security interests are harmed, they may take unilateral actions challenging existing rules, while the international community lacks effective enforcement mechanisms.

**Third, great power competition becomes proxy-based.** The US-Iran confrontation in the Strait of Hormuz is actually a microcosm of broader geopolitical competition. Through enhanced naval deployments and diplomatic coordination, the US attempts to maintain its dominant regional order; Iran seeks negotiating leverage through "asymmetric strategy." This competition pattern may repeat in other critical waterways.

**Fourth, regional countries' strategic choices.** Asian energy-consuming countries face difficult choices: dependent on Middle Eastern energy imports on one hand, unwilling to pick sides in great power competition on the other. Rubio's warning essentially demands these countries take clear positions, potentially increasing regional diplomatic complexity.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US Position**: Rubio emphasized that "maintaining freedom of navigation is the shared responsibility of the international community," calling on regional countries to "stand together to meet the challenge."

**Iran Position**: Tehran stated its policy adjustments are "legitimate responses to unjust sanctions," emphasizing "the right to protect its security interests."

**Asian Countries' Reactions**: Japan's Foreign Ministry expressed "high concern about the Strait of Hormuz situation," emphasizing that "freedom of navigation is crucial to Japan's energy security." India stated it "hopes to resolve differences through dialogue."

**International Law Experts**: Multiple international law scholars pointed out that unilaterally changing navigation rules for critical waterways "violates fundamental principles of international law," while also acknowledging "limitations in existing enforcement mechanisms."

**Energy Market Analysis**: The International Energy Agency warned that Strait of Hormuz tensions have already increased oil price volatility, and further escalation could have "significant impact" on global economic recovery.
