**[Summary]** At least 11 people have died and 19 are missing in a wildfire in Almeria, southern Spain, with 6 injured. Some victims are believed to be British nationals. Around 150 firefighters are battling the blaze, with over 1,000 residents evacuated. The tragedy comes amid a sustained heatwave across Southern Europe, with France and Portugal also facing severe fire conditions.

**[Event Details]**

According to the BBC, the fire broke out near Los Gallardos in the province of Almeria, Andalusia. Victims' bodies were found in and around the small village of Bedar. Andalusia's health and emergencies minister Antonio Sanz described the fire as complex and rapid. Four victims found trapped in a car were driving a right-hand drive vehicle, suggesting they were British nationals.

Witnesses reported the fire may have been caused by a downed power line before spreading to wooded areas. Authorities have not confirmed the cause. Spain's Military Emergency Unit (UME) has been deployed to assist firefighting efforts.

Andalusia regional president Juanma Moreno wrote on X: "Our hearts are heavy and we are devastated by grief." Among the injured, one person was hospitalized with smoke inhalation and another suffered burns.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**

This fire underscores the profound impact of climate change on the Mediterranean region. Data shows Europe is the fastest warming continent, heating up twice as fast as the global average. In 2025, Spain experienced its worst wildfire season on record, with 393,000 hectares burned - more than six times the 2006-2024 average. The EU as a whole suffered its most devastating fire season since records began in 2006, with over 1 million hectares consumed.

Research by the World Weather Attribution group at Imperial College London has directly linked the worsening Mediterranean fire season to climate change. Experts warn that as global temperatures continue to rise, Europe will face increasingly frequent and intense wildfire threats, making preventive infrastructure investment and strengthened cross-border firefighting cooperation urgently necessary.

**[Multiple Perspectives]**

Spanish PM Pedro Sanchez had previously announced the deployment of the largest-ever summer wildfire response force. However, critics point out that despite improved warning systems, grassroots emergency response capacity remains inadequate, particularly in evacuation notices and information delivery for foreign tourists. Climate scientists emphasize that firefighting alone is insufficient - reducing carbon emissions at the source and adjusting land use strategies are essential.
