# Ukraine Launches Third Strike on Russian Oil Refinery, Triggering Major Fire and Evacuations

On April 28, 2026, Ukraine's military confirmed it launched a third drone strike on the Tuapse oil refinery in Russia's Krasnodar region, triggering a large-scale fire. Over 160 firefighters were deployed to the scene, and local residents were evacuated.

## Rescue in "Extremely Difficult Conditions"

Krasnodar regional governor Veniamin Kondratyev said on Telegram on Tuesday that firefighters and other rescue workers were operating in "extremely difficult conditions," describing their efforts as "true heroism."

No casualties have been reported so far. However, the thick smoke and toxic gas emissions from the fire have raised health concerns among local residents.

## Previous Strikes Caused Severe Pollution

Earlier strikes on the Tuapse refinery over the past two weeks have already caused significant collateral damage. The previous attacks resulted in a major oil spill into the sea, with residents reporting "black" rain falling on the city and leaving oily residue everywhere.

These environmental consequences highlight the profound civilian impact that military operations against energy infrastructure can have.

## The Kremlin's Response

The Kremlin accused Ukraine of "further increasing the oil shortage in global energy markets... and provoking further destabilization." Russian officials claimed that these strikes not only target Russia's energy infrastructure but are also intended to pressure Russia's economic allies by disrupting global oil supplies.

## Ukraine's Position

Ukraine's military confirmed the latest drone strike, stating that such refineries "help fund Russia's full-scale invasion launched in 2022." Kyiv considers striking Russia's energy production and export capabilities a key strategy to weaken its war economy.

This is part of Ukraine's ongoing escalation of attacks on Russian energy infrastructure in 2026. Since the beginning of the year, Ukraine has repeatedly used long-range drones to strike refineries, oil storage facilities, and pipelines across Russian territory.

## Global Energy Market Impact

Analysts note that Russia is one of the world's largest oil exporters, and continued damage to its refining capacity could have ripple effects on global oil supply. While no major supply disruption has occurred so far, markets remain highly alert to the potential risks posed by these attacks.
