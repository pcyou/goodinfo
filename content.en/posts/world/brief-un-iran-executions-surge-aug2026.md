## [Brief] UN Rights Chief Alarmed by Rise in Iran Executions Since March

**[Core Summary]** UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk issued a statement on August 5 expressing "serious concern" over the significant increase in executions in Iran since March. The statement noted that 56 people have been executed on national security-related charges, including 27 in cases linked to January's protests.

### Event Details

According to BBC reports, Türk stated at a UN Human Rights Council meeting in Geneva that Iran's execution pace is "alarming." He noted that many of those executed were convicted after trials that "did not meet international standards," with some cases involving allegations of "confessions extracted through torture."

The Iranian government has not yet issued a formal response to this statement. Iran's judiciary previously stated that all executions were "strictly conducted in accordance with the law," with those executed convicted of "serious crimes including endangering national security, terrorism, and drug trafficking."

### Panoramic Perspective

Iran's death penalty issue has long been a focal point of international human rights controversy. According to Amnesty International data, Iran has long been one of the world's top executing countries, second only to China (whose death penalty data is treated as a state secret).

In September 2022, large-scale protests erupted in Iran (triggered by the death of 22-year-old Mahsa Amini while in morality police custody), followed by a severe crackdown on demonstrators by Iranian authorities. Human rights organizations reported hundreds arrested and dozens sentenced to death and rapidly executed.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Human Rights Organization Position**: An Amnesty International Middle East researcher stated: "Iran uses the death penalty as a tool of political repression, a blatant violation of basic human rights. The international community must take stronger action."

**Iran's Official Position**: Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson previously stated the judicial system "operates independently" and execution decisions are "based entirely on legal evidence." Iran considers external criticism "interference in internal affairs."
