# Marine Le Pen Found Guilty but Court Clears Way for Presidential Run with Electronic Tag

## Core Summary

A French court has ruled that far-right National Rally leader Marine Le Pen is guilty of EU fund misuse, but in an unprecedented decision, allowed her to run for the next presidential election while wearing an electronic monitoring tag. This surprising ruling has sparked widespread debate across French politics and the legal community.

## Event Details

According to BBC, after a lengthy trial, the French court ultimately ruled Le Pen guilty of misusing EU funds during her tenure as a Member of European Parliament. She was accused of using European Parliament assistant salary funds for National Rally's domestic party activities, involving hundreds of thousands of euros.

Despite the guilty verdict, the court issued a compromise sentence: allowing Le Pen to wear an electronic monitoring tag rather than serve prison time, while preserving her political right to run for president. This means Le Pen can still campaign for France's highest office while being subject to electronic monitoring in her daily movements.

Le Pen's legal team expressed partial satisfaction with the ruling, arguing that the decision to allow her to run while wearing a tag upheld the defendant's political rights. However, opponents consider the ruling too lenient, arguing it undermines judicial seriousness.

## Panoramic Perspective

This ruling has virtually no precedent in French constitutional history, sparking deep discussion about whether convicted political figures should retain eligibility for election. France's legal tradition emphasizes the moral responsibility of political figures while also protecting citizens' political participation rights. The court's compromise ruling attempts to find balance between the two, but its effectiveness remains to be seen.
