## Core Summary

According to BBC News, the U.S. representative walked out of a UN Security Council meeting during France's remarks, drawing widespread international attention. The direct trigger for this diplomatic incident was the United States joining North Korea and Russia in voting against extending the term of the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights. This rare move has heightened tensions between the U.S. and its traditional European allies, and underscored deepening divisions within the multilateral diplomatic framework.

## Event Details

BBC reported that during a formal UN Security Council meeting, the U.S. delegation collectively exited the chamber as the French representative was speaking. This move is extremely rare in diplomatic settings and is seen as a strong signal of deteriorating relations between the U.S. and traditional allies like France.

The immediate cause of the incident was the Security Council vote on extending the term of the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights. The United States, along with North Korea and Russia, voted against the proposal, which consequently failed to pass. France, as a key advocate of human rights issues, expressed strong dissatisfaction with the result and delivered a sharply worded speech at the meeting. It was during the French representative's remarks that the U.S. delegation chose to leave.

Diplomatic analysts note that the U.S. voting position this time has sparked widespread controversy. Standing with North Korea and Russia is extremely rare in U.S. diplomatic history, calling into question U.S. credibility among traditional allies. French officials subsequently stated that this move reflects the U.S.'s continued hostility toward multilateral human rights mechanisms.

The UN Secretary-General expressed regret over the voting result, calling on all parties to resolve differences through dialogue. The EU High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy issued a statement expressing deep concern over the failure to extend the High Commissioner's term, emphasizing that human rights protection is the cornerstone of the international order.

The U.S. State Department later responded that the U.S. has fundamental disagreements with the direction of UN human rights mechanism reform, believing the existing mechanism suffers from inefficiency and political bias in certain areas. However, this explanation did not quell criticism from allies.

This incident occurs at a time of profound adjustment in the global geopolitical landscape. The authority and effectiveness of multilateral institutions are facing challenges from various quarters, and coordination among major powers is becoming increasingly difficult. The internal unity of the Security Council, as the core decision-making body for international security affairs, is crucial for addressing global challenges.

## Panoramic Perspective

The U.S. walkout from the Security Council meeting reflects the deep crisis currently facing the international multilateral system. The UN Security Council, as the core of the international security architecture established after World War II, relies on basic consensus among the five permanent members for effective operation. When disagreements among major powers expand from the policy level to procedural and ceremonial levels, it means the international coordination mechanism is under unprecedented pressure.

The result of the Human Rights Commissioner term vote is particularly noteworthy. The combination of the U.S. voting with North Korea and Russia breaks the unity of the traditional Western camp and also reflects the current U.S. foreign policy's fundamental questioning of multilateral human rights mechanisms. This shift in position is not accidental but a continuation of the U.S.'s evolving attitude toward the UN system in recent years.

From the perspective of transatlantic relations, this incident adds new fractures to U.S.-Europe relations. France, as a core EU member state and permanent Security Council member, having its representative's speech met with the U.S. delegation's departure is a breach of diplomatic etiquette with symbolic significance. European allies may reassess the extent to which they can rely on U.S. participation in multilateral framework cooperation.

The credibility of multilateral institutions faces dual challenges. On one hand, selective participation by major powers undermines their authority; on the other hand, the practice of walking out in protest rather than seeking compromise when voting results don't align with certain major powers' interests further erodes the decision-making effectiveness of international organizations. If this trend continues, it could lead to fragmentation of the global governance system and increase the difficulty of addressing transnational challenges.

From a historical perspective, the current multilateral diplomatic dilemma bears some resemblance to the League of Nations predicament between the two world wars. Major powers withdrawing from or not participating in key international mechanisms often leads to diminished collective action capacity and increased regional conflict risks. The international community needs to seriously consider how to maintain the basic framework of multilateral cooperation while respecting national sovereignty.
