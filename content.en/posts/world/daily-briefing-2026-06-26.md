### 1. Venezuela Twin Earthquakes Kill at Least 235, Devastating Caribbean Nation

Venezuela was struck by two shallow earthquakes above magnitude 7.0 within seconds of each other, killing at least 235 people and injuring over 4,300. The capital Caracas suffered the worst damage, with multiple buildings collapsing into rubble. Rescue teams continue searching for survivors in the debris. The disaster strikes at a critical political transition period, less than six months after former leader Maduro was taken into U.S. custody, testing the new government's crisis management capabilities.

---

### 2. Iran Strikes Cargo Ship on UN-Backed Route in Strait of Hormuz

Iran fired an "unidentified projectile" at a cargo vessel operating on a UN-supported evacuation route in the Strait of Hormuz. The attack forced the United Nations to suspend its ship evacuation operations in the strait. International oil prices surged in response. This marks the latest escalation in Middle East tensions, threatening one of the world's most critical maritime chokepoints through which roughly 20% of global oil supply passes daily.

---

### 3. Apple Slashes MacBook and iPad Prices After Chip Cost Hike

Apple announced across-the-board price increases for MacBook and iPad lines, with some models rising nearly 20%, citing surging chip and memory costs driven by the AI boom. Following the announcement, Apple stock posted its worst single-day decline in over a year. Microsoft subsequently raised Xbox console prices as well, signaling a broader industry cost cycle driven by AI computing demand.

---

### 4. JPMorgan Leadership Shakeup Reshapes CEO Succession Race

JPMorgan Chase underwent a major leadership overhaul: potential CEO successor Marianne Lake announced her retirement, while Daniel Petno and Mary Callahan Erdoes were appointed as new co-presidents. The move fundamentally reshaped the succession competition under CEO Dimon, with the bank signaling a shift toward its commercial banking and wealth management strengths.

---

### 5. White House Asks OpenAI to Delay New Model Release

The White House has reportedly asked OpenAI to postpone the release of its latest AI model on safety grounds. This marks the first time the U.S. government has directly intervened in a commercial AI company's product launch timeline, signaling a new phase in AI regulation. The move reflects growing tension between rapid AI advancement and national security concerns.
