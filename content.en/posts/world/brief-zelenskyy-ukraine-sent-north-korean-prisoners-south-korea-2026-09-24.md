## Core Summary

AP News reports that Ukrainian President Zelenskyy has announced that Ukraine transferred two captured North Korean soldiers to South Korea, an unusual diplomatic move at the intersection of inter-Korean relations and the Russia-Ukraine war.

## Event Details

**Background to the handover**: According to the report, the two North Korean soldiers were captured in eastern Ukraine and held in a security facility under Ukrainian control. Zelenskyy said in a public address that Ukraine had decided to transfer the two to South Korea, where the relevant South Korean agencies will be responsible for subsequent handling. The report notes that this is the first publicly confirmed transfer of North Korean prisoners of war from the Ukrainian battlefield to South Korea.

**Diplomatic significance**: Analysts cited in the report argue that Ukraine's active transfer of the North Korean POWs signals an intent to deepen bilateral ties with South Korea and, at a broader level, to reinforce the alliance network opposing Russian military action. The report underscores that the very presence of North Korean POWs on the Ukrainian battlefield is itself a marker of deepening Russia-North Korea military cooperation, and the prisoner transfer represents a counter-ripple in that complex landscape.

**Reactions and open questions**: The report catalogs initial reactions from the relevant parties. South Korea's Ministry of Unification expressed appreciation for the Ukrainian decision and stressed that the two prisoners will be handled in line with international law and South Korean domestic law. Russia has not yet issued a formal response, and North Korean state media has also not reacted publicly. The report argues that questions about the status of the two prisoners, the handover procedures, and any future inter-Korean dialogue window remain to be clarified.
