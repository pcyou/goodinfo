## Core Summary

U.S. President Donald Trump announced on August 2 that he is cancelling planned military strikes against Iran, subject to a deal being made "rapidly." Trump claimed that Iran and other Middle Eastern countries had asked Washington to hold off on any attack, and that the "perimeters" of a deal had been agreed upon. This marks a significant de-escalation in weeks of heightened U.S.-Iran tensions.

## Event Details

According to BBC News, Trump made the announcement at a cabinet meeting at Camp David. He stated that substantial progress had been made in negotiations with Iran, and that both sides had reached preliminary consensus on core elements of an agreement.

Trump emphasized: "Iran and other Middle Eastern countries asked us to hold off, and the perimeters of a deal have been agreed." He simultaneously warned that military options remain on the table if negotiations fail to produce results "quickly."

Axios reported that Trump's statement represents a notable shift from his previous hardline stance toward Iran. In recent weeks, the U.S. had deployed additional military forces to the Middle East and repeatedly hinted at possible strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities.

## Panoramic Analysis

This development has profound implications for Middle Eastern geopolitics. First, it demonstrates that diplomatic engagement can function even under extremely tense conditions, providing a model for resolving long-standing disputes through negotiation. Second, Iran's willingness to address differences through dialogue rather than confrontation reflects a pragmatic approach under economic sanctions pressure.

From a regional security perspective, the easing of U.S.-Iran relations is expected to reduce the risk of military conflict in the Persian Gulf, which has positive implications for global energy supply stability. Gulf states such as Saudi Arabia and the UAE welcome this development, as they would be on the front lines of any potential conflict.

However, significant obstacles remain to reaching a final agreement, including the scope of restrictions on Iran's nuclear program, the timeline for sanctions relief, and Israel's role. The international community will closely monitor subsequent negotiation progress.

## Multiple Perspectives

**U.S. Position**: The Trump administration emphasizes that its "maximum pressure" strategy has worked, arguing that economic sanctions and military deterrence forced Iran back to the negotiating table. A White House spokesperson stated the president's decision reflects "peace through strength."

**Iran's Stance**: Tehran has not yet formally responded to Trump's statement, but its previously submitted 14-point peace proposal suggests Iran is willing to limit nuclear activities under certain conditions in exchange for sanctions relief and economic cooperation.

**Israel's Attitude**: The Israeli government has taken a cautious approach to U.S.-Iran negotiations. Israel's recent military operations in Gaza (resulting in 8 deaths) demonstrate the complex security situation in the region. Israel is concerned that any U.S.-Iran deal may overlook its security concerns.

**International Response**: Both the EU and the United Nations have welcomed the easing of U.S.-Iran relations, calling on both sides to seize the opportunity to resolve disputes through diplomatic channels.
