## Core Summary

According to BBC and The Wall Street Journal, the US government is preparing to announce a landmark agreement allowing Saudi Arabia to develop a civilian nuclear program. This decision fulfills a long-standing Saudi strategic aspiration but may raise concerns in Israel and among some US lawmakers. The deal is seen as a major diplomatic victory for Saudi Arabia and marks a significant shift in US Middle East policy.

## Event Details

### Deal Background

Saudi Arabia has sought for years to develop civilian nuclear energy to meet growing domestic energy demands and drive economic diversification. Under the upcoming agreement, Saudi Arabia will construct nuclear power plants under International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) supervision for peaceful power generation.

The Wall Street Journal exclusively reported that the Trump administration has approved this "landmark nuclear deal," viewing it as a major diplomatic win for the Kingdom. Details are expected to be formally announced in the coming days.

### Regional Reactions

The deal has triggered complex reactions across the Middle East. Israel, the region's only nuclear-armed state (though undeclared), is approaching Saudi nuclear capabilities with caution. Israeli security teams have begun assessing the deal's impact on their strategic advantages.

Some US lawmakers have also expressed concerns about nuclear technology proliferation risks. However, supporters argue that under a strict regulatory framework, the deal strengthens the US-Saudi alliance and helps counterbalance Iranian influence in the region.

### Economic Impact

The Saudi nuclear program will create significant economic opportunities. Nuclear plant construction is estimated to generate billions of dollars in infrastructure investment, providing major opportunities for international engineering firms and nuclear technology suppliers. Nuclear energy will also help Saudi Arabia reduce its reliance on oil for power generation, freeing more crude for export.

## Panoramic Perspective

This deal's significance extends far beyond bilateral nuclear cooperation—it marks a profound adjustment in Middle East geopolitical dynamics.

**First, US Middle East strategic rebalancing.** Against the backdrop of ongoing Iranian nuclear tensions, the US choice to strengthen strategic cooperation with Saudi Arabia reflects a "Saudi counterbalance to Iran" strategy. By supporting an ally's nuclear development, the US attempts to maintain favorable regional power dynamics.

**Second, nuclear non-proliferation double standards.** Critics point out that US acquiescence to Saudi nuclear development contrasts sharply with its hardline stance on Iranian nuclear activities. This selective enforcement of non-proliferation standards may undermine the credibility of the international nuclear non-proliferation regime.

**Third, energy transition's geopolitical dimension.** Saudi nuclear development is not just energy diversification but a key component of its "Vision 2030" economic reform plan. By reducing oil revenue dependence, Saudi Arabia is preparing for a post-oil era, which will profoundly impact global energy market dynamics.

**Fourth, regional arms race risks.** Although the deal emphasizes civilian nuclear energy, acquiring nuclear technology objectively raises Saudi Arabia's nuclear capability threshold. If other regional countries follow suit, the Middle East could face a new round of nuclear capability competition, increasing regional instability.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US Government Position**: White House spokesperson stated the deal "meets an ally's legitimate energy needs under strict safeguard frameworks while strengthening America's strategic position in the region."

**Saudi Official Response**: Saudi Energy Minister said the nuclear program is "an important component of national energy diversification strategy" that will help achieve sustainable development goals.

**Israeli Concerns**: Israeli security officials said they are "assessing the deal's comprehensive impact," emphasizing "the importance of maintaining regional power balance."

**US Congressional Reaction**: Senate Foreign Relations Committee members said they would "carefully review deal details" to ensure "nuclear non-proliferation standards are strictly enforced."

**International Atomic Energy Agency**: IAEA Director General said the agency is "prepared to provide necessary supervision and technical support for Saudi Arabia's nuclear program" to ensure all activities meet international safety standards.
