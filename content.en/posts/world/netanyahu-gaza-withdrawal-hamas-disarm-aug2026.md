## Netanyahu: No Israeli Withdrawal from Gaza Until Hamas Completely Disarmed

**[Core Summary]** Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has declared that Israeli forces will not withdraw from Gaza until Hamas is "completely disarmed." The hardline stance sets an extremely high bar for ongoing ceasefire negotiations and casts doubt on international optimism about the conflict's resolution.

### Event Details

According to the Associated Press, Netanyahu emphasized in a new statement that Israel's security bottom line requires Hamas to be "fully disarmed," with no consideration of withdrawal until that condition is met. This position diverges significantly from ceasefire frameworks being promoted by mediators.

The US and other mediators have been pushing phased ceasefire plans including hostage releases, humanitarian aid corridors, and gradual Israeli withdrawal. Netanyahu's statement effectively rejects a "ceasefire first, disarmament later" approach, demanding disarmament as a precondition.

Analysts note this position reflects domestic political pressure. Netanyahu's coalition government depends on right-wing party support, and any move seen as "conceding to Hamas" could topple the government.

### Broader Perspective

Netanyahu's hardline stance reveals the core dilemma in resolving the Gaza conflict: the irreconcilability between security demands and political reality. From Israel's perspective, without completely dismantling Hamas's military capabilities, future security threats cannot be eliminated. But practically speaking, requiring an armed organization deeply embedded in Gaza's social structure to "completely disarm" has almost no historical precedent for success.

This position also challenges international mediation efforts. Egypt, Qatar, and the US had promoted phased plans based on "ceasefire first, negotiations later" logic. If Israel makes disarmament a non-negotiable precondition, the entire negotiation framework needs redesign.

### Multiple Perspectives

**Israeli Government**: Insists on security-first principles, believing lasting peace is only possible when Hamas loses military capability. Netanyahu faces immense pressure from right-wing coalition partners.

**Palestinian Side**: Hamas views disarmament demands as an "existential threat" and is unlikely to accept. The Palestinian Authority calls for international pressure on Israel to accept a ceasefire.

**US Position**: The Biden administration continues pushing ceasefire talks, but Israel's hardline stance increases mediation difficulty. The US tries to balance supporting Israel's security rights with promoting humanitarian ceasefire.

**International Community**: The UN and several Arab states call for immediate ceasefire, warning of deteriorating humanitarian conditions in Gaza. The EU emphasizes civilian protection while acknowledging Israel's security concerns.
