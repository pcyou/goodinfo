## Core Summary

The US Supreme Court has issued an important ruling that states may count mail-in ballots received after Election Day, even if they arrive later. This decision overturns previous strict deadline restrictions in some states and is seen as a major victory for voting rights advocates.

## Event Details

According to The Washington Post, the Court ruled 6-3 in favor of counting late-arriving ballots. The majority opinion held that the Constitution does not prohibit states from counting mail ballots received within a reasonable time after Election Day, as long as they were postmarked before the election. The Chief Justice wrote that mail voting is an essential part of democracy and should not be denied due to normal postal delays.

Three conservative justices dissented, arguing that counting ballots after Election Day could create uncertainty and increase fraud risk. However, the majority noted there is no evidence of systematic fraud in mail voting.

The ruling directly impacts upcoming midterm elections. Millions of mail ballots arrive days after elections each year due to postal delays. In the 2020 election, several key swing states faced intense legal disputes over mail ballot processing.

## Broader Perspective

This ruling has far-reaching implications for the US electoral system. First, it substantially expands access to mail voting, enabling citizens unable to vote in person due to work, travel, or other reasons to exercise their voting rights more conveniently. This is particularly important for remote area residents, overseas citizens, and elderly with mobility issues.

Second, the ruling may reshape the political landscape of future elections. Democrats traditionally support expanding mail voting while Republicans tend to restrict voting methods. After the ruling, several Republican-led states announced they would revisit election laws, while Democratic-led states pledged to leverage the ruling to expand voter participation.

From an institutional perspective, the ruling also reflects the Supreme Court's shifting stance on voting rights. In recent years, the Court has gradually expanded voting rights protection across multiple cases, demonstrating emphasis on democratic participation values.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Voting Rights Advocacy Groups**: Organizations like the ACLU welcomed the ruling as a "victory for democracy." They argue mail voting is essential for ensuring all citizens can participate and should not be denied for technical reasons.

**Conservative Legal Experts**: Some conservative scholars expressed concern, arguing this could open doors to election fraud. They note ballots arriving after Election Day are harder to verify and may delay timely confirmation of results.

**Election Law Experts**: Multiple election law scholars noted the ruling essentially confirms existing legal practice. Before this ruling, many states already allowed counting of mail ballots received within reasonable time after Election Day. The Supreme Court simply elevated this practice to a constitutional level for nationwide uniform application.
