## Core Summary

Super typhoon Dolphin is approaching China's southeastern coast at an alarming speed. In response to one of the strongest typhoons in recent years, the Chinese government has evacuated over 1 million residents. Multiple airports have closed, and transportation systems are largely suspended. Meteorological departments have issued the highest-level typhoon warning, expecting strong winds, heavy rainfall, and severe flooding.

## Event Details

According to BBC and multiple international media reports, Typhoon Dolphin has entered the affected range of China's coast, reaching super typhoon intensity. The storm affects not only mainland China but also surrounding areas including Okinawa, Japan.

**Key response measures include:**

- Over 1 million residents evacuated to safe areas
- Multiple airports closed with mass flight cancellations
- Coastal cities activated typhoon emergency response plans
- Transportation, power, and communications infrastructure entered emergency status

Okinawa, Japan is also affected by the typhoon, with some flights canceled and local authorities urging residents to take precautionary measures.

## Broader Perspective

The approach of Typhoon Dolphin once again highlights the increasing frequency of extreme weather events against the backdrop of climate change. China's southeast coast, as an economically developed region with dense population and concentrated infrastructure, faces enormous economic losses with each super typhoon landfall. This million-person-scale evacuation demonstrates China's strong organizational capacity and mobilization efficiency in disaster emergency management.

In the long term, as global warming continues, the intensity and uncertainty of typhoon seasons in the northwest Pacific are increasing. Balancing personnel safety with minimizing economic disruption remains a challenge for urban managers.
