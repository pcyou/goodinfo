US President Donald Trump announced on May 3 that he plans to "a lot further" reduce the number of US troops stationed in Germany. The declaration has reignited concerns among European NATO allies about the future of transatlantic security architecture.

## The Reduction Plan

Responding to questions about the future scale of US forces in Germany, Trump said he intends to significantly reduce troop numbers ("a lot further"). While specific figures and timelines have not been released, this statement is consistent with Washington's previous military adjustment direction.

Currently, approximately 35,000 US military personnel are stationed in Germany, making it the largest host of US forces in Europe. Germany serves as NATO's strategic hub in Central Europe, hosting critical military installations including US European Command headquarters and Ramstein Air Base.

## NATO Allies' Reactions

Germany's NATO allies have expressed varying degrees of concern about this decision:

- **Germany**: While Germany has been pushing for European defense autonomy in recent years, a large-scale US withdrawal would directly impact its security guarantees.
- **Eastern European nations**: Poland and the Baltic states are particularly concerned that a US pullback from Europe would weaken the strategic deterrent against Russia.
- **NATO headquarters**: The NATO Secretary-General has repeatedly called on allies to maintain military presence in Europe to address the evolving security environment.

## Strategic Context

Trump's troop reduction decision is consistent with his long-standing "America First" foreign policy. He has consistently criticized European NATO allies for "free-riding" on defense spending and demanded that countries take greater responsibility for their own security.

However, in the current complex geopolitical environment — with the US-Iran war ongoing and the Russia-Ukraine conflict unresolved — a US military pullback from Europe's heartland could have multiple strategic consequences:

1. **European security vacuum**: US withdrawal may create a security gap in Central Europe, prompting Russia to reassess its strategic posture on the continent.
2. **Accelerated European defense autonomy**: This decision could further drive the EU to accelerate the development of independent defense capabilities.
3. **Transatlantic trust crisis**: Allies' confidence in US security commitments may be further eroded, affecting NATO cohesion.

## What's Next

Analysts expect that specific reduction plans will be drafted by the Pentagon in the coming weeks and submitted to Congress for review. However, given the current international situation, actual implementation may face dual pressure from both Congress and allied nations.
