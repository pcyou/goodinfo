## Executive Summary

The United States has launched a fresh wave of military strikes against targets inside Iran, while President Donald Trump has publicly threatened to carry out a "massive attack." Tehran has responded by vowing "appropriate and decisive" retaliation. The latest developments mark a dangerous new phase in the US-Iran military confrontation, with Middle East tensions escalating sharply.

## Details

NBC News, citing multiple US defense officials, reported that the strikes targeted Iranian military facilities and command centers. The operation began in the early hours of Thursday local time and lasted approximately two hours. Officials said the action was intended to "degrade Iran's military command capabilities" but declined to disclose specific targets or damage assessments.

Axios subsequently reported that President Trump is "close" to making a final decision on a larger-scale military operation. Sources familiar with the matter told the outlet that the White House National Security team has presented the president with multiple options, including plans to strike Iranian nuclear facilities and Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps bases.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Nasser Kanaani told a press conference in Tehran that Iran's armed forces are fully prepared to take "appropriate and decisive" action in response to any aggression. He stressed that Iran does not seek war but will never tolerate violations of its sovereignty and territorial integrity.

The Israeli Prime Minister's Office issued a brief statement saying Israel is "closely monitoring developments in the region" and reaffirmed its right to "take all necessary measures to defend itself." Gulf states including Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates have called for restraint and a diplomatic resolution to the crisis.

UN Secretary-General spokesperson Stephane Dujarric said the Secretary-General is "deeply concerned" about the escalation and called on all parties to immediately cease military action and avoid a full-scale regional conflict.

## Analysis

The sharp escalation in US-Iran military confrontation reflects deeper fractures in the Middle Eastern geopolitical landscape. At the strategic level, the Trump administration's "maximum pressure" policy has evolved into direct military engagement, marking a dangerous shift from deterrence to active combat operations.

First, the diplomatic path to resolving the Iran nuclear issue has effectively collapsed. Since the US unilaterally withdrew from the JCPOA, Iran has progressively breached its limits, enriching uranium to near weapons-grade levels. Military strikes cannot fundamentally resolve the nuclear question and may instead accelerate Iran's nuclear weapons program. The lessons of the Iraq War remain unlearned, and new military adventures will only bring greater uncertainty.

Second, the risk of uncontrolled proxy warfare has risen significantly. Iran has built a network of allied militias spanning the Middle East, including Hezbollah in Lebanon, the Houthis in Yemen, and Shia militias in Iraq. If direct US-Iran conflict expands, these proxy forces could be fully activated, drawing Israel, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE into the conflict.

Third, global energy security faces a severe challenge. The Strait of Hormuz carries approximately one-fifth of the world's oil shipments. Any military conflict could lead to the closure of this strategic waterway. International oil prices have already surpassed $100 per barrel; if the conflict persists, prices could surge to $150 or higher, dealing a fatal blow to the global economic recovery.

From a broader perspective, an expanded US-Iran conflict would profoundly reshape great-power competition. Russia and China, as Iran's key partners, will face difficult diplomatic choices. America's military investment in the Middle East will also divert strategic resources from the Indo-Pacific, with far-reaching implications for the global balance of power.

## Perspectives

**US official position**: The Trump administration insists the military action aims to "prevent Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons" and "protect the United States and its allies." The White House stated the president "reserves the right to take all necessary measures" and called on Congress to support the administration's "firm stance."

**Iran's response**: Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei said in a national television address that the US military action was a "strategic mistake," warning that "the aggressor will pay a heavy price." IRGC commander Hossein Salami stated Iran is prepared to "make the enemy regret its decision."

**International reaction**: EU foreign policy chief Josep Borrell called on all parties to "return to the negotiating table immediately," stressing that "military means cannot resolve the Middle East crisis." China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson expressed "deep concern" and urged "maximum restraint." Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said Russia opposes "any action that could lead to regional catastrophe."

**US domestic divide**: Democratic Senator Tim Kaine criticized Trump's action as "unauthorized by Congress and unconstitutional." Republican Senator Lindsey Graham supported the president's "tough stance," saying "only showing strength can stop Iran." Polls show the American public broadly opposes being drawn into another Middle East war.
