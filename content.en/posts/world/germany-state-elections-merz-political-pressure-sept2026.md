## Core Summary

Germany held key state elections on Sunday, widely viewed as a significant test of Chancellor Merz's political fortunes. International media outlets including the BBC reported that if Merz's Christian Democratic Union (CDU) performs poorly in these state elections, the political pressure on the chancellor will intensify further. The elections will affect not only Germany's domestic political landscape but also European policy directions on immigration, security, and economic issues.

## Event Details

**Background and Basic Arrangements**: According to BBC and other media reports, Germany held multiple state-level elections on Sunday, September 20. The elections are widely seen as an important mid-term test of Chancellor Merz's governance performance, and the results will have a direct impact on Germany's federal political landscape. Although German state elections are nominally internal affairs of each state, they often reflect voters' true attitudes toward federal government policy directions, especially on key issues such as the economy, immigration, and security.

**Political Pressure Facing Merz**: Since taking office as German Chancellor, Merz has faced a series of complex domestic and diplomatic challenges. On the domestic front, the German economy continues to struggle, with manufacturing facing transformation pressures, energy prices remaining high, exports blocked, and industrial relocation creating severe tests for the new government. On the immigration front, Germany has faced multiple pressures in recent years including illegal immigration, refugee resettlement, and social integration, with policy disagreements within the ruling coalition. On the international front, Germany needs to balance European security, transatlantic relations, aid to Ukraine, and relations with China, with multi-line diplomacy consuming the government's political resources. Multiple political analysts have pointed out that Merz needs to demonstrate a clear sense of political direction in these state elections, otherwise his leadership will face dual questioning from within and outside the party.

**Political Significance of State Elections**: Voter turnout, party vote shares, and coalition changes in German state elections are often seen as a "thermometer" of voter sentiment. For the CDU, the result will directly affect Merz's authority within the party and the stability of the ruling coalition. If the CDU performs well in multiple key states, it will consolidate Merz's leadership and provide support for policy advancement at the federal level. Conversely, if the CDU loses, it may trigger questions about Merz's leadership within the party and even prompt reassessments within the ruling coalition. Multiple political analysts have noted that while state election losses do not immediately lead to political changes at the federal level, they significantly increase political pressure on the governing party.

**Impact of Immigration and Economic Issues**: The campaign for this state election focused on two major areas: immigration policy and economic issues. On the immigration front, multiple parties have proposed different positions on refugee resettlement, border control, and illegal immigration deportation, with voters' attention continuing to rise. On the economic front, voters are generally concerned about the transformation of Germany's manufacturing industry, the direction of energy policy, rising living costs, and changes in the labor market. The performance and policy commitments of the Merz government on these issues will directly affect voter choices in the state elections. Multiple polls show that immigration and the economy are the two issues voters care most about and are key factors in determining election outcomes.

**Potential Impact on European Policy Directions**: As Europe's largest economy and a core EU member state, Germany's political landscape changes have important implications for overall European policy directions. If the Merz government faces political pressure after these state elections, its positions on European security, aid to Ukraine, EU fiscal policy, trade policy, and energy policy may be constrained by domestic politics. Germany's political instability may affect Europe's coordination capability on major issues and may provide more political space for far-right parties and Eurosceptic parties.
