## Core Summary

US President Trump claimed during a primetime TV address that China obtained up to 220 million American voter registration files, presenting them as core evidence for his election security initiative. However, analysis by multiple major media outlets found the documents do not directly prove large-scale election fraud.

## Event Details

According to USA Today, President Trump displayed voter registration files during a Friday evening primetime address, claiming they proved China's "massive infiltration" of the US election system. Trump simultaneously called for revoking the broadcast licenses of ABC and NBC for refusing to air his speech on election integrity.

The New York Times and Associated Press conducted independent analyses of the documents. They found the files primarily contain basic registration information — names, addresses, birth dates, and party affiliation — data that is publicly available in many US states through legal channels. The documents contain no voting records or direct evidence of election manipulation.

CNN reported an FCC commissioner's response calling Trump's license revocation demand "unconstitutional under the First Amendment." Politico reported that Trump's threats to TV licenses are "getting serious."

## Strategic Analysis

This event reflects several deep structural issues in American politics. First, the eternal tension between data security and public transparency. US state voter registration systems vary dramatically, with some treating voter information as fully public records while others impose strict access limitations.

Second, the escalating trend of "information weaponization" — the documents themselves may be real, but the interpretation of their meaning may be seriously distorted.

Third, the potential impact on US-China relations cannot be ignored. Linking election security directly to China will further intensify bilateral tensions across multiple domains.

## Perspectives

Reactions are clearly divided. USA Today presented Trump's claims alongside objective descriptions of file contents. The New York Times and AP took a more cautious analytical stance, noting the lack of direct connection between file contents and election fraud. CNN focused on constitutional controversy, while Politico analyzed the political strategy behind the move.
