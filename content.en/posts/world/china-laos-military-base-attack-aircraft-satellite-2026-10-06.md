**[Core Summary]** Reuters has obtained the latest commercial satellite images showing China deploying attack aircraft and military vehicles at a newly-built military base inside Laos. This is the first time China has routinely deployed offensive air power abroad, drawing regional and global strategic attention. Experts believe this base will significantly enhance China's power projection and rapid-response capability in the region.

**[Event Details]**
- **Base Location**: The base is located in northern Laos mountainous terrain, approximately 100 kilometers from the China-Laos border. Although small in scale, its runway and hangars can accommodate multiple tactical aircraft models.
- **Equipment Assets**: Images identify suspected attack trainer aircraft (JJ-7/F-7 modified variants) and various military vehicles (including armored personnel carriers and tactical trucks), with some vehicles suspected to be equipped with air defense systems.
- **Infrastructure**: The base features reinforced hangars, fuel storage facilities, and helicopter pads, with the overall deployment pattern resembling the Chinese military's "small forward operating base" model.
- **Strategic Implications**: This base will significantly shorten the response time of Chinese air power toward the South China Sea and Malacca Strait directions, and can serve as a core node for responding to Southeast Asian internal shipping incidents and emergency support lines.
- **Official Stance**: The Lao government has not made a formal comment; when asked at a routine press conference, China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs stated that "China-Lao cooperation in the defense field falls within the normal scope of state-to-state relations."

**[Panoramic Analysis]**
**First**: This base is a concrete example of China's "forward presence" military concept. Over the past decade, the Chinese Navy has established a support base in Djibouti, and the Air Force has built long-term personnel training cooperation in Cambodia/Laos, but routinely deploying "offensive air power" abroad is a first. This signals a shift in Chinese overseas military assets from "support-type" to "combat projection-type," which will profoundly impact regional military equilibrium.
**Second**: Pressure on Southeast Asian countries to pick sides between China and the United States will further intensify. Thailand, the Philippines, Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, and Vietnam will all need to reassess the depth and balance of defense cooperation with China. Some Southeast Asian countries may accelerate engagement with the US-India "Indo-Pacific Strategy," further compressing the "hedging" space of maintaining distance from both sides.
**Third**: Global supply chains and shipping insurance will see new risk pricing. The base is only about 1,500 kilometers from the Malacca Strait, bringing new uncertainty to the security pattern of global trade "choke points." Shipowners, insurers, and crude oil-maritime trade stakeholders will raise premiums for South China Sea and Malacca Strait routes, with associated macroeconomic spillover effects gradually becoming apparent.
