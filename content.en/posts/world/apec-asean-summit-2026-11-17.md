## Key Facts

- **Location**: Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia
- **Attendees**: Including US President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping, Russian President Putin and other heads of state
- **Core topics**: International trade rules, supply chain security, digital economy cooperation, climate change response
- **Timing context**: Summit held during critical phase of US-China trade negotiations, with expectations for breakthroughs in bilateral leader meetings
- **Regional focus**: ASEAN nations emphasize neutrality amid US-China competition while deepening regional economic integration
- **Expected outcomes**: Joint statement anticipated, reaffirming support for multilateral trading system and reaching preliminary consensus on digital governance and green transition

## Event Details

The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation and Association of Southeast Asian Nations summits are being held this week in Malaysia, bringing together leaders from multiple countries in preparation for upcoming meetings between Trump, Xi and Putin. The conferences focus on trade relations, regional security and economic cooperation, seeking consensus against a backdrop of intensifying global geopolitical tensions.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Southeast Asian countries generally welcome the restart of major power dialogue.** ASEAN member states believe direct communication among US, Chinese and Russian leaders helps alleviate regional tensions and provides more diplomatic space for smaller nations. The Malaysian Prime Minister stated that ASEAN should not become a pawn in great power games, but should serve as a bridge to promote understanding and cooperation.

**US business leaders express cautious optimism about trade prospects.** They hope the summit will bring clear signals on tariff policies and reduce uncertainty. However, they worry that geopolitical factors may override economic rationality, turning trade agreements into tools of strategic competition.

**Chinese scholars emphasize the importance of multilateralism.** They argue that in the current context of rising unilateralism, regional cooperation mechanisms like APEC and ASEAN are particularly valuable. Resolving differences through dialogue rather than confrontation serves the long-term interests of all participants.

## Broader Analysis

What makes this APEC and ASEAN summit distinctive is its timing—it occurs at a critical juncture of global order restructuring. After the Trump administration's return to the White House, US foreign policy has exhibited greater transactionality and unpredictability, prompting traditional allies and partners to reassess their strategic positioning. Meanwhile, China continues advancing its Belt and Road Initiative and the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership, attempting to construct alternative economic architectures that do not depend on the United States.

In this context, Southeast Asian nations face a particularly delicate situation. They need both China's markets and investments, and rely on US security guarantees and technological ecosystems. ASEAN's long-standing "great power balancing" strategy faces unprecedented pressure testing. This summit can be viewed as a collective probe: national leaders are attempting to gauge the Trump administration's true intentions while simultaneously signaling to Beijing and Moscow that Southeast Asia will not easily pick sides.

From an economic perspective, this meeting may mark the entry of the global trading system into a new negotiation phase. The globalization of the past thirty years was built on relatively stable rules, but now those rules themselves have become bargaining chips. Supply chain "friend-shoring," key technology export controls, digital sovereignty claims—these concepts are reshaping the fundamental logic of international commerce. As regional cooperation platforms, the value of APEC and ASEAN lies not only in facilitating specific agreements, but more importantly in keeping dialogue channels open to prevent miscalculations from escalating into conflicts.

Notably, this summit may also serve as a testing ground for examining the boundaries between "de-risking" and "decoupling." Western countries claim to want to reduce dependence on China, but the reality is that deeply intertwined economic connections make complete separation nearly impossible. How to manage security risks while maintaining economic openness will be the core challenge facing policymakers in various countries in coming years. The gathering of leaders here at least indicates that all parties still believe dialogue is more effective than isolation in solving problems.
