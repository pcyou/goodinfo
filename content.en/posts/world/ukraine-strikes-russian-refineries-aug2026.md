## Core Summary

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy announced on August 6 that Ukrainian forces launched strikes on two oil refining facilities deep inside Russian territory. Zelenskyy stated the operation aims to limit Russia's oil revenues used to fund its war effort, thereby reducing its capacity to continue military operations. This is the latest in a series of recent Ukrainian attacks targeting Russian energy infrastructure.

## Event Details

According to a statement from the Ukrainian military, the strikes targeted refining facilities located deep within Russia, hundreds of kilometers from the Ukrainian border. In his evening address, Zelenskyy emphasized that these refineries produce petroleum products directly used to fund Russia's military machine.

Russian regional officials confirmed the attacks, stating that air defense systems intercepted some incoming drones, but facilities were still damaged. No casualties have been reported, but refinery operations may be temporarily affected.

The strikes come as the Russia-Ukraine conflict continues, with both sides seeking to change the battlefield dynamics by targeting critical infrastructure. Ukraine has recently intensified its campaign against Russian energy infrastructure, attempting to undermine Moscow's war financing capabilities.

## Panoramic Perspective

Ukraine's strategy of targeting Russian energy infrastructure reflects the growing importance of economic warfare in modern conflicts. Energy exports are the primary source of Russian fiscal revenue, accounting for 30% to 40% of the federal budget. By striking refineries and oil facilities, Ukraine aims to directly cut off Russia's war funding chain.

From a military strategy perspective, this "deep strike" approach has multiple implications. First, it forces Russia to disperse air defense resources across a broader rear area, relieving pressure on the front lines. Second, damage to energy infrastructure affects Russia's oil export capacity, which in turn impacts global energy markets. Third, sustained pressure may undermine Russian public support for the war.

However, this strategy also faces controversy. Critics argue that targeting civilian energy infrastructure may violate international humanitarian law and could cause hardship for ordinary civilians. Supporters contend that facilities funding military operations are legitimate military targets during wartime.

## Conclusion

Ukraine's strikes on Russian refineries highlight the deep integration of economic and military warfare in modern conflicts. As the conflict continues, both sides are seeking strategic advantages by targeting critical infrastructure, with profound implications for global energy security and geopolitical dynamics.
