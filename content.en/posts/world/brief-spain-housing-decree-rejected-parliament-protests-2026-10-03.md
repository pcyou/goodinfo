The Associated Press reports that Spain's parliament has voted by a majority to reject the package of housing relief measures put forward by the Sanchez government, marking the governing coalition's most significant legislative defeat in recent months. The decree was originally designed to ease structural pressure on rental inflation and home affordability in major cities including Madrid and Barcelona, but was rejected on the grounds that its measures were deemed insufficient.

The legislative defeat comes against a backdrop of multi-day, large-scale demonstrations outside parliament. Housing protesters in multiple cities have demanded more aggressive rent controls, social housing construction, and restrictions on speculative purchases. The failure not only exposes coordination difficulties for the ruling coalition inside the chamber, but also risks further inflaming social discontent, raising the prospect that housing will become a pivotal variable shaping Spain's political trajectory over the next phase.
