[Key Summary]
Ethiopian federal forces have announced they have regained control of Mekelle, the capital of the Tigray region, after opposition fighters briefly held the city. The heads of state of Kenya, South Africa, Tanzania and Rwanda issued a joint declaration calling for an immediate ceasefire and a return to talks.

[Background]
In late September 2026, residual forces of the Tigray People's Liberation Front launched a sudden offensive on Mekelle and briefly took government buildings and several districts, triggering a fresh humanitarian crisis. The Ethiopian National Defence Force regrouped within days and retook the city.

[Multiple Perspectives]
The federal government has said it will hold the planners accountable while pledging to keep humanitarian corridors open. Tigrayan authorities accuse the federal government of "excessive use of force." Regional observers warn that without external mediation, similar conflict patterns could be replayed in other northern Ethiopian regions.

[Big Picture]
The Tigray conflict has caused hundreds of thousands of deaths since 2020 and millions still rely on humanitarian assistance. The renewed fighting will again hit northern Ethiopia's economic corridor and may affect Red Sea and Gulf of Aden shipping security and the stability of the Horn of Africa. Whether regional mediation succeeds will determine whether Ethiopia can break out of its long cycle of political and military crisis.
