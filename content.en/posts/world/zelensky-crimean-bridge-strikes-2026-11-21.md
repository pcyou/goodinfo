## Core Summary

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has justified Kyiv's repeated attacks on a critical bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea, calling it a "fair response" to 109 separate Russian attacks on bridges in Ukraine. In comments to the BBC in Kyiv, Zelensky said the intense attacks on Ukraine by Russia that have followed the strikes on the Crimean Bridge were only a "signal that Vladimir Putin does not want to stop. He wants a reason to escalate".

## Event Details

Zelensky rejected the claim the Russian president made in China that the bridge - which links Russia to the peninsular it illegally annexed from Ukraine - was "exclusively civilian" in use. He pointed out that Russia has conducted systematic strikes on Ukrainian infrastructure since launching its full-scale invasion in February 2022.

UK Health Secretary Wes Streeting met with Zelensky during his visit to Kyiv. Streeting stated: "The more Putin tries to divide us, the more resolute we become." He described Friday's incident of Russian missiles hitting Poland as an "outrageous provocation" during military exercises of the so-called Coalition of the Willing.

Asked by the BBC about Nato's response, Streeting said the best way was to "do more for Ukraine" to defend itself against Putin whom he called a "cold-blooded killer". Streeting's visit to Kyiv came after European leaders met in Berlin on Thursday alongside US envoys to discuss Ukraine.

Speaking to the BBC, Streeting added: "Putin has absolutely no business playing the victim. He is the aggressor in this war. He provoked an illegal invasion of Ukraine. And all of us, right throughout the world, need to judge Putin not on his words, but on his actions."

In a separate development on Saturday, Finland said it "detected a drone strike on a Russian merchant vessel" in the eastern Gulf of Finland, "in an international maritime area".

## Panoramic Perspective

The Crimean Bridge, as a strategic corridor connecting mainland Russia to the Crimean Peninsula, has been a key target of military operations by both sides since the outbreak of the Russia-Ukraine conflict in 2022. This bridge not only has military logistics value but also serves as a symbolic structure demonstrating Russia's control over Crimea.

Zelensky's statement reflects a shift in Ukraine's war strategy: from passive defense to actively striking Russian logistics supply lines and strategic assets. By attacking the Crimean Bridge, Ukraine aims to weaken Russia's ability to transport supplies to the southern front while demonstrating its counterattack capabilities to the international community.

However, this strategy also carries risks. Russia's subsequent fierce retaliation against Ukrainian infrastructure indicates that such actions may trigger a larger escalation cycle. The international community is divided on this issue: some countries support Ukraine's right to self-defense, while others worry that further expansion of the conflict could endanger regional stability.

From a broader geopolitical perspective, this incident highlights the vulnerability of infrastructure in modern warfare. Key bridges, energy facilities, and communication networks have become important targets in hybrid warfare, forcing countries to rethink security protection strategies for civilian infrastructure.

## Multiple Perspectives

**The Ukrainian position** emphasizes the legitimacy and necessity of its actions, arguing that this is a proportional response to Russia's continued attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure. The Zelensky government points out that Russia has conducted 109 separate attacks on Ukrainian bridges, and Ukraine has the right to take defensive measures.

**The Russian side** insists that the Crimean Bridge is a purely civilian facility and accuses Ukraine of terrorist attacks. The Putin government uses this as an excuse to further escalate military operations, intensifying strikes on Ukrainian cities.

**Western allies** generally support Ukraine's right to self-defense but express concern about conflict escalation. UK official Streeting clearly stated that Putin is an "aggressor" and "cold-blooded killer," calling on the international community to judge the Russian leader based on actions rather than words.

**Neutral observers** point out that such infrastructure attacks may lead to long-term humanitarian consequences, affecting civilian life and regional economic recovery. They call on all parties to comply with international humanitarian law and protect civilian infrastructure from military strikes.
