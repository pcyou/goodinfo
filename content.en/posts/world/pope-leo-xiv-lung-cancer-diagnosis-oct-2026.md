**[Core Summary]**

The Vatican announced on October 13 that Pope Leo XIV has been diagnosed with lung cancer following a surgery that officials described as 'successful' and is now receiving further treatment. The Pope's elder brother Louis Prevost is also battling a different form of cancer.

**[Event Details]**

- **Diagnosis Disclosure**: The Vatican press office confirmed through multiple international media outlets on October 13 that Pope Leo XIV was found to have a malignant lesion in his lung after hospital examination, and the surgery removed the relevant tissue with what was characterized as 'successful' results, although post-operative pathology confirmed lung cancer.
- **Treatment Plan**: The Vatican stated that the Pope is currently receiving further treatment, without disclosing specific medical institutions or medication regimens, but emphasized that the Pope is 'in good spirits' and will continue to perform some of his daily duties.
- **Brother's Condition**: The wife of the Pope's elder brother Louis Prevost, Judith Prevost, revealed for the first time in an interview with CBS on October 13 that Louis is also fighting a different type of cancer, describing his experience as 'very difficult'.
- **Historical Context**: This is the first time in recent decades that the Vatican has publicly disclosed a specific cancer diagnosis for a sitting Pope, drawing widespread attention from the global Catholic community and the media.

**[Panorama]**

The public disclosure of Pope Leo XIV's lung cancer diagnosis represents a major choice of information openness by the Catholic Church in the modern media environment. Over the past few decades, the health information of successive Popes has typically been communicated by the Vatican using euphemisms such as 'flu' or 'brief indisposition'. The decision to directly disclose the cancer diagnosis this time reflects a clear shift in the Vatican's transparency posture. Meanwhile, the public revelation of the Pope's brother's cancer battle highlights the prevalence and familial aggregation of cancer globally, offering implications for public health education. From the perspective of church governance, the Pope's health status is directly tied to his ability to fulfill religious duties, and the Vatican is likely to make more refined arrangements for its public activity schedule in the coming period to balance treatment with the continuity of church leadership.

**[Multiple Perspectives]**

- **Vatican Official Position**: Emphasizes that the surgery was 'successful' and the Pope is in good spirits, hoping the public will remain calm and avoid excessive speculation.
- **Church Observers**: Believe this disclosure approach is more transparent than before, reflecting an evolution in the Vatican's public communication strategy.
