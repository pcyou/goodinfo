## Core Summary

The New Zealand government announced it will introduce the Online Safety Bill to parliament, banning under-16s from using social media platforms including TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat, and Facebook. Prime Minister Christopher Luxon said social media was exposing children to harmful content, addictive technology, and pressures they are not equipped to deal with. Non-compliant platforms could face fines up to 10% of their global revenue.

## Event Details

### Legislative Background and Motivation

Prime Minister Luxon stated that social media was affecting children's family life, mental health, sleep, and education. "We have protections to keep children safe in the real world and we need them in the virtual world too," he said.

The bill requires social media companies to take "reasonable steps" to verify users are over 16, using methods including:
- Existing account information
- Facial age estimation technology
- Digital identity checks

### Political Divisions

Despite government pushing for this legislation, it's unclear if the bill will gain enough support. Both the Act and New Zealand First parties—coalition partners in the government—have said they will oppose it.

Deputy Prime Minister and Act leader David Seymour said the ban won't work. Foreign Affairs Minister Winston Peters called Australia's December ban a "colossal failure," noting many children under 16 have still been able to access and use banned apps.

### International Comparison

New Zealand's move follows Australia, which has implemented a similar ban but faces enforcement difficulties. The UK and Greece plan to introduce restrictions next year. However, France's top court struck down a similar social media ban earlier this month, saying it infringed upon freedom of expression.

## Analysis

Restrictions on youth social media use have become a global policy hotspot, reflecting deep societal thinking about child protection in the digital age. Behind this trend are concerns about social media algorithm recommendation mechanisms, addictive design, and insufficient content moderation.

From a policy effectiveness perspective, relying solely on age restrictions may not achieve desired goals. Technical circumvention methods (such as false age information, using parent accounts) make enforcement challenging. A more effective strategy might be multi-pronged: strengthening platform content moderation, promoting digital literacy education, and encouraging parental supervision.

From an industry perspective, such regulatory measures may force social media platforms to redesign products and reduce dependence on teenage users. This could affect platforms' user growth strategies and advertising revenue models.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporters**: View this as a necessary measure to protect children's mental health, citing cyberbullying, body image anxiety, and addiction issues on social media that seriously affect youth development.

**Opponents**: Argue the ban infringes on personal freedom and is difficult to enforce. They advocate solving problems through education rather than restrictions.

**Platforms**: Have not formally responded but are expected to emphasize existing age verification measures and parental control features.

**Parent Groups**: Reactions vary—some support government intervention, others believe this is family responsibility rather than government duty.
