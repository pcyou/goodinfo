## Core Summary

Japan has strongly protested Russian President Putin's visit to the southern Kuril Islands (claimed by Japan as the Northern Territories). According to Russian state media, this was Putin's first visit to the disputed territory. Tokyo called the move "completely unacceptable," saying it seriously damages bilateral relations. The territorial dispute has persisted for decades and remains the biggest obstacle to normalizing Japan-Russia relations.

## Event Details

According to BBC reports, Russian President Putin recently visited the southern Kuril Islands (called the Northern Territories by Japan), triggering strong protests from the Japanese government. This was Putin's first visit to the disputed territory, confirmed by Russian state media.

The Japanese government immediately issued a statement strongly protesting Putin's visit, calling it "completely unacceptable" and reaffirming its sovereignty claim over the islands. Japan's Chief Cabinet Secretary told reporters that Japan would convey its protest through diplomatic channels.

The southern Kuril Islands, located at the southern end of the Kuril chain, include Iturup, Kunashir, Shikotan, and the Habomai islets. The Soviet Union occupied these islands at the end of World War II, but Japan has consistently claimed sovereignty over them. This territorial dispute has prevented the two countries from signing a formal WWII peace treaty.

## Panoramic Perspective

Putin's visit to the disputed islands carries multiple strategic implications. From Russia's perspective, it signals consolidation of Far East territorial control, indicating Moscow will not make any concessions on territorial issues. In the current international climate, Russia's visit by its top leader demonstrates to the outside world its "effective control" over these islands.

For Japan, this incident again highlights the complexity of the Northern Territories issue. Despite years of diplomatic efforts, substantive progress has been limited. Putin's visit is essentially a direct rejection of Japan's position, making resolution through negotiations even more remote.

From a geopolitical perspective, this dispute also involves broader Pacific strategic dynamics. These islands control important passages from the Sea of Okhotsk to the Pacific Ocean, holding significant importance for Russia's Far East fleet strategic deployment. Meanwhile, the area's rich fishing resources and potential energy reserves are also focal points of contention.

This incident may also affect Japan-Russia cooperation in other areas, including energy development and trade. In the current international environment, further deterioration of Japan-Russia relations may prompt Japan to strengthen security cooperation with the United States and its allies, further altering the regional balance of power in Northeast Asia.
