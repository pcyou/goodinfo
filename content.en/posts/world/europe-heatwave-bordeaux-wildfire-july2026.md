## [Core Summary]

On July 28, Spain and France braced for a new heatwave, with temperatures expected to exceed 40°C in some regions. Meanwhile, a wildfire near Bordeaux in southwestern France has raged for nearly four days, burning over 420 square kilometers and forcing more than 300,000 people to evacuate. The extreme heat has further complicated firefighting efforts as Europe faces one of its most severe wildfire threats in recent years.

## [Event Details]

According to BBC, as a heatwave sweeps across southwestern Europe, temperatures in France and Spain have risen sharply. Meteorological departments forecast that maximum temperatures in some areas will exceed 40°C. This extreme heat not only threatens public health but also poses significant challenges for firefighting operations.

Near Bordeaux, France, a wildfire that began four days ago has evolved into one of the country's most severe wildfire events in recent years. According to official statistics, the burned area has exceeded 420 square kilometers (approximately 162 square miles), forcing over 300,000 residents and tourists to evacuate. Local residents described to the media the fear and desperation as "walls of flame" approached.

The Madrid region is also experiencing its worst wildfires on record. Spanish fire departments report that multiple fire points are burning simultaneously, and rescue forces are nearing their limits.

French President Macron stated on social media that the government has mobilized all available resources to respond to the disaster, including requesting assistance through the EU Civil Protection Mechanism. Spanish Prime Minister Sánchez announced that affected areas would enter a state of emergency, with government financial support provided to help victims rebuild their homes.

## [Analysis]

This European wildfire crisis once again highlights the real threats climate change poses to human society. Over the past few years, the frequency and intensity of extreme heat events in Europe during summer have been increasing, with scientists warning this will become the "new normal."

From an environmental perspective, wildfires not only cause direct property damage and casualties but also release large amounts of carbon dioxide, further exacerbating global warming and creating a vicious cycle. It is estimated that the Bordeaux wildfire alone has released approximately 2 million tons of CO2.

From an economic perspective, tourism and agriculture have suffered heavy blows. Bordeaux is a world-famous wine region, and losses to vineyards may have long-term impacts on the local economy. Additionally, the costs of mass evacuations and firefighting operations will place a heavy burden on local governments.

From a policy perspective, the EU needs to reassess its climate adaptation strategy. While current emergency response mechanisms have proven effective, preventive investment and long-term planning are even more critical in the face of increasingly frequent extreme weather events.

## [Multiple Perspectives]

**Affected residents** expressed deep concern and anxiety. One resident evacuated from Bordeaux told the media: "We've never seen anything like this. The flames advanced like a wall, and we had only minutes to gather essentials before fleeing."

**Fire departments** face enormous pressure. A French fire commander stated: "High temperatures, dry conditions, and strong winds make the fire difficult to control. We're doing everything possible to protect residents' safety."

**Climate scientists** warn that such extreme weather events will become routine. Professor Johan Rockström of the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research noted: "We're at a turning point in the climate crisis. Without more aggressive emission reduction measures, disasters like this will become increasingly frequent."

**EU officials** emphasized the importance of unity in response. EU Crisis Management Commissioner stated: "Mutual assistance among member states is a core EU value. We've activated the Civil Protection Mechanism to provide support to affected countries."

**Tourism operators** expressed pessimism about the outlook. A spokesperson for the French Hotel Association noted: "Summer is peak tourist season, but wildfires and high temperatures have caused many tourists to cancel their trips. This is a heavy blow to the entire industry."
