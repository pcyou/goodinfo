## Event Summary

The Guardian reported on September 12 that Yemen's Houthis have rapidly advanced along the Red Sea coast, forcing roughly 50,000 Yemeni civilians to flee their homes. Most of the displaced come from frontline coastal towns and have moved toward relatively safer inland provinces and the borders of southern neighboring countries.

## Broader Perspective

The mass displacement is a humanitarian byproduct of the Houthis' recent capture of the port of Mokha and strategic Red Sea islands. From a governance standpoint, years of military operations have failed to weaken non-state armed capabilities and instead enabled them to entrench a more solid military presence along the coast. UN agencies and the International Committee of the Red Cross have signaled that an emergency humanitarian response will be activated, focusing on drinking water, medical care, and temporary shelter.

## Comparison of Perspectives

**The Yemeni government** has blamed the Saudi-led coalition's years of airstrikes for failing to curb Houthi combat power, arguing that this allowed the group to expand toward the coast. **The Houthis** state that their military operations target "foreign occupation forces" and will continue until government forces fully withdraw from coastal areas. **The United Nations** has called on all parties to protect civilians and civilian infrastructure and to open humanitarian corridors to prevent further escalation.
