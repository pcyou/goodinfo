## US Senate Passes Iran War Powers Resolution as Oil Plunges to Pre-War Levels

### Core Summary

The United States Senate passed an Iran war powers resolution on Wednesday, aimed at restricting the president's authority to conduct military operations against Iran without congressional authorisation. Simultaneously, as shipping through the Strait of Hormuz gradually returns to normal, the international benchmark Brent crude oil price has fallen to its lowest level since the US-Iran conflict began. The simultaneous occurrence of these two developments signals a profound shift in the Middle East's geopolitical landscape.

### Event Details

According to the BBC, President Trump has requested billions of dollars from Congress for Iran war funding, but the budget faces an uphill battle as some members of his own Republican party have sparred with the president over the issue. The war powers resolution passed by the Senate explicitly requires the president to obtain congressional authorisation before continuing military operations against Iran, posing a direct constraint on executive war-making authority.

On the diplomatic front, Vice President Vance recently held direct talks with Iranian counterparts at a luxury Swiss resort. IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi stated that inspectors would visit Iranian nuclear sites as part of a final deal. An Iranian minister said such access would only be possible within the framework of a comprehensive agreement with the US.

Meanwhile, energy markets have undergone a dramatic shift. Bloomberg reports that Brent crude has erased all wartime gains as the Strait of Hormuz reopening boosts supply, falling to levels not seen since before the US-Iran confrontation escalated. The BBC notes that energy prices have been on a wild ride since Iran effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz in response to US and Israeli strikes.

### Panoramic Perspective

The passage of the Senate war powers resolution represents another significant chapter in the constitutional struggle between legislative and executive authority in the United States. Since the Vietnam War era, the War Powers Act has been the core tool for Congress to constrain presidential military freedom of action, but in practice, successive presidents have often found ways to circumvent its restrictions. The resolution's passage amid an active Iran conflict sends a strong bipartisan signal of dissatisfaction with unlimited military engagement.

From an energy security perspective, oil prices returning to pre-war levels represent a significant boon for the global economy. The Strait of Hormuz carries approximately one-fifth of global oil shipments, and its navigation status directly affects international energy market stability. The strait's reopening not only alleviates supply concerns but also creates a more favourable economic environment for ongoing peace negotiations.

However, the rapid decline in oil prices also creates fiscal pressure for oil-producing nations. For Middle Eastern countries dependent on petroleum revenue, falling prices mean increased urgency for budget tightening, which could in turn affect their flexibility in peace negotiations.

### Multiple Perspectives

Senators supporting the war powers resolution argue it is a necessary step to restore Congress's constitutionally mandated war-declaring authority and prevent presidents from unilaterally dragging the nation into prolonged military conflicts. Opponents worry that constraining executive flexibility during an active conflict could undermine national security response capabilities.

Energy market analysts broadly welcomed the oil price decline but warned that uncertainty persists in the Middle East, and any breakdown in negotiations could trigger a price rebound. Foreign policy scholars note that Vance's direct contact with Iran represents a significant shift in US diplomatic strategy, but nuclear verification remains the largest obstacle to reaching a comprehensive agreement.
