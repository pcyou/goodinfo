## Core Summary

Keiko Fujimori was officially sworn in as Peru's president on Monday. After three previous election defeats in 2011, 2016, and 2021, Fujimori finally won this election by a narrow margin. This result not only ends months of political deadlock in Peru but also marks the continuation of Latin America's rightward political shift. The Wall Street Journal commented that Fujimori's victory further increases the number of right-wing governments in Latin America.

## Event Details

According to NPR and Reuters, Fujimori pledged to pursue economic reform and social stability at her inauguration ceremony. In her speech, she emphasized: "Peru needs unity and pragmatic policies to address current economic challenges."

Fujimori is the daughter of former Peruvian President Alberto Fujimori. Her father cracked down on terrorism and pushed economic reforms in the 1990s, but was also controversial for corruption and human rights issues. Alberto Fujimori remains in prison, but his political legacy still has considerable influence in Peru.

This election was fiercely contested. Fujimori defeated her left-wing opponent by less than two percentage points in the runoff vote. This result reflects Peru's deep social divisions and suggests that Fujimori's government will face significant resistance in pushing reforms.

## Panoramic Perspective

Fujimori's victory is the latest manifestation of the political pendulum effect in Latin America. In recent years, Argentina, Chile, Uruguay and other countries have successively elected right-wing governments, ending the dominance of the left-wing wave at the beginning of this century. The main drivers of this shift include: economic weakness leading voters to lose confidence in left-wing economic policies; deteriorating public security making people crave stronger governance; and vigilance against the Venezuelan-style socialist model.

However, Fujimori faces equally severe challenges. Peru's Congress is currently controlled by the opposition, meaning her reform agenda may face strong resistance. Additionally, the political controversies of the Fujimori family—particularly her father's historical baggage—could become a focal point for opposition attacks.

From a regional perspective, Fujimori's victory may further strengthen the power of Latin America's right-wing alliance. But analysts warn that if right-wing governments fail to effectively address economic inequality and social justice issues, they may sow the seeds for the next left-wing backlash.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporter View**: Fujimori's supporters believe she is a "pragmatic reformer." Carlos Pardo, president of the Lima Chamber of Commerce, said: "Fujimori's business background and stable stance will bring much-needed economic growth to Peru. She understands how to work with the private sector, which is what the previous administration lacked."

**Opposition Concerns**: María Espinoza, spokesperson for the left-wing coalition "Broad Front," warned: "Fujimori's victory could mean democratic backsliding. Her father's authoritarian rule brought disaster to Peru, and we cannot let history repeat itself."

**International Observation**: The Economist analyzed that Fujimori's policy stance is relatively moderate and unlikely to adopt radical right-wing measures. But she must find a balance between pushing reforms and maintaining social stability.

**Chinese Perspective**: A researcher at the Institute of Latin American Studies at the China Institutes of Contemporary International Relations noted that Fujimori's government may take a more pragmatic approach to China. "She focuses more on economic cooperation than ideological confrontation, which provides stable expectations for China-Peru relations."
