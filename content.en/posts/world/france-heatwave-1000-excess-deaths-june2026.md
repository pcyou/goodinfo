## Core Summary

France is enduring its most intense heatwave in recorded history. Official data shows over 1,000 excess deaths across the country during the past week of extreme temperatures. Records have been shattered across multiple cities, and the crisis is spreading eastward across Europe, underscoring the accelerating impact of climate change on public health.

## Event Details

According to Reuters, French public health authorities confirmed that excess mortality has surpassed 1,000 during the nearly two-week-long heatwave. Excess deaths refer to fatalities above the baseline for this time of year, typically linked to heatstroke, cardiovascular failure, and respiratory distress.

France's meteorological service reported temperatures exceeding 45°C in several southern cities, with Paris consistently above 40°C for multiple consecutive days. This is the most severe heat event since the 2022 European heatwave, with broader geographic impact and longer duration.

The Guardian reports the heatwave is sweeping eastward across the continent. Spain, Italy, and Germany face similar challenges. Multiple European airports have closed due to warped runways, rail services are under speed restrictions, and agricultural irrigation water reserves are critically low.

France has activated its "Red Heat Emergency Plan," opening public cooling centers, extending pool hours, and restricting outdoor construction work.

## Broader Analysis

This event once again places climate adaptation at the center of European policy. Despite Europe's relatively robust heat warning systems and public health infrastructure, the death toll reveals significant gaps in current adaptation measures.

The frequency and intensity of extreme heat events are accelerating. The European insurance alliance has warned that without major adaptation investment, heat-related economic and health costs will grow exponentially by 2050. This crisis may catalyze accelerated EU climate adaptation legislation.

## Perspectives

**Scientific community**: The World Meteorological Organization's European director stated such extreme heat will become the "new normal" within a decade, requiring fundamental rethinking of urban planning and public health resilience.

**Government**: French authorities claim to have taken the "most aggressive response in history," but opposition parties criticize the insufficient number of cooling centers and inadequate support for elderly people in rural and low-income areas.

**Civil society**: Environmental organizations have jointly called for the EU to enshrine a 2040 carbon neutrality target in law, accusing governments of "talking more than acting" on climate.
