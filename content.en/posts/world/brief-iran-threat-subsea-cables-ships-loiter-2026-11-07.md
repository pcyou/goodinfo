## Key Facts

Ars Technica reports that Iran has threatened to act against subsea cables if the United States escalates military strikes again. The report also notes that several ships have been loitering in waters near the relevant cables.

## Wider Perspective

Placing subsea cables on the list of potential targets changes the nature of the contest. Subsea cables are the physical substrate of global data flows and financial settlement, and their normal operating risk comes from earthquakes, trawling and equipment failure rather than armed action; once they are included in a list of strikeable targets, the threat extends from military confrontation into the infrastructure the global economy depends on. The practical force of such a threat usually rests less on demonstrable capability than on how many routes it drives to a level requiring additional insurance and redundant backup. For telecommunications and financial operators, the indicators worth tracking are navigational notice density in the relevant waters, changes in insurance rates, and whether repair or maintenance filings appear against specific routes.
