## Core Summary

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu arrived at the White House on Monday for a highly anticipated high-level meeting with U.S. President Donald Trump. The meeting takes place against the backdrop of ongoing tensions in the Middle East, with the United States facing increasing domestic pressure to take a stronger stance on regional conflicts.

## Event Details

According to NBC News, the Trump-Netanyahu meeting has officially begun at the White House. This follows Trump's recent rejection of calls to escalate military action, signaling that the U.S. prefers strategic restraint at this stage.

The meeting agenda covers multiple sensitive issues: first, the future of Iran's nuclear program is a core topic. Trump previously stated that if Iran is willing to suspend its nuclear program for 20 years, the U.S. could consider meeting its demands, opening new possibilities for a diplomatic solution. Second, tensions at the Israel-Lebanon border are also a key discussion point. Recent Israeli airstrikes in Lebanon have caused multiple casualties, and the U.S. has announced an extension of the ceasefire agreement. Third, reconstruction and humanitarian aid in the Gaza Strip will also be on the table.

Netanyahu's visit comes at a delicate time. On one hand, there are domestic divisions in Israel over his handling of regional conflicts; on the other hand, with the U.S. election approaching, Middle East policy has become a focal point of bipartisan competition. Analysts note that Trump needs to balance supporting the Israeli ally with maintaining regional stability.

## Panoramic Perspective

The deeper significance of this Trump-Netanyahu meeting lies in its reflection of a major shift in U.S. Middle East policy. Since taking office, the Trump administration has adopted a markedly different strategy from its predecessors—shifting from multilateral diplomacy to bilateral transactional diplomacy, from pressure and sanctions to conditional negotiations.

The Iran nuclear issue is the litmus test for this transformation. Trump's proposal for a 20-year suspension essentially trades long-term security guarantees for Iran abandoning its nuclear weapons capability. If successful, this ultra-long-term framework could completely reshape the Middle East's geopolitical landscape; if it fails, it could accelerate a regional arms race.

Israel's role is equally critical. The Netanyahu government has consistently taken a hardline stance on Iran's nuclear program, but may be willing to accept some form of compromise if the U.S. provides clear security commitments. The outcome of this dynamic game will determine the Middle East's security architecture for the next decade.

From a broader perspective, the trend of U.S. strategic retrenchment in the Middle East is now irreversible. Whether Trump or any other candidate, reducing ground military presence, relying on air power and regional allies, and focusing on great power competition has become a bipartisan consensus. This means regional countries need to assume more security responsibility, while also gaining greater autonomy.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporters of the meeting** believe Trump's conditional negotiation strategy offers a new path to break the deadlock. Former U.S. Ambassador to Israel Dan Shapiro points out: "The 20-year suspension proposal is bold, but it opens a window for diplomatic resolution. If Iran responds seriously, this could be a historic breakthrough."

**Critics** worry that Trump is too eager to reach a deal and may make too many concessions on key issues. Senior member of the House Foreign Affairs Committee Michael McCaul warns: "Any agreement must include strict verification mechanisms and consequences for violations, otherwise it will only give Iran more time to develop nuclear capabilities."

**Regional observers** note that the timing of Netanyahu's visit is telling. On the eve of the U.S. election, the Israeli prime minister's visit itself carries strong political signaling. Regardless of the meeting's outcome, it will have subtle implications for the U.S. election.
