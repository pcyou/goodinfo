**[Core Summary]**
Deutsche Welle reported that Germany's former intelligence chief has been formally arrested and placed in pretrial detention on charges of treason. Prosecutors allege the former official leaked German state secrets to a foreign intelligence service. Major German and English-language outlets have followed up with background details and potential implications.

**[Event Details]**
- **Subject**: A former head of a German intelligence agency has been formally charged with "conducting secret activities on behalf of a foreign intelligence service" and remanded into pretrial detention.
- **Core Allegations**: Prosecutors believe the official passed internal documents and operational information from the German Federal Intelligence Service to a foreign intelligence service, implicating German national security interests.
- **Investigation Background**: The case originated from anomalies detected by German counter-intelligence in internal audits and cross-checks against external intelligence, after months of investigation before formal judicial proceedings began.
- **Legal Framework**: Under provisions of the German Criminal Code on "conducting secret activities for a foreign intelligence service," the suspect faces serious criminal liability, with potential sentences of multiple years of incarceration.
- **International Reaction**: Azerbaijani authorities have officially denied any link to the case. Several European allied countries have expressed concern and requested that Germany brief them on developments in a timely manner.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**
**First**, the case directly challenges trust in the internal governance of the German intelligence system. The German Federal Intelligence Service is one of Europe's most important foreign intelligence agencies; a former head suspected of working for a foreign intelligence service means the intelligence system has been compromised at the "input end," and will push Germany to revisit vetting, rotation and oversight of intelligence personnel.

**Second**, the case will create subtle pressure on Germany's diplomatic relations with relevant countries. In a complex context where Russia, Iran or Azerbaijan may be implicated, the way Berlin weighs judicial independence against diplomatic cost will directly affect the depth and breadth of its intelligence partnerships.

**Third**, the case reflects the broader shift in the European security environment. With the Ukraine crisis ongoing and NATO internal trust under strain, cases of penetration of a single country's intelligence agency often have "spillover" effects — they signal to European states that intelligence security is no longer just a technical issue but an institutional one, requiring renewed resource commitment at the top of the system.

**[Comparative Perspectives]**
- **German Prosecutors**: Stated that the case is still in the investigation stage and will proceed according to law, emphasizing the independence of the German judicial system.
- **German Government Spokesperson**: Reaffirmed that intelligence agency operations are subject to dual parliamentary and judicial oversight, and pledged to launch an internal review as the case progresses.
- **European Partners**: Are closely monitoring the case details and emphasize that the reliability of transatlantic intelligence cooperation will not be shaken by a single case.
- **Azerbaijani Authorities**: Have explicitly denied any connection to the case and oppose any association of their country with the proceedings.
- **German Opposition Parties**: Demanded that the government submit a complete report to the parliamentary intelligence committee and assess whether similar risk points exist.
