## Core Summary

The United States and Iran have exchanged another round of missile strikes, with the five-month-old conflict showing no signs of resolution. Both sides have escalated military operations, worsening regional security. Iraq has stated it had "no prior knowledge" of US-Saudi joint operations, highlighting the complexity of the situation.

## Event Details

According to AP News, the US and Iran have once again traded missile attacks in a conflict that began in February and has now lasted five months with no quick resolution in sight. Both sides blame each other for provoking the hostilities and have vowed to take all necessary measures to protect their interests.

Sources indicate that the latest exchange involved multiple target areas including military facilities and strategic sites. US Central Command stated it successfully intercepted several incoming missiles and conducted precision strikes on targets inside Iran. Iran claimed its air defense systems shot down several US drones and retaliated with strikes on US military bases.

An Iraqi government spokesperson said Iraq had "no prior knowledge" of military operations occurring near its airspace, calling on all parties to respect Iraqi sovereignty. This statement reveals the complexity of the regional situation — even countries on the periphery of the conflict zone struggle to fully grasp developments.

Meanwhile, Saudi Arabia announced that 14 countries now support establishing a maritime alliance to protect shipping routes. This move is seen as a direct response to threats to shipping security in the Red Sea and Persian Gulf. Analysts note this indicates the conflict is evolving from bilateral military confrontation into a broader reshaping of regional security architecture.

## Panoramic Perspective

This five-month US-Iran conflict has far exceeded initial expectations in scale and intensity. Strategically, this is not merely a military contest between two nations but a reflection of deep adjustments in the Middle East power structure. The US attempts to use military pressure to force Iran to concede on nuclear issues and regional influence, while Iran maintains its strategic autonomy and refuses to compromise on core interests.

The spillover effects are becoming apparent. Threats to shipping security directly impact global energy supply chains, with oil price fluctuations increasing global economic uncertainty. Regional countries are being forced to choose sides, with traditional Middle East alliance relationships facing reshuffling. Saudi Arabia's move to lead a maritime alliance marks Gulf states shifting from passive response to actively shaping regional security architecture.

International mediation efforts have repeatedly failed. The UN Security Council has held multiple emergency meetings without reaching substantive resolutions. European countries call for restraint but lack effective intervention means. Russia and China advocate dialogue but have not driven meaningful progress. The trend toward prolonged conflict is reshaping the Middle East geopolitical landscape, with impacts extending far beyond military dimensions to profoundly alter regional economic cooperation models and national security strategies.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US Position**: The White House emphasizes military operations are self-defense aimed at reducing Iran's offensive capabilities and forcing it back to negotiations. The US government states it will continue working with allies to maintain regional stability.

**Iran Position**: Tehran insists its actions are legitimate responses to US provocation, emphasizing it will not yield to external pressure and calling on the international community to stop US "hegemonic behavior."

**Regional Countries**: Neighboring countries like Iraq express serious concern about conflict spillover and call for restraint. Gulf states like Saudi Arabia are actively engaging through alliance-building, attempting to influence the conflict trajectory while protecting their own interests.

**International Observation**: The UN and multiple international organizations call for immediate ceasefire and dialogue restart, but given the wide gap between both sides' positions, prospects for a peace agreement in the short term are not optimistic.
