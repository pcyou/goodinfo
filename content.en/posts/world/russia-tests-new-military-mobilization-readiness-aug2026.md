## Core Summary

The Wall Street Journal exclusively reports that Russia has initiated internal preparations for a new round of military mobilization, testing conscription capabilities and personnel databases across regional military offices. Ukrainian President Zelensky warns that Moscow may conscript at least 300,000 additional troops for the Ukraine battlefield, signaling a potential escalation of the two-and-a-half-year conflict with major implications for European security and global energy markets.

## Event Details

According to sources familiar with the matter, Russia's Defense Ministry has directed multiple federal subjects to update service-eligible personnel data and conduct systematic mobilization drills covering communications, conscription procedures, and medical facilities.

Zelensky stated in an evening address that intelligence indicates plans for at least 300,000 new conscripts, potentially pushing Russia's total force presence past one million. He called on Western allies to accelerate military aid deliveries and impose stricter sanctions.

The Kremlin declined to comment, stating only that "Russia has the capacity to protect its security interests when necessary."

## Analysis

The timing of this mobilization test is significant. With the U.S. election approaching and uncertainty over Washington's Ukraine aid policy, Moscow may see a strategic window. If 300,000 new troops are deployed, it would significantly alter the front-line balance of power, particularly in the Donbas and Avdiivka directions.

From a macro perspective, this reflects Russia's "war-sustaining" strategy — using continuous personnel deployment to exhaust Ukrainian resistance and Western aid patience. However, large-scale mobilization carries domestic political risk; the fall 2022 mobilization triggered an exodus of hundreds of thousands and strong middle-class backlash.

For global markets, escalation expectations have already pushed European natural gas futures up roughly 8%, with Brent crude also edging higher. A formal mobilization order could trigger new volatility in energy and grain markets.

## Perspectives

**Western Intelligence**: U.S. and NATO intelligence agencies confirm they are closely monitoring Russian movements, assessing the mobilization tests as "genuine preparation" rather than mere deterrence posturing.

**Russian Domestic**: Some military bloggers support new mobilization as necessary for "completing special military objectives," while others worry about economic pressure from labor shortages.

**Ukrainian Side**: Kyiv views this as a marker of Russia's "strategic failure" — the collapse of quick-victory hopes and a turn to attrition warfare.

**International Observers**: The UN Secretary-General's spokesperson called for restraint, warning that large-scale mobilization makes peace prospects even more remote.
