## Summary

Following FIFA's withdrawal of its private equity plan for the World Cup, UEFA has escalated its stance by formally declaring it has "lost confidence" in FIFA President Gianni Infantino's leadership. This rare public statement marks a historic low in relations between European football and FIFA.

## Details

AP News reported that UEFA's declaration of no confidence comes after FIFA was forced to abandon its plan to sell stakes in World Cup and other major events to private equity firms. BBC Sport simultaneously reported analysis of potential successors to Infantino, suggesting his position may face unprecedented challenges.

Infantino, who has led FIFA since 2016, has been pushing commercial reforms, but the failure of the private equity plan has significantly weakened his political capital.

## Perspectives

European football officials argue that Infantino's leadership style has been too autocratic, disregarding the interests of member associations. The public no-confidence vote reflects deep dissatisfaction with FIFA's governance model.

Reactions from other regions are more mixed, with some developing football associations still supporting Infantino's reform direction, believing his policies help expand football's global reach.
