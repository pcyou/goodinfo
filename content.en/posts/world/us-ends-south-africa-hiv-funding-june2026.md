## Core Summary

The US government formally announced on June 19 that it will terminate financial support for South Africa's HIV prevention programs. The stated reason is South Africa's position on the alleged "persecution of Afrikaners," which the US claims is inconsistent with American values. This decision has immediately sent shockwaves through the global public health community, as the United States has been the largest international funder of South Africa's fight against the HIV epidemic. US annual aid reportedly accounts for a significant proportion of South Africa's total HIV prevention budget, and the funding interruption could directly affect millions of patients receiving antiretroviral therapy.

## Event Details

According to BBC News, the US State Department stated in a declaration that, after a comprehensive assessment of South Africa's domestic human rights situation, the US has decided to redirect aid funding to South Africa. The statement specifically mentioned "systemic discrimination and violent threats facing the Afrikaner community," claiming that the South African government has failed to adequately protect the legitimate rights and interests of this minority group.

The South African government responded swiftly, strongly denying the existence of systemic persecution against Afrikaners. South Africa's Minister of International Relations stated at a press conference in Pretoria that the US accusations are "baseless and politically motivated," warning that this move would have "serious consequences" for bilateral relations. South Africa's Health Ministry expressed deep concern about the potential interruption of HIV prevention programs, emphasizing that this would directly affect millions of patients who rely on US aid for antiretroviral drugs.

Reportedly, the US provides approximately several hundred million dollars annually in HIV prevention aid to South Africa through the President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR). PEPFAR is a global public health initiative launched in the early 2000s that has successfully saved tens of millions of lives. South Africa is one of the program's largest recipients, with funding covering the full chain from testing and treatment to prevention.

## Panoramic Perspective

This decision has profound implications for the landscape of global public health governance. First, it marks a further trend toward the "politicization" of US foreign aid policy, linking public health assistance to diplomatic policy objectives. This approach could undermine the effectiveness of international aid, as disease prevention requires long-term, stable financial support rather than support that shifts with political winds.

From South Africa's domestic perspective, the funding interruption could trigger a public health crisis. South Africa has one of the highest HIV infection rates in the world, with approximately eight million people living with the virus. Antiretroviral treatment programs supported by PEPFAR funding cover millions of patients; once funding is severed, this could lead to treatment interruptions, increased viral drug resistance, and even rising mortality rates.

This decision may also affect US-China competition in the global health arena. China has been expanding its medical aid to Africa in recent years, and US reductions in aid to the continent could create space for China to expand its influence. However, China's aid model differs from the US approach, focusing more on infrastructure construction and personnel training, making it difficult to fully replace PEPFAR's direct support for drug treatment in the short term.

From a broader perspective, this incident highlights the fragility of the global public health system. When major donor countries politicize aid, global health security faces systemic risks. The World Health Organization and other multilateral institutions need to rethink how to establish more independent and sustainable financing mechanisms to reduce dependence on single donor countries.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US Government Position**: The US emphasizes this is a "difficult but necessary" decision based on human rights concerns. The State Department spokesperson stated that the US remains committed to global HIV prevention but requires recipient countries to meet basic human rights standards. Officials hinted that funding could be redirected to other countries or programs that "better align with American values."

**South African Government Response**: The South African Presidency issued a statement calling the US decision "disappointing and short-sighted." The South African government emphasized that the country has made significant progress in protecting the rights of all ethnic groups, and that the Afrikaner community enjoys full rights in political, economic, and cultural life. South Africa's Health Ministry has launched emergency plans, attempting to compensate for the funding gap through domestic budget adjustments and aid from other international partners.

**International Public Health Community**: The WHO Director-General expressed "deep concern" about the decision, calling on both sides to resolve differences through dialogue and avoid making AIDS patients victims of political disputes. The Global Fund stated it is assessing the specific impact of funding interruption on South Africa's HIV prevention work and is prepared to provide emergency technical support.

**Afrikaner Community**: Afrikaner rights organizations have complex attitudes toward this development. Some organizations welcomed international attention to their situation but worried that the HIV funding interruption would affect all ethnic groups. Some Afrikaner leaders stated they do not want to solve human rights issues at the expense of public health.

**Medical Expert Views**: Multiple international AIDS research experts have warned that treatment interruptions could lead to catastrophic consequences. A public health professor at the University of Johannesburg pointed out that antiretroviral therapy requires continuous medication; once interrupted, it not only leads to viral rebound but may also produce drug-resistant mutations, threatening broader public health security.
