## Core Summary

The Guardian reported that US President Trump called off a major airstrike campaign against Yemen's Houthi forces at the last minute, after which the Houthis took advantage of the window created by the suspension of US military action to advance and seize strategically significant terrain across multiple parts of Yemen. This rapid shift in the situation has quickly drawn widespread international concern about Middle Eastern security dynamics, the coherence of US Middle East policy, the safety of Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb Strait shipping, and the trajectory of regional proxy conflicts.

## Event Details

**Last-Minute Cancellation of Airstrikes and Decision Context**: According to The Guardian, the Trump administration had previously deployed preparations for a major airstrike campaign against Houthi-controlled areas, with related military preparations called off at the last minute. The report noted that the timing and specific decision-making mechanism for the cancellation remain not entirely clear, but the cancellation directly provided the Houthis with a window to readjust deployments and consolidate control areas. Defense analysts noted that the temporary cancellation of planned military operations often carries the strategic cost of opponents consolidating gains, and the relevant decision poses a challenge to the credibility of US deterrence in the Middle East region.

**Houthi Advances and Seizure of Strategic Terrain**: Following the cancellation of US airstrikes, the Houthis quickly advanced across multiple strategic directions within Yemen. The report noted that the terrain seized by the Houthis carries significant strategic importance, possibly including key transportation nodes connecting various regions of Yemen, terrain with potential surveillance and strike capabilities against Red Sea shipping, and forward positions that exert pressure on or threaten areas controlled by Yemeni government forces. Middle East affairs analysts noted that the Houthis' seizure of strategic terrain will further enhance their negotiating leverage and military initiative in Yemen's domestic conflict, and will continue to exert pressure on Gulf states such as Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates as well as international shipping security.

**Cascading Impacts on Red Sea Shipping and International Maritime Security**: The Houthis' advances in seizing strategic terrain have further heightened international concern about the safety of shipping in the Red Sea and the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. Since late 2023, the Houthis have frequently launched attacks against international shipping targets in the Red Sea, forcing many international shipping companies to divert around the Cape of Good Hope and significantly increasing global shipping costs and transit times. Many international shipping companies, insurance firms, and energy-importing countries maintain close attention to Red Sea security, and the Houthis' further control of strategic terrain may exacerbate their potential threats and leverage against international shipping.

**Coherence of US Middle East Policy and Strategic Credibility**: The Trump administration's decision to temporarily cancel the airstrikes has triggered extensive domestic and international discussion about the coherence of US Middle East policy, strategic credibility, and deterrence credibility. US congressional representatives, defense policy analysts, and Middle East affairs experts noted that frequent policy reversals and last-minute changes may weaken the credibility signals the US sends to regional allies and adversaries, and pose challenges to regional allies' security assurance expectations. At the same time, some analysts noted that the cancellation of airstrikes may reflect the Trump administration's reassessment of the complexity of the Yemen situation, the cost of military operations, and the risk of escalation, and the underlying logic of the decision merits further attention.

**Regional Allies and Multilateral Diplomatic Mediation**: The report noted that the Houthis' advances in seizing strategic terrain have attracted close attention from Gulf states such as Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, the UN Special Envoy for Yemen, and multiple international actors. Diplomatic analysts noted that the Houthis' strategic advance may prompt relevant parties to accelerate diplomatic mediation efforts and may also lead regional countries to adopt more direct intervention postures toward the Yemen situation. Progress on multiple fronts, including the UN framework for the Yemen peace process, regional security coordination, and the international community's joint response to Red Sea shipping security, warrants continued attention.
