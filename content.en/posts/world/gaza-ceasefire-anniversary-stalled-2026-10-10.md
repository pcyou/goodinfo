**[Key Points]**

The US-brokered Gaza ceasefire deal marks its first anniversary this week, and reviews from parties to the agreement show that progress has stalled on the key issues. The deal, struck a year ago to end hostilities in Gaza and open humanitarian access, has not delivered substantive breakthroughs on the main agenda items.

**[Details]**

- **Deal background**: The US-brokered Gaza ceasefire took effect a year ago and aimed to end large-scale hostilities and open humanitarian access corridors.
- **Progress review**: As the anniversary arrives, the parties' reviews of implementation show stalled progress on the key issues, with several milestones on the multi-stage path still unmet.
- **Humanitarian picture**: Humanitarian-access corridors into Gaza remain restricted under the anniversary review; international aid agencies are flagging fresh concerns over material access and onward distribution.
- **Multiple positions**: Regional mediators and UN agencies are calling for substantive talks to restart, emphasising that the central agenda items now cluster around security guarantees and reconstruction mechanisms.
- **What to watch**: A multilateral talks window has yet to be set; the coordination rhythm between regional mediators and the US side will be a key signal in the days ahead.
