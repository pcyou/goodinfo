BBC reports that a medical transport plane carrying six people has gone missing off the Massachusetts coast on a flight from Bermuda to Boston. The crew declared an emergency before contact was lost; air-traffic-control records show the aircraft appeared to be on an instrument approach near the Massachusetts coast when contact was severed.

The US Coast Guard and Massachusetts authorities have launched search operations covering offshore and coastal areas; search scope and duration will be adjusted based on the latest information. The aircraft is a small medical transport plane widely used for over-water patient transfers and organ transport, with relatively mature experience in such missions.

The FAA and the National Transportation Safety Board have opened investigations focused on aircraft maintenance, weather conditions, crew status and over-water operating procedures. The incident has reignited broader discussion within the aviation industry about over-water medical transport, emergency handling under instrument conditions and maintenance standards for small medical aircraft. BBC notes that, while the final cause awaits the official inquiry, the high-risk nature of over-water medical transport in adverse weather and instrument conditions is once again in focus, and the industry may undertake systematic safety reviews and procedural improvements in response.
