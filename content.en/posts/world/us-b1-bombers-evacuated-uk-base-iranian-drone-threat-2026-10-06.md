**[Core Summary]**
The U.S. Air Force has withdrawn all B-1 long-range bombers from its UK base amid concerns over a potential Iranian drone attack. President Trump publicly confirmed the evacuation was a preventive decision based on thoroughly verified intelligence, signaling an unprecedented pull-back of high-value assets from a key NATO forward base.

**[Event Details]**
- **Scale of Withdrawal**: All B-1B Lancer bombers have been pulled from RAF Fairford and are returning to U.S. domestic bases for redeployment.
- **Official Statement**: Trump told reporters at the White House "we had a threat," framing the pull-back as a measured precaution based on cross-agency intelligence, not overreaction.
- **Diplomatic Coordination**: Secretary of State Marco Rubio held parallel talks with his British counterpart, briefing London on the situation and coordinating next steps; the UK Ministry of Defence said it would share intelligence with NATO allies.
- **Iran Posture**: Iranian-backed proxy forces have escalated drone and anti-ship missile activity in the Persian Gulf and Red Sea in the past week, prompting U.S. Central Command to raise its regional alert level by one notch.

**[Panoramic Perspective]**
**First**, the B-1B is a supersonic penetrating bomber capable of deep strikes inside Iran, making it a cornerstone of U.S. deterrence. The withdrawal breaks with the long-standing "forward presence" doctrine and reflects a shift toward "resilient dispersal," keeping high-value assets at home or on mobile sea bases rather than fixed forward sites.

**Second**, Iran and its proxies have steadily upgraded their drone tactics over the past two years, and "swarm attacks + anti-ship missiles" now cover the entire Persian Gulf. The fact that large bombers must yield to cheap drones signals that unmanned warfare is rewriting the rules of great-power competition.

**Third**, the episode exposes a "coordination seam" within the transatlantic alliance on Iran. Britain did not publicly endorse a hardline posture but quietly fell in line with the U.S. decision, showing that NATO still defers to Washington on Iran policy while Europe lacks an independent deterrence capability.

**Multiple Perspectives**
- **Donald Trump**: The withdrawal is a precautionary step based on intelligence; U.S. security commitments to itself and its allies remain unchanged.
- **U.S. Department of Defense Spokesperson**: The redeployment does not affect overall U.S. combat capability in the Middle East; B-1s can continue missions from other bases.
- **Iranian Foreign Ministry**: Calls the threat "exaggerated," saying it is being used to manufacture a pretext for pressure on Iran.
- **UK Ministry of Defence**: Issued a low-key response, saying it will coordinate closely with NATO allies to ensure collective security.
- **Military Analysts (e.g., former U.S. defense officials)**: This is the first time since the Cold War that the U.S. has pulled B-1Bs back from a UK forward base, reflecting how the drone era is reshaping American war-fighting philosophy.
