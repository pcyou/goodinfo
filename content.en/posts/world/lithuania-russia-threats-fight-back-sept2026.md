## Core Summary

Lithuanian PM Sinkevičius told BBC Newsnight that Lithuanians have long felt security threats from Moscow and that the country was prepared to fight back if attacked while also having evacuation plans in place. The remarks highlight the deep security anxiety of NATO's eastern flank amid the ongoing Ukraine war.

## Event Details

Lithuanian PM Gintautas Sinkevičius, in an exclusive interview with BBC Newsnight, said Lithuania's geographic proximity to Russia has left its citizens in a state of long-running security anxiety. He emphasized that the Lithuanian military is fully prepared to fight back resolutely if attacked, but that the government has also prepared evacuation plans for civilians.

Lithuania sits on NATO's eastern flank and borders the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad. The country was occupied by the Soviet Union in the 1940s, and that historical memory has made Lithuania particularly sensitive to Russian security threats. Sinkevičius's remarks come as the Ukraine war enters its fourth year and NATO members continue to increase defense spending.

The interview also touched on Lithuania's progress in decoupling from Russian energy, NATO's eastern flank military deployments, and transatlantic solidarity. Lithuania has consistently advocated stronger sanctions on Russia and actively supports military aid and reconstruction efforts for Ukraine.

## Full Picture

From a geopolitical security perspective, Lithuania's stance reflects the broader mindset of NATO's eastern flank. Poland, Estonia, and Latvia face similar security pressures, and this collective anxiety is driving deeper NATO coordination on forward defense, command architecture, and force pre-positioning.

From a military defense perspective, although Lithuania has a population of less than three million, its defense spending exceeds 3% of GDP, well above NATO's 2% minimum threshold. The country has also actively pushed for European defense autonomy and supports expanding EU internal defense industrial capacity.

From a societal psychology perspective, the long-running security anxiety has had a profound impact on Lithuanian society. The existence of evacuation plans indicates that even within the NATO collective defense framework, Lithuania remains cautious about relying solely on external powers to guarantee its security, a mindset that will continue to shape its domestic and foreign policy decisions.

## Comparison of Viewpoints

NATO internal supporters argue that Lithuania's firm stance reflects legitimate concerns of eastern flank members and that NATO should further strengthen forward presence and rapid response capabilities to send clear strategic signals to Russia.

Some observers caution that while the existence of evacuation plans is pragmatic, it should not be over-interpreted as doubt about NATO's collective defense capability. Lithuania's remarks are more a security commitment to its domestic population than a strategic signal sent externally.

Russia typically denies posing a threat to NATO members and accuses the West of using the so-called Russian threat narrative as a pretext for military expansion. These opposing narratives will continue to shape the evolution of the European security landscape.
