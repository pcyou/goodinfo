A volcano in Indonesia erupted on a large scale on September 7, with the resulting ash cloud forcing multiple airports to extend closures and leaving approximately 270,000 travelers stranded. Authorities have activated large-scale emergency evacuation and rescue operations.

Indonesia's Center for Volcanology and Geological Hazard Mitigation announced on September 7 that a volcano in the eastern part of the country had a major eruption, with an ash column exceeding 15 kilometers in height. Affected by the volcanic ash cloud, multiple airports in the region were forced to extend their closures. Data released by Indonesia's Ministry of Transportation shows that the closed airports cover multiple domestic and international routes, stranding approximately 270,000 passengers. Authorities have activated large-scale emergency response, organizing the evacuation of nearby residents to safe areas, setting up temporary shelters, and providing basic living supplies. Airlines are offering affected passengers free rebooking and refund services. Volcanologists warn that volcanic activity may continue in the coming days and advise nearby residents to remain vigilant. Indonesia is located on the Pacific Ring of Fire and has numerous volcanoes, with frequent volcanic eruption disasters.

The volcanic eruption reflects the vulnerability of regions within global volcanic activity belts in coping with extreme natural disasters. As one of the countries with the most frequent volcanic activity globally, Indonesia has long faced systemic challenges including volcano monitoring, emergency evacuation, and post-disaster reconstruction. Although Indonesia has improved its volcano warning system in recent years, issues such as the impact of volcanic ash on air transportation, the difficulty of coordinating cross-regional evacuations, and the pressure of accommodating stranded travelers remain prominent. This incident also reminds countries that, against the backdrop of climate change and intensified geological activity, establishing transnational cooperation mechanisms for disaster emergency response and enhancing the aviation industry's ability to cope with volcanic ash are important topics in building a global disaster prevention and mitigation system.
