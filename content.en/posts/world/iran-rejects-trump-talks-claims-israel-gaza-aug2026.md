## Core Summary

Iran's Foreign Ministry explicitly denied on August 3, 2026 that nuclear talks with the United States would restart, directly contradicting President Trump's public claim that "negotiations will resume Monday." Meanwhile, Israel has intensified military operations in the Gaza Strip, moving in the opposite direction of Trump's proposed peace framework. The Middle East situation oscillates violently between diplomatic hopes and military escalation.

## Event Details

According to The Washington Post and BBC News, Trump claimed over the weekend that US-Iran talks would restart on Monday, but Tehran swiftly denied this. Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson stated that Iran is only communicating indirectly through Oman and has not engaged in direct negotiations with the US.

Iran emphasized that since the US and Israel launched military strikes against Iran in February, Tehran has repeatedly closed the Strait of Hormuz in retaliation. Iran is willing to dialogue on the basis of "equality and mutual respect" but refuses to negotiate under military pressure.

Meanwhile, according to Al Jazeera, Israel's military operations in Gaza continue to escalate, running counter to Trump's previously proposed peace plan. The Israeli military stated it would continue targeting operations until "complete security" is achieved.

## Panoramic Perspective

These events expose deep contradictions in the Middle East peace process. First, the trust deficit between the US and Iran remains enormous — even though both sides have expressed willingness to talk, they remain deadlocked over "who takes the first step." Iran insists the US must lift sanctions and cease military threats first, while Washington demands Iran halt uranium enrichment.

Second, Israel's independent operational capacity has become the biggest variable in the peace process. Even when the US proposes a peace framework, Israel may take unilateral military action based on its own security considerations, risking the "hollowing out" of any diplomatic effort.

From a geo-economic perspective, Middle East uncertainty continues to drive global energy price volatility. The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly one-fifth of global oil transport passes, directly affects international oil prices and global supply chain stability.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US Position**: The Trump administration insists the diplomatic window remains open, with the White House stating "Iran will eventually return to the negotiating table." However, domestic critics point out that repeated public statements disconnected from actual progress damage US diplomatic credibility.

**Iran Position**: Tehran emphasizes it will not "negotiate at gunpoint" but has kept indirect communication channels through Oman open. Analysts believe Iran still has motivation for dialogue under economic sanctions pressure, provided the US demonstrates sincerity.

**Israel Position**: The Netanyahu government has made clear it will not halt military operations, believing "security takes priority over the peace process." This stance diverges significantly from the US peace plan.

**International Observation**: EU diplomats called for "maximum restraint," China's Foreign Ministry reiterated support for resolving differences through dialogue, and Gulf Arab states seek balance between containing Iran and avoiding regional war.
