**[Core Summary]**

A Washington Post in-depth report on October 11 revealed that Ukraine has systematically crippled more than 50% of Russia's oil refining capacity over the past 18 months through precision drone tactics. Several major Russian refineries were severely damaged in recent strikes, with Kyiv claiming it had "taken out" Russia's largest refinery, while Moscow accused Ukraine of violating the just-announced energy ceasefire.

**[Event Details]**

- **Scale of impact**: Ukrainian officials stated that over the past 18 months, Ukrainian drones have destroyed or disabled more than 50% of Russia's oil refining capacity, spanning facilities from Krasnodar to the Volga region.
- **Latest strike**: Hours after Trump's energy ceasefire announcement, Ukraine launched a fresh drone attack on Russia's largest oil refinery, with Russian sources confirming severe damage to refining units.
- **Tactical profile**: Ukraine relies primarily on domestically produced long-range drones, some with ranges exceeding 1,000 km, capable of penetrating Russian air defenses through low-altitude night operations.
- **Russian response**: The Kremlin accused Kyiv of "negotiating while striking" and emphasized that any ceasefire requires simultaneous compliance.
- **Industrial impact**: Russian domestic fuel prices have tightened in recent months, with localized queuing at gas stations; the Russian government has activated export restrictions to stabilize domestic supply.
- **Western stance**: The United States and several European governments expressed "understanding" of Ukraine's precision strike strategy while voicing concern that it could undermine diplomatic mediation space.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**

Ukraine's drone campaign represents a paradigmatic example of modern "small beats big" warfare. Without deploying manned aircraft or large-scale missile forces, Ukrainian forces have achieved precision strikes deep inside Russian territory using relatively low-cost long-range drones. This asymmetric approach not only degrades Russia's war machine but also gives Kyiv critical leverage at the negotiating table — by continuously striking Russian energy infrastructure, Ukrainian strategy forces Moscow to confront the reality that "ceasefire equals damage limitation."

From an industrial chain perspective, the damage to Russian refining capacity is not the result of any single strike but the cumulative effect of hundreds of small- to medium-scale attacks over 18 months. Even as Russia repairs individual facilities, the persistent harassment makes stable recovery difficult, in turn affecting Russian refined-product exports to Asian markets. This "boiling frog" campaign of attrition is quietly reshaping the geopolitical economics of Russian energy exports.

However, precision drone strikes also carry escalation risks. If Russia concludes that Ukrainian attacks directly threaten its core economic interests, it may adopt more aggressive retaliation — including large-scale strikes on Ukrainian energy grids or cross-border operations targeting drone supply chain nodes upstream. Furthermore, if Kyiv strictly complies with Trump's just-announced energy ceasefire, Ukraine will lose this most important asymmetric lever, potentially ushering the attrition campaign into a new phase.
