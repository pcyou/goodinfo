**[Summary]**

The BBC reported on October 12 that Russian oligarch Roman Abramovich used backdated documents to evade substantial tax, with yachts and mansions worth hundreds of millions of pounds disappearing from the accounts of related entities. The report has once again put the operating models of Russian oligarchs in Western jurisdictions under the spotlight.

**[Event Details]**

- **Investigation method**: BBC reporters obtained and cross-checked financial filings submitted by Abramovich-linked entities in the UK and several European offshore jurisdictions, identifying systemic inconsistencies in document dates.
- **Asset concealment**: Yachts including Eclipse and other vessels with a combined value exceeding £600 million were reported to have disappeared from the balance sheets of related shell companies during specific periods; similar techniques were used for certain London and French Riviera properties.
- **Tax impact**: Through the backdated documents, related assets were reclassified as no longer belonging to taxable entities, with estimated evaded taxes in the tens of millions of pounds.
- **Legal reaction**: HMRC said it had noted the report's findings and would assess whether to launch a new investigation; Abramovich's legal team denied any wrongdoing.
- **Political context**: The revelations come amid Western sanctions on Russian oligarchs following the Russia-Ukraine conflict, intensifying debates over asset transparency.
