**[Core Summary]**
Axios, Fox News and NBC News reported that the US military has been ordered to prepare for possible strikes on Iran, with the White House weighing the timing ahead of the midterm elections. Oil prices rose and risk-off sentiment strengthened on Middle East tensions.

**[Event Details]**
- **Military Posture**: Axios reported that the Pentagon has directed relevant military departments to prepare plans and force deployments for possible action, spanning multiple US bases in the Middle East.
- **Political Timeline**: The White House is weighing the optimal moment within the US domestic political cycle; Fox News cited sources saying the president is choosing among several paths.
- **Pressure Signal**: At the same time, the White House has communicated an "easy way or hard way" choice to Iran through public channels, attempting to use diplomatic pressure to build political legitimacy for any military move.
- **Market Reaction**: CNBC and Bloomberg reported that international crude prices rose notably after the reports, reflecting concerns about supply disruption.
- **Congress**: Both parties remain divided on sanctions and a hard line on Iran, but the debate over an Authorization for Use of Military Force has not yet entered substantive procedures.
- **Regional Linkage**: Israel and Gulf states have moved into urgent consultations on intelligence sharing and coordinated defense, with the regional alliance system preparing for a potential escalation.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**
**First**, the order to prepare for strikes on Iran signals that the US-Iran contest is transitioning from sanctions and diplomacy into a "military preparation phase." Although the final decision still rests on political judgment, the military deployment itself has become part of the negotiating leverage. If Iran perceives genuine military pressure, it may be forced into larger concessions on its nuclear program and regional proxy networks; conversely, if it judges that the US will not truly act, it may harden its position.

**Second**, the timing of any action is being assessed against the "midterm election" political coordinate, giving the contest a clearly domestic-driven character. In the short term, the US administration tends to frame a hawkish foreign policy as a "guardian of security" image; but if military action escalates and produces casualties and oil price spikes, it could also backlash on the election calculus. The White House is therefore more likely to opt for a "limited, scalable and reversible" strike model rather than all-out war.

**Third**, oil prices and global risk-off sentiment are already leading the response. The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of global oil shipments; once the security of that corridor is in doubt, international crude markets reprice rapidly. Middle East oil producers and energy importers in Asia and Europe will all face a new round of imported inflation pressure, forcing central banks to again walk a difficult line between "fighting inflation" and "supporting growth."

**Fourth**, the Middle East may enter a new "multi-front linkage" phase. A potential strike on Iran is intertwined with Houthi attacks on Saudi airports, the Lebanon-Israel border conflict, and the reshuffling of forces in Syria and Iraq. Any major military move could trigger a multi-front response from Iran and its proxies, lifting the regional security situation from "stacked hotspots" to "systemic turbulence."

**[Comparative Perspectives]**
- **White House**: Emphasizes "all options are on the table," describing military preparation as a necessary means of "peace through strength," while leaving diplomatic space open.
- **Pentagon**: Has moved into the ordered preparation state, but officials have spoken cautiously, stressing that the final decision rests with civilian leadership.
- **Iran**: Warns that any military action will trigger a "full regional war," and is signaling pressure on US targets through proxy networks.
- **International Allies**: The UK, France, Germany and other traditional US allies reaffirm the position that "the Iran nuclear issue must be resolved diplomatically," while underscoring the importance of regional stability.
- **Market Observers**: See oil and gold risk premia persisting in the short term, with the medium-term trajectory depending on whether the US and Iran actually move toward military conflict.
