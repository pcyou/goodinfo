## Core Summary

U.S. intelligence agencies have delivered a significant assessment to the White House warning that Israel is likely to take military or diplomatic actions to undermine the recently reached U.S.-Iran peace agreement. The assessment, compiled from multiple intelligence agencies, reflects Washington's deep concerns about ally Israel's behavioral patterns in the Middle East. This comes as a ceasefire between Hezbollah and Israel was just agreed upon, leaving the entire Middle East in a delicate balance.

## Event Details

According to The Washington Post, citing multiple anonymous officials, U.S. intelligence has been monitoring Israel's military deployments and diplomatic movements over the past weeks, discovering that Israeli military and intelligence officials are developing contingency plans to address what they see as "threats" to their security interests from the U.S.-Iran deal.

The report indicates Israel's core concerns center on the deal potentially allowing Iran to retain some nuclear infrastructure and providing a pathway for lifting economic sanctions. Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu has long maintained a stance of "never allowing Iran to obtain nuclear weapons" and has repeatedly hinted at not ruling out unilateral military action.

The intelligence assessment also mentions Israel could influence the agreement process through strikes against Iran-linked targets in Syria or Lebanon, lobbying pressure on the U.S. Congress, or sharing "selective intelligence" to influence decision-making.

President Trump, when asked about the matter, said he "expects a complete ceasefire between Israel and Hezbollah" but avoided the question of whether he would constrain Israel. Meanwhile, Trump envoy Witkoff has headed to Switzerland to restart U.S.-Iran talks following the ceasefire agreement.

## Analysis

This intelligence assessment reveals a long-standing structural contradiction in U.S. Middle East policy: the conflict between America's role as a peace broker and Israel's role as a security stakeholder. Historically, similar tensions emerged during the 1978 Camp David Accords and the 1993 Oslo Accords negotiations.

The current situation is unique in that the U.S.-Iran peace deal is the cornerstone of the Trump administration's "New Middle East Order" strategy, and Israel's potential undermining actions could directly destabilize this strategic foundation. If the assessment is accurate, it means the U.S. must not only negotiate with Iran but also invest significant diplomatic resources in ally management.

From a geopolitical perspective, this also reflects deeper shifts in Middle East power dynamics. As Gulf states like Saudi Arabia ease relations with Iran, Israel's sense of isolation in the region may be growing. If U.S.-Israeli divisions on Middle East policy become more public, it could reshape the entire alliance system.

## Perspectives

**U.S. Position**: The Trump administration is pursuing a "fuzzy strategy" — unwilling to publicly criticize Israel while needing to advance the U.S.-Iran deal. The leak of the intelligence assessment may itself be a form of "signaling" to pressure Israel while preparing domestic opinion.

**Israeli Position**: Israel has not officially responded to the assessment, but its ambassador stated "Israel is committed to regional peace but reserves the right to self-defense." Israeli right-wing forces strongly oppose the deal, calling it a "betrayal of Israel's security interests."

**Iranian Position**: Iran's Foreign Ministry stated "no third party should interfere in bilateral U.S.-Iran negotiations," suggesting willingness to advance the deal excluding Israeli interference.

**Analysts**: Multiple Middle East experts note the Trump-Netanyahu relationship is at a "complex and delicate" stage, with both sides needing to maintain a surface alliance while having fundamental disagreements on core interests.
