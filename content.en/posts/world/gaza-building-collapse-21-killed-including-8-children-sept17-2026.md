## Executive Summary

A six-story residential building in northern Gaza City that had been damaged in previous fighting collapsed on September 16, killing at least 21 people including 8 children and injuring dozens more. About 10 families had been living in the structure, which was partially damaged in an Israeli strike last year. The incident has again drawn international attention to the fragility of civilian infrastructure across Gaza.

## What Happened

### The Collapse and Rescue

The collapse occurred in the early morning hours, when most residents were at home. Rescue teams worked for more than 24 hours using excavators and search dogs to look for survivors. Gaza civil defense authorities said the layered nature of the debris made the search extremely difficult and that some of those still trapped are unlikely to have survived. Shortages of medical supplies and heavy equipment further constrained the response.

### The Building's War-Damage Background

Residents and rescuers said the building had suffered structural damage in an airstrike last year, with key load-bearing elements and floor slabs compromised. Limited repairs were attempted afterward, but shortages of materials and ongoing safety risks prevented full structural restoration. At the time of the collapse, the building was being used by multiple families as one of the relatively safer shelters available.

### International Reaction

The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs expressed "deep sorrow" and called on all parties to keep rescue corridors open. Several international humanitarian organizations are coordinating emergency relief, including temporary shelter, medical supplies, and psychological support. Qatar and Egypt are using mediation channels to push for a temporary ceasefire to facilitate the response.

## The Bigger Picture

The incident has thrust Gaza's humanitarian crisis back into the international spotlight, with implications that go well beyond a single building collapse — touching civilian infrastructure, humanitarian access, and the enforcement of international law.

On civilian infrastructure, UN figures indicate that more than half of residential structures across Gaza show varying degrees of war-related damage. Buildings that should have undergone systematic post-conflict assessment and reconstruction remain in a "dangerous-use" state because of material blockades and ongoing hostilities. The result is a hidden but constant threat to daily life.

On humanitarian access, the emergency response was again constrained by restrictions on heavy equipment, medical supplies, and engineering personnel entering the area. Casualties that might have been avoided through timely rescue became unavoidable, underscoring the urgent need for reliable humanitarian corridors in conflict zones.

On international law, the protection of civilian infrastructure in conflict has returned to the top of the international agenda. The Geneva Conventions and their Additional Protocols explicitly require parties to protect civilians and civilian objects, but enforcement in Gaza has been severely challenged. The incident may become a catalyst for renewed accountability efforts.

## Comparing Perspectives

**UN view**: A spokesperson for OCHA said the protection of civilian infrastructure in conflict is a non-negotiable principle of international law and called on all relevant parties to take immediate steps to safeguard civilians.

**Mediator view**: Qatar's Ministry of Foreign Affairs expressed condolences for the victims and said it is coordinating with all parties to push for a temporary humanitarian ceasefire so the rescue operation can proceed unimpeded.

**Human rights view**: Amnesty International's regional director for the Middle East and North Africa called the incident a "wholly preventable human tragedy" and urged the international community to undertake systematic assessment and accountability for damage to civilian infrastructure.
