## Core Summary

Two firefighting helicopters collided mid-air near Alexandroupolis, Greece, killing two crew members while participating in wildfire suppression operations. The victims were a Danish citizen and a Greek citizen. A British pilot and another Greek crew member survived the accident. This incident highlights the high risks of aerial firefighting operations under extreme conditions.

## Event Details

According to Reuters, the accident occurred in northeastern Greece while both helicopters were participating in efforts to contain local forest fires. Greek fire services said the cause is under investigation, with initial speculation pointing to reduced visibility caused by thick smoke.

The surviving British pilot was transported to a local hospital in stable condition. Greek authorities have extended condolences to the victims' families and announced a full investigation into the accident's cause.

This incident occurred against the backdrop of southern Europe experiencing severe heatwaves and wildfire season. Greece, France, Spain, and other countries have recently facedsevere fire situations, with firefighting aircraft operating at high intensity and crews under tremendous work pressure.

## Panoramic Perspective

Aerial firefighting is one of the world's most dangerous professions. When helicopters fly low over fire zones to drop water, they must contend with thick smoke, strong air currents, high temperatures, and complex terrain. When multiple aircraft operate simultaneously, airspace coordination becomes significantly more challenging.

In recent years, as climate change has increased the frequency of extreme weather events, global wildfire seasons have lengthened and intensified, driving up demand for aerial firefighting resources. However, pilot training cycles are long, aircraft maintenance costs are high, and firefighting aviation forces in various countries generally face resource constraints.

The EU has strengthened fire resource sharing among member states through the "rescEU" mechanism, but during peak periods, demand still far exceeds supply. This incident once again reminds all parties to focus on firefighting aviation safety, including improving airspace management systems, introducing drones for auxiliary operations, and strengthening pilot fatigue management.
