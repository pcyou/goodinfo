**[Key Facts]**

Voters in the Canadian province of Alberta voted to reject a move toward independence in a referendum, backing continued membership in Canada. Alberta holds the world's fourth-largest oil reserves and sits at the centre of Canada's energy sector and federal politics.

**[What We Know]**

- **Referendum outcome**: The result showed voters choosing to remain in the federation, with the independence agenda put forward by separatist campaigners not carried.
- **Regional context**: Alberta and the federal government have long disagreed over energy policy, carbon pricing and cross-border pipeline projects, and the independence question had been seen as a marker of a new phase for the separatist movement.
- **Industry implications**: As the location of the world's fourth-largest oil reserves, Alberta's choice has direct implications for the direction of Canadian energy policy and for cross-border pipeline projects.

**[Wider Perspective]**

The outcome means energy-policy disagreements between Alberta and Ottawa have not disappeared, but they have temporarily lost the momentum of the independence question. Referendums of this type are often designed to preserve procedural space for later stages, so whether the issue returns will depend on how provincial party politics interact with the federal electoral cycle. For global energy markets the immediate impact is limited: nothing changes in the pace at which Canadian export pipelines and production capacity adjust. What merits tracking is whether the province can use the result to regain negotiating leverage on carbon policy and permitting, and whether the federal government moves to revive long-delayed cross-border pipeline projects.
