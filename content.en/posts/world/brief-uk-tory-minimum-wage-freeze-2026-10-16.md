**[Summary]**

The UK's Conservative Party outlined on October 16 a proposal to freeze the national minimum wage for five years to address rising youth unemployment, alongside expanded apprenticeship subsidies. Unions criticised the plan as harmful to low-income workers, while some employer groups expressed support.

**[Event Details]**

- **Proposal**: The current minimum wage rate would be frozen for five years with only limited indexation against inflation; subsidies for apprenticeships and youth employment programmes would be expanded in parallel.
- **Unions**: The Trades Union Congress criticised the plan as harmful to low-income workers' real earnings, urging the government to maintain regular minimum wage adjustments.
- **Business**: Some small business federations and hospitality/retail associations backed the plan, arguing that minimum wage increases have raised labour costs and depressed youth employment.
- **Government**: A shadow cabinet spokesperson said the proposal is part of internal policy debate and has not entered the legislative pipeline; the government will continue to review minimum wage adjustments.
