## Core Summary

The funeral of Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei commenced on July 4 at Tehran's Grand Mosalla. Thousands of black-clad mourners gathered in the mosque courtyard to pay their final respects to the supreme leader who was killed in joint US-Israeli airstrikes in late February. Khamenei's body will lie in state in Tehran for three days before being transported to his hometown of Mashhad for burial. Authorities expect 15-20 million people to participate in the commemorative events spanning Iran and Iraq, making it the largest funeral ceremony in Iran's history by population proportion.

## Event Details

### Unprecedented Scale

According to BBC News, much of central Tehran will be locked down over the weekend. Khamenei's body currently lies in the Grand Mosalla alongside the remains of family members who were also killed in the airstrikes. The mourning period will span six days: three days in Tehran followed by three days in other cities.

On Tuesday, the body will be moved to Qom, south of Tehran, where funeral prayers will be led at Jamkaran, one of Iran's most prominent and symbolic religious sites. The body will then travel to Mashhad, where it is expected to be finally buried next Thursday.

### Complex Public Sentiment

In the mosque courtyard, 37-year-old professor Reza told AFP: "We came to the funeral because we promised the supreme leader we would stand by him to the very end. For a long time, we shouted that we would sacrifice our lives for the leader, but it was he who sacrificed himself for us."

Arash Rahimi, 40, told Reuters: "Everyone here has come to avenge the blood of their supreme leader. As our leader has said, we have a blood feud with the United States. Our relations with the United States will never be good."

### International Context

Khamenei was killed during joint Israeli and US strikes on Iran in late February, an event that quickly escalated into a wider regional conflict. US President Donald Trump stated on Friday that Iran's government was "dying to settle" a peace deal to end the war, adding: "We gave them a week off for a funeral because we're nice."

## Comprehensive Analysis

The unprecedented scale of Khamenei's funeral represents not only a farewell to a leader but also a concentrated expression of Iran's political ecology and social sentiment. From a geopolitical perspective, this event marks a critical transition period as Iran enters the post-Khamenei era.

First, the funeral's scale reflects the cohesion of the Iranian regime under external pressure. Despite domestic dissent, the backdrop of the supreme leader's assassination has intertwined nationalist sentiment with religious emotion, creating powerful social mobilization. This sentiment may strengthen the current regime's legitimacy in the short term but could also intensify confrontation with Western countries.

Second, the revenge sentiment expressed during funeral proceedings warrants close attention. References to "blood feuds" represent not only accusations against the United States but also suggest Iran may adopt a more hardline Middle East policy. This stance will directly affect Iran's strategic choices on key issues including nuclear negotiations, regional proxy conflicts, and attitudes toward Israel.

Third, from a regional perspective, Khamenei's passing and subsequent power transition will reshape the Middle East's balance of power. As the core supporter of Shia forces, changes in Iran's leadership could affect the strategic direction of proxy forces such as Hezbollah in Lebanon and the Houthi movement in Yemen, thereby altering the entire Middle East's security architecture.

Finally, while Trump's remarks about "giving a week for the funeral" appear casual, they actually reflect America's strategy to find balance between hardline military action and diplomatic resolution. This posture demonstrates toughness to domestic audiences while leaving space for future negotiations.
