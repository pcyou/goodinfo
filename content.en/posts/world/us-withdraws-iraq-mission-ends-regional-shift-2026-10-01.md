## U.S. Withdraws All Forces From Iraq, Ending 12-Year Military Mission as Regional Power Balance Shifts

### Core Summary

According to The Washington Post, the United States has formally withdrawn all forces from Iraq, concluding a 12-year military presence. Pentagon officials confirmed the historic moment in an official statement, marking the end of large-scale U.S. military operations in Iraq that began in the era of counterterrorism campaigns. President Trump publicly described the withdrawal as the end of an "excursion from hell," and the Middle East regional balance of power is entering a period of deep recalibration. Iran's regional influence is expected to grow, while the potential threat from residual extremist organizations cannot be ignored.

### Event Details

According to The Washington Post, over the past several months the United States and the Iraqi government conducted multiple rounds of negotiations regarding the status of U.S. forces in Iraq. Ultimately the two sides reached agreement on a withdrawal timetable and residual military arrangements. The last U.S. military units withdrew in phases over the past week, and the Pentagon has formally confirmed that no permanent combat troops will remain in Iraq.

Pentagon Chief Spokesman Sean Parnell issued a statement detailing the historical context of the end of the U.S. military mission in Iraq. The statement noted that the original deployment began as a response to international terrorism, and gradually transitioned to training Iraqi security forces, supporting local counterterrorism operations, and providing logistical support for U.S. and coalition forces. Over the past twelve years, U.S. forces and Iraqi security forces fought side by side and achieved significant results in multiple critical campaigns.

The statement also emphasized that the withdrawal does not mean the end of U.S. commitments to regional security. The United States will continue to maintain security cooperation with regional allies through diplomatic coordination, intelligence sharing, and flexibly deployed rapid response forces. Parnell pointed out that any threat to U.S. interests will receive a swift and decisive response.

President Trump described the withdrawal in public remarks as the United States' return from an excursion from hell, calling it a fulfillment of promises to voters. Multiple media reports note that domestic U.S. support for long-term overseas deployments has continued to decline, and the withdrawal is also seen as a signature decision of the Trump administration's "results-oriented, no-indefinite-engagement" foreign policy.

At the same time, Middle Eastern research institutions and diplomatic circles are closely watching the post-withdrawal regional rebalancing. Multiple analysis institutions point out that over the past several years Iran has significantly expanded its political influence in Iraq through engagement with Shia political forces, economic cooperation, and security coordination. After the U.S. withdrawal, Iran-affiliated armed forces are expected to see further growth in regional influence, which may reshape the Middle East political and security landscape.

### Panoramic Analysis

The U.S. withdrawal from Iraq is a watershed event in the evolution of Middle Eastern geopolitics in the 21st century. From a broader historical perspective, the U.S. military presence in Iraq has gone through multiple role transitions from invasion, counter-occupation, stabilization operations to counterterrorism cooperation, each accompanied by profound changes in the U.S. domestic political climate and international strategic environment. This full withdrawal is not only a symbol of the end of the Middle East counterterrorism era, but also a clear signal that U.S. global military deployments are entering a new cycle of strategic contraction.

From the regional security perspective, the withdrawal has left a significant power vacuum. Over the past twelve years, U.S. forces not only provided training and operational support to Iraqi security forces, but also to some extent played the role of a balancer among Iraq's various domestic political factions. Once U.S. forces withdraw, this external balancing mechanism will face a vacuum, and political coordination among Sunni, Shia, and Kurdish forces inside Iraq will enter a more autonomous phase, which could both bring new political balance and trigger new internal tensions.

A deeper impact lies in the fact that Iran, as the most influential Shia power in the region, will see its decades-long "sectarian ties" with Iraq gain more room for maneuver after the withdrawal. Iran-affiliated armed forces, Shia political alliances, and economic cooperation networks are expected to further deepen integration with Iraq, which will give Iran more solid strategic depth in confronting Israel, influencing situations in Syria and Lebanon, and competing in the Gulf region.

From the perspective of U.S. strategy itself, the full withdrawal signals a shift in the focus of U.S. Middle East policy from forward military presence to flexible response and diplomatic leadership. This strategic adjustment reflects both the U.S. domestic fatigue with the political costs of long-term overseas deployments and the broader trend of great power competition shifting toward the Indo-Pacific and high-tech domains. However, whether this strategic contraction will bring new instability to the Middle East and whether it will expose traditional U.S. allies in the region to greater security pressures will become important topics for international relations observation in the coming years.

### Multi-Perspective Comparison

Proponents of the withdrawal argue that the twelve-year military operation in Iraq has essentially achieved its core objectives, and the marginal returns of maintaining large-scale forces continue to decline while political and personnel costs continue to rise. Proponents also point out that the withdrawal frees up valuable strategic resources for the United States, enabling focus on more pressing national security issues such as the Indo-Pacific and high-tech competition. Iraq's own security capabilities have also improved significantly over the past decade, providing a basis for assuming responsibility for its own security.

Cautious voices emphasize that after the U.S. withdrawal, residual extremist organizations in the Middle East may exploit the power vacuum to expand again, and there is also a risk of breakdown in domestic Iraqi factional coordination. The cautious side also points out that Iran's further rise in influence could trigger a new round of Iran-Israel confrontation in the Middle East, further complicating an already turbulent regional security landscape. In addition, some traditional U.S. allies worry that the signal sent by the withdrawal may affect U.S. credibility regarding security commitments to its global allies.
