## Core Summary

President Trump issued a stark warning, threatening to bomb Oman if it "gets in the way" of US-Iran nuclear negotiations. The statement comes as the 60-day negotiation period nears its end, with significant disagreements remaining between the two sides.

## Event Details

According to multiple media reports, Trump directly threatened Oman in an interview, stating that military action would be considered if Oman "obstructs" the US-Iran negotiation process. Oman has long played a mediating role in US-Iran relations, and this threat has drawn widespread international attention.

The US-Iran negotiations have now entered a critical phase, but significant disagreements remain on core issues such as nuclear program restrictions and sanctions relief. Trump's threatening remarks are seen as part of a strategy to apply maximum pressure on Iran and its regional allies.

## Panoramic Analysis

These threatening remarks reflect the complexity and unpredictability of US Middle East policy. A traditional mediator receiving direct threats may undermine regional countries' trust in America's mediation capabilities.

From a geopolitical perspective, this move could backfire, prompting Iran to adopt an even harder line rather than making concessions as expected. It also reflects internal struggles within the US during its strategic adjustment in the Middle East.

## Multiple Perspectives

**The White House** has neither clarified nor withdrawn Trump's specific remarks. A spokesperson only stated that the president reserves all options to protect US interests.

**Regional analysts** worry that such threats could undermine Oman's position as a neutral mediator, potentially affecting the effectiveness of future diplomatic efforts.

**International observers** note that issuing military threats during a critical negotiation period may be counterproductive, prompting the other side to take a harder stance rather than compromise.
