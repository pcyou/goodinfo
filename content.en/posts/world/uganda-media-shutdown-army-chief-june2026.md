## Core Summary

Uganda's military chief has ordered the shutdown of the country's two most prominent media organizations — NTV and Daily Monitor. Both outlets say their offices in the capital Kampala are under "military siege," with all editorial operations forced to halt. The move has triggered international concern over press freedom in Uganda.

## Event Details

According to the BBC, General Muhoozi Kainerugaba, commander of the Uganda People's Defence Forces, issued the order on June 28 to immediately cease operations at Daily Monitor and NTV. Both are Uganda's most influential independent news organizations, known for investigative reporting and government accountability.

The two media houses issued emergency statements on social media, saying their offices are surrounded by military personnel and journalists are barred from entering or leaving. NTV's broadcast signal has been cut, and the Daily Monitor website is inaccessible.

The specific justification for the shutdown has not been made clear. Analysts speculate it may be linked to recent investigative reports by both outlets into government military operations and corruption allegations. The Ugandan government spokesperson has not issued an official response.

## Broader Analysis

This media shutdown is the latest in a pattern of press freedom erosion across the African continent. In recent years, multiple African governments have cracked down on independent media citing "national security" and "social stability."

As a key member of the East African Community and African Union, Uganda's governance trajectory carries significant demonstration effect for the region. If the military can shut down major media without accountability, it sends a chilling signal across East Africa.

Reporters Without Borders' latest global press freedom index shows Uganda's ranking has been declining for years. This event may further damage the country's international image and affect foreign investment and aid programs.

## Perspectives

**Media**: Both shut-down outlets condemned the military action as "unconstitutional and illegal," calling on the international community to pressure authorities to restore press freedom immediately.

**International organizations**: Amnesty International and the Committee to Protect Journalists issued a joint statement expressing "grave concern" and urging Uganda to rescind the shutdown order.

**Regional observers**: Diplomatic sources from neighboring East African countries expressed concern privately but declined to publicly comment on "internal affairs." The African Union has not issued an official statement.
