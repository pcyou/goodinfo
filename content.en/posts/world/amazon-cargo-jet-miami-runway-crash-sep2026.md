An Amazon cargo plane veered off the runway and caught fire at Miami International Airport on September 7, killing at least five people. Federal investigators have begun probing the cause of the crash, with preliminary reports suggesting the accident may be related to severe weather.

The Amazon Air cargo plane skidded off the runway upon landing at Miami International Airport and subsequently caught fire. The accident killed at least five people and injured multiple others. Rescue workers rushed to the scene, and the fire was brought under control within two hours. The U.S. Federal Aviation Administration and the National Transportation Safety Board have jointly launched an investigation. Preliminary reports indicate that the runway area may have experienced heavy rain and crosswinds during landing, but the specific cause remains under investigation. Amazon issued a statement expressing deep condolences to the families of the victims and pledged full cooperation with the investigation. Miami International Airport was temporarily closed, with multiple flights canceled or delayed.

The incident has once again raised concerns about safety oversight amid the rapid expansion of the air cargo industry. As one of the world's largest cargo airlines, Amazon Air has rapidly expanded its fleet in recent years, but accompanying issues such as safety standards, personnel training, and operational pressure have become increasingly prominent. At the same time, the impact of increasingly frequent extreme weather on aviation operational safety is also growing. How to balance aviation efficiency with operational safety in the context of climate change is becoming one of the core challenges facing the global aviation industry.
