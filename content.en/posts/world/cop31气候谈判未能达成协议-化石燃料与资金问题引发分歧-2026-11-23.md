**Core Summary**

COP31 climate summit concluded without a major agreement after record-breaking extended negotiations, with deep divisions over fossil fuel phase-out and climate finance.

**Event Details**

The 31st UN Climate Change Conference (COP31) failed to reach consensus on key issues even two days after the scheduled deadline. Main points of contention included whether to explicitly mention "fossil fuel phase-out" in the final document and how much financial support developed nations should provide to developing countries.

Negotiators noted that the gap between developed economies and developing nations remains substantial. The former emphasizes the urgency of global emissions reduction, while the latter demands that major historical emitters take greater responsibility and provide substantive financial support.

**Panorama Analysis**

The failure of COP31 climate negotiations once again highlights the deep divisions globally on addressing climate change. The contradictions between developed and developing nations regarding financial support and technology transfer have become increasingly sharp, while lobbying activities by fossil fuel interest groups have also influenced the negotiation process. This outcome means that global emission reduction targets may be further delayed, and the frequency and intensity of extreme weather events are expected to continue increasing. Governments worldwide need to reassess their climate commitments and seek more inclusive and feasible solutions in future negotiations.

**Multiple Perspectives**

Environmental organizations criticized negotiators for failing to demonstrate sufficient political courage, calling for immediate adoption of more aggressive emission reduction measures. Representatives from developing countries emphasized the principle of historical responsibility, demanding adequate financial support from developed nations. Fossil fuel-producing countries warned that too rapid a transition could harm economic stability. The scientific community pointed out that current progress is far insufficient to achieve the 1.5-degree temperature control target set by the Paris Agreement.
