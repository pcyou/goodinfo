## 📌 Editorial Commentary

The evolution speed of the Nvidia storyline exceeds market expectations. Huang's agreement to testify before Congress formally elevates a corporate scandal to a national security issue. The board's contact with Rene Haas sends two signals: first, successor selection has entered substantive stages; second, external candidates are being seriously considered. Haas worked at Nvidia for seven years, gaining deep understanding of company culture and technical direction, while also accumulating experience managing complex ecosystems at Arm. This background combination precisely addresses Nvidia's current dual challenges: maintaining technological leadership while coping with increasingly complex compliance environments.

OpenAI's ChatGPT Wallet launch marks a key milestone for agent commerce moving from concept to reality. Previously, agents could only provide recommendations or generate content; now they can complete purchases on behalf of users with item-by-item confirmation. The core innovation lies in security design: agents do not handle real card numbers, each transaction completed with tokens issued by card organizations limited to that specific transaction. The initial phase targets only adult professional subscribers in the US on a limited basis, and OpenAI is not taking transaction fees, indicating the company's primary goal is establishing merchant relationships and card organization channels rather than short-term profitability. This restraint may actually accelerate ecosystem formation.

SpaceX Starship Flight 15's success and failure need separate evaluation. Successful orbit achievement and satellite deployment prove the spacecraft body's reliability continues improving, but booster catch failure exposes the complexity of recovery systems. The company retained the spacecraft body's return catch attempt for Saturday's mission tail segment; if successful, it will be the first time in human spaceflight history to capture an orbital-class spacecraft. This gradual iterative strategy is precisely why SpaceX can advance rapidly: each test flight validates part of the capability, leaving failed parts for next improvement.

The FAA nationwide ground stop reveals a long-neglected issue: technical debt in critical infrastructure. The Transportation Secretary attributed the cause to air traffic control systems still using decades-old analog equipment, which is not just a funding issue but systemic risk. When an airline depends on cloud services from a few tech companies, one interruption can affect millions of users; similarly, when air traffic control systems rely on outdated hardware, one communication failure can ground all national flights. This fragility becomes amplified in more digitized systems.

Bitcoin's current decline is most notable for its synchronization with macro environment. Fed Chair Warsh will host an AI and inflation forum in San Francisco on November 16, adding another variable to interest rate paths. With 10-year Treasury yields at high levels and producer prices up 6.5% year-over-year, capital costs for all long-duration assets rise simultaneously. Bitcoin breaking below the 200-week moving average for the first time and triggering nearly $800 million in liquidations represents typical risk appetite contraction rather than any single narrative driver. What truly needs attention is changes in liquidity sources: spot funds continuing net outflows, institutional holdings beginning to diverge, plus regulatory uncertainty pushing up compliance costs, the industry's capital inflow channels are simultaneously narrowing and becoming more expensive.
