## Executive Summary

A US-Saudi nuclear cooperation agreement widely regarded as a Middle East diplomatic breakthrough has encountered unexpected complications. According to The Washington Post, the Trump administration suddenly added new conditions related to Israel's security concerns at the final stage, pushing the deal toward potential collapse. Saudi officials expressed dissatisfaction, arguing the last-minute changes violated previous negotiation consensus.

## Event Details

Previously, the US administration had approved in principle on July 22 a framework agreement allowing Saudi Arabia to develop a civilian nuclear program. Under this agreement, Saudi would build nuclear power plants under International Atomic Energy Agency supervision to meet growing domestic energy demand and advance economic diversification.

However, The Washington Post's latest reporting reveals that during the final text finalization stage, the Trump administration added conditions requiring Saudi to accept additional Israel security-related provisions within the nuclear framework. While specific conditions remain undisclosed, sources indicate this may involve some form of coordination or restrictions between Saudi and Israel in nuclear technology.

Saudi's Energy Minister stated carefully that Saudi "respects all partners' concerns" but emphasized it "will not accept any unilateral demands that undermine national sovereignty and development rights."

## Panoramic Analysis

This development reflects the complex dynamics of Middle Eastern geopolitics. First, while Israel has not publicly opposed Saudi's nuclear program, it has privately pressured the US through diplomatic channels to impose strict caps on Saudi nuclear capabilities. Trump's new conditions likely represent a response to Israeli security concerns.

Second, Saudi faces a dilemma. On one hand, the nuclear energy plan is central to Crown Prince's "Vision 2030" economic transformation strategy; on the other hand, overly stringent conditions could trigger nationalist backlash domestically.

Third, delays in this agreement could produce chain effects. If Saudi cannot obtain nuclear capabilities through peaceful channels, it may reconsider alternative options, including cooperation with other major powers, further complicating the Middle Eastern nuclear landscape.

From a broader perspective, this incident reflects the persistent tension in US Middle East policy between "ally balancing" and "strategic interests." The Trump administration must maintain its traditional alliance with Saudi while responding to Israeli security concerns and preventing nuclear proliferation risks—balancing these three objectives is extremely delicate.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Saudi Position**: Saudi officials emphasize the peaceful nature of their nuclear program and state willingness to accept the strictest international supervision. However, Saudi media and commentators generally believe the US additional conditions "exceed reasonable bounds."

**Israeli Position**: Israeli security institutions approach Saudi nuclear capabilities with caution, but official statements remain relatively restrained. Some Israeli lawmakers publicly support the US stance of "maintaining maximum transparency on nuclear issues."

**US Domestic Division**: Lawmakers supporting the agreement argue that linking nuclear cooperation to Israel issues "unnecessarily complicates a bilateral agreement"; opponents worry that proceeding without fully safeguarding Israel's security advantage is "irresponsible."

**International Observation**: Former IAEA officials note that Saudi's civilian nuclear needs are legitimate, but politicizing the agreement could undermine the credibility of the international nuclear non-proliferation system.
