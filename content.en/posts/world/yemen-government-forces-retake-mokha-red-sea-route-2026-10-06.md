**[Key Facts]**
Yemeni government forces on October 5 announced the recapture of Mokha, the strategic Red Sea port city, and declared effective control of the Bab el-Mandeb waterway — the first time since 2024 that pro-government troops have pushed to this critical chokepoint. The advance compresses the Houthi foothold that has threatened global shipping for nearly two years.

**[What We Know]**
- **Military progress**: A government spokesperson said on state media that within the past 72 hours forces had taken full control of Mokha port and surrounding high ground, including multiple Red Sea coastal observation posts.
- **International backing**: The Saudi-led coalition provided air cover and intelligence; US Central Command carried out at least four strikes on Houthi missile positions over the past two weeks.
- **Shipping impact**: About 12% of seaborne oil and 30% of container traffic transits the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. War-risk insurance premiums on the Singapore–Rotterdam route have dropped roughly 18% since mid-September.
- **Next phase**: Government forces are advancing along the coast toward Hodeidah, but the Houthis still control that city and Yemen's mountainous interior, making a full recapture unrealistic in the short term.

**[Wider perspective]**
**First** — Control of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait has long been a barometer of Middle Eastern geopolitical influence. The Saudi-led coalition's first decisive maritime-chokepoint gain in nearly a decade reshapes the contest: the next battleground will shift inland toward Houthi-held Hodeidah and Sana'a.

**Second** — Red Sea shipping safety is a critical node in global supply chains. The fall of Mokha should gradually ease the "hidden surcharge" on European consumer goods, autos, and energy imports. However, if the Houthis pivot to asymmetric tactics — unmanned surface vessels, sea mines, coastal anti-ship missiles — residual shipping risk will remain significant.

**Third** — The episode reveals subtle realignments in Middle Eastern proxy dynamics. Saudi-Emirati-Qatari coordination on Yemen has visibly tightened, while Iranian resupply to the Houthis has been degraded by Israeli and US airstrikes on northern Yemen, raising the cost of sustaining the "Iran-Houthi" axis.

**[Viewpoints Compared]**
- **Yemeni government spokesperson**: Frames the Mokha recapture as "the first stage of restoring national sovereignty," with the next goal being the liberation of Sana'a.
- **Houthi senior official**: Via Houthi media, calls the advance an "occupation" and threatens retaliation against targets inside Saudi Arabia and the UAE.
- **Saudi Defense Ministry**: A muted statement "supports the legitimate government in maintaining territorial integrity," without detailing military involvement.
- **US Central Command**: Says it has coordinated with the coalition to strike "illegal armed groups threatening international shipping" and reserves the right to "further action."
- **International Chamber of Shipping (ICS)**: A cautiously welcome response, noting that meaningful insurance premium reductions require at least four more weeks of observation.
