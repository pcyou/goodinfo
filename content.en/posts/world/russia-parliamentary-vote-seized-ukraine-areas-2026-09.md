## Core Summary

The Associated Press reports that Russia held parliamentary elections this Saturday in several territories seized from Ukraine since 2022. The Ukrainian government and multiple Western governments have clearly stated they do not recognize the legitimacy or results of these elections, accusing Russia of further violating international law and Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity.

## Event Details

According to reports, Russia's Central Election Commission has published relevant voting data, claiming the elections covered regions in southern Ukraine including Zaporizhzhia and Kherson, as well as eastern Ukraine's Donetsk and Luhansk regions. The voting format included both in-person polling stations and remote electronic voting, with Russian authorities claiming voter turnout met the legal threshold.

The Ukrainian Presidential Office reiterated that these territories remain part of Ukraine under international law, and any elections held there are "illegal and invalid." The European Union, the United Kingdom, and the United States have all reaffirmed their support for Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity and do not recognize any form of election results. International observers were not invited to monitor the voting process, and the transparency and fairness of the elections have been widely questioned.

Analysts note that Russia's move is intended to consolidate de facto control over occupied areas through institutional means, laying an administrative foundation for possible long-term management while demonstrating an image of "normal governance" to domestic and foreign audiences. However, the broad non-recognition by the international community means the relevant election results are unlikely to receive any legitimate diplomatic endorsement.
