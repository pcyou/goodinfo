## Event Summary

Sustained Middle East tensions have kept international oil prices elevated, with US diesel retail prices breaking through the $6 per gallon threshold. According to multiple mainstream media reports, geopolitical risk premia combined with supply chain factors have once again made energy markets a focus of attention.

Market analysts noted that security conditions around the Strait of Hormuz, the standoff between Iran and Gulf states, and continued disruption to Red Sea shipping have all provided significant support to crude oil and refined product prices. The fact that diesel prices have crossed the psychologically important $6 per gallon threshold will further push up logistics and transportation costs, and may leave fresh marks on upcoming inflation data. Energy-intensive industries and consumer goods sectors that depend on road transport are expected to bear the brunt of higher operating costs.

From a policy perspective, decision makers face a dilemma in responding to the energy price shock: on one hand, geopolitical tensions make it difficult for strategic reserve releases to address the underlying problem; on the other hand, allowing prices to pass through to consumers will further increase the burden on households and businesses. Multiple economists have warned that persistently high energy prices may delay the process of inflation returning to target, and could have a significant impact on the next direction of global monetary policy.
