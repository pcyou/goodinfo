## Core Summary

Peru's electoral court has officially declared right-wing candidate Keiko Fujimori the winner of the June 7 presidential election runoff. She defeated left-wing candidate Roberto Sánchez with 50.135% of the vote to 49.865%, a margin of less than 50,000 votes. This is the fourth time the 51-year-old has sought the presidency, having previously run unsuccessfully in 2011, 2016, and 2021. Her victory, coinciding with the election of Abelardo de la Espriella in Colombia, marks a significant shift toward the right in Latin American politics.

## Event Details

### Extremely Close Results

According to Peru's electoral court's final certified results, Fujimori received 50.135% of valid votes while Sánchez received 49.865%, a difference of just 0.27 percentage points. This result reflects Peru's deep social divisions.

In her victory statement, Fujimori said she would assume the role of president "with responsibility, humility and a deep sense of duty." She added: "Each day of this transition process is an opportunity to listen, engage in dialogue and arrive prepared at the start of the new government." This statement was interpreted as an acknowledgment of her thin mandate.

### Controversy and Legal Challenges

Losing candidate Sánchez, 57, a former foreign trade minister, has alleged the runoff election was "seriously compromised" and threatened legal action. He argued that strong support for Fujimori among Peruvian voters abroad was a sign of irregularities. After the electoral court declared the result, Sánchez's party appealed, calling for the vote to be nullified.

### The Fujimori Political Legacy

Fujimori is the daughter of controversial former President Alberto Fujimori. The elder Fujimori was eventually jailed for crimes against humanity during his 1990s presidency, including extrajudicial killings and forced sterilizations.

In this campaign, Fujimori leaned on her father's controversial political legacy, promising a military crackdown on organized crime, particularly extortion incidents that have soared in recent years. She also pledged to attract private investment to promote economic growth and to immediately expel any undocumented immigrants found committing crimes in Peru.

## Comprehensive Analysis

Fujimori's victory represents not only an important turning point in Peru's domestic politics but also a microcosm of the evolution of Latin America's political landscape. This result requires analysis from multiple dimensions.

First, from the perspective of Peru's domestic political ecology, Fujimori's victory reflects voters' strong concerns about security and order issues. In recent years, organized crime and extortion cases have increased dramatically in Peru, seriously affecting daily life and the business environment. Fujimori's promise of a "military crackdown on crime" directly addresses voter pain points. While this stance is controversial, it has gained broad support against the backdrop of security anxieties.

Second, from a regional political perspective, Fujimori's victory, along with Colombia's new president's election, constitutes an important signal of Latin America's political shift to the right. In recent years, Latin America has experienced the so-called "pink tide," with left-wing governments regaining power in multiple countries. However, economic stagnation, corruption scandals, and deteriorating public security have led some voters to turn to right-wing candidates, hoping to restore order and economic growth through hardline policies.

Third, Fujimori faces significant governance challenges. Her narrow electoral advantage means she lacks a strong popular mandate and must balance various interests during her administration. Meanwhile, her family background and political legacy will continue to be focal points of controversy. The opposition may continue to question her governing legitimacy, potentially intensifying social divisions.

Fourth, from an international relations perspective, Fujimori's right-wing stance may affect Peru's relationships with major powers including the United States and China. Her pro-market policy orientation may lead Peru to lean more toward traditional allies in foreign policy while adopting more market-oriented strategies in economic and trade policies.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Fujimori Supporters**: Believe she is the only candidate capable of restoring law and order. Supporters emphasize the need for strong leadership in the face of escalating crime. They particularly appreciate Fujimori's commitment to combating extortion, viewing it as key to solving Peru's most pressing social problems.

**Sánchez and Opposition**: Maintain that electoral irregularities occurred, particularly the anomalous growth in overseas votes. They fear Fujimori's victory will lead to democratic backsliding and human rights issues, especially given her father's controversial governing history. The opposition calls for international attention to Peru's democratic situation.

**International Observers**: Have expressed concern about the closeness of the election results but generally acknowledge the electoral court's final decision. Analysts point out that regardless of the outcome, Peru needs to address deep socioeconomic problems, including structural challenges such as inequality, corruption, and weak institutions.

**Business Community Reaction**: Generally cautiously optimistic about Fujimori's victory. The business sector expects her promised pro-market policies to promote investment and economic growth but worries that political instability may affect the business environment.

**Social Movement Organizations**: Have expressed concern about human rights conditions, particularly given the Fujimori family's history. They call on the new government to respect democratic institutions and protect minority rights while monitoring whether crime-fighting policies will infringe on civil liberties.
