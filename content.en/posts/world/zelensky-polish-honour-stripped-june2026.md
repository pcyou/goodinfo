## Core Summary

The Polish Presidential Palace officially announced on June 19 the revocation of the highest honour previously awarded to Ukrainian President Zelensky. The direct trigger was a Ukrainian military unit named after a controversial WW2-era figure — the organization associated with the name had carried out violence against Polish civilians during World War II, and is regarded by Poland as a symbol of "historical wounds." Ukraine's Foreign Ministry quickly issued a strong response, calling Poland's decision a "strategic mistake" and "disrespectful," and indicated it would reassess the trajectory of bilateral diplomatic relations.

## Event Details

According to a statement from the Polish Presidential Palace, the decision to revoke the honour was based on "Ukraine's failure to properly address historically sensitive issues." The statement noted that the continued use of controversial WWII-era organization names in the Ukrainian armed forces "hurts the feelings of the Polish people" and "contradicts the historical justice both nations should jointly uphold."

The focus of this dispute is a Ukrainian military unit named after the "Ukrainian Insurgent Army" (UPA). This organization carried out large-scale violence against Polish civilians in the Volhynia region during WWII, resulting in tens of thousands of Polish civilian deaths. This historical event is known in Poland as the "Volhynia Massacre" and remains one of the most sensitive historical issues in Polish-Ukrainian relations.

Poland had previously raised the issue through diplomatic channels multiple times, requesting the renaming of the military unit, but received no substantive response. The revocation of the honour is seen as Poland's "ultimatum"-style diplomatic pressure.

Ukraine's Foreign Ministry strongly condemned Poland's decision in a statement, saying it "disrespects a nation fighting for freedom" and that the honour awarded to Zelensky was "earned with the blood of the Ukrainian people" — not something Poland could unilaterally revoke. The statement also hinted that if Poland continues to pressure on this issue, Ukraine may reassess its support for Poland's candidacy for a UN Security Council non-permanent seat.

## Analysis

This diplomatic storm is not accidental but a concentrated manifestation of long-accumulated contradictions between historical memory and practical interests in Polish-Ukrainian relations. From a historical perspective, the Volhynia events were among the most tragic ethnic conflicts in Eastern Europe during WWII, and remain a trauma in Poland's collective memory. Ukraine, meanwhile, tends to view the WWII-era nationalist movement as a "just struggle against foreign oppression" — the two countries have fundamental disagreements in historical narrative.

From a political perspective, the timing of this dispute is particularly sensitive. Ukraine is at a critical juncture in its conflict with Russia and urgently needs continued support from Western allies. As one of Ukraine's most steadfast supporters, Poland's attitude shift could significantly affect Ukraine's international position. However, Polish domestic politics are also changing — with the 2025 presidential election approaching, a tough stance on Ukraine has become a campaign tool for some political forces.

From the EU perspective, the deterioration of Polish-Ukrainian relations could affect the EU's unified position on Ukraine aid. Poland has been the "vanguard" of supporting Ukraine within the EU, and if bilateral relations continue to deteriorate, it could create rifts in the EU's approach to Ukraine.

Notably, this event also reflects the special position of WWII history in contemporary Eastern European politics. In Poland, Ukraine, Lithuania and other countries, historical memory is not just an academic issue but an important tool for domestic political mobilization and diplomatic competition. Finding balance between respecting history and maintaining current alliances is a shared challenge for Eastern European nations.

## Perspectives

**Polish Position**: The Presidential Palace emphasized the revocation is "a decision based on historical justice," not anti-Ukrainian sentiment, but demands Ukraine "face historical issues."

**Ukrainian Position**: Ukraine's Foreign Ministry called Poland's decision "shortsighted and disrespectful," hinting at possible reciprocal diplomatic measures and emphasizing "a fighting nation should not be held hostage by historical issues."

**EU Attitude**: The EU's High Representative for Foreign Affairs called on both sides to "resolve differences through dialogue" to avoid affecting EU unity and support for Ukraine.

**Historians**: Multiple Eastern European history scholars noted that WWII historical issues are far more sensitive in Eastern Europe than in Western Europe, and "simple historical justice narratives often obscure the complex reality of ethnic interactions."
