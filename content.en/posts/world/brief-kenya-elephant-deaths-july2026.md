## Core Summary

According to BBC, 14 elephants have been found dead in southern Kenya, marking the highest number of elephant deaths recorded in this area in decades. The Kenya Wildlife Service has launched an urgent investigation to determine the cause of death. Disease, poisoning, and poaching have not yet been ruled out as possible causes.

## Event Details

BBC reports that the elephant carcasses were discovered in southern Kenya, with the cause of death unclear. This is the highest number of elephant deaths recorded in this area in decades, drawing widespread attention from the wildlife conservation community.

The Kenya Wildlife Service stated that expert teams have been dispatched to the site for necropsies and environmental sample collection. The initial investigation will focus on several key directions: whether the mass deaths were caused by disease; whether human poisoning is involved; and whether there are signs of poaching.

Elephant conservation organizations point out that African elephant populations face multiple threats in recent years, including habitat loss, human-elephant conflict, and poaching. If this mass death event is confirmed to be from non-natural causes, it will deal a significant blow to Kenya's elephant conservation efforts.
