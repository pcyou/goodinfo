## Core Summary

Canadian-American legendary voice actor Peter Cullen has passed away from natural causes at age 85. His career spanned over half a century, with his most iconic roles including Optimus Prime, the Autobot leader in Transformers, and Eeyore the donkey in Disney's Winnie the Pooh series. His voice accompanied the growth of multiple generations of audiences.

## Event Details

According to ABC News, Peter Cullen's family confirmed he passed away peacefully recently, though the specific cause of death was not disclosed. Cullen's career began in the 1960s at the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation in radio, before moving to Hollywood for animation voice work.

His most iconic role was Optimus Prime in Transformers — the Autobots' leader's deep, dignified, and justice-filled voice became an important symbol of pop culture. Cullen revealed in multiple interviews that Optimus Prime's voice was inspired by his brother — a Canadian Marine veteran who served in World War II.

Additionally, Cullen took over the role of Eeyore in the Winnie the Pooh series in 1988, and for over thirty years continued to voice this melancholic but loyal little donkey, becoming an indispensable voice in Disney parks and animated films.

## Panoramic Perspective

Peter Cullen's passing marks the fall of another star from animation voice acting's golden age. In the animation industry, voice actors often work behind the scenes, but their voices shaped countless audiences' emotional memories of characters. Cullen's uniqueness lies in simultaneously creating two vastly different yet equally beloved characters: a brave and fearless robot leader, and a gentle, melancholic cartoon donkey.

From an industry perspective, Cullen's career witnessed the transformation of animation voice work from "ancillary work" to "professional art." Early animation voice acting was often done part-time by comedians or radio hosts, while Cullen demonstrated with his professionalism that voice acting itself is a performing art requiring deep skill.

His legacy lies not only in those classic characters but in setting a professional benchmark for subsequent voice actors — voices can transcend culture and language, becoming emotional bonds connecting audiences across generations.
