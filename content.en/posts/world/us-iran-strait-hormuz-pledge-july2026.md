## Core Summary

The United States has demanded that Iran issue a public statement declaring the Strait of Hormuz remains open and pledge not to attack ships. Vice President JD Vance is among senior US officials expected to participate in negotiations resuming Saturday in Oman. This diplomatic move aims to ease tensions in the global energy shipping corridor and ensure international maritime safety.

## Event Details

According to reports from the Associated Press and BBC, US officials made clear they expect Iran to release a public statement declaring the Strait of Hormuz open to international shipping and committing that Tehran will not continue attacking vessels. This demand is one of the core issues in US-Iran negotiations.

Vice President Vance will participate in talks held in Oman, indicating the high level of importance the US places on this matter. The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most critical oil transportation routes, with approximately one-fifth of global oil supply passing through daily. Any threat to the strait's security directly impacts global energy markets and economic development.

Previously, Iran had repeatedly threatened to close the Strait of Hormuz in response to Western sanctions. In recent months, several ship attack incidents occurred in the region, heightening international concerns. This US diplomatic action aims to resolve disputes through negotiation and avoid further escalation.

## Panoramic Perspective

The US demand for Iran to make a public commitment on Strait of Hormuz security reflects deeper changes in the global energy security landscape. As the "chokepoint" of global energy transportation, the strait's security status directly relates to stable global economic operations.

From a geopolitical perspective, these negotiations involve multi-party interest dynamics. The US attempts to ensure freedom of navigation through diplomatic pressure while avoiding military conflict; Iran hopes to gain more leverage in negotiations in exchange for sanctions relief or other economic benefits; Gulf Arab states closely monitor negotiation progress since their economies are highly dependent on oil exports.

From an energy security perspective, this incident once again highlights global vulnerability to critical maritime shipping routes. In recent years, countries have accelerated efforts toward energy diversification and supply chain resilience, but the Strait of Hormuz's strategic position remains difficult to replace in the short term. Any threat to the strait triggers global oil price fluctuations, subsequently affecting inflation levels and economic growth expectations.

Additionally, the negotiation outcome will also impact the broader Middle East situation. If the US and Iran can reach consensus on the Strait of Hormuz issue, it may create conditions for negotiations on other pending issues (such as nuclear matters). Conversely, negotiation breakdown could escalate regional tensions or even trigger military confrontation.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Those supporting the US position** believe ensuring free passage through international waterways is a fundamental principle of international law. Iran's attacks on passing vessels violate international maritime regulations and must cease. US pressure through diplomatic channels is a responsible approach that helps maintain regional stability and global economic security.

**Voices sympathetic to Iran's position** point out that long-term US sanctions against Iran are the root cause of regional tensions. Iran has the right to protect its legitimate interests through lawful means, including seeking more favorable conditions in negotiations. Some analysts believe US demands are too one-sided, lacking consideration for Iran's security concerns.

**International relations experts** generally believe the negotiation's success probability depends on whether both sides are willing to make substantive concessions. The US may need to consider partial sanctions relief in exchange for Iran's commitments, while Iran needs to demonstrate it will not use the strait as a strategic weapon. The mediation role of neutral countries (such as Oman) is also crucial.
