[Key Summary]
Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has called a snap general election for November 29 after his coalition collapsed over a housing bill that triggered nationwide protests, plunging Europe's fourth-largest economy into months of political uncertainty.

[Background]
The housing bill was defeated in parliament in late September, stripping Sánchez's minority government of its last key ally. Opposition lawmakers immediately demanded his resignation, while major cities including Madrid and Barcelona have seen large street demonstrations over the past two weeks. At a televised press conference, Sánchez said the snap poll was "the only path that lets the people decide the country's direction."

[Multiple Perspectives]
Supporters argue the housing crisis has become a cross-class, cross-regional social problem and that Sánchez needs a fresh mandate to push more aggressive supply-side reforms. Opponents call the call an attempt to deflect blame for coalition failures and warn it will deepen market jitters. EU observers caution that prolonged political gridlock in Spain could drag on the eurozone recovery.

[Big Picture]
Sánchez is betting the campaign on a "social justice" narrative, but Spain's housing affordability gap is not a short-term problem: in major cities, prices have risen more than 80% over the past decade and the home-ownership rate among young Spaniards has hit a historic low. The election outcome will shape how quickly EU green-and-digital transition money lands on the Iberian peninsula and influence the wider eurozone fiscal integration agenda.
