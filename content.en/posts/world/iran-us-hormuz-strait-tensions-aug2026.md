## Core Summary

Iran and the United States have escalated their confrontation over control of the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian officials have declared that the strait is under Tehran's complete control, while President Trump has responded with warnings that U.S. naval power will "ensure" freedom of passage through this critical waterway. The diplomatic crisis comes as nuclear negotiations between the two nations remain stalled, adding another layer of complexity to an already volatile regional security situation.

## Event Details

According to multiple international media reports, a senior Iranian Foreign Ministry official stated at a press conference in Tehran that the Strait of Hormuz, as the strategic channel connecting the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman, is under Iran's complete security and operational control. The official emphasized: "Any external military presence would constitute a serious threat to regional stability."

President Trump immediately responded on social media, stating that the United States would "spare no effort" to maintain the Strait of Hormuz's status as an international waterway. The U.S. Fifth Fleet has increased patrol operations in the Persian Gulf region, with multiple carrier strike groups moving toward the area.

This confrontation occurs against the backdrop of long-stalled U.S.-Iran nuclear negotiations. Washington demands that Tehran completely abandon its uranium enrichment program, while Iran insists on its right to peaceful nuclear energy. The two sides remain fundamentally divided on multiple key issues, making diplomatic mediation extremely difficult.

## Panoramic Perspective

The Strait of Hormuz, as one of the world's most important energy transportation corridors, handles approximately 21 million barrels of crude oil daily, representing nearly one-third of global seaborne oil trade. Any disruption in this narrow waterway immediately impacts international oil prices and creates cascading effects throughout global supply chains.

From a geopolitical perspective, this confrontation reflects deeper shifts in the Middle East's power dynamics. Iran is attempting to enhance its leverage in regional affairs by strengthening its control over the strait, while the United States seeks to maintain its traditional influence in the region. This competition concerns not only energy security but also the struggle for regional dominance.

For the global economy, stability in the Strait of Hormuz is directly tied to energy supply security. If the situation escalates to the point of blocking the waterway, international oil prices could surge by more than 50% in the short term, triggering global inflationary pressures. European and Asian energy-importing nations would be most severely affected, facing acute supply shortages.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Iran's Position**: Tehran emphasizes its legitimate sovereign rights over the Strait of Hormuz, considering this central to its national security and economic interests. Iranian military officials state they are prepared to respond to any provocation while remaining open to dialogue.

**U.S. Position**: Washington maintains that the Strait of Hormuz is international waters where no country has the right to unilaterally restrict passage. The U.S. Department of Defense stated it will strengthen military coordination with regional allies to ensure waterway security.

**Regional Reactions**: Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and other Gulf states have expressed concern about the escalation, calling for restraint from all parties. These nations' economies are highly dependent on oil exports, and waterway disruption would directly harm their core interests.

**International Response**: The UN Secretary-General's spokesperson has called on both the U.S. and Iran to resolve disputes through diplomatic channels and avoid actions that could worsen the situation. The International Energy Agency stated it is closely monitoring developments and is prepared to coordinate strategic petroleum reserve releases from member countries if necessary.
