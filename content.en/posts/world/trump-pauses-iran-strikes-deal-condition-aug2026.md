## Core Summary

US President Trump announced on August 2 that he would order a halt to military strikes against Iran, provided a nuclear deal can be reached "rapidly." This major policy shift indicates that after weeks of military confrontation, Washington is willing to give diplomatic negotiations a window of opportunity. Iran has not yet responded officially, but sources suggest Tehran is open to dialogue.

## Event Details

According to CNN's live coverage, Trump posted on social media: "If Iran wants to make a deal quickly, I will call off the strikes." The White House later confirmed this position, stating that the US is prepared to hold indirect negotiations in Oman or Qatar.

Sources revealed that the US's core demands include:
- Iran halting all uranium enrichment activities
- Allowing full International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) inspections
- Limiting the ballistic missile program

Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi previously stated that Iran is willing to engage in dialogue "on the basis of equality and mutual respect," but refused to negotiate under pressure.

## Panoramic Perspective

This statement has far-reaching implications for Middle Eastern geopolitics. First, it eases tensions in global energy markets, with international oil prices falling approximately 2% following the announcement. Second, it provides breathing room for stability in the Gulf region, avoiding potential military escalation that could lead to full-scale conflict.

From a broader perspective, this reflects an adjustment in US Middle East strategy. Facing domestic economic pressures and electoral considerations, Washington needs to balance a tough stance with pragmatic diplomacy. For Iran, the economic temptation of lifting sanctions and resuming oil exports may prompt certain concessions, but domestic hardliner pressure cannot be ignored.

The international community has generally welcomed this de-escalation move. The EU High Representative for Foreign Affairs called it "an important step back to the diplomatic track," urging all parties to seize the opportunity. China's Foreign Ministry also expressed support for resolving differences through dialogue.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporting Voices**:
- US centrist lawmakers believe diplomatic resolution is preferable to military conflict, serving America's long-term interests
- European allies generally welcome de-escalation, concerned that military conflict could trigger refugee flows and energy crises
- Gulf Arab states are cautiously optimistic, hoping to contain Iran while fearing the spread of conflict

**Skeptical Voices**:
- Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu expressed "serious concerns," worried the deal cannot effectively limit Iran's nuclear capabilities
- US domestic hawks criticize Trump as "weak," arguing this gives Iran breathing room
- Iranian hardliners warn they will not make substantive concessions "at gunpoint"

**Expert Analysis**:
Former US Ambassador to Israel Daniel Shapiro noted that the key lies in defining "rapid" — if the US expects a comprehensive agreement within weeks, it may be overly optimistic; but if this is just the first step in building trust, it has strategic value.
