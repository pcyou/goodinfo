# US Launches Fresh Strikes on Iran After Tanker Attacks in Strait of Hormuz

## Core Summary

The United States announced new military strikes against Iranian targets following attacks on multiple tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. US officials stated the operation aims to impose "heavy costs" on Iran in response to attacks on international shipping. Iran has issued stern warnings of "decisive measures" in retaliation. The latest escalation has intensified already tense Middle East dynamics, posing unprecedented threats to global energy supply chain security.

## Event Details

According to BBC reporting, the US announced strikes against Iran after three tankers were hit in the latest round of attacks in the Strait of Hormuz. US officials described the military action as a direct response to attacks on international commercial vessels by Iran and its proxy forces, aimed at holding Tehran accountable for undermining freedom of navigation.

Earlier, British maritime security authorities confirmed that three tankers were simultaneously attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz, marking another serious security incident in this strategic waterway. The Strait of Hormuz handles approximately 21 million barrels of crude oil daily, accounting for about 20% of global seaborne oil trade, making it a critical chokepoint for global energy security.

Iran has not yet issued an official statement on the latest US strikes, but previously warned of "decisive measures" in response to any military action against its interests. Analysts note that the US timing of strikes during Iran's national mourning period for the late Supreme Leader Khamenei carries strong political signaling implications.

International oil prices surged following the news, with Brent crude breaking through the $95 per barrel mark during Asian trading. Maritime insurance companies have significantly increased war risk premiums for the region, and some shipping companies are considering rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope in Africa, despite the substantial increase in transport costs and time.

## Panoramic Perspective

The renewed escalation in US-Iran military confrontation marks a more dangerous phase in the Strait of Hormuz crisis. From a strategic perspective, the impact of this event extends far beyond mere military conflict, profoundly reshaping the global energy security landscape and international order.

First, the vulnerability of global energy supply chains has been exposed once again. Despite rapid development of renewable energy in recent years, oil still accounts for over 30% of global energy consumption. Major Asian economies—China, Japan, South Korea, and India—rely heavily on Middle Eastern energy imports. These countries may accelerate strategic petroleum reserve construction and diversify energy sources as a result. In the long term, this trend will accelerate global energy transition, but in the short term it will push up energy costs, creating pressure on global economic recovery.

Second, the stability of the international shipping insurance system faces severe tests. Major insurers like Lloyd's have designated the region as high-risk, with premiums soaring. These costs will ultimately be passed on to oil prices, borne by global consumers. For countries in critical economic recovery periods, this compounds existing challenges.

From a geopolitical dimension, the continued escalation of US-Iran confrontation is altering the Middle East power balance. The US attempts to force Iran back to the negotiating table through military means, but historical experience suggests that military pressure alone often proves counterproductive. Iran may strengthen cooperation with other major powers to offset US pressure, further complicating regional dynamics.

The more profound impact lies in the fragmentation of international order. The ongoing US-Iran confrontation is testing the effectiveness of multilateral institutions like the United Nations. Significant divisions exist within the international community on how to respond to this crisis, divisions that may further undermine the authority and effectiveness of global governance systems.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US officials** emphasized the "self-defense" nature of the strikes. White House spokespersons stated the US has the right to protect its citizens and international shipping freedom from threats. The Pentagon announced it will deploy additional military forces to the region to "ensure freedom of navigation." However, questions about the legality of military action have emerged within Congress, with some lawmakers demanding clarification on authorization.

**Iran** issued stern warnings. The Iranian Foreign Ministry stated it will take "decisive measures" in response to any aggression, but did not specify concrete actions. Analysts suggest Iran may launch retaliatory attacks through proxy forces in the region rather than directly confronting US military forces.

**European allies** adopted cautious positions. The EU High Representative for Foreign Affairs called for restraint from all parties to avoid further escalation. France and Germany expressed support for resolving disputes through diplomatic channels but did not explicitly endorse US military action.

**Energy markets** reacted sharply. Brent crude prices broke through $95 per barrel, shipping indices rose in tandem, while airline stocks fell on fuel cost concerns. Analysts warn that if the situation continues to escalate, oil prices could break through the $100 per barrel mark.

**Regional countries** expressed serious concern. Saudi Arabia and the UAE called for international measures to ensure Gulf navigation security. The oil exports of these two major producers are highly dependent on the Strait of Hormuz, and any disruption would cause enormous economic losses.

**Security experts** are divided. Some believe US strikes are necessary deterrent actions aimed at preventing Iran from continuing to undermine navigation freedom; others worry that military escalation could lead to larger-scale conflict and call for immediate resumption of diplomatic negotiations.
