## Core Summary

Iran launched renewed missile attacks on the evening of July 29, prompting joint US-Saudi strikes against Tehran-backed militia positions in Iraq. President Trump warned Iran is "going to get a beating" as regional tensions escalate dramatically. The conflict marks the collapse of fragile ceasefire agreements and raises international concerns about full-scale regional war.

## Event Details

According to the Associated Press, Iran fired multiple missiles on the evening of July 29, targeting US military facilities and allied targets in the Middle East. In response, the United States and Saudi Arabia launched joint airstrikes against multiple Iran-backed militia positions in Iraq.

President Trump issued a strong statement on social media, warning that Iran is "going to get a beating" and cautioning the Tehran regime not to underestimate the military response capabilities of the US and its allies. The US Department of Defense confirmed that American forces have strengthened their military deployment in the Middle East, including additional carrier strike groups and air defense systems.

The Iraqi government expressed serious concern, calling for restraint from all parties. The Iraqi Prime Minister's office issued a statement saying Iraqi territory should not become a battlefield for regional conflicts, demanding all foreign military forces respect Iraqi sovereignty.

## Panoramic Perspective

This direct military confrontation between Iran and the US along with its allies marks a significant turning point in Middle East geopolitics. First, it has broken the relatively stable regional balance of power in recent years, pushing countries like Iraq and Syria back into the vortex of great power competition. Second, Saudi Arabia's active participation indicates that Gulf Arab states are shifting from passive defense to actively containing Iran's regional influence, potentially triggering a new arms race.

From an energy security perspective, escalating tensions in the Middle East directly threaten global oil supply stability. The Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint for approximately 20% of global oil transportation, could see dramatic oil price fluctuations if any military conflict occurs, impacting global economic recovery. Additionally, the spillover effects of regional conflict could extend to Lebanon, Yemen, and other countries, worsening already fragile humanitarian situations.

The international community faces a difficult choice: rebuild dialogue mechanisms through diplomatic mediation, or allow military confrontation to continue escalating. The outcome of emergency UN Security Council consultations will serve as an important indicator of great power competition dynamics.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US Official Position**: The White House emphasized this military action is a "defensive response" aimed at protecting American personnel and allied security. The State Department spokesperson stated the US remains willing to engage in diplomatic dialogue with Iran, but only if Tehran stops supporting regional proxy forces.

**Iran's Response**: Iran's Foreign Ministry condemned the joint US-Saudi strikes as "blatant sovereignty violations" and warned of "all necessary measures" to protect national interests. The Iranian Revolutionary Guard claimed missile attacks targeted "occupation force facilities" in accordance with international law self-defense rights.

**Regional Countries' Attitudes**: The Iraqi government faces a dilemma, needing to maintain national sovereignty while balancing relations with both the US and Iran. Moderate Arab countries like Jordan and Egypt called for mediation through the Arab League framework. Turkey warned that external military intervention will only increase regional instability.

**International Observers**: Most Middle East experts believe this conflict reflects the exhaustion of Iran's strategic patience. A senior researcher at the Washington Institute for Near East Policy noted that the Tehran regime faces dual pressures of domestic economic strain and declining regional influence, potentially adopting more aggressive foreign policies to divert internal tensions.
