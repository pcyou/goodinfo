## Core Summary

The Vatican press office announced on October 11 that Pope Leo XIV has been diagnosed with a small growth on his lung during a recent routine medical examination and will undergo surgery at a hospital in Rome within days. The Holy See emphasized that the procedure is preventive in nature, with preoperative assessments indicating the lesion is small and clearly demarcated. During his hospital stay and recovery, the Pope will suspend selected public audiences and major liturgical engagements while senior Vatican officials assume interim responsibilities for daily governance.

## Event Details

- **Detection and diagnosis**: The growth was identified through follow-up imaging after the Pope experienced mild respiratory symptoms. Preoperative imaging and laboratory work did not show signs of malignancy, leading clinicians to recommend preventive surgical removal combined with postoperative pathology review.
- **Surgical plan**: The procedure will be performed at a long-established Roman hospital that has historically provided medical care to pontiffs. The surgical team combines thoracic and pulmonary specialists using a minimally invasive approach, with an expected operative time of moderate length.
- **Recovery timeline**: Initial hospital observation is expected to last several days to one week, with full return to public activities projected over several weeks. Outdoor audiences and large liturgical celebrations will be paused during recovery.
- **Vatican communications**: The Holy See press office characterized the procedure as preventive, not emergency. All clinical updates will be issued through official Vatican channels to maintain transparency and limit speculation.
- **Interim governance**: The College of Cardinals and the secretaries general of Vatican dicasteries will continue to handle routine affairs under established procedures, ensuring institutional continuity during the Pope's reduced schedule.
- **Public response**: Catholic faithful in Italy and worldwide have organized prayer services at major basilicas and cathedrals, with several bishops' conferences issuing pastoral letters inviting prayer for the Pope's health.
