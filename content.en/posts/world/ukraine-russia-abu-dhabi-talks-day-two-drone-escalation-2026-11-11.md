## Key Facts

- **Talks continue**: The trilateral talks entered their second day on Wednesday in Abu Dhabi. Zelensky described the first day as constructive and quite substantive.
- **The American framing**: The White House called on day one for a new security framework. Kushner said the United States had narrowed the gaps between the sides without providing details, and hoped to move toward a solution to Europe's worst conflict since World War II.
- **The mediators**: President Trump's envoys, his son-in-law Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, brought the parties together.
- **Russian airspace**: The governor of Leningrad Region said 92 drones were shot down early on Wednesday. The region hosts the Baltic ports of Ust-Luga, Primorsk and Vysotsk, as well as Kirishi, Russia's second-largest oil refinery.
- **Port fire**: A drone attack at Ust-Luga, one of Russia's most important western oil hubs and a target of repeated strikes, caused a fire; teams were working to extinguish it.
- **At sea**: Russia's defence ministry said its forces struck a cargo vessel in the Black Sea southwest of Odesa that was carrying weapons and military equipment for Ukraine's armed forces.
- **Inside Ukraine**: Russia said drones hit three electricity substations in Ukraine's Kryvyi Rih and Odesa regions overnight.
- **Casualties**: Drone debris hit an apartment building in Russia's Smolensk region, killing two people, the regional governor said.
- **A separate front**: Russia's Federal Security Service said it had arrested two people it described as agents of the Polish secret services for gathering information on military facilities in the Kaliningrad region, and that seven Polish citizens had been warned and barred from Russia.

## Competing Views

**The word "new" in the American formulation deserves attention.** Each of the four previous US-brokered rounds worked through the same list: the ceasefire line, sanctions relief, security guarantees, with all three stuck in the same place. Introducing a "new" framework on day one concedes that the old list can no longer carry the substance of this round. But Washington did not say where the newness lay, and that omission is itself information. It may mean binding security guarantees to a Russian economic arrangement, or it may mean resolving Ukrainian sovereignty and security first and leaving the rest for later. Kushner said the gaps had narrowed while offering no detail, so it is not yet possible to tell which.

**Zelensky's "happy" is a tactical formulation.** A year ago the Kremlin insisted the trilateral format was fixed and that Europe had no seat; now representatives from Britain, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission were in the room for the opening session. For Kyiv, that resolves the old question of who speaks for Europe while creating a new one: being present without being able to decide. In that structure, expressing public dissatisfaction with the mediators carries no upside, and "happy" is the cheapest available statement.

**Escalation on the battlefield is not evidence that talks are failing; it is frequently the precondition for talks to move.** On day one Russia said it launched 162 drones of various types and one ballistic missile overnight, while Ukraine said it shot down 155 drones; day two brought 92 drones shot down, a port fire, a cargo vessel struck and substations hit. The pattern matches every contact this year: force levels up before talks, force levels kept high during them. Both sides are betting on the negotiating table for things they cannot take on the battlefield.

**The easiest line to overlook is the Federal Security Service one.** Poland is a core European participant in the Abu Dhabi talks, and Eastern European security anxieties sit directly alongside the bargaining allocation in any Russia negotiation. The arrest of two people described as Polish intelligence agents, and the warning and entry ban against seven Polish citizens, sit outside the negotiating table but turn European internal unity into a variable: if the Eastern European camp fears being treated as bargaining chips in a deal with Moscow, its position at the table hardens. The downstream effect of that thread may matter more than the talks themselves.
