The de facto top US diplomat stationed in Taiwan has publicly warned that any military conflict across the Taiwan Strait would bring "disastrous consequences" for the region and the world. Speaking at a public event jointly hosted by the American Institute in Taiwan and a Taiwanese think tank, the senior diplomat stressed that peace and stability in the Taiwan Strait concern not only the people on both sides of the strait but also the common interests of the entire international community, and called on all parties to maintain the status quo through dialogue and restraint.

## Event Details

The remarks were delivered at a public event organized by the American Institute in Taiwan and a Taiwanese think tank. The senior diplomat stated clearly that "any attempt by any party to change the cross-strait status quo by non-peaceful means will have disastrous consequences." The official emphasized that peace and stability in the Taiwan Strait are "the common interest of the international community," that the United States will continue to support peace and stability in the Taiwan Strait area in line with its long-standing policy, and that all parties are urged to resolve differences through dialogue.

The remarks are widely read as the latest attempt by Washington to strike a balance between strategic ambiguity and strategic clarity. On one hand, the US side reiterated that its long-standing One China policy has not changed; on the other, the explicit use of strong language such as "disastrous consequences" is read as a "red line" signal sent simultaneously to both Beijing and Taipei. Analysts believe this may herald a more proactive public stance from Washington in handling Taiwan Strait issues going forward.

Taiwan's leader's office expressed "sincere gratitude" for the US remarks, viewing them as reflecting Washington's high regard for peace and stability in the Taiwan Strait. Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council issued a statement saying it will continue to deepen cooperation with the United States and other like-minded countries to jointly safeguard the cross-strait status quo and regional peace. Taiwanese public opinion broadly believes the US remarks carry more weight than before and have positive significance for Taiwan's security.

China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson, when asked about the US remarks, stressed that the Taiwan issue is China's internal affair and tolerates no foreign interference. China urged the US to abide by the one-China principle and the three Sino-US joint communiqués and to stop sending any wrong signals to "Taiwan independence" separatist forces. China's Ministry of National Defense spokesperson said the People's Liberation Army is confident and capable of defeating all "Taiwan independence" separatist activities and resolutely safeguarding national sovereignty and territorial integrity.

Japan's Chief Cabinet Secretary said at a press conference that peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait are vital to the international community and that Japan will continue to monitor the situation closely and maintain tight coordination with allies including the United States. Australia's foreign minister issued a statement emphasizing that a Taiwan Strait conflict is not in anyone's interest and calling for differences to be resolved peacefully. The EU's foreign affairs spokesperson expressed a similar stance, calling on all parties to exercise restraint.

It is worth noting that the US remarks come amid persistently active regional military dynamics. The US Navy's Seventh Fleet has recently sent warships through the Taiwan Strait on multiple occasions and conducted high-frequency reconnaissance aircraft operations; the People's Liberation Army has continued to carry out regular military exercises around the Taiwan Strait, including joint sea and air drills and missile tests. The tense military standoff across the strait is believed to be an important backdrop to the US remarks.

If a Taiwan Strait conflict broke out, it would deliver a devastating blow to global semiconductor supply chains. Taiwan is the core hub of global advanced chip manufacturing, with firms such as TSMC occupying a dominant position in the global market for sub-7nm advanced process chips. Any military action targeting Taiwan would plunge the global electronics, automotive and artificial intelligence industries into a severe supply crisis, with related economic losses potentially running into trillions of dollars.

The remarks also reflect the complex game around the Taiwan Strait. The United States hopes to maintain the status quo and deter China from expanding regional influence; China treats "national reunification" as an important task of national rejuvenation. The US remarks can be seen as an effort to "manage differences" amid strategic competition, but they also reflect deeper US concerns about the risk of escalation.

## Editorial Analysis

The de facto US ambassador's remarks that a Taiwan Strait conflict would have "disastrous consequences" represent one of the most substantive recent US public statements on the Taiwan issue, and their strategic implications deserve deeper analysis.

From the perspective of US Taiwan policy, the remarks continue the long-standing US tradition of strategic ambiguity, but with clearer wording. The US neither explicitly committed to "defending Taiwan" nor ruled out military involvement; instead, by using "disastrous consequences," Washington sought to deter both Beijing and Taipei simultaneously. This "two-way deterrence" approach reflects Washington's effort to balance maintaining the status quo with avoiding direct military conflict.

From a cross-strait relations perspective, the US remarks further compress the diplomatic maneuvering space on the Taiwan Strait question. Beijing has long insisted that Taiwan is an internal affair of China and opposes any foreign interference; Taipei hopes to obtain more international support, particularly a clear security commitment from Washington. The US remarks, while reiterating the One China policy, also expressed deep concern for cross-strait peace and stability, effectively providing a degree of security assurance for Taiwan.

From a US-China strategic competition perspective, the Taiwan Strait issue has become one of the core issues in US-China contest. The United States hopes to maintain the status quo to deter China from expanding regional influence; China treats "national reunification" as an important task of national rejuvenation. The US remarks can be seen as an effort to "manage differences" amid strategic competition, but they also reflect deeper US concerns about the risk of escalation.

From a regional security perspective, a Taiwan Strait conflict could trigger chain reactions that destabilize the security architecture of the entire Western Pacific. Regional US allies such as Japan, Australia, the Philippines and South Korea would all be drawn into a potential conflict and face the difficult choice of whether to join US military action. Southeast Asian countries worry that regional conflict would hurt their economic development and trade.

From a global economic perspective, the cost of a Taiwan Strait conflict would far exceed that of traditional geopolitical conflicts. Taiwan's central position in the global semiconductor supply chain means any military action targeting the island would trigger a severe supply crisis in the global electronics industry. Downstream sectors such as automotive, AI, communications equipment and consumer electronics would face massive production disruptions, with potential losses running into trillions of dollars.

From an international law perspective, the Taiwan Strait issue involves a series of complex international law questions, including sovereignty, territorial integrity and the peaceful resolution of disputes. The international legal community generally believes the Taiwan Strait issue should be resolved in line with basic international law principles, through peaceful dialogue rather than force or threat of force. This position is broadly consistent with the spirit of the US remarks.

From a military strategy perspective, a Taiwan Strait conflict would be one of the largest and most technologically sophisticated military confrontations of the 21st century, involving maritime and air combat, cyber warfare, space warfare, electronic warfare and other dimensions, with the potential to fundamentally reshape future warfare. Both sides' militaries are undergoing modernization, and the contest between area denial and anti-access capabilities would form the core scenario of any conflict.

## Multiple Perspectives Comparison

Within the US government, there are divisions on Taiwan Strait policy. Hawks, including some Pentagon and State Department officials, advocate providing Taipei with a clearer security commitment to deter "Chinese military coercion." But some senior diplomats and military leaders worry that an explicit security commitment could drag the United States into a costly military conflict and damage American credibility with other regional allies. The "disastrous consequences" formulation can be seen as a compromise between these two positions.

Within Taiwan, reactions to the US remarks are mixed. The ruling Democratic Progressive Party's senior leadership welcomed the remarks, viewing them as reflecting Washington's high regard for Taiwan's security. But some opposition politicians and commentators worry that the US stance remains ambiguous and does not commit to defending Taiwan in the event of conflict. Taiwanese public opinion broadly believes Taiwan should continue to deepen its security relationship with the United States but should also avoid excessively provoking Beijing.

China's official position is firm and clear. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Ministry of National Defense and Taiwan Affairs Office have reiterated on multiple occasions that Taiwan is an inalienable part of the People's Republic of China and resolutely oppose any form of "Taiwan independence" separatist activities. Beijing has also repeatedly stated that it will continue to seek peaceful reunification with the utmost sincerity and effort, but will never renounce the use of force. This position was emphasized again after the US remarks.

The Japanese government has expressed deep concern about the Taiwan Strait issue. Japan's cabinet has issued statements on multiple occasions emphasizing that peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait are vital to the international community, and Japan maintains close coordination with the United States on the Taiwan Strait issue. Japan has also continued to strengthen its own military capabilities in recent years, including developing long-range strike capabilities and deepening joint training with US forces, moves interpreted as preventive positioning against a Taiwan Strait contingency.

Regional allies such as Australia and New Zealand are calling on all parties to exercise restraint. Australia's foreign minister has emphasized on multiple international occasions that a Taiwan Strait conflict is not in anyone's interest and has called for differences to be resolved peacefully. New Zealand has taken a relatively low-key stance, advocating that all parties resolve differences through dialogue to avoid regional escalation.

Major European powers such as the EU and the UK have taken increasingly clear positions on the Taiwan Strait issue. The EU's foreign affairs service has repeatedly expressed concern about cross-strait peace and stability and emphasized opposition to any unilateral attempt to change the status quo. The British Foreign Secretary has also expressed "deep concern" about the Taiwan Strait issue in recent statements, showing Europe's growing engagement with the topic.

The international business community has expressed deep concern about the Taiwan Strait situation. Senior executives of major global corporations have publicly warned that a Taiwan Strait conflict would deliver a devastating blow to global supply chains. Executives in semiconductors, automotive and technology industries have broadly called on all parties to resolve differences through peaceful dialogue and avoid escalation. The International Chamber of Commerce and multiple industry associations have issued joint statements emphasizing the importance of Taiwan Strait peace to global economic stability.

The international academic and think tank community has conducted extensive research and discussion on the Taiwan Strait issue. Multiple international relations scholars believe the US remarks reflect Washington's deeper concerns about the risk of escalation but could also trigger a tougher response from Beijing. Think tanks broadly believe the cross-strait situation will remain highly tense in the coming years and that all parties should manage differences through dialogue and diplomatic means to avoid miscalculation and conflict.
