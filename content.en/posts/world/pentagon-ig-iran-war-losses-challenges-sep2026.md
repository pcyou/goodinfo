## Core Summary

The Office of the Inspector General of the US Department of Defense has released its latest report, detailing the specific scale of US losses and operational challenges in the Iran war. For the first time, the report systematically documents the extensive damage caused by Iranian missile and drone strikes on US bases, candidly acknowledging significant shortfalls in wartime logistics, intelligence coordination, and defensive deployment. A Congressional Budget Office assessment released in parallel shows the war is costing $2 to $3 billion per month and has become a key driver of US domestic inflation.

## Event Details

### Core Disclosures in the IG Report

According to The New York Times, the Pentagon Inspector General's latest quarterly report is the first to systematically disclose US personnel casualties, equipment losses, and base damage from the Iran war. Photographs accompanying the report show that Iranian missile and drone strikes caused "extensive and severe physical damage" to multiple US bases across the Middle East.

CBS News further reported that some bases' defense systems suffered interception failures, radar blind spots, and communications disruptions, allowing Iran to repeatedly penetrate US defenses across multiple waves of strikes.

### Cascading Fiscal Impact

**Congressional Budget Office**: The latest assessment states the Iran war is costing American taxpayers an average of $2 to $3 billion per month, primarily for munitions resupply, equipment maintenance, military pay, and overseas deployment logistics.

**Yahoo News** disclosed that this figure does not yet include potential veterans' medical care, equipment replacement, and reconstruction costs; if fully incorporated, the annualized total could exceed $50 billion.

**Inflation Pass-Through**: The CBO report also warned the war has significantly driven up US domestic inflation. Energy prices have continued to rise due to tensions in the Strait of Hormuz, and combined with supply-chain pressures on the defense industrial base, this is making the Federal Reserve's path to taming inflation even more difficult.

### Three Internal Challenges Identified

The Inspector General report also highlighted three major challenges facing US forces in the Iran war:

1. **Slow Intelligence Coordination**: Iranian missile launch positions and drone production facilities are highly mobile, and US intelligence agencies have repeatedly experienced targeting delays.
2. **Saturated Defense Systems**: When simultaneously defending multiple bases in the Middle East against multiple Iranian waves, US missile defense systems experienced "munitions and radar resource constraints."
3. **Logistics Sustainment Pressure**: The ability to rapidly project forces from Europe and the homeland to the Middle East has exposed gaps in US strategic power projection in the Euro-Asian theater.

## Panoramic Analysis

**First, the political effect of the IG report should not be underestimated.** The report comes at a moment when Congress is engaged in fierce debate over the defense budget and Iran policy. The loss data it discloses will provide sharply different ammunition to hawks and doves in Congress: hawks may use it to push for expanded military investment, while doves may use it to argue for a swift end to the conflict. This internal rift is likely to intensify further ahead of the midterm elections.

**Second, fiscal sustainability is becoming the war's biggest constraint.** With $2 to $3 billion per month in direct costs, plus inflation pass-through, the "economic premium" of this war far exceeds the nominal numbers. If the war continues through year-end, cumulative spending could exceed $30 billion, likely triggering a legislative battle in Congress over whether a new war appropriation is needed. This fiscal uncertainty will directly affect the pace of US military operations in the Middle East.

**Third, Iran's "asymmetric counterstrike" model is now mature.** The combined "missile + drone + fast attack craft" model Iran has adopted shows a deep understanding of US tactical weaknesses. This low-cost, high-density, distributed form of asymmetric warfare is draining US missile-defense resources and strategic patience, becoming the main risk of the US sliding into a "protracted war of attrition" in the Middle East.

**Fourth, the impact on global energy markets continues to spread.** The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly 20% of global oil shipments, and uncertainty over the Iran war has significantly pushed up international oil prices. For energy-importing nations in Europe and Asia, this means a new round of energy-security anxiety, which may accelerate diversification of energy sources and strategic reserve construction.

## Multiple Perspectives

**The White House**: The Biden administration stated that "the IG report will help us improve operations," but declined to comment on specific casualty figures, emphasizing that US forces "still maintain strategic superiority in the Middle East."

**Congressional Republicans**: Members of the Senate Armed Services Committee publicly criticized the White House for "underestimating the Iranian threat," calling for immediate supplemental defense funding and a strengthened US military posture in the Middle East.

**Congressional Democrats**: Some lawmakers cited the "significant challenges" language in the report to demand the president "establish a clear withdrawal timeline" and initiate a congressional debate on the war's legal authorization.

**Veterans' Organizations**: Groups such as the American Legion have expressed concern that prolonged Middle East deployments will sharply worsen service members' mental health and family stress, calling on the government to strengthen military family support and psychological intervention.

**International Allies**: The United Kingdom, France, and other NATO allies continue to publicly express support for US operations, but privately have voiced concerns about the war's prolongation and rising costs; some countries have begun discussing "whether a reassessment of Iran policy is needed."
