## Core Summary

The United States and Israel are preparing to launch joint strikes on energy-related targets inside Iran, according to CBS News reports citing multiple sources. The move would represent a significant escalation in the five-month military confrontation between Washington and Tehran, with profound implications for global energy supply chains and regional security.

## Event Details

CBS News reported that the US and Israel are actively preparing coordinated strikes on Iran's energy infrastructure. The development comes as the military confrontation between the two nations has persisted for five months, with both sides exchanging missile barrages but stopping short of targeting energy facilities until now.

If the strike plan is ultimately carried out, it would represent a qualitative leap in conflict intensity. Energy facilities form the lifeline of Iran's economy, and attacks on them would not only cause enormous economic damage but could also trigger severe turbulence in global oil markets. The specific scope of targets — whether including refineries, oil export terminals, and related storage facilities — remains unclear.

The timing of this report is particularly sensitive. The international community has repeatedly called on both sides to resolve disputes through diplomatic channels, and strikes on energy infrastructure could effectively close the window for negotiations. Multiple countries in the Middle East have expressed heightened concern, fearing that further expansion of the conflict could destabilize the entire region.

## Panoramic Analysis

The preparation for joint US-Israeli strikes on Iran's energy infrastructure marks the entry of Middle Eastern geopolitical competition into an entirely new and dangerous phase. From a strategic perspective, selecting energy facilities as targets involves multiple calculations: on one hand, energy exports are the core source of Iran's fiscal revenue, and strikes in this domain could exert maximum pressure on the Iranian economy without causing large-scale civilian casualties; on the other hand, it sends a clear signal to Tehran that if the conflict continues to escalate, its economic foundations face the risk of systematic destruction.

However, the risks of this strategy are equally enormous. The Middle East accounts for a substantial share of daily global oil trading volumes. Once Iranian energy facilities are struck, international oil prices could experience violent fluctuations in the short term, further impacting the global economic recovery process. For central banks already struggling with inflationary pressures, this represents yet another major challenge.

Additionally, Iranian retaliation would be virtually inevitable. Tehran has repeatedly warned that if its critical infrastructure is attacked, it will launch comprehensive counter-strikes against all US military bases and allied facilities in the region. This pattern of mutual escalation is progressively narrowing the space for diplomatic resolution, and the Middle East stands on the brink of full-scale conflict.

## Multiple Perspectives

Analysts supporting strike action argue that only a demonstration of overwhelming military resolve can compel Iran back to the negotiating table. They point out that five months of limited military exchanges have produced no substantive results, with Tehran consistently refusing to make concessions on core issues.

Critics warn that strikes on energy facilities would have serious humanitarian consequences. A sharp reduction in energy export revenue would directly affect the basic livelihoods of Iranian citizens while potentially triggering a global energy price crisis. Relevant United Nations agencies have called on all parties to exercise maximum restraint and avoid military actions that could render the situation irreversible.
