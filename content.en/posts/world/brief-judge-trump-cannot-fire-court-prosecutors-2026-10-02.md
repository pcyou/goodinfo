## Federal Judge Rules Trump Cannot Fire Court-Appointed Prosecutors

Politico reports that a federal judge ruled on October 1 that the President cannot fire court-appointed independent prosecutors, drawing sharp pushback from the White House and the Department of Justice. The ruling rekindles debate over the independence of the special counsel system and is likely headed for fast-track review at the federal appeals court.

The ruling's core thrust is to safeguard the legal tradition of keeping prosecutors appointed by the courts institutionally independent, particularly in investigations touching senior government officials. In the opinion, the judge stressed that court-appointed prosecutors should not be removed at will under political pressure, given the credibility implications for any Department of Justice probe into its own leadership. The White House has signaled it is weighing an appeal, arguing that the President holds broad constitutional authority over executive-branch subordinates. The trajectory of the case will have meaningful implications for the boundaries of separation of powers in the United States.
