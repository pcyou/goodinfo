**[Core Summary]** According to news reports, after Skydance Media completed its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery, the new CEO David Ellison has launched a brand overhaul internally referred to as "Skydance Corp." The move signals a definitive end of the historic Warner Bros. brand era.

**[Event Details]**
- **Merger Background**: After months of federal and state regulatory review, Paramount Skydance completed its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, creating one of the largest entertainment companies in the United States.
- **Rebrand Details**: Internal memos reveal that the company will adopt "Skydance Corp." as its overall brand, with "Warner Bros. Discovery" and "WBD" identities gradually stepping down.
- **Strategic Shift**: Ellison positioned the company in internal letters as a "technology-driven global entertainment group," hinting at heavier investment in streaming, interactive entertainment, and AI content factories.
- **Personnel Moves**: Most former WBD executives remain, but creative decision-making tilts toward Ellison and his core team.
- **Industry Reaction**: Writers' guilds and independent directors' associations expressed cautious attitudes, fearing the replacement of original content investment; streaming competitors began reassessing content acquisition strategies.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**
**First**: The effective repackaging of the nearly-century-old Warner Bros. brand signals the entry of a new phase in American content industry: "tech + entertainment" as the dominant logic. The marginal cost of streaming, AI content generation, and global distribution is being pushed further down; traditional studios' brand equity is no longer a competitive advantage. Scale technology and distribution network become the new moat. Ellison's rebrand strategy essentially signals to capital and talent markets that the Warner story is over and the Skydance story is just beginning.
**Second**: This rebrand will deeply impact the global content rights procurement market. Warner Bros. was the core supplier of DC adaptations, classic film remake rights, and HBO drama licensing. Once negotiation shifts to "Skydance Corp.", global streaming platforms, broadcasting groups, and advertisers need to reassess copyright cost structures. In the short term, the industry will experience a "price discovery" period; in the medium to long term, it may catalyze a new round of content consolidation.
**Third**: Content creators' "creative independence" will face new tension. The Ellison family has a deep background at the intersection of technology and media, but Hollywood traditionalists naturally have reservations about trading efficiency for creativity. In the next 12-18 months, the company may see clear adjustments in original scriptwriting, independent production, and documentary investment, potentially triggering a new round of labor negotiation focus points for writers' and directors' guilds.
