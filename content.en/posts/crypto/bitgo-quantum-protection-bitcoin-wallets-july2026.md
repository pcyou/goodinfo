## Core Summary

Cryptocurrency custody provider BitGo has officially launched quantum computing protection features, adding a layer of security against quantum computing attacks for its institutional clients' Bitcoin wallets. Although current quantum computers have not yet reached the capability to break traditional encryption algorithms, BitGo's move demonstrates forward-looking security strategy aimed at preventing potential "store now, decrypt later" threats.

## Event Overview

According to TheBlock, BitGo's quantum protection solution uses post-quantum cryptography algorithms, layering quantum-attack-resistant protection over existing encryption layers. The technology can provide future-oriented security for custodied assets without changing user operational habits.

BitGo CEO Mike Belshe stated that while quantum threats are not yet reality, institutional clients need to deploy protective measures in advance. He also noted that single-digit percentage ownership levels of Bitcoin supply make sense for large holders like Strategy.

## Panoramic Perspective

BitGo's quantum protection initiative reflects the crypto industry's emphasis on long-term security challenges. Quantum computing development may pose threats to Bitcoin addresses based on elliptic curve encryption. Although experts estimate this threat is at least 10-15 years away from becoming reality, advance preparation is crucial.

This development may also accelerate the industry's transition to post-quantum cryptography. Other custody providers and exchanges may follow with similar measures, forming new security standards. Simultaneously, this creates business opportunities for quantum security startups.
