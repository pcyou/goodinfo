## Key Facts

Across Protocol said on Wednesday that its website had been restored after hackers hijacked the domain. A social engineering attack targeting the domain registrar Namecheap allowed attackers to redirect users to a phishing site, and the cross-chain bridge said it was investigating a potential security incident. Several users reported losing funds after interacting with the platform while the domain was under attacker control. The protocol had warned users to avoid the app during the investigation.

## Wider Perspective

Attacks of this kind deserve separate attention because they sidestep every defensive lesson the crypto industry has accumulated in recent years. For any past theft, the defensive investigation starts on-chain: transaction hashes, token flows, approval records, traced layer by layer. A domain hijack cuts that path entirely. The assets did leave the chain, but the approving action happened off-chain, and the attacker was working with a page that looked entirely normal and a signing request that raised no alarms.

The chain of responsibility is more fragmented, which makes the problem harder. The domain registrar, the DNS provider, the protocol's frontend, the browser and the wallet extension — the response speed of any single link determines whether users still have a chance to notice. The attacker's cost is trivial by comparison: socially engineering a registrar account is likely orders of magnitude cheaper than writing a contract vulnerability that survives an audit. What actually gets tested is the maturity of the defensive process, not the security of the crypto code itself.

The protocol's decision to warn users away from the app during the investigation rather than restore functionality first and investigate afterwards is the right order of operations. But in the first hours after an attack, what decides the outcome is the registrar's response speed on the DNS side. Had resolution been restored before users started reporting, the amount lost would have been smaller. Most of the loss here was likely incurred inside that window.
