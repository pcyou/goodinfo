## Summary

Taiwan has passed a comprehensive cryptocurrency regulation law requiring all digital asset platforms to obtain licenses from the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) before operating. The legislation introduces reserve requirements and strict penalties for non-compliance, positioning Taiwan as one of the most rigorously regulated crypto jurisdictions in Asia.

## Details

The bill, passed on July 1 and sent for presidential approval, rests on three pillars: mandatory licensing for all exchanges, custodians, and wallet providers; reserve mandates requiring platforms to hold a percentage of client assets; and severe penalties including fines and criminal liability for unlicensed operators.

The legislation ends years of legal uncertainty for Taiwan's crypto industry, which previously operated under soft administrative guidelines without a clear legal framework. The FSC drew on regulatory models from Singapore, Japan, and the EU in drafting the law.

## Analysis

Taiwan's move is a significant node in Asia's crypto regulatory competition. The island is benchmarking against Singapore's Payment Services Act and Hong Kong's VASP licensing regime, with even stricter reserve requirements that signal a strong commitment to investor protection.

While short-term compliance costs will rise, the clear regulatory framework is expected to attract traditional financial institutions seeking legal certainty, particularly after a series of global exchange collapses in 2025.

## Industry Reactions

Supporters say the law provides much-needed legal clarity and could position Taiwan as a compliant crypto hub in Asia. However, smaller firms worry that high capital requirements may create barriers to entry, concentrating the market among larger players. International analysts note Taiwan's model could become a template for other Asian economies, particularly its reserve mandate approach.
