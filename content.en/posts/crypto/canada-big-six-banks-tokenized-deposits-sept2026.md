## Core Summary

Canada's six largest commercial banks — Bank of Montreal, Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce, National Bank of Canada, Royal Bank of Canada, Scotiabank and TD Bank Group — have jointly announced an exploration of a Canadian-dollar tokenized deposit system. The first phase will focus on moving digital representations of bank deposits across participating institutions, with a longer-term view of linking to broader digital asset ecosystems. TD Bank disclosed the initiative on Tuesday, with Canadian media reporting in parallel.

## Event Details

**How the system would work**: According to CoinDesk, a tokenized deposit is a digital representation of money already held at a bank, rather than a separate stablecoin issued by a crypto-native company. The participating banks say the design aims to enable faster, programmable, around-the-clock payments while keeping customer funds inside the regulated banking perimeter.

**Phased rollout**: The first phase of the project targets the efficient movement of tokenized deposits across Canadian financial institutions. The longer-term goal, the joint statement said, is to connect with other emerging digital asset initiatives. More banks could join later, but the group has not committed to a launch date or to actually issuing tokenized deposits.

**Global context**: The Canadian move lands alongside a broader international push. In the United States, regional lenders are building a shared tokenized-deposit network on ZK Sync to rival stablecoins, while JPMorgan, Citi and Wells Fargo are pursuing their own institutional offerings. Swift has begun testing tokenized deposits for 24/7 cross-border payments with banks across six continents. Domestically, the Bank of Canada, RBC and TD completed Project Samara in March, issuing, trading and settling a 100 million Canadian-dollar bond on a distributed ledger using tokenized wholesale Canadian dollars. Canada is also building out a domestic stablecoin market, with Shopify and National Bank of Canada among backers of a regulated digital Canadian dollar.

## Full Picture

The Big Six banks coming together on tokenized deposits is not an isolated product experiment. It is the traditional banking system responding in a coordinated way to the rise of stablecoins and on-chain payment networks. Over the past two years, dollar stablecoins such as Tether and USDC have absorbed meaningful share in cross-border payments, trade settlement and the digital asset ecosystem, and commercial banks have felt the squeeze in both retail and wholesale corridors. Building a shared, programmable tokenized rail inside the regulated banking perimeter is an attempt to reclaim that ground using the same architectural advantages — programmability, real-time settlement, fewer intermediaries — that have powered stablecoin adoption.

The decisive feature of this initiative is coordination. Previous tokenized-deposit efforts have generally been led by a single bank, which limits cross-bank network effects. When six of the largest Canadian banks commit jointly, the resulting infrastructure inherits institutional liquidity and interoperability from day one. If the project advances, Canada could become the first major jurisdiction in which tokenized deposits function as a fully fledged interbank settlement medium within a mainstream banking system. The challenges are non-trivial: regulators still need to clarify the legal boundary between tokenized deposits and stablecoins, banks must reconcile legacy core systems with distributed-ledger settlement, and six roughly comparable institutions need a governance model that holds over multiple cycles.

At a macro level, this announcement is one of several signals that the world's largest central banks and commercial banks are taking the "tokenized deposit" middle ground seriously. The ECB has rolled out Project Pontes to invest in tokenized securities; Swift is piloting tokenized settlement with global partners; now Canada's biggest banks are pooling resources around the same idea. The narrative in the public conversation may shift from "can crypto replace fiat" to "can incumbents rebuild their moat on-chain."

## Viewpoints

CoinDesk's reporting uses the joint bank statement and the prior Samara pilot to frame Canada as a country pursuing a coherent strategy for regulated, bank-led tokenization. Canadian regulators have generally welcomed the direction, noting that keeping client funds inside supervised institutions reduces systemic risk; some industry observers, however, warn that the joint Big Six effort could create new barriers to entry, leaving smaller banks and fintechs on the periphery of an on-chain Canadian-dollar ecosystem.

International views are split. Supporters argue that incumbents bring compliance, risk management and institutional trust that stablecoins cannot match, making a bank-led tokenized rail more likely to win regulatory endorsement and large-customer adoption. Critics counter that commercial banks have historically lagged crypto-native firms on product velocity and user experience, and that even a successful tokenized-deposit system may not reclaim the retail payment share stablecoins have taken. The ultimate test for this Canadian project will be whether it delivers real improvements in interbank settlement speed, supports interoperability with stablecoin-based networks, and obtains a clear legal characterization for "deposit-type tokens."
