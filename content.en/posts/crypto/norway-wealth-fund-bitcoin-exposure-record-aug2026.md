## Core Summary

According to the latest report from K33 Research, Norway's sovereign wealth fund (Norges Bank Investment Management) has reached an all-time high in Bitcoin exposure through indirect investments. The report shows that Strategy accounts for 86% of the fund's Bitcoin exposure composition. Additionally, the fund disclosed an $88 million stake in Ethereum treasury firm Bitmine. This data indicates that one of the world's largest sovereign wealth funds is gradually increasing its allocation to crypto assets through indirect channels, signaling a fundamental shift in traditional financial institutions' attitude toward crypto assets.

## Event Details

K33 Research's report released on August 14 indicates that Norway's sovereign wealth fund has gained significant Bitcoin and Ethereum exposure indirectly through holdings in crypto-related companies such as Strategy and Bitmine. Strategy, as the world's largest corporate Bitcoin holder, dominates the fund's crypto asset allocation.

The report also shows that the fund recently increased its Bitmine stake, significantly boosting its Ethereum exposure. Bitmine is a publicly listed company focused on Ethereum treasury strategy, with a business model similar to Strategy, achieving asset appreciation through holding cryptocurrencies.

This disclosure has attracted widespread market attention. Analysts point out that as one of the world's largest sovereign wealth funds, Norway's investment strategy is typically viewed as a bellwether for institutional investment. The fund's indirect increase in crypto asset allocation indicates that traditional financial institutions' attitude toward crypto assets is shifting from observation to active participation.

## Panoramic Perspective

Norway's sovereign wealth fund's crypto asset allocation strategy reflects deeper changes in the institutional investment landscape. The fund manages over $1.4 trillion in assets, and its investment decisions typically follow strict risk assessment and compliance review procedures. By indirectly holding shares in crypto-related companies rather than directly purchasing cryptocurrencies, the fund maintains risk control while gaining exposure to the crypto asset market.

This 'indirect allocation' strategy has important demonstration effects. Other sovereign wealth funds, pension funds, and insurance companies may follow this model, participating in the crypto asset market by investing in crypto-related publicly listed companies. This approach satisfies regulatory requirements for risk exposure review while enabling participation in gains from crypto asset price appreciation.

From a market impact perspective, sovereign wealth fund participation will bring long-term stable capital inflows to the crypto asset market. Compared to retail investors, institutional investors have longer investment horizons and more rational investment decisions, which will help reduce market volatility and promote the maturation and standardization of the crypto asset market.

However, this also brings new regulatory challenges. Regulatory authorities in various countries need to reassess the risks of public funds such as sovereign wealth funds participating in the crypto asset market and develop corresponding regulatory frameworks. Balancing innovation encouragement with risk prevention will become the core issue for future regulatory policy.
