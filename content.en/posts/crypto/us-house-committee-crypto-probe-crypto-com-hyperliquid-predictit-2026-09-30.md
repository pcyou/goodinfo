# U.S. House Committee Expands Crypto Probe Targeting Crypto.com, Hyperliquid and PredictIt

## Core Summary

The Block reports that the U.S. House Committee has expanded its regulatory investigation into the crypto industry, formally requesting records from Crypto.com, Hyperliquid, and PredictIt covering government insider activity, employee trading behavior, and suspicious activity reports. The escalation signals continued congressional pressure on compliance issues in crypto and prediction markets, with the named platforms facing closer scrutiny of their identity verification mechanisms and internal monitoring systems.

## Event Details

According to The Block's reporting, the investigation was initiated by the chairman of the relevant House Committee, and the targets span centralized exchanges, decentralized derivatives protocols, and prediction market platforms. The formal letters request internal documentation on identity verification mechanisms, transaction records involving government insiders, employee trading activity data, and platform compliance materials covering anti-money-laundering and suspicious activity reporting.

The reporting emphasizes that the committee is paying particular attention to trading activity by so-called "government insiders." This means the committee is trying to clarify whether crypto and prediction market platforms have implemented sufficient identity verification and transaction monitoring measures for users with government backgrounds or public office status. This focal issue has been raised in multiple regulatory discussions before, but this congressional investigation marks its entry into substantive advancement.

Among the three named platforms, Crypto.com is a globally significant centralized crypto exchange by user scale, Hyperliquid is an emerging decentralized perpetual contract protocol, and PredictIt is a prominent U.S.-based political and event prediction market platform. The three platforms differ substantially in business model, user base, and regulatory path, but their inclusion in the same probe indicates that the committee's concerns have expanded from individual platforms to the industry's overall compliance structure.

The reporting also notes that the data being requested covers an extended time period, suggesting investigators want to systematically analyze the platforms' historical compliance performance on identity verification and suspicious transaction monitoring rather than focusing on any single event or user. This kind of long-term and broad-coverage investigation typically foreshadows broader legislative or regulatory action.

## Panoramic Analysis

The House Committee's expansion of the probe reflects new dynamics across multiple dimensions of crypto and prediction market regulation, with profound implications for the industry's compliance trajectory.

First, **identity verification is becoming a central regulatory concern**. For a long time, identity verification mechanisms across the crypto industry have differed significantly between platforms, with some adopting relatively loose verification standards at user onboarding. This differentiated compliance posture has been a long-standing focus of regulators, but the formal congressional investigation elevates identity verification from industry self-regulation to congressional legislative attention. Platforms that cannot demonstrate effective identity verification may face stricter legal obligations in the future.

Second, **the regulatory path for prediction markets is accelerating**. The inclusion of PredictIt in the probe signals that U.S. legislators are accelerating their exploration of a regulatory path for prediction markets. The compliance boundaries for prediction markets under U.S. law have long been ambiguous, and this investigation may provide an important empirical foundation for subsequent legislation. Once the regulatory framework for prediction markets is clarified, affected platforms will face costs from business model adjustments, but they will also gain clearer legal expectations.

Third, **the regulatory arbitrage space for decentralized protocols is narrowing**. The inclusion of Hyperliquid as a decentralized derivatives protocol in the probe signals a substantive shift in regulators' attitudes toward decentralized protocol arbitrage space. Decentralized protocols have long evaded traditional financial regulation under a "code is law" posture, but the congressional investigation indicates that regulators are beginning to define regulatory scope based on business substance rather than technical architecture. If this trend is further strengthened, it will have profound implications for the compliance strategy of the entire decentralized finance ecosystem.

From a broader perspective, the investigation also reflects the gradual advancement strategy of U.S. crypto regulation. Unlike the EU's unified legislative path through MiCA, the U.S. has not yet formed a complete crypto regulatory framework at the federal level, with regulatory progress relying more on congressional case-by-case investigations, regulatory agency enforcement, and state-level regulatory exploration in parallel. The further trajectory of this investigation will to some extent determine the pace and specific content of federal crypto regulatory legislation in the United States.

## Multiple Perspectives

The committee chairman made clear in the investigation that the goal is to clarify the actual performance of crypto and prediction market platforms on identity verification and suspicious transaction monitoring, and emphasized that the findings will be used to assess the necessity of related legislation. This signals the legislature's active posture in advancing crypto regulation.

Some voices in the crypto industry have expressed understanding of the investigation, arguing that stronger identity verification and suspicious transaction monitoring will help the industry's long-term healthy development. However, other voices worry that overly strict compliance requirements may suppress innovation space, particularly for decentralized protocols where the decentralized nature of the technical architecture is in natural tension with centralized compliance requirements.

The attitude of prediction market platforms such as PredictIt is more cautious, because the regulatory path for prediction markets under the U.S. legal system has not been fully clarified. How these platforms respond to identity verification and suspicious activity reporting requirements in this investigation will to some extent shape the specific form of the future prediction market regulatory framework.
