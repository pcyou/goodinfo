[Core Summary]

According to The Block on Oct. 17, Turkish authorities confirmed the arrest of Hex founder Richard Heart in Turkey. During searches of 15 addresses, police seized approximately $7.15 million in cash, six cold wallets, seven firearms and 1,400 rounds of ammunition. The report, citing Turkey's interior ministry, notes that the operation is part of a multi-stage investigation into cross-border illegal activity in the crypto sector, again focusing outside attention on cross-border enforcement and regulatory risk for crypto-industry principals.

[Event Details]

The Block, citing the official notice from Turkey's interior ministry, disclosed the details of the arrest. The notice said law-enforcement authorities had arrested Richard Heart following a multi-stage investigation and simultaneously searched 15 addresses in Turkey, seizing about $7.15 million in cash, six cold wallets, seven firearms and 1,400 rounds of ammunition. The notice did not publicly disclose all the charges, stating only that the operation falls within the framework of the country's investigation into "cross-border illegal activity in the crypto sector."

In its independent investigation, The Block added that Richard Heart's long-term residence had not been fixed, that he had moved between several European and Asian countries over the past few years, and that his Hex project had been listed on multiple crypto exchanges with an investor base spanning multiple jurisdictions. The reason this Turkish action has drawn wide attention in the crypto industry is that it involves the project's "central figure" rather than ordinary team members — the whereabouts and asset status of a central figure are directly tied to project governance, token liquidity and investor confidence.

The report also noted that the Hex token saw significant volatility immediately after the news. Multiple market-data platforms showed that Hex quotes on several exchanges fell sharply, with some smaller exchanges seeing an "order-pulling tide" in quote depth amid panic selling. Some long-term holders told The Block that the incident made them reconsider the vulnerability of "projects driven by a few central figures" under regulatory pressure.

[Multi-Party Viewpoints]

Turkey's interior ministry and judicial authorities stressed that the operation was based on lawful authorization and multi-stage investigation, in accordance with the country's legal procedures, and emphasized that cracking down on illegal activity in the crypto sector is a routine task of the country's financial regulation. Turkey's regulators have repeatedly stated in recent years that they will continue to step up enforcement against crypto-asset service providers and cross-border illegal activity to protect domestic investors and maintain financial market order.

The crypto industry and legal community have reacted in mixed ways. Observers supportive of the enforcement argue that the crypto industry should not become a "safe harbor" for evading the law, and that cross-border enforcement is a necessary step toward compliance. Critics counter that, in the absence of a complete cross-border judicial-assistance mechanism, key figures in the crypto sector are prone to legal difficulties stemming from differences in jurisdiction and information asymmetry, and that the industry needs a clearer international regulatory coordination framework, otherwise similar "raid-style enforcement" will continue to roil the market.

Within the Hex community, opinion has split. Some supporters believe that the incident will eventually "settle," and that Richard Heart's personal legal issues will not affect Hex's protocol code and operational logic. Other long-term holders worry that the absence of the central figure could leave project governance, partnerships and ecosystem development in limbo, and even trigger a medium- and long-term decline in token price.

[Panoramic Perspective]

From a broader perspective, the incident reflects the "central-figure risk" of the crypto industry in the context of regulatory normalization. Unlike traditional finance, some crypto projects still rely heavily on a few central figures for public communication and decision-making — a structure that is particularly fragile under regulatory pressure. When a central figure is subject to cross-border enforcement, the token market can see violent short-term swings, with investor confidence and project continuity both under strain.

The deeper impact lies in the recalibration of industry governance paradigms. In the short term, some "central-figure-driven" projects may accelerate a shift toward "de-central-figure governance" structures — for example, introducing multi-signature wallets, independent governance committees and more transparent on-chain financial disclosures. In the medium and long term, regulators in different jurisdictions need to establish more efficient cross-border enforcement and judicial-assistance mechanisms, while crypto projects need to treat "central-figure compliance" as a risk topic on par with "code compliance." Otherwise, similar incidents will keep recurring across jurisdictions, further eroding mainstream institutional and ordinary investor trust in crypto assets.
