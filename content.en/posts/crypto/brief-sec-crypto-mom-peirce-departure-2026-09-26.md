## Core Summary According to CoinDesk, US Securities and Exchange Commission Commissioner Hester Peirce will officially depart on October 2. Peirce — known in the industry as "Crypto Mom" — has been one of the most crypto-friendly and engaged commissioners on US SEC digital-asset policy in recent years; news of her departure has drawn broad attention from the industry and regulatory observers.

## Event Details

Reports indicate Peirce's departure is widely anticipated, with her term as commissioner nearing its end. Peirce has long advocated for a clear and predictable crypto-asset regulatory framework and has offered constructive input in several key policy debates. Before leaving she also contributed to the SEC's latest guidance on topics including token buybacks, network upgrades, and promises of profit. Industry voices cited in the coverage said her departure will affect the tone of crypto-policy discussions within the SEC in the short term, while the longer-term impact will gradually become apparent through the appointment of a new generation of commissioners and the progress of bipartisan crypto legislation.

## Comparison of Viewpoints

Crypto industry representatives widely expressed regret over Peirce's departure, emphasizing her role in pushing for clear regulation and fair enforcement; some academic and regulatory researchers argued that her departure will create room for a new generation of crypto-friendly commissioners, while also meaning the industry will need to rebuild new channels of dialogue in the policy arena.

## Full Picture Viewed at a deeper level, Peirce's departure reflects a generational shift in the US crypto regulatory landscape. In recent years the SEC's stance on crypto-asset regulation has swung back and forth — from broad crackdowns to gradual acceptance, from enforcement-first to rules-led — and Peirce's presence provided rare continuity and stability to this complex policy debate. Her departure means US crypto regulation will enter a phase that relies more heavily on bipartisan legislation and policy coordination with a new administration. For the crypto industry, the key questions over the coming months — including stablecoin legislation, the approval paths for spot Ethereum and altcoin ETFs, and the regulatory perimeter for tokenized assets — will increasingly depend on multilateral bargaining between the two parties in Congress, the regulators, and the industry itself, rather than on the individual stance of any single commissioner.
