## Core Summary

CoinDesk and CNBC reported on September 15 that the CLARITY Act, which seeks to establish a unified regulatory framework for the US crypto market, failed to secure the 60 votes needed to pass the Senate cloture vote. The core obstacles to passage were the ethics controversy over President Trump's family crypto wealth and the long-standing partisan divide over the regulatory boundary between the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC).

## Event Details

The September 15 cloture vote was designed to push the CLARITY Act toward final passage. Under US Senate rules, a cloture motion requires 60 votes. However, the affirmative vote tally fell short of this threshold on September 15, leaving the CLARITY Act with no realistic path to full Senate passage in the short term.

Two major legislative obstacles emerged. The first was the ethics controversy. Multiple Democratic senators explicitly opposed the special exemption provisions for Trump family crypto assets in the bill, arguing this would set a dangerous precedent where a US president's family uses public power to endorse personal commercial interests. Senator Warren publicly stated before the vote that she supports passing a crypto bill quickly, but the CLARITY Act is "just not it."

The second obstacle was the fundamental partisan divide over regulatory authority: Republicans favored CFTC oversight of spot crypto markets, while Democrats insisted on retaining SEC authority over the securities classification of most crypto assets.

Industry reaction to the vote was a mix of "deep disappointment but continued hope." Major US crypto industry associations noted that passage of the CLARITY Act would have provided unprecedented clarity on US spot Bitcoin and Ethereum ETF market structure, stablecoin reserve asset management, and decentralized finance (DeFi) protocol compliance paths. Its short-term failure means the industry will continue operating in an environment lacking federal-level unified legislation, relying on state licensing regimes and existing regulatory guidance.

On the path forward, the Senate Majority Leader has not yet announced whether the CLARITY Act will be re-introduced for a vote in the current Congressional session. One possible path is to strip the ethics controversy provisions and seek bipartisan consensus on the pure regulatory framework. Another is to incorporate core CLARITY Act content into larger must-pass omnibus legislation for fiscal year 2027. Either way, the US crypto industry will not gain the federal-level regulatory certainty it has long awaited in the short term.

## Panoramic Analysis

The CLARITY Act's failure in the Senate cloture vote appears to be a routine legislative setback but actually reflects a deeper point of contention as US crypto regulation enters a more complex phase of negotiations.

From a legislative technical perspective, the failure exposes the fundamental bipartisan divide over crypto regulation — this is not a simple partisan dispute, but a philosophical divide over whether crypto assets should be classified as securities or commodities. Republicans favor placing most crypto assets under CFTC oversight, emphasizing market innovation and global competitiveness; Democrats prefer retaining SEC authority over substantive review of token issuance, emphasizing investor protection and systemic risk control. Reconciling these positions will require longer political negotiations than simple bill text amendments.

From a global regulatory coordination perspective, the US CLARITY Act setback will have subtle effects on crypto legislative processes in the EU, UK, Singapore, and other jurisdictions. On one hand, the continuation of US regulatory uncertainty means the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) becomes relatively more attractive, and European crypto firms may accelerate the migration of compliance architectures to the EU. On the other hand, Hong Kong, Japan, South Korea, and other Asian jurisdictions may seize this opportunity to increase their outreach to US crypto firms, further intensifying the phenomenon of regulatory arbitrage in the global crypto compliance architecture.

From a market structure perspective, in the short term the US domestic crypto industry will continue relying on state licenses (such as New York's BitLicense) and existing regulatory guidance. This impact is relatively manageable for large compliant crypto firms (such as Coinbase, Kraken), but for small and medium-sized innovative projects, the lack of federal-level clear rules means higher legal risk premiums and financing difficulty. Although the issuance structure of spot Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs is established, the underlying spot market mechanisms for market making, short selling, and leverage remain in regulatory gray areas.

From a political economy perspective, the ethics controversy triggered by Trump family crypto wealth has effectively reignited the fundamental US domestic debate about the boundary between presidential personal commercial interests and public policy formulation. The aftereffects of this issue will extend far beyond the CLARITY Act itself and may affect the standards by which the US Congress reviews all future legislation, appropriations, and regulatory personnel nominations involving crypto assets.

From a long-term trend perspective, this vote setback does not end US crypto legislative progress but rather postpones it to a more politically negotiable window. Historically, major US financial legislation (such as the Dodd-Frank Act and the Inflation Reduction Act) experienced multiple setbacks before ultimately passing. CLARITY Act supporters expect to relaunch the legislation in the new Congress following the 2026 midterm elections.

## Multiple Perspectives Comparison

**US crypto industry associations**: Major crypto firms including Coinbase, Kraken, and Ripple issued a joint statement through industry associations expressing "disappointment but understanding" over the vote outcome, emphasizing continued cooperation with bipartisan senators to seek bill restart. They also called on federal regulators to provide "good-faith regulatory space" for compliance innovation by the crypto industry in the absence of legislation.

**Senator Warren's position**: Democratic Senator Warren made clear after the vote that she still hopes to push through a crypto bill, but the prerequisite is that it must include mandatory conflict-of-interest recusal provisions regarding the president's family crypto wealth and strengthen retail investor protections. She argued that the CLARITY Act should be "rewritten and reintroduced" rather than simply restated for a vote.

**Senator Lummis's position**: Republican Senator Lummis, who led Senate crypto legislative work, expressed strong dissatisfaction with the vote outcome, arguing that a historic opportunity to provide a globally leading regulatory framework for the US digital asset industry had been missed. She hinted at seeking to incorporate core CLARITY Act provisions into omnibus legislation.

**Wall Street institutional views**: Internal research notes from major Wall Street institutions including Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan following the vote indicated that the CLARITY Act setback would slow the pace at which US institutional capital enters the crypto market on a large scale but would not change the long-term trend. Institutions expect that regulatory uncertainty will cause US spot crypto ETF inflows in the second half of 2026 to fall short of prior expectations.

**International regulator reactions**: The UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), and European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) all refrained from issuing official statements after the vote result was announced, but industry observers noted that all three had recently been conducting regulatory sandbox engagement talks with US crypto firms and may use this opportunity to attract more US crypto business to their jurisdictions.
