## Core Summary

Stablecoin giant Circle officially launched the Arc blockchain mainnet on September 16, with BlackRock, Visa, Citi, and BNY Mellon among the first validators and 10 billion ARC tokens minted at launch. Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire called Arc 'more consequential' than USDC, marking the issuer's strategic transformation from a single-product company into a blockchain infrastructure operator and signaling that institutional capital is moving into crypto infrastructure at unprecedented speed.

## Event Details

### Arc Mainnet Officially Live

According to The Block on September 16, Circle officially launched the Arc blockchain mainnet, with BlackRock, Visa, Citi, BNY Mellon and other institutions among the first validators. Arc positions itself as an institutional-grade application layer, adopting a 'permissioned public chain' model that preserves the openness of public chains while attracting institutional capital through a compliance framework. Circle simultaneously minted 10 billion ARC tokens for network launch.

### Circle's Strategic Transformation

Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire stated in the announcement that Arc is 'more consequential' than USDC. USDC, as the world's second-largest stablecoin, has already become the infrastructure of the cryptocurrency market, but Circle hopes Arc will establish an 'institutional-grade financial application platform' covering payments, securities, derivatives, central bank digital currencies, and other fields.

### Strategic Value of Validator Nodes

The participation of top institutions such as BlackRock, Visa, and Citi brings three layers of strategic value to Arc: first, providing institutional-grade credit endorsement; second, ensuring compliance frameworks meet traditional financial regulatory requirements; third, laying the foundation for large-scale institutional capital to enter the Arc ecosystem in the future. This is the deepest engagement of traditional finance with public chains since the Enterprise Ethereum Alliance (EEA) in 2017.

## Panoramic Analysis

**First, stablecoin issuers are evolving into 'financial infrastructure operators'.** Circle's launch of the Arc blockchain marks an upgrade in the stablecoin race from 'single product competition' to 'full-stack infrastructure competition.' Over the past few years, stablecoin issuers have primarily competed on scale, compliance, and liquidity, but with the head products USDC, USDT, and Tether gradually consolidating, extending downward to the blockchain base layer and upward to application scenarios has become an inevitable choice for leading issuers. Circle's 'full-stack layout' will reshape the competitive boundaries of the stablecoin industry.

**Second, institutional capital is systematically moving into crypto infrastructure.** The participation of top institutions such as BlackRock, Visa, and Citi is not a simple matter of 'investing in crypto assets' but rather a deep involvement in blockchain governance through validator nodes. This form of participation means traditional financial institutions are no longer merely 'users' of cryptocurrency but 'participants' and 'governors' of the infrastructure. This profound shift in roles will deeply influence the direction of future cryptocurrency regulatory legislation.

**Third, the 'permissioned public chain' model is becoming the mainstream choice for institutional applications.** For a long time, the public chain world has been divided between 'fully open' and 'fully closed' routes. Fully open implies high transparency and censorship resistance, but is hard to meet institutional compliance requirements; fully closed sacrifices the core value of public chains. Arc's 'permissioned public chain' model attempts to find a balance between the two. If accepted by the market, this model will become the mainstream architecture for future institutional-grade blockchain projects.

**Fourth, ARC token issuance has sparked debate between 'inflation concerns' and 'network effects'.** Circle's one-time minting of 10 billion ARC tokens has triggered 'inflation concerns' among some community members. But Circle explained that ARC tokens will primarily be used for network incentives, ecosystem building, and node rewards, not entering circulation directly. This 'high initial supply + strict unlock' model is typical of institutional-grade public chains, though its market acceptance still requires time to validate.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Circle's Position**: Jeremy Allaire emphasized that Arc is not intended to 'replace' Ethereum or Solana and other general-purpose public chains, but rather to establish a 'dedicated layer for institutional-grade financial applications.' He argued that institutional capital needs a 'compliant, transparent, and supervisable' blockchain, and Arc is designed for exactly that.

**BlackRock's Position**: BlackRock's head of digital assets stated that BlackRock has invested in multiple blockchain projects, including spot Bitcoin ETFs. Arc's 'permissioned public chain' model meets BlackRock's strict compliance requirements, and BlackRock may issue tokenized fund products on Arc in the future.

**Visa's Position**: Visa's head of cryptocurrency business said Visa has already processed billions of dollars in stablecoin payments on the USDC network. Arc's launch will further expand Visa's influence in crypto payments and provide Visa with more efficient cross-border payment solutions.

**Ethereum Community Position**: Some Ethereum core developers have expressed an 'open attitude' toward Arc's rise, believing that a multi-chain ecosystem is in line with blockchain industry evolution. But there are also voices criticizing Arc for 'attempting to kidnap the crypto ethos with compliance frameworks,' deviating from the 'permissionless' original intention of blockchain.

**Regulatory Position**: The US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has not yet made a formal statement on Arc, but it is widely believed in the industry that Arc's 'permissioned public chain' design is likely to receive a friendly regulatory stance. This aligns with the SEC's earlier approval logic for spot Bitcoin ETFs—regulators tend to favor 'compliance-framework' crypto projects.
