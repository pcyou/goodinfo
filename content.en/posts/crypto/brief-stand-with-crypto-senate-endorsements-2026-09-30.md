## [Brief] U.S. Crypto Advocacy Group Stand With Crypto Unveils First Round of Senate Endorsements to Advance Digital Asset Legislation

### Core Summary

According to The Block, U.S. crypto advocacy group Stand With Crypto unveiled its first round of Senate endorsements after the digital asset market structure bill was blocked in a procedural vote, aiming to advance crypto asset-related legislation through grassroots mobilization and policy advocacy. The group mobilized millions of voters on crypto issues during the 2024 election cycle, and the new list is seen as an important signal of the crypto industry's continued push on the legislative front.

### Event Details

According to The Block, the first-round Senate endorsement list released by Stand With Crypto represents the organization's latest policy advocacy action after the crypto market structure bill suffered a setback in a Senate procedural vote. The organization said the initial list focuses on senators with open attitudes toward digital asset legislation, and that subsequent rounds will be dynamically adjusted based on lawmakers' actual voting records on crypto-related bills.

Reports note that the organization mobilized millions of voters on crypto issues during the 2024 election cycle and is regarded as an important symbol of the rising political influence of the crypto industry in Washington. Its capabilities in grassroots mobilization, policy research, and communication with lawmakers have made it an important bridge between the crypto industry and the legislature. The organization said it will continue to push forward key legislation such as the crypto market structure bill in Congress through voter outreach, social media mobilization, and industry resource coordination.

The U.S. crypto industry has repeatedly pushed for crypto market structure bills, stablecoin legislation, and revisions to tax provisions, but related bills have frequently encountered procedural hurdles and cross-party differences during congressional advancement. Industry insiders believe that the release of this list is both a way for the crypto industry to express dissatisfaction with the legislative process and a strategic move to reshape the legislative agenda through grassroots political influence.

Industry commentators noted that the policy game of the crypto industry in the United States is expanding from relying solely on industry lobbying to a comprehensive strategy combining voter mobilization, social media communication, and cross-party communication with lawmakers. This strategic shift is closely related to the voting influence of crypto voter groups in key swing states, and may also make crypto issues an important policy topic in the 2026 midterm elections.
