## Core Summary

The FASB proposed on August 18 a new accounting standard allowing companies to treat qualifying stablecoins as "cash equivalents" for accounting purposes. This means companies holding stablecoins could treat them identically to cash on balance sheets, significantly reducing accounting complexity and compliance costs for crypto asset holdings.

## Event Details

According to CoinDesk, the FASB proposal sets strict conditions: only stablecoins pegged to fiat currency, fully backed by high-quality liquid assets, and redeemable at par would qualify for "cash equivalent" status. This standard would include compliant stablecoins like USDC and PYUSD but potentially exclude algorithmic stablecoins.

The FASB chair stated the proposal reflects stablecoins' increasingly important role in commercial payments. "As more businesses begin accepting and using stablecoins for settlement, accounting standards need to evolve," he noted in a statement.

Meanwhile, White House crypto adviser Patrick Witt expressed optimism about stablecoin legislation prospects but acknowledged ongoing congressional disputes. Wyoming simultaneously announced a state-level stablecoin initiative, showing local governments accelerating crypto regulatory deployment.

## Panoramic Analysis

While appearing as a technical adjustment, the FASB proposal carries milestone-level institutional significance. The "cash equivalent" designation means stablecoins have achieved equal status with traditional fiat currency at the accounting level, producing far-reaching cascading effects.

From an enterprise adoption perspective, many companies previously hesitated due to accounting complexity — holding stablecoins required fair value measurement, with price fluctuations directly impacting income statements. If the new standard passes, this barrier will be eliminated. More multinational corporations and e-commerce platforms are expected to accelerate stablecoin payment acceptance.

From a financial stability perspective, FASB's strict conditions function as a "regulatory screening" mechanism. Only stablecoins with adequate reserves and high transparency receive preferential treatment, accelerating industry consolidation and promoting "good money driving out bad."

From an international competition perspective, the U.S.'s rapid advancement in stablecoin regulatory frameworks may force the EU, Asia, and other financial centers to accelerate legislation. The global stablecoin regulatory competition is heating up.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporters**: The crypto industry broadly welcomes the proposal. Circle (USDC issuer) stated it would "clear the last accounting barrier to enterprise stablecoin adoption." Multiple accounting firms also said the new standard would significantly simplify audit processes.

**Cautious Parties**: Some banking associations worry that "cash equivalent" status for stablecoins could accelerate deposit migration to crypto, increasing traditional banks' funding costs. They call for higher reserve requirements.

**Academic Views**: Multiple finance professors note that while the direction is correct, supporting regulatory mechanisms must be established. "Cash equivalent designation cannot substitute for substantive regulatory oversight," one Stanford professor stated.

**Legislative Attitudes**: Congressional reactions are mixed. Pro-crypto lawmakers see this as "market-driven regulatory innovation," while cautious factions call for clear stablecoin legal status through legislation.
