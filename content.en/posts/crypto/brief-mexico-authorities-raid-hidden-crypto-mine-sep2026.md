## Summary

The Block, citing Reuters, reported on September 13 that Mexican authorities raided a hidden cryptocurrency mining operation on suspicion of power theft and money laundering. The site, located near a local hydroelectric dam, is the fourth crypto farm discovered in the area since early 2025. The incident reflects rising tension in Latin America between energy-intensive crypto mining activity, local power supply stability, and regulatory compliance.

## Details

According to Reuters and The Block, the raided mine was located near a local hydroelectric dam in Mexico and had been operating a hidden Bitcoin and other cryptocurrency mining setup connected to the local grid. Authorities seized mining equipment and electrical connection devices at the scene and launched an investigation into those involved. Initial findings suggest the site allegedly bypassed normal electricity metering through illegal wiring, raising suspicions of power theft and money laundering. This is the fourth such crypto mine discovered in the area since early 2025, and the local power utility and regulators say they will continue to strengthen inspections of similar premises.

The action is part of a series of enforcement operations by Mexican federal and state-level law enforcement agencies targeting the gray industry chain of energy-intensive crypto mining. In recent years, several Mexican states have seen hidden mining operations illegally tapping hydropower stations, natural gas pipelines, or industrial power lines. Some sites' electricity consumption has reached a notable share of local public electricity use, significantly affecting residential and commercial power stability. Mexico's national power utility and state regulators have repeatedly warned that such activities not only constitute electrical crime but can also trigger major power safety incidents.
