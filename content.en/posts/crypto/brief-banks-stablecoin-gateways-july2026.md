# [Brief] Banks Now Asking How to Integrate Stablecoins, Not Whether They Belong in Finance

## Key Summary

According to CoinDesk, global financial institutions are undergoing an attitude shift — from questioning whether stablecoins belong in traditional finance to actively researching how to incorporate them into their business models. Multiple banks are competing to become "secure gateways" for stablecoins, preparing for what is projected to be a dramatic surge in digital asset transaction volumes by 2030. This trend marks a critical leap for stablecoins from crypto-native tools to mainstream financial infrastructure.

## Analysis

The banking sector's attitude shift toward stablecoins carries profound implications. Over the past five years, stablecoins have undergone a triple jump: from "crypto trading medium" to "cross-border payment tool" to "programmable money infrastructure." Banks transitioning from bystanders to participants reflects three structural changes: first, client demand — corporate clients are beginning to request stablecoin custody and settlement services; second, regulatory clarity — stablecoin legislation in multiple countries has provided legal certainty for bank participation; third, competitive pressure — the first-mover advantage of fintech companies and non-bank institutions in stablecoins is forcing banks to accelerate their entry. Notably, banks' emphasis on positioning themselves as "secure gateways" signals their intent to inject their risk management capabilities and compliance advantages into this emerging field, rather than simply serving as conduits.

## Perspectives

- **CoinDesk Analysis**: Banks shifting from "whether they belong" to "how to integrate" marks a critical inflection point for stablecoin mainstreaming.
- **Industry Observation**: Digital asset volumes are projected to surge dramatically by 2030; banks that don't actively participate risk losing clients.
- **Regulatory View**: Bank participation in stablecoin business requires strict capital adequacy and anti-money laundering standards — compliance thresholds must not be lowered in the pursuit of speed.
