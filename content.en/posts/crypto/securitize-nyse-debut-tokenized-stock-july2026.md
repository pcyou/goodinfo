## Core Summary

Blockchain fintech company Securitize has made history by becoming the first company to simultaneously list shares on the New York Stock Exchange and on blockchain. The company tokenized $295 million of its own stock across the Solana and Avalanche blockchains, making it the largest issuer-sponsored tokenized stock at launch. According to The Block, Securitize President Brett Redfearn stated the company is in discussions to tokenize other IPOs "definitely within the next year," suggesting this model could become an industry norm.

## Event Details

According to CoinDesk, Securitize's tokenized shares represent approximately $295 million of the company's own stock, issued on both Solana and Avalanche blockchains. This is the largest issuer-sponsored tokenized stock to date, aiming to demonstrate the superiority of its model against rival third-party stock token issuers.

The Block reported that NYSE President Brett Redfearn said at the listing ceremony that Securitize is in discussions with other companies to tokenize more IPOs "definitely within the next year." This means the integration of traditional securities issuance with blockchain technology is moving from experimental stages to scaled application.

What makes this listing special is that Securitize chose not to rely on third-party platforms to tokenize its stock, instead using its own technology infrastructure to issue directly on-chain. This "issuer-sponsored tokenization" model contrasts sharply with the current market approach where third-party platforms map traditional stocks onto blockchain.

From a technical architecture perspective, choosing Solana and Avalanche rather than Ethereum reflects the issuer's consideration of transaction speed and costs. Solana is known for high throughput and low fees, while Avalanche is favored by institutions for its subnet architecture and fast finality. Dual-chain deployment also reduces single blockchain platform risk.

## Broader Analysis

Securitize's simultaneous listing on the NYSE and blockchain marks a new phase in the convergence of traditional capital markets and decentralized finance. The significance of this event extends far beyond a single company's listing — it represents the birth of an entirely new paradigm for securities issuance and trading.

From the perspective of capital market evolution, this model breaks down the boundary between traditional securities issuance and blockchain token issuance. Previously, tokenized stocks were typically created by third-party platforms "mapping" already-issued traditional stocks onto blockchain, creating a derivative-style token. Securitize's approach issues original stock directly on-chain, giving on-chain tokens the exact same legal status and rights as traditional stock. If widely adopted, this model would fundamentally transform securities issuance infrastructure.

From a regulatory perspective, Securitize's ability to list simultaneously on the NYSE and on-chain indicates US regulators' attitude toward blockchain securities is shifting from cautious observation to conditional acceptance. The New York Stock Exchange, as one of the world's oldest exchanges, embracing blockchain technology sends a powerful signal. This could push other traditional exchanges to accelerate blockchain integration exploration.

From an industry competitive landscape analysis, Securitize's move directly challenges other players in the RWA tokenization track. By proving issuers can complete tokenization independently without third-party intermediaries, Securitize is attempting to redefine how value is distributed across the industry value chain. This could trigger a wave of issuers building their own tokenization infrastructure.

## Perspectives

**Supporters**: Blockchain industry advocates see this as a milestone in "the convergence of traditional and decentralized finance." They note that when traditional giants like the NYSE begin accepting on-chain stock issuance, the digital transformation of the entire financial industry becomes irreversible.

**Cautious Voices**: Traditional finance practitioners warn that on-chain stock issuance still faces numerous challenges, including cross-chain interoperability, smart contract security, and legal remedies for investors who lose on-chain assets.

**Regulatory View**: Securities law experts note this model's successful implementation depends on clear regulatory frameworks. The SEC's attitude toward tokenized securities will be the key factor determining whether this model can scale.

**Competitor Response**: Other tokenization platforms may face pressure to demonstrate unique value of their third-party tokenization model versus issuer-sponsored approaches.
