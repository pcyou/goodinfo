Crypto exchange OKX and Wall Street infrastructure giant Intercontinental Exchange jointly filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on October 4 to launch tokenized versions of NYSE-listed stocks under the SEC's innovation exemption, supporting 24/7 on-chain trading. If approved, it would mark the first deep integration of traditional exchange infrastructure with on-chain assets under a federal regulatory framework.

**[Key Facts]**

- The application was jointly signed by OKX subsidiaries and ICE, with blue-chip NYSE stocks as the underlying securities.
- The tokenized stocks are intended to carry the same shareholder rights as the underlying shares, including voting and dividends.
- The on-chain trading window would break traditional U.S. equity trading hours, covering Asian, European and African off-hours.
- The application invokes the SEC's 2025 "Innovation Exemption" pilot mechanism, emphasizing no new securities-law authorization is being sought.
- ICE will handle underlying clearing and custody; OKX will handle on-chain issuance, circulation and user experience.

**[What We Know]**

- **Cooperative architecture**: OKX handles token issuance, on-chain circulation and matching engine; ICE provides underlying securities custody, settlement and regulatory reporting.
- **Compliance basis**: The filing invokes the SEC's 2025 "Innovation Exemption" mechanism, allowing regulated on-chain securities experiments within capped scale.
- **Investor coverage**: The application does not disclose specific retail or institutional thresholds, but emphasizes KYC/AML compliance consistent with traditional broker-dealers.
- **Settlement mechanism**: Conversion between tokens and underlying shares will be completed by ICE's clearinghouse on a T+0 basis, avoiding the traditional T+2 delay.
- **Regulatory dialogue**: The SEC Chairman's office has repeatedly expressed public support for tokenization experiments, while stressing that investor protection cannot fall below traditional broker standards.

**[Wider Perspective]**

**First, traditional exchange infrastructure is proactively engaging on-chain rails for the first time.** Unlike scattered "crypto tokenized U.S. stock" products previously introduced by Coinbase and similar platforms, this application is led by an established clearing and custody giant — meaning traditional infrastructure is embracing tokenization from the top down rather than defensively. This aligns with the SEC's overall direction after launching the Innovation Exemption mechanism in early 2026.

**Second, the settlement cycle compression from T+2 to T+0 could be a structural shift in financial plumbing.** If tokenized stocks achieve instant settlement, the clearing business model of traditional brokers, custodians and market makers will be partially reshaped; global capital flow velocity could increase, posing new risk-management challenges for participants reliant on overnight financing.

**Third, the regulatory core is "whether tokenization constitutes a new security."** The SEC has long held the Howey Test, treating most tokenized securities as falling under federal securities law. OKX and ICE's use of the Innovation Exemption is, in effect, a request for the SEC to render a "non-unsecured" determination within the "exemption sandbox" — paving the way for larger-scale tokenization.

**Fourth, the compliant channel for cross-border capital flows may be rewritten.** 24/7 trading combined with tokenized clearing means Asian and European institutional investors can participate in U.S. equities during their local trading sessions, without the FX and cross-border settlement friction of traditional brokers. This will have long-term consequences for global asset allocation patterns.

**Fifth, the challengers and potential resistance include BlackRock, Coinbase and other incumbents' stated stance.** The OKX-ICE partnership may spur other large asset managers and exchanges to accelerate. Coinbase, Galaxy and other companies with public-market experience have already filed similar products — this application may accelerate the industry into a "two-layer regulatory" era — SEC overseeing off-chain, CFTC and state regulators overseeing on-chain.

**[Viewpoints Compared]**

- ICE CEO: Described tokenization as "the next stage of Wall Street infrastructure," emphasizing full compatibility with the existing regulatory system.
- SEC Commissioner: In a public speech reiterated that "tokenization cannot come at the cost of weakening investor protection," demanding complete clearing and risk-control documentation from applicants.
- OKX Chief Legal Officer: Framed it as a "key step for compliant entry into the U.S. market," promising fund segregation on par with traditional brokers.
- Coinbase executive: On social media called it "a marker of the on-chain world reverse-engineering Wall Street."
- Harvard Law School professor of financial law: Warned that if the Innovation Exemption lacks a clear exit mechanism, it may allow systemic financial events to slip through undetected.
- China Securities Regulatory Commission representative: At an industry forum said China is studying "on-chain securities" pilots, emphasizing financial infrastructure sovereignty and cross-border regulatory coordination as core issues.
