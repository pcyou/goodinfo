## Core Summary

Tokenized asset giant Ondo Finance is embroiled in an intense battle for control following the death of founder Nathan Allman. Delaware court filings reveal that Allman's mother is seeking to take over the company and remove current CEO Ian De Bode. The inheritance dispute exposes systemic deficiencies in corporate governance and succession planning within the crypto industry.

## Event Details

According to three Delaware court filings, Ondo Finance's corporate governance has descended into chaos following the death of founder Nathan Allman in May. Allman's mother is now seeking control of the company and demanding the removal of current CEO Ian De Bode.

After Allman's death, Ondo Finance announced that Ian De Bode would take over as CEO. However, this arrangement has not been accepted by the founder's family. Allman's mother believes that, as the founder's immediate family member, she should have a greater say in company decisions.

Ondo Finance is a major player in the tokenized asset space, managing billions of dollars in on-chain assets. The company focuses on tokenizing traditional financial products such as U.S. Treasuries and stocks, issuing them on blockchains like Solana. This power struggle could affect the company's strategic direction and investor confidence.

## Panoramic Perspective

This incident reveals deeper issues in corporate governance within the crypto industry. Unlike traditional enterprises, many crypto projects are led by technical founders without robust succession planning or board oversight mechanisms. When founders pass away suddenly, companies often fall into power vacuums.

From an industry perspective, Ondo Finance's predicament could impact the development of the entire tokenized asset sector. Tokenization is seen as a core application of blockchain technology, expected to reshape trillions of dollars in financial markets over the next decade. Investors need to pay attention to the governance structures of crypto projects, not just technological innovation.

Additionally, this incident has sparked discussions about protecting founder rights. In the crypto industry, founders often hold large token holdings, yet inheritance issues are rarely addressed. As the industry matures, establishing more standardized corporate governance frameworks has become urgent.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporting Current Management**: Some industry observers believe Ian De Bode, as a seasoned practitioner, has the capability to lead Ondo Finance through this period. They emphasize that company operations should not stagnate due to the founder's passing, and the value of professional management teams lies in maintaining business continuity.

**Supporting Founder's Family**: Legal professionals point out that the founder's family rights should be respected. They argue that in the absence of a clear will or succession plan, immediate family members should logically play a central role in company decisions.

**Investor Concerns**: Some investors have expressed concern about this public power struggle. They believe internal disputes could distract management and affect the company's position in the competitive tokenized market.
