**[Core Summary]**
A report from the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) finds that some US banks have engaged in "debanking" of crypto-related firms, limiting their ability to open accounts and access payment and clearing services. While the report does not cite specific cases, it acknowledges that crypto activity has become a frequent trigger for banks unilaterally ending relationships.

**[Event Details]**
- **Report Content**: The OCC report groups crypto activity alongside oil and gas, firearms and other industries as a key area of concern for bank "debanking."
- **Industry Reaction**: Crypto firms and some civil rights groups have long accused US banks of unilaterally closing crypto-related accounts in the absence of clear regulatory guidance.
- **Policy Signal**: The report is being read as an initial warning from US regulators about excessive bank avoidance of the crypto industry, and may push for clearer compliance guidance.

**[Panoramic Analysis]**
The OCC report's acknowledgment of "debanking" of crypto firms signals that the issue has moved from isolated complaints to a systemic one. The regulator's stance will directly shape the compliance path of US crypto firms and the global flow of crypto capital; in the longer term, it may push banks and the crypto industry toward more standardized cooperation frameworks.
