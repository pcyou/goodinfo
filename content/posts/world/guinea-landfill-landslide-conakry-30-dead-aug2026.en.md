## Core Summary

A severe landslide occurred at a landfill in Conakry, the capital of Guinea, on August 23, killing at least 30 people. The tragedy struck in a waste disposal area on the city's outskirts, with victims mostly impoverished residents who lived or scavenged nearby. The government has declared a national day of mourning and promised a full investigation into the cause of the accident.

## Event Details

According to multiple international media reports including The Guardian, BBC, and Reuters, the landslide occurred at a landfill in the suburbs of Conakry. Due to heavy rainfall in recent days, the waste mound became saturated and collapsed, sending a massive surge of trash and mud that engulfed nearby shantytowns and temporary settlements.

A Guinean government spokesperson said rescue operations are underway, but the complex conditions at the scene have made search and rescue extremely difficult. At least 30 people have been confirmed dead, but the death toll could rise further as several people remain missing.

BBC reports that this incident exposes serious problems with waste management in Guinea's capital. As a metropolis with over 2 million residents, Conakry severely lacks waste treatment infrastructure, with large amounts of garbage dumped untreated, creating significant safety hazards.

## Panoramic Perspective

This tragedy is not an isolated incident but a microcosm of the urban waste management dilemma facing developing countries worldwide. According to UN Environment Programme data, approximately 2 billion tons of municipal solid waste are generated globally each year, with about 33% not properly managed. In many cities across Africa and South Asia, open landfills have become the norm, polluting the environment and threatening the lives of nearby residents.

The 2010 landfill landslide in Lagos, Nigeria, and the 2017 landfill collapse in Colombo, Sri Lanka, both caused significant casualties. Experts point out that the fundamental cause lies in the contradiction between rapid urbanization and insufficient infrastructure investment. In many cities, waste generation far exceeds treatment capacity, while impoverished residents are forced to live or make a living near landfills, creating a vicious cycle.

From a technical perspective, modern waste management requires comprehensive consideration of various approaches including sorting and recycling, sanitary landfills, and waste-to-energy incineration. However, for developing countries with tight budgets, the costs of these solutions are often unbearable. The international community needs to increase aid for urban infrastructure construction in developing countries to help them establish sustainable waste management systems.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Guinean Government**: The government expressed deep condolences to the victims in a statement and promised to provide financial compensation to victims' families. The president said there will be a thorough investigation into the cause of the accident and strengthened safety management of landfills nationwide.

**International Organizations**: The World Health Organization (WHO) expressed concern and offered to provide technical assistance. UN-Habitat pointed out that this incident once again highlights the urgency of urban solid waste management.

**Local Residents**: Citizens of Conakry expressed shock and grief over the incident. Some residents criticized the government for long neglecting waste management issues and called for increased infrastructure investment. Environmental organizations noted that in addition to government responsibility, public environmental awareness also needs to be improved.
