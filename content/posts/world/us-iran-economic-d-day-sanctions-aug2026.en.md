## Core Summary

According to CNN, the US government is set to launch a new round of sanctions against Iran codenamed "Economic D-Day." This unprecedented sanctions package aims to completely cut off Iran's oil export channels and access to international finance. Iran has responded with strong warnings to regional countries against cooperating with US "economic warfare," as Middle East tensions escalate sharply.

## Event Details

US Treasury officials revealed that "Economic D-Day" sanctions will take full effect within days, targeting Iran's oil sales networks, shipping companies, and financial institutions. This represents the largest and broadest economic sanctions imposed on Iran since the US withdrawal from the nuclear deal.

Sources indicate the sanctions will employ "secondary sanctions" mechanisms, meaning third-country entities conducting significant business with Iran could also face punitive measures. This implies that not only Iran itself, but any foreign companies maintaining economic ties with Iran could face restrictions on US market access.

Iran's Foreign Ministry responded swiftly, with its spokesperson calling the unilateral sanctions "a violation of international law and UN Security Council resolutions," while warning neighboring countries against becoming tools of US "economic hegemony." Iran simultaneously stated it would take all necessary measures to protect national economic sovereignty and regional interests.

## Panoramic Perspective

This "Economic D-Day" sanctions package marks a significant escalation in the US "maximum pressure" policy toward Iran. Unlike previous sanctions, this action employs a more aggressive "secondary sanctions" strategy, attempting to completely isolate Iran's economy by threatening third-country entities. This "long-arm jurisdiction" approach is controversial under international law but often proves effective in practice at compelling multinational corporations to comply with US sanctions orders.

From a geopolitical perspective, the timing of these sanctions is noteworthy. The Middle East currently faces multiple overlapping crises: ongoing Israeli-Palestinian conflict, Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping, and instability in Syria. The US choice to impose maximum-scale sanctions on Iran at this moment serves both as a response to Iran's nuclear program developments and as a signal to regional allies of America's "steadfast commitment."

However, the actual effectiveness of sanctions remains questionable. Historical experience suggests unilateral economic sanctions rarely achieve regime change objectives and may instead strengthen nationalist sentiment in targeted countries. Iran has developed relatively sophisticated sanctions evasion mechanisms over decades, including third-country transshipment trade and alternative financial systems. Whether these sanctions can truly sever Iran's economic lifelines remains to be seen in implementation.

## Multiple Perspectives

**US Official Position**: The White House stated these sanctions aim to prevent Iran from developing nuclear weapons and supporting regional "terrorist organizations," emphasizing America reserves "all necessary means" to address Iranian threats. The Treasury Secretary said sanctions target the regime's core revenue sources while avoiding unnecessary harm to Iranian civilians.

**Iranian Government Response**: Tehran strongly condemned US "economic terrorism," calling sanctions violations of international law and humanitarian principles. Iran's president stated the country is fully prepared for economic pressure and will strengthen economic cooperation with regional countries and Eastern powers. The Foreign Ministry warned that any country cooperating with US sanctions will face "serious consequences."

**Regional Countries' Stance**: Gulf Arab states have maintained cautious silence. Analysts note these countries face a dilemma: maintaining security alliances with the US while avoiding complete severance of economic and geopolitical ties with Iran. Iraq, Oman, and other countries with close Iran relations may become important channels for sanctions evasion.

**International Observer Analysis**: European Council on Foreign Relations experts point out that America's unilateral sanctions approach undermines multilateral frameworks and may further strain transatlantic relations. The UN Secretary-General called for restraint from all parties and dialogue-based resolution. China's Foreign Ministry reiterated opposition to unilateral sanctions and "long-arm jurisdiction," advocating maintenance of the nuclear deal framework.
