## Core Summary

According to the BBC, Chinese regulatory authorities are investigating vendors in certain regions who used formaldehyde solution to preserve cabbages. Formaldehyde is a carcinogenic chemical explicitly banned for food processing, and its use in food preservation is illegal in China. As the world's largest cabbage producer, this food safety incident in China has attracted widespread domestic and international attention.

## Event Details

Investigations revealed that some unscrupulous vendors used formaldehyde solution to soak cabbages during transportation and storage to extend shelf life and maintain fresh appearance. Formaldehyde has preservative and bactericidal properties that can keep vegetables fresh at room temperature for extended periods, but its health hazards have been widely documented by the scientific community. Long-term formaldehyde intake can cause respiratory diseases, liver damage, and even increase cancer risk.

China's State Administration for Market Regulation has intervened in the investigation, requiring local authorities to strengthen supervision of agricultural wholesale markets and crack down on illegal use of chemicals. Regulators also urge consumers to purchase vegetables through legitimate channels and be alert to products with abnormal brightness or pungent odors.

After the incident was exposed, multiple provinces and cities launched special inspection campaigns, focusing on checking procurement channels and storage conditions at agricultural wholesale markets. Law enforcement authorities stated they will severely punish vendors who illegally use formaldehyde according to law, and those suspected of committing crimes will be transferred to judicial organs.

## Panoramic Perspective

This formaldehyde cabbage incident reflects deeper problems in China's agricultural product supply chain. From an industry perspective, China's vegetable distribution involves many links and long chains, often passing through multiple intermediaries from field to table. Without cold chain logistics support, some vendors resort to cheap chemical preservatives to reduce costs and minimize losses. This reflects the lag in agricultural product distribution infrastructure construction and inadequate regulatory coverage.

From a regulatory perspective, although China has established a relatively complete food safety legal system, challenges remain at the grassroots implementation level. Agricultural wholesale markets are numerous and widely distributed, with limited regulatory personnel unable to achieve round-the-clock, comprehensive supervision. Additionally, insufficient adoption of rapid detection technology makes it difficult to promptly identify illegal activities.

From a consumer perspective, this incident once again highlights information asymmetry in food safety. Ordinary consumers lack professional testing methods and cannot determine by sight whether vegetables have been chemically treated. Establishing a traceable agricultural product supply chain system that allows consumers to query product sources and handling processes is a long-term solution to such problems.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Regulatory Position**: China's State Administration for Market Regulation stated it will increase sampling inspections of agricultural wholesale markets and improve the application of rapid detection technologies. Simultaneously, it will promote establishing a quality and safety traceability system for agricultural products, achieving full-chain supervision from production to sales. Illegal use of chemicals will be severely punished according to law to create effective deterrence.

**Industry Association Response**: The China Vegetable Marketing Association issued a statement condemning individual vendors' illegal activities for damaging the industry's overall image. The association called for strengthened industry self-discipline and promotion of compliant preservation technologies and materials. It also recommended increased government investment in cold chain logistics infrastructure to fundamentally reduce vendors' reliance on chemical preservatives.

**Food Safety Expert Views**: Nutrition and food safety experts pointed out that formaldehyde preservation is not an isolated case; similar illegal practices have been found in aquatic products, bean products, and other sectors in certain regions. Experts recommend improving food additive usage standards, clearly specifying permitted preservative types and limits. Simultaneously, strengthen science popularization to improve consumers' awareness of food safety risks.

**Consumer Reaction**: After the incident was exposed, some consumers developed panic about easily perishable vegetables like cabbage, with market sales declining in the short term. Consumers called for increased daily regulatory transparency, regular publication of inspection results, and public access to real food safety information. Some consumers also recommended promoting organic vegetable certification and traceability labels to provide more choices for safe consumption.
