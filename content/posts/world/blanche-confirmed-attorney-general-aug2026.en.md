## Core Summary

The US Senate confirmed Todd Blanche as attorney general in a narrow 50-49 vote, with Republican Senator Bill Cassidy casting the deciding vote after a lengthy political standoff. Blanche, who served as Trump's personal lawyer, has been acting attorney general since April.

## Event Details

According to BBC News, the Senate completed its confirmation vote on Friday. After months of political maneuvering, Republican Senator Cassidy announced his support for Blanche, calling him "the best option the president would put forward." The vote ended a prolonged standoff between Senate Republicans and the Trump administration over the attorney general nomination.

The final tally was 50-49, with Republican Senators Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski voting against confirmation. All Democrats opposed the nomination.

Cassidy stated before the vote: "This is not a referendum on President Trump, it is a decision regarding Mr Blanche, in very specific circumstances. All considered, I will vote for Mr Blanche."

Notably, Cassidy had voted to convict Trump during his second impeachment in 2021. After Trump returned to office, he successfully forced Cassidy into retirement by backing a primary challenger for his Louisiana Senate seat.

## Points of Contention

Blanche's confirmation was marked by controversy. Several Republican senators raised concerns about his handling of Jeffrey Epstein-related document releases and his role in establishing Trump's $1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund.

The fund was designed to compensate individuals alleging government persecution, but critics including Murkowski feared it would benefit those prosecuted for the January 6 Capitol attack. Blanche eventually pledged to walk back the fund, though it remains unclear whether this commitment is legally binding.

Collins expressed concerns about Blanche's role in settling Trump's $10 billion IRS lawsuit but ultimately trusted him to provide "candid legal advice" to the president.

## Analysis

Blanche's confirmation represents a significant shift in the Justice Department's power structure. As a former personal lawyer becoming attorney general, his appointment has sparked deep discussions about judicial independence.

Institutionally, this breaks from the traditional pattern of appointing senior prosecutors or legal scholars as attorney general. Blanche's criminal defense background and close personal relationship with the president have raised questions about whether the Justice Department can maintain political neutrality.

The 50-49 vote reflects internal Republican divisions. Collins and Murkowski's opposition shows that even within the party, there are standards for attorney general nominees. Cassidy's deciding vote highlights the complexity of political compromise.

This event will also impact the 2026 midterm elections. Democrats are using the "politicization" of the Justice Department as an attack point, while Republicans emphasize law and order. Blanche's policy direction, particularly regarding the "anti-weaponization" fund, will be a key window into Trump's second-term justice policies.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Supporters**: Cassidy believes Senate confirmation gives Blanche "greater leverage to push for things that should be pushed for" and emphasized reluctance to impede the department's work fighting violent crime.

**Opponents**: Murkowski cited her lack of confidence in Blanche to "check the worst impulses of this administration" and concerns over "politicisation" of the justice department. The unanimous Democratic opposition reflects deeper concerns about judicial independence.

**Media Analysis**: Most mainstream media noted that Blanche's personal relationship with the president made his confirmation unusually difficult. The New York Times commented that the vote was "both a validation of Blanche and a test of Trump's political influence."
