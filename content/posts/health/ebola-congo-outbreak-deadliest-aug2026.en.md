## Core Summary

The ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) has set a historical record, becoming the most severe since the Ebola virus was first discovered in 1976. According to The Guardian, the death toll continues to climb, placing enormous pressure on the healthcare system. The World Health Organization (WHO) and the international community are urgently coordinating response efforts, but local conflicts and instability pose serious challenges to epidemic prevention work.

## Panoramic Perspective

The severity of this Ebola outbreak has exceeded expectations, exposing numerous shortcomings in the global public health emergency response system. First, epidemic control in conflict zones remains a worldwide challenge. The long-standing armed conflict in eastern Congo not only directly threatens medical personnel safety but also leads to massive population displacement, accelerating virus transmission.

Second, vaccine hesitancy has played a negative role in the epidemic. Some local communities harbor suspicions toward Western aid and refuse vaccination, creating additional obstacles for prevention efforts. Building community trust has become a key factor in controlling the epidemic.

From a global perspective, this outbreak once again reminds the international community that infectious diseases know no borders. In an era of globalization, a public health crisis in one region can quickly escalate into a global threat. Strengthening global health governance, enhancing medical capabilities in developing countries, and establishing more effective early warning mechanisms are essential paths to preventing similar crises.

Additionally, climate change and ecological environmental destruction may exacerbate infectious disease risks. Deforestation and wildlife habitat destruction increase human contact with virus-carrying animals, which is an important background factor for the frequent outbreaks of zoonotic diseases like Ebola.

## Multiple Perspectives

**WHO Position**: The WHO Director-General called on the international community to increase aid to Congo, emphasizing the importance of "solidarity in fighting the epidemic." The organization has activated an emergency response mechanism to coordinate resources from various countries to support Congo's epidemic prevention work.

**Local Community Voices**: Some Congolese citizens expressed dissatisfaction with the government and international organizations' epidemic prevention measures, believing aid has been insufficiently timely and adequate. Community leaders called for respect of local cultural traditions and more grounded approaches in epidemic prevention communication.

**International Aid Donor Perspectives**: Multiple countries and non-governmental organizations have pledged assistance but emphasized the need for the Congolese government to strengthen transparency and coordination. Some donors worry that funds and materials may not reach those most in need due to corruption or conflict.

**Public Health Expert Recommendations**: Experts recommend adopting a "comprehensive prevention and control" strategy that integrates Ebola prevention with basic medical services, while strengthening community participation and health education. In the long term, investment in Congo's medical infrastructure construction is needed to enhance its capacity to independently respond to public health crises.
