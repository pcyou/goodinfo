## Core Summary

Thirty US states have filed a joint lawsuit against Meta, alleging that its Instagram and Facebook platforms have caused serious harm to the physical and mental health of young users, and demanding a complete overhaul of how these platforms operate. BBC reports that if Meta loses this trial, Instagram and Facebook could face "permanent changes." This lawsuit is considered a landmark case in holding tech giants accountable for protecting minors in the United States.

## Event Details

According to BBC Technology, the lawsuit was jointly filed by attorneys general from thirty states, accusing Meta of knowingly failing to take adequate measures to prevent the addictive and mental health risks its products pose to teenagers, despite being fully aware of them. The lawsuit cites internal Meta research data showing the company was aware of the link between Instagram and teenage anxiety, depression, and body image issues.

The lawsuit asks the court to force Meta to implement the following reforms: eliminate algorithmic recommendation mechanisms targeting minors, ban addictive design features like "infinite scroll" for young users, establish stricter age verification systems, and disclose more research data about the platforms' impact on youth.

Meta responded by stating it has invested significant resources in youth protection, including launching parental monitoring tools and restricting teen advertising features. The company said it will vigorously defend itself and believes its current products comply with relevant legal requirements.

## Panoramic Perspective

The significance of this lawsuit extends far beyond a single case and could reshape the regulatory framework for the entire social media industry. First, the joint action by thirty states demonstrates rare bipartisan consensus in the US on youth online protection. Regardless of the outcome, it will set a precedent for other tech platforms.

From a technical perspective, if the court requires eliminating algorithmic recommendations and addictive design, Meta would be forced to fundamentally restructure its core product logic. Instagram's Explore page and Facebook's news feed algorithms are the cornerstones of the company's business model — if forced to change, it would directly impact its advertising revenue model.

The deeper implication is that this lawsuit could drive federal-level youth online protection legislation in the US. Currently, the US lacks unified federal law regulating social media's impact on minors, with state regulations varying widely. The outcome of this lawsuit could provide important reference for Congressional legislation.

Additionally, this case has triggered a re-examination of the "technology neutrality" principle. Traditionally, tech companies argue they are not responsible for user-posted content, but if courts determine that platform design itself is harmful, the "technology neutrality" shield may be weakened, requiring tech companies to bear greater responsibility for product design choices.

## Multiple Perspectives

**Plaintiff Position**: State attorneys general emphasize that Meta placed profits above youth health and must be held legally accountable. They cite extensive parent testimony and medical research showing social media addiction has become a public health crisis.

**Meta Defense**: The company's legal team argues the lawsuit exaggerates the platforms' impact on youth and notes Meta has proactively implemented multiple protection measures. The company also claims the First Amendment protects its algorithmic recommendation rights.

**Tech Industry Concerns**: Other social media companies are closely monitoring the case. The industry worries that if Meta loses, it could trigger a wave of lawsuits against all tech platforms, significantly increasing compliance costs.

**Parent and Education Community Response**: Parent groups broadly support the lawsuit, believing it is necessary to protect children. Educators also welcomed it, noting social media addiction has seriously affected student attention and mental health.

**Privacy Advocate Perspectives**: Some privacy rights organizations expressed concern about stricter age verification, arguing it could infringe on user anonymity and privacy rights, especially in discussions of sensitive topics.
